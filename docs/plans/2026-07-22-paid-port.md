# Paid port — the growth engine, tracked over time (concrete plan)

> **Status:** Plan for owner (Tim) approval — 2026-07-22. NO implementation until approved.
> **Anchors:** product-contract-reset Part 0 (paid contract) + Phase E (paid consolidation); the data-board free scan (now shipped); Tim's thesis — *"paid = the growth engine: what keywords to actually rank for, tracked over time as the discoverability score rises, plus referrers / competitor lessons / customer communities."*
> **Method:** corpus-first (extend the paid-surface rubric), capability-ledger per shared producer, live-verify each phase on a real paid scan, one-path/one-lever.

---

## 0. What the audit found (grounded, `file:line`)

The single most important finding, and it makes the port cheap:

1. **Paid ALREADY computes + persists the full free market model.** `full-scan.ts:824` calls the SAME `gatherFreeSearchVisibility` and persists `report_payload.searchVisibility.categoryCard / nicheCard / categoryOpportunities` (`:845`) — identical to free. **But `/app` renders only the scalar `searchVisibility.score`** (`lib/app/dashboard-hero-props.ts` → `dashboard-hero.tsx`). The rich "what to rank for" cards render **only on the free/shareable `/scan` report**. → Porting is a RENDER job on `/app`, not a compute job.
2. **"What to rank for" exists TWICE, independently computed:** the free `categoryOpportunities`/`categoryCard.gaps` (pipeline A, `/scan`) AND the intel `keywords.gaps` (pipeline B, `gatherKeywordGap`, `/app`). They can diverge. Must become one.
3. **Two pipelines that never call each other:** A = `report_payload` (write-once at scan, `full-scan.ts`); B = `/api/app/intel` (recomputed **on every paid tab load**, metered DFS/Tavily/LLM via `costedIntelStep`). B is the live `/app` surface (referrers, cohort, keyword-gap, demand pockets, communities, plan).
4. **Tracking over time:** overall `score_snapshots` (rendered: DashboardHero trend + ProgressView) + coarse weekly `market_snapshots` exist. **Per-keyword rank week-over-week does NOT exist** — `track_rank` is one-shot inside action verification only. → This is the real new build for "tracked as the score rises."
5. **Large write-only surface** (persisted by `full-scan.ts`, rendered ONLY by orphaned `components/report/*-section.tsx`): `competitiveLandscape`, `channelOpportunities.keywordClusters`, `creatorsToReach` (**`audienceProxy` hardcoded 0** — the 2nd YouTube call is never made), `reviewThemes`/`strengthsAndWeaknesses`, `surfaces`, `icpSignals`. Off-contract → cut/reclaim.
6. Referrers + competitor-referrers + customer-communities ALREADY render on `/app` (pipeline B: CompetitorsView + CustomersView). Gap: no explicit competitor-referrer **"lessons"** framing.

Arc 2 is internal de-duplication that does NOT gate selling (per the owner's standing priority: ship the funnel to validate, then consolidate).

## 2. The fixes/improvements this closes (what gets better)

- **The paid scan currently shows a number where it should show a plan.** `/app` renders `searchVisibility.score` and drops the market/opportunity model that's already sitting in the payload. P1 turns that into the growth engine.
- **Two "what to rank for" models can disagree** (free `categoryOpportunities` vs intel `keywords.gaps`). P3 collapses to one spine (one-path rule; ledger-enforced).
- **No rank-over-time.** The whole pitch is "watch your keywords climb" — P2 builds the missing time series.
- **Money spent on data nobody sees:** creators (audienceProxy≡0), reviewThemes/strengths, competitiveLandscape — all write-only. E2 reclaims/cuts it.
- **Paid intel recomputed (and re-billed) on every tab load** — E1 makes it compute-once/render-many.

## 3. Decisions — RESOLVED by owner (2026-07-22)

1. **Canonical "what to rank for" = free `categoryOpportunities` spine + PAID rival-gap enrichment → feeds the action plan.** ✅ The rival fetch (the added cost) is **paid-only**: look at the competitors, see what THEY rank for, use that as context for the action plan. The free spine stays cheap; the rival gap is the paid depth.
2. **Rank tracking = the ACTION PLAN's keywords, week-over-week.** ✅ Critical. The plan's target keywords are the tracked series — "these are the moves, here's them climbing."
3. **Write-only cut — Arc 2 fast-follow** (explained below; owner deciding scope after understanding it). Default: cut the clearly-dead (creators/`audienceProxy≡0`) opportunistically, heavy consolidation post-sell.
4. **Ship to sell — YES, top priority, 24–48h.** ✅ Get users upgrading to paid ASAP; marketing runs right after. Bar: **bulletproof, complete value end-to-end.** Arc 2 is internal quality, post-first-paying-users.

## 3a. The through-line requirement (owner, 2026-07-22) — the action plan is ALWAYS specific + actionable, NEVER general

Every plan item — free AND paid — must push the user to a **concrete, executable move**: *create this blog/page targeting this keyword (this volume, your position #N), post it on this platform, because this competitor ranks for it.* Never "improve your content," "increase visibility," or any general advice. This is the value spine of the whole product (free fixes already meet it — "Create a page targeting 'X' · 1,600/mo"; the paid plan must too, enriched with the rival "why"). **Durable home:** a rubric rule on the paid plan surface (corpus-first) that every rendered action names a specific artifact (keyword/content-type/platform) + a real number, and no action is generic prose. Encoded when P1/P3 land; watched fail first.

## 3b. Conceptual consolidation — one spine across the whole app

The whole product is ONE spine: **discoverability = the specific searches your buyers make, and the specific moves to win them.** Free and paid are the same spine at two depths, so the funnel consolidates cleanly:

- **Free** = *what* to rank for: your category (market size), your niche, where you stand, 3 specific moves. The user sees real, useful, specific info → the wow.
- **Paid** = *how* to win it, informed by rivals, tracked over time: the full opportunity set, enriched with what competitors rank for (the rival gap = the "why this move"), turned into a specific content/distribution action plan, with each target keyword's rank climbing week-over-week as the score rises.

Everything that isn't on this spine (creators, review-themes, competitive-landscape prose) is off-contract noise — the write-only surfaces below. Cutting them IS the consolidation.
