## Why does AI visibility matter for your business?

Buyers now ask ChatGPT and Google's AI what to use before they search further, and the answer names only a handful of companies. If yours isn't one of them, that buyer never meets you, and nothing on your side tells you it happened. You have three ways to change that: passive ranking, reactive scanning, or active page publishing. Which one fits depends on how much time you have, what budget you can spend, and how many rivals are already being named instead of you.

## What are the three approaches to getting found by AI?

Each approach trades speed for cost differently, so the right pick depends on how urgent the problem is.

**Passive ranking.** You publish content and hope Google and AI engines discover and cite it eventually. This costs little upfront but can take months or years, with no guarantee rivals won't occupy that space first.

**Reactive scanning.** You run a free scan to see where AI answers and Google search send buyers to your rivals instead of you. This shows the gap, but it's a single measurement — useful for diagnosis, not a fix on its own.

**Active page publishing.** You write and publish pages built to answer the exact questions buyers ask AI and Google.

## How do you choose the right approach for your market?

Start with a scan if you're unsure how wide the gap is — it costs nothing and takes under a minute. Rivals shown by name in AI answers are a sign that passive ranking won't close the gap fast enough on its own.

If buyers in your market consult AI before they search Google, waiting months for passive ranking to work means ceding that ground. Ongoing visibility needs ongoing measurement, not a one-time snapshot, which is why weekly re-measurement matters more than a single report.

Choose passive ranking if you have months to spare and few rivals publishing toward AI engines. Choose reactive scanning if you need to see the shape of the problem before committing.
