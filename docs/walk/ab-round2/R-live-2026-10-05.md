## What's the difference between ReachKit and AEO Engine?

ReachKit measures how your business shows up in AI answers and Google search with a scan that produces a Discoverability Score. AEO Engine is a managed content service at $1,597/mo or $2,997/mo that publishes content built to improve that visibility over time.

## What does each cost?

ReachKit is one plan at one price: a page written and published for your site every day, with your market re-measured every week. AEO Engine has two tiers—Growth at $1,597/mo and Scale at $2,997/mo—each bundling content, authority work, tracking, and strategist support into a managed program.

AEO Engine instead sells two tiers. Growth is described as a focused managed program for one site, with consistent content, authority, AI visibility tracking, and strategist support, priced at $1,597/mo. Scale is the higher-velocity version — full-site optimization, doubled content output, and broader authority coverage — at $2,997/mo (both from [AEO Engine Pricing Plans](https://aeoengine.ai/pricing)).

The spread between the two matters on its own. Going from Growth to Scale roughly doubles the monthly cost, in exchange for doubled content output and full-site rather than single-site optimization. A business deciding between AEO Engine tiers is really deciding how much ground it needs covered at once, not just how much content it wants.

ReachKit's single-plan structure removes that tier decision entirely. There's one page a day, every day, and one weekly re-measurement of your market. You're not picking between a narrower and a broader program — the scope is fixed, and the only variable is how long you run it.

What this means for budgeting: AEO Engine's lower tier starts above a thousand dollars a month before any work begins beyond the program itself, and the step to full-site coverage costs significantly more. ReachKit's cost structure is comparatively simple to reason about, because it's one page, one plan, one price point, with the re-measurement included rather than sold as an upgrade.

## How do they measure your AI visibility differently?

ReachKit's method is a scan. Point it at a site and it shows how that business shows up in AI answers and Google search — and where its rivals are found instead ([reachkit.app/scans](https://reachkit.app/scans?sort=highest&shown=32)). That scan produces a Discoverability Score — for example, reachkit.app itself was measured on 28 September 2026 and scored 19 out of 100. The score is a single number you can track over time and compare against competitors in the same market, re-measured weekly as part of the paid plan.

AEO Engine doesn't publish a measurement product in the same sense. Its own pages describe a managed program: content, authority building, and AI visibility tracking bundled into a monthly service, with a strategist attached ([AEO Engine Pricing Plans](https://aeoengine.ai/pricing)). Tracking is a feature inside the program, not something you get standalone before committing to spend.

That difference matters for sequencing. With ReachKit you get the number first, independent of any commitment to content work, and you decide afterward what to do about it. With AEO Engine, visibility tracking arrives bundled with the content and authority work already underway — you don't separate the measuring from the doing.

The gap this closes is the one in the title: knowing where you're invisible to AI systems versus acting on it. A business that has never been scanned doesn't know if its problem is thin content, missing structure, or simply not existing where AI answers pull from. ReachKit's scan surfaces that gap directly, by market, against named rivals. AEO Engine's tracking instead reports on progress against a program it's already running for you.

If your real question is "where do we stand versus competitors right now," that's a measurement question — answered by a scan, not a managed program. If your question is "we know we're behind, now what," that's a different need, and tracking becomes a secondary feature of a larger content engagement rather than the main tool.

## When should you pick ReachKit over AEO Engine?

Pick ReachKit when the question you're asking is "where do we stand," not "how do we fix it yet." The scan shows how any business shows up in AI answers and Google search, and where its rivals are found instead ([reachkit.app/scans](https://reachkit.app/scans?sort=highest&shown=32)), which is the right tool when you don't yet have a number to act on.

It suits a business that wants a baseline before committing budget. A Discoverability Score, like the 19 out of 100 reachkit.app itself measured on 28 September 2026, gives you something concrete to track — a single figure you can watch move over weeks rather than a program you're already paying for regardless of whether the number moves.

It also suits ongoing competitor tracking rather than a one-off check. Because the market is re-measured every week as part of the plan ([reachkit.app/pricing](https://reachkit.app/pricing)), you can watch not just your own score but where rivals are found instead of you, on a rolling basis, without re-running a scan yourself each time.

And it suits testing before a bigger commitment. Because ReachKit is one plan with one page written and published for your site every day, you're not locking into a tier or a strategist relationship to find out whether daily published content and weekly measurement are even the right lever for your site. You get the output and the number, and you decide afterward whether a larger managed spend — like AEO Engine's Growth or Scale tiers — is worth adding on top.

Where ReachKit doesn't fit is a business that already knows its score is bad and wants someone else running a full content and authority program for it. That's not what the scan or the daily page are built to replace — they tell you where you stand and keep publishing a page, but they aren't a managed strategist engagement. If what you need is consistent, higher-volume content output across a whole site with someone else doing the planning, that's a different kind of spend than a single daily page represents.

## When should you pick AEO Engine instead?

Pick AEO Engine when you already know you need more content than one page a day, and you want someone else running the program. Its Growth tier is described as a focused managed program for one site, with consistent content, authority, AI visibility tracking, and strategist support, for $1,597/mo ([AEO Engine Pricing Plans](https://aeoengine.ai/pricing)).

It suits a business with budget set aside for managed work rather than a tool it runs itself. Strategist support on Growth means someone else is making the calls on what content goes out and why, which is a different kind of spend than a fixed daily page — it's priced as a program, not a single output.

It also suits a business that needs full-site coverage, not just a single page added daily. The Scale tier, at $2,997/mo, covers full-site optimization with doubled content output and broader authority coverage ([AEO Engine Pricing Plans](https://aeoengine.ai/pricing)) — roughly double the Growth price, for roughly double the reach. That tier step is worth naming on its own: if one page a day on one part of a site isn't enough ground, Scale is built for the broader version of the same problem, at the cost that implies.

And it suits a business past the baseline question. If you've already measured where you stand — through a scan or otherwise — and the answer is clearly "behind, and by a lot," the next decision isn't whether to measure again, it's how much managed output to buy. AEO Engine's two tiers are built around that decision: one site or full site, consistent output or doubled output, at two fixed monthly prices.

Where it doesn't fit is a business that hasn't yet checked where it stands, or wants to test the water before signing on to $1,597 or $2,997 a month. Both AEO Engine tiers are ongoing managed programs, not single scans, so the commitment starts well above a one-off check — better suited to a business ready to commit budget than one still deciding if it has a problem worth fixing.
