## How do AI answers and Google search send buyers to your rivals instead of you?

Buyers ask ChatGPT and Google's AI what to use. The answer they get names companies. If your company isn't one of them, that buyer never meets you — they go straight to a rival instead.

This happens on your own site without you knowing. Nothing on your side tells you when it happens. A competitor can be named in an AI answer for a question your business could answer better, and you'd have no record of it.

Google itself is still the largest search engine, processing an estimated 16.4 billion searches a day ([position.digital](https://www.position.digital/blog/ai-seo-statistics/)). That volume means the gap between being named and not being named plays out constantly, not as a rare event. Every day buyers type a question, Google or an AI answer responds, and somebody gets picked.

What decides who gets picked is whether a page exists that answers the exact question being asked, using facts the AI or search engine can read. Not a brochure page. Not a homepage that talks about the company in general terms. A page that matches the buyer's question directly.

Most small business sites don't have that page. They have a services page, an about page, maybe a blog that hasn't been updated in a year. The specific questions buyers ask — the ones that name a rival by comparison — go unanswered on your domain, so the answer comes from somewhere else.

Closing that gap means finding which questions are being asked, checking whether your site answers them, and publishing the ones that are missing. That's the mechanism behind every AI answer and every Google result: existing content that matches the question wins the spot.

## Why does a free scan show you where AI answers and Google send your buyers?

A free scan reads your site, your access rules, and your market, then checks the questions buyers ask. It finds your rivals, asks AI those questions, and scores what it sees — so you get a concrete picture instead of a guess.

The scan starts by reading your own site and your access rules. That matters because an AI reader blocked from parts of your site can't surface what's there, even if the content is good. The scan checks this first because it's a foundation problem: fix the content all you want, if the reader can't get in, nothing changes.

It then finds your market and your rivals, and checks the twelve biggest searches in that market. This is where you see who else is competing for the same buyer questions — not in the abstract, but by domain name, on searches that get asked.

From there it asks AI the twelve questions directly and records what comes back. This is the part that shows you what's happening when a buyer asks ChatGPT or Google's AI what to use. If a rival's name comes back and yours doesn't, that's a specific, visible gap rather than a suspicion.

All of this rolls up into a Discoverability Score, with every report published as a permanent public link. Anyone can see how a business shows up in AI answers and Google search, and where its rivals are found instead — yours included. That openness is the point: the scan doesn't just tell you privately, it shows the same page a buyer or competitor could see.

Every scan comes with your first page, proposed and free. So the scan doesn't stop at diagnosis — it hands you the first concrete fix alongside the score, ready to review.
