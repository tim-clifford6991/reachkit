## What does "AI SEO software" actually mean?

The label covers two different jobs sold under one name: tools that measure whether you're being found in AI answers and search, and tools that write or publish content meant to earn that visibility. Semrush One does measurement at scale across many prompts and regions. ReachKit pairs a narrower measurement with a daily publishing habit aimed at specific gaps.

### Measurement tools

These track how often your brand or your rivals get cited in AI answer engines and search results. [OneLittleWeb](https://onelittleweb.com/top-tools/best-ai-seo-tools/) reports Semrush One "tracks over 100 million prompts across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Perplexity, and AI Overviews in six regions: US, UK, Canada, Australia, India, and Spain."

### Content and publishing tools

These generate or automate the pages meant to earn those citations. [SEO.AI](https://seo.ai/) describes itself as "the AI SEO agent that creates ranking content, publishes it to your website, builds relevant backlinks, and keeps your visibility growing — 24/7."

## How accurate is the data these tools give you?

Accuracy varies by tool and by how a reviewer checked it, and none of these figures are a promise. One [OneLittleWeb](https://onelittleweb.com/top-tools/best-ai-seo-tools/) reviewer says they "cross-checked Semrush data against GSC for six months across multiple client accounts" and found "the variance was typically under 10%, which is solid for any third-party tool." The same reviewer sets a personal cutoff: "If keyword volumes are consistently off by more than 15-20%, or the tool suggests optimizations that contradict GSC data, I move on."

### Why this matters for AI-answer tracking specifically

AI answers shift more often and less predictably than search rankings, which makes any single accuracy figure a snapshot, not a guarantee. ReachKit's own terms state its scores are "measured from public signals — your site's HTML, search rankings, search volumes and the AI answers we read — using fixed rules," and that "none of this is a guarantee of rankings, citations, traffic, revenue or any other outcome."

## What results do these tools claim, and how should you read them?

Some vendors publish outcome numbers from specific client work, and those numbers describe one account, not a typical result. [OneLittleWeb](https://onelittleweb.com/top-tools/best-ai-seo-tools/) reports: "One AI SaaS client grew SEO traffic 76% and LLM traffic 1,900% in a year with us." [SEO.AI](https://seo.ai/) states "most see growing traffic after 1–2 months," without naming a customer or a study.

Treat any single-client number as exactly that — one account, one period, one starting point. ReachKit doesn't publish a comparable growth figure; its own terms state plainly that measurement is "guidance, not guarantees."

## Who is behind these tools, and does that matter?

Experience claims are common in this space, and they're worth reading alongside the product rather than instead of it. The [OneLittleWeb](https://onelittleweb.com/top-tools/best-ai-seo-tools/) author states, "I've run SEO campaigns at OneLittleWeb since 2018" and has "tested more than 40 AI-powered SEO tools on real client accounts" over the past three years. [SEO.AI](https://seo.ai/) says its "team is made up of some of Denmark's leading specialists in SEO and organic growth," with "several team members" having "more than 20 years of SEO experience."

None of that tells you whether a tool fits your specific gap — a large marketing team and a five-person B2B shop need different things from the same feature list.

## How does ReachKit handle this?

[ReachKit](https://reachkit.app/) starts with a free scan showing "where AI answers and Google search send buyers to your rivals instead of you," built from facts read on your own live pages rather than an industry benchmark. Onboarding, per its founder, "confirms rivals and category and connects where pages publish: a hosted subdomain, or their WordPress," and every Monday, per the founder, "the market is re-measured and the next pages are picked."

The product then writes one page a day targeting a specific question your market asks where a rival gets named and you don't, grounding each page in facts from your own site. Every page "waits a day for you to read it — or stop it — before it goes live." The subscription is "€49 a month, VAT included," billed monthly, and can be cancelled "any time from Settings, in one click," per the [pricing page](https://reachkit.app/pricing).

ReachKit doesn't track prompts across six regions the way Semrush One does, and it doesn't publish a GSC-variance benchmark the way the OneLittleWeb reviewer describes doing for Semrush. It is a narrower tool built around one habit — one page a day — rather than a full measurement suite.

### Where ReachKit fits, and where it doesn't

ReachKit fits a business that already knows, roughly, who its rivals are and wants a steady, checkable process for closing specific AI-visibility gaps without hiring a writer. It's a weaker fit if what you need is broad multi-region prompt tracking, deep backlink analysis, or a platform your whole marketing team logs into for reporting — those are jobs the larger platforms above are built around.

## What should you check before you commit to any of these tools?

Read the terms and privacy pages, not just the homepage, before handing over a card number. Check whether a vendor names a specific data source for its rankings and AI-answer data — ReachKit names DataForSEO as the source for "search rankings, search volumes and AI-answer data used while measuring." Check the cancellation terms: ReachKit is month-to-month, cancel any time, with payments already made not refunded unless the law requires it. Check who writes the words that go live under your name — ReachKit states pages are "grounded in facts from your own live pages, names no invented author, avoids anything on your never-claim list," while its language model is used specifically "to label your market and propose the questions it asks, and to write the pages," with every fact's source recorded.
