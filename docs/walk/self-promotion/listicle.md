## What should you look for when choosing a tool?

Buyers ask ChatGPT and Google's AI what to use, and the answer names a handful of companies. If yours isn't one of them, that buyer never meets you — and [nothing on your side tells you it happened](https://reachkit.app/). So the first thing to check is whether a tool measures that gap at all, or only writes content and hopes.

A few criteria matter more than the rest:

- **Does it measure before it writes?** A tool that shows a discoverability score or tracks AI answers gives you a baseline. Without one, you can't tell if anything moved.
- **What does it publish?** Some tools hand you a document; others publish directly to your own domain. Check which, since a document still needs someone on your side to put it live.
- **How much content, how often?** Daily, weekly or monthly output changes how fast a gap closes — and how much review work lands on you each month.
- **Who reviews it before it goes live?** A page written in your name should be one you can stop before it publishes, not after the fact, once it's already on your domain.
- **What's the actual price, and is it published?** Some vendors list a flat monthly fee you can check in a minute. Others quote custom pricing only after a sales call, which matters if you want to compare costs quickly across several options.
- **Does it re-measure, or just publish once?** A market shifts week to week as rivals publish their own pages. A tool that re-checks weekly tells you whether the new pages are working, not just whether they went live.

## What does AEO Engine do, and who is it for?

AEO Engine is built around volume. Its pricing page states [60 on-brand SEO/AEO articles per month with supporting media](https://aeoengine.ai/pricing), and its platform page describes a range of [30-60 Articles/Month - Expertly researched and written in your brand's voice](https://aeoengine.ai/platform). That's considerably more output per month than a one-a-day model, which suits a company that wants a large content library fast, or has multiple product lines each needing separate coverage across its own pages and categories. The trade-off: more volume means more to review each month. A team producing up to 60 articles needs a process for checking brand voice and factual accuracy at that scale, and AEO Engine's own pages don't describe a built-in review window before publishing, unlike a daily, smaller-batch approach.

## What does Meltwater do, and who is it for?

Meltwater is the largest and most established name of the three, and its pricing page states plainly: [Meltwater uses tailored pricing rather than a one-size-fits-all list](https://www.meltwater.com/en/pricing). That fits a company already running media monitoring at scale, or one that wants a single vendor covering PR monitoring alongside AI discovery, and that has the budget and sales process to negotiate a custom contract. It suits a larger team with a procurement process already built for enterprise software, where a quoted price is normal practice rather than a barrier. The limit, by its own pricing page: there's no published monthly figure to compare against a flat-fee option, so a smaller team can't size the cost without a sales conversation first, which slows down an early comparison.

## Which one should you choose?

If you're a small or mid-sized B2B SaaS company that wants a flat, published monthly price, daily output, and a chance to review each page before it goes live, ReachKit fits that case directly — it's built for a founder who owns the site and wants buyer questions answered without managing a content team.

If you need high volume — 30 to 60 articles a month across several product lines — and have an in-house team to review brand voice at that scale, AEO Engine is the better pick for that specific case, by its own stated output on its platform page.

If you're already running enterprise media monitoring and want one vendor, with budget for a negotiated contract rather than a flat monthly fee, Meltwater is the better pick there, given its own stated approach to tailored pricing.

None of these is the right choice for every buyer. The deciding factor is usually simple: how much content you need per month, whether you want a published price or a quoted one, and who on your team reviews each page before it reaches a buyer.
