## What are the steps to get named in AI answers?

The process runs from a free scan to a daily publishing habit. Each step produces something you can check before moving to the next.

1. **Run a free scan of your domain.** This takes under a minute and needs no account. It shows where AI answers and Google search send buyers to your rivals instead of you.

3. **Confirm your market and rivals.** The scan suggests a market and a set of competitors drawn from your domain, and you approve or adjust the list.
4. **Let the first page publish on your schedule.** One live dashboard showed a page queued with the label ["What buyers ask before they switch, answered"](https://reachkit.app/), publishing in 6 hours 12 minutes unless the owner said otherwise.
5. **Use the 24-hour veto window.** Every page waits a day before it goes live, so you can read it or stop it first.
6. **Watch pages accumulate day by day.** One dashboard tracked 17 pages published against a target of writing one a day.
7. **Track AI answers directly.** The same dashboard showed AI answers at 2 out of 12 for that account's tracked questions, a number meant to move as pages go live.

9. **Let your market get re-measured weekly.** Rivals change their own pages too, so the comparison is rebuilt every week rather than left to go stale.

## How do you check it worked?

You check three numbers: your Discoverability Score, your AI answers count, and how many searches you appear in. All three should move up, week over week, not stay flat.

One dashboard showed searches you appear in climbing from 12 to 31 to 44 to 61 to 96 to 128 across six weekly readings, alongside a Discoverability Score of 62 marked up 8 points for the week. That is the shape of working: a rising line, not a single big jump. The same account showed AI answers at 2 out of 12, a number to watch separately from search appearances, since the two move for different reasons.

A flat line for more than a week or two after pages start publishing points to a mismatch between the questions your pages answer and the questions buyers ask. That is where to look first.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

The first mistake is treating this as a one-time fix. A scan tells you where you stand on the day you run it, and the score can move the next week as pages publish or rivals change their own pages. The loop is a scan, a report, and a page going live, run weekly, not once.

The second mistake is publishing pages that guess at what AI wants rather than answering a real buyer question with facts from your own site. A page that names no specific feature, price, or step gives AI nothing concrete to repeat back to a buyer.

The third mistake is skipping the review window. Every page gets 24 hours before it goes live specifically so you can catch something wrong before a buyer sees it, and skipping that check defeats the point of having it.

The fourth mistake is ignoring your rivals' own movement. Your market gets re-measured weekly because competitors publish too, and a gap that closed last month can reopen if you stop watching it.
