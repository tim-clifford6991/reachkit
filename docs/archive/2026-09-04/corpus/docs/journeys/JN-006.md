# JN-006 — The domain owner — correcting or removing a public report about my business

Someone the owner has never met ran a free scan on their domain and shared the
link. The report is a permanent public verdict about their business, written
without them, and it may be wrong about the market it judged them in. This
persona is not JN-001's stranger scanning their own site, nor JN-002's buyer:
they arrive with no interest in the product and one demand — that a public page
about their business is either right or gone. The journey succeeds when the owner
holds that page: it is measured against the market they actually operate in, or
it is gone from its address.

Steps 2 and 3 are not two journeys. Correction and removal are the two
dispositions of one demand — one persona, one outcome, decided at the step they
reach it — and splitting them would copy step 1 into a second journey, which is
the second copy rule 2.4 forbids. The fork JN-001 refuses is a different one: a
different persona reaching a different outcome.

In v1 the two dispositions are open on different terms, and the journey is honest
about it. The correction at step 2 is offered to whoever is reading the report,
once per free scan; nothing here proves that this reader owns the domain, and a
correction a stranger already spent is one this owner no longer has. Removal at
steps 3 and 4 is a written request a person judges, not a control on the page.
Proving control of a domain — and everything that would rest on it, including a
correction that outranks a stranger's and a self-service takedown — is deferred
to v1.1; until then this persona's authority over the page about them is a
request, not a right they can exercise unaided.
