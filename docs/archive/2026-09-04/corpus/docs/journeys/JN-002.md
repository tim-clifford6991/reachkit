# JN-002 — Buying ReachKit and starting it

A founder has decided to buy. Most arrive from their free report at
`/scan/{domain}` knowing the answer to the question that brought them: buyers
asking AI about their market are told about their rivals. They arrive already
convinced, with nothing invested but an email address. What they will not
tolerate is work before value — an account, a password, a configuration screen,
a sales call. So the journey trades in the other order: they pay first, and the
product asks for the only three things it cannot derive (their market, their
competitors, how and where pages publish) once, after the money has moved.

Two kinds of buyer walk this path, and what setup asks depends on the purchase
rather than the person. The common one arrives from their own report on their
own domain, so we already hold their domain, their market and their rivals:
setup shows them the domain that report measured to confirm, and the rest is
three confirmations rather than three answers. The other bought with nothing
measured for the domain their account will use — from a price surface with no
report behind it (a shared link, a pricing page, a conversation), or from a
report that measured someone else's site, since a report reaches them shared as
easily as run. They give their own site address at setup, and they answer the
three rather than confirming them: nothing measured for another site follows
into their account, so the market is theirs to state and the rivals are drawn
from what they state. From there both are the same customer, and neither is
asked to know anything about SEO to get through this.

Not everyone walks it in one sitting. A founder who stops partway is neither
paused nor written off: what they bought keeps running, and the product asks
them back to the one screen they left until they finish it.

One founder on this path does not get there unaided. Where the product cannot
reach the site address they give, setup will not take it, and no answer they can
type moves them on — this is the single point in the whole product where a paying
customer needs a person. What they are owed there is not a dead end: the refusal
names a way to reach one, says they may cancel without finishing, and keeps every
answer they have already given for when they return.

The journey succeeds when the founder is inside the app with the product
already working on their behalf — and it still succeeds when the deep pass
comes back thin. A measurement that found little releases them into the app
with an honest sentence about what it could not see; it never holds them on a
progress screen, and it never invents a page, a rival or a number to make the
arrival look better than it was. A thin pass owes them one thing more: how much
work it actually found. A founder whose pass turned up less than a month of pages
hears it here, at the moment it is known, rather than three weeks later from a
calendar that has run out — and hearing it is never a condition of getting in.
