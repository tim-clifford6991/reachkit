# Evidence — REQ-099 (the landing page)

## The owner's statement

Owner, verbatim, 2026-09-02, classified under §9 as a change to an existing
artifact (`BUILD.md` §3):

> "The landing page is obviously much too light. This is the first touchpoint
> users have with our application. Immediately above the fold, the user should
> see our primary tagline, sub line, CTA, and a enticing image/component giving
> them an immediate feel for what the app is and looks like. This is then
> followed by a product demo video. The whole page should walk the user through
> the journey of 'why should they care', 'what it does for them', 'what they need
> to do to start today'"

## What it supersedes

`BUILD.md` §3, verbatim, the whole of what the source says about the landing:

>     /                     Landing: one field, one button. "See what AI tells
>                           buyers about your market — and write your way in."

The tagline survives — it is approved copy and only the owner replaces a
customer-visible string (§1, decision-rights table). "One field, one button"
stops being a description of the whole page; what it still constrains — the scan
form, or every control on the page — is the open question below, not settled
here.

## The conflict with REQ-001, and why it is the owner's

REQ-001 criterion 1, verbatim: "Given a visitor on the landing page, when the
page renders, then it presents exactly one text input and one submit control and
no other input control of any kind — no market, competitor, keyword or country
field, selector or toggle, optional or required — and no sign-in or payment is
required before submitting."

Two collisions. One is derived; the other was derived in the first draft and that
resolution does not survive.

1. **A repeated CTA lower down the page would be a second submit control.** A
   page that walks a visitor through three sections and offers no way to act at
   the end of them sends them back up by hand. Resolution taken under rule 1.1:
   every further call to action brings the visitor to the one field rather than
   owning a field of its own (criterion 3). Cost of reversing: one criterion,
   nothing implemented. **This resolution survives on the bounded reading and
   falls on the whole-page reading** — a call-to-action button that scrolls and
   focuses is still a control — so it is inside the open question, not outside it.
2. **Whether a video's play control is forbidden.** The first draft read the
   dash-list as the bound: "market, competitor, keyword or country field,
   selector or toggle" are all scan parameters, and a media control supplies
   none, so criterion 5 was recorded as costing REQ-001 nothing. **That reading is
   withdrawn.** The criterion's operative clause is "no other input control of
   any kind", with the list introduced by a dash as illustration; on that reading
   the play control and every repeated call to action are controls an approved
   requirement forbids. The two readings differ on what was promised — one field
   and one button as a rule about the *scan form*, or as a rule about the *whole
   page* — and the owner's landing-page direction is the thing that turns on it.
   That is a promise, not a parameter, so rule 1.1 does not reach it and rule 1.3
   does not let it be derived: it is REQ-099's first open question, and REQ-099
   carries `blocked-by: [REQ-001]` until it is ruled.

REQ-001's non-goal "No market, competitor, keyword, or country input on the
landing page (criterion 1)" is untouched on either reading: nothing REQ-099 adds
supplies a scan parameter.

## The small-screen criterion ADR-093 says is owed, and how criterion 1 answers it

ADR-093 Consequences, verbatim: "This ADR therefore rests on an owner ruling with
no requirement under it, and the gap is the analyst's to close with a criterion
stating what the customer is promised at a small screen." REQ-099 criterion 1 is
part of that closure — for this one surface — and is written in the ADR's own
vocabulary rather than against it. Four things follow, all chosen under rule 1.1:

1. **"On any device" is replaced by ADR-093 Decision 2's floor**, verbatim: "the
   supported range is 320 CSS px and up". Decision 2 refuses the literal reading in
   terms — "'Every screen size' is unbuildable read literally" — so a criterion
   promising it would promise past what the system commits to build.
2. **What the customer is promised at `compact` is the three, and the component
   follows directly below.** Derivation: ADR-093 Decision 4 fixes which side of a
   pair gives way — "the value is rigid and the decoration is flexible, never
   the reverse". The tagline, the subline and the one field are the promise and
   the action; the component is the illustration of them. That is a promise per
   band, which ADR-093's Consequences say the analyst owes; which registered
   variant of each item renders at each band is the design system's, and
   criterion 1 no longer cites Decision 3 or forbids a registered narrower
   state — it asks that the three be present and readable, and Decision 3 is how
   the design makes them so. Cost of reversing: one clause in criterion 1 and
   one arm of the surface. Nothing is measured here and nothing is claimed about
   any device.
3. **The floor viewport is 320 × 480 CSS px.** ADR-093 Decision 2 commits a width
   floor and its Decision 6 suite renders five widths and no height, so "within
   the first screen" had no viewport at which it could fail. 480 is chosen the
   way Decision 2 chose 320: it is the conventional shortest CSS viewport and
   the smallest of the three common phone viewports (320×480, 320×568, 360×640)
   — a chosen parameter, not a device census. The criterion holds at the floor
   and at every wider or taller viewport, so a short-and-wide window is inside
   the promise. Cost of reversing: one parameter in the suite and one clause.
   The rejected-submission state (REQ-001 criterion 3's line) is the state most
   likely to overflow the floor, so criterion 1 states it: the line and the field
   are within the viewport; whether the tagline still is at 320×480 with the
   line present is the design's, and the criterion does not promise it.
4. **The one text input is above the first screen, not only a call to action.**
   The owner's statement names "our primary tagline, sub line, CTA"; REQ-001
   criterion 1 puts exactly one text input on this page, and a first screen whose
   CTA scrolls to a field elsewhere costs a stranger an action before they can
   type — the page's whole conversion. Criterion 1 therefore names the field and
   its submit control as the third item. Cost of reversing: one clause.

**What criterion 1 needs that does not exist yet.** ADR-093 Decision 6 renders
each route at five *widths* — "the floor (320), each of the three bands, and each
boundary minus one pixel" — and asserts nothing about height, so no check in the
corpus can fail a first-screen criterion today. The suite gains the 480 px
height at each of its five widths and a containment assertion for criterion 1's
items, which is the architect's to add to BP-018's `tests/ui/layout/**`, not
this requirement's to specify. Recorded here as downstream impact (rule 6).

**Criteria 3 and 5 say which reading of REQ-001 criterion 1 they stand on.**
Both are contingent on the bounded reading; the criteria now say so in their own
text rather than only here, so approving the requirement as written does not
rule open question 1 by default. Criterion 5 is also conditioned on the video
existing (open question 2, the `rests-on` row): a page shipped before the asset
exists meets criteria 6 and 7 and is not failed by criterion 5.

**Criterion 4 binds the component's words to the product's own.** REQ-093
criterion 1 reaches "every text the product renders … on every surface", and a
component on the landing page that shows the interface renders that interface's
text on the landing page. That settles that no model wrote it; it does not
settle that the words are the product's rather than words invented for the
picture. Criterion 4's "shows the product's own running interface" is read as
meaning exactly that — every word visible inside it is one the product renders
on that screen, its registered strings (BP-019) — and the criterion now says so.
Derived, not asked: the owner's direction is "an immediate feel for what the app
is and looks like", and a picture of copy the app does not carry is not that.
Owed-strings item 10 is narrowed to the registered strings accordingly.

**What criterion 3 fixes that was ambiguous.** "Brings the visitor to that one
field" is read as: the field is scrolled into view and focused, with no page load.
Derivation under rule 1.1 — on a phone the alternative reading (a jump to the top
of the page) costs the visitor an extra action at every call to action, which is
what a repeated call to action exists to remove. Cost of reversing: one clause.

## Publisher of record, and what the video's words are

`BUILD.md` §14 point 6, verbatim: "Customer is publisher of record: their domain,
their identity; `*.reachkit.app` noindex forever (§9)." That assigns
publisher-of-record for pages served on a customer's own domain and says nothing
about ReachKit's own marketing page. Derived, not asked: the landing page is
ReachKit's own surface on ReachKit's own domain, so ReachKit is publisher of
record for everything on it, including the component of criterion 4 and the video
of criterion 5. The operative promise that follows is criterion 8 — every figure
on either one traces to a stored measurement with its date (REQ-093 criterion 4;
`00-project.md` standing law 5, verbatim, with the source's own emphasis: "**One
claim, one home.** Every constant — price, cap, limit, copy string — is defined
once. Every UI number traces to a stored measurement with a date.") or is a
declared example about a reserved domain. Without it, the first draft forbade
figures only "about the visitor's own site", which left invented scores about
somebody else's — the false-public-claim failure §8 names.

**Whether an example figure may stand on a public surface at all is the owner's.**
Standing law 5 and REQ-093 criterion 4, read as written, admit no figure that is
not a stored measurement with its date; criterion 8's second branch and REQ-098
criterion 5's are a carve-out no approved artifact grants. Whether a score no
scan produced may stand on ReachKit's own page under an example label is a
public claim (§1, decision-rights table), so it is open question 4 rather than a
derivation, asked once for both requirements. If admitted, the carve-out's
permanent home is REQ-093 criterion 4 — an edit to an approved requirement,
through its own history — and criteria 8 and REQ-098 criterion 5 cite it.

**The example rule has one home, criterion 8, and REQ-098 criterion 5 cites it.**
Three things in it are chosen under rule 1.1, recorded once here for both files:
the register is RFC 2606's reserved names — `example.com`, `example.net`,
`example.org`, and names under `.example`, `.test` and `.invalid` — a property of
the string, so a test asserts against a list and not against live DNS (the
earlier "resolves to no real site" is withdrawn; `example.com` resolves); the
line sits on the same component or frame as the figure and renders wherever and
whenever the figure does, so a reader cannot meet the figure without the line;
and the line says both that the figures are an example and that they measure no
site, because saying only that the domain is an example would leave a real-looking
score standing under it. Cost of reversing each: one clause. REQ-099 is the home
because it is JN-001's first step and approval proceeds one journey at a time
(rule 7.4); REQ-098, on JN-004, cites forward into an earlier journey's
requirement rather than the reverse.

**The video's own words are owed.** Its on-screen text and narration script are
customer-visible strings a stranger meets on this page; the non-goal that keeps
producing and captioning the video out of scope does not make its words anyone
else's. They are item 12 of the owed list.

**Whether REQ-093 reaches a narrated or captioned video is not unruled**, and the
first draft's note that it was is withdrawn. REQ-093 criterion 1, verbatim,
governs "every text the product renders or sends, on every surface it speaks on —
a screen and a mail alike — including surfaces added after this requirement". A
caption, an on-screen title and a narration script are text the product sends on a
surface added after REQ-093. REQ-093 is the single home and REQ-099 states no
criterion of its own about it (rule 2.4); its non-goal names the reach and stops.

## What has no home in the corpus

The demo video. Searched: `BUILD.md` §16 (the ten milestones and their checks),
`00-project.md` (the cost envelope and the delivery spine), and every
requirement. No milestone names a video, no cost-envelope row covers producing or
serving one, and `00-project.md`'s non-goals list neither includes nor excludes
it. Carried as REQ-099's second open question and as its one `rests-on` row.
Criterion 6 is what holds the page together if the row is refuted, and criterion 7
now states the order for the case where the video was never produced — the first
draft anchored the page's ordering to an asset nothing in the corpus produces.
