# Evidence — REQ-096 (rival right-sizing: the band boundaries, the cadence they can run on, and the cost of moving them)

Source: `BUILD.md` v1.1, 2026-08-28 · `DATA-COSTS.md` v1.1 (both in this repo, at
`/BUILD.md` and `/DATA-COSTS.md`).

REQ-096 carries two counts — 100 searches or twice the customer's own, and 500
searches or five times the customer's own — as the boundaries of the three rival
size bands (criterion 2). Both are floors, which the decision-rights table (§1)
puts in the system's column, so both were chosen by the system under rule 1.1 and
are derived here rather than in the requirement (rule 2.1 — a REQ carries no
argument).

## What the finding is, in the source's own terms

§6.6's rival score is `top10Appearances + 2 × aiCitations` over the market's 12
question-SERPs, taking the top 5. Both terms are measures of market presence, so
the ordering the customer is offered is an ordering *by dominance*: the largest
incumbent in a market is first by construction. §6.6 states the property
approvingly for a different reason — "the output is identical whether the customer
ranks for 10,000 searches or none" — and that is exactly the gap. The derivation
is deliberately independent of the customer, which makes it correct as *discovery*
and silent as *fit*. Nothing anywhere in §6.6, §4.3 or §4.5 puts the two sides
back together, so a founder at zero can accept a rival at 12,400 and read an
unmoving ratio every Monday for the life of the subscription.

§7 already holds the missing half, for targets rather than rivals: right-sizing
against the customer's own count, expressed as three bands. R1 rules that rivals
are banded and the customer told, not filtered, and that §7's *shape* — three
bands cut by two bars against the customer's own count — is reused rather than a
parallel one invented.

## Why max(100, 2×) and max(500, 5×) (derived, rule 1.1)

The two expressions are §7's own, transposed from "the weakest domain in a
target's top-10" to "the rival itself". They are the same two bars REQ-047
criteria 9 and 10 already apply to targets:

| Band (REQ-096's internal handle) | Rival's ranked count R against customer's C | §7 reading |
|---|---|---|
| `near` | R ≤ max(100, 2×C) | a site roughly the customer's own size |
| `middle` | max(100, 2×C) < R ≤ max(500, 5×C) | bigger, but inside §7's qualifying bar |
| `far` | R > max(500, 5×C) | outside the bar §7 itself calls displaceable |

**The vocabulary was separated from the bars on 2026-08-31; the bars were not.**
Owner ruling of that date: targets keep §7's Winnable / Reach / Not-yet
(REQ-047 criterion 10) and rivals take three distinct words of their own, because
a target's band on the day panel (REQ-047 criterion 12, JN-003) and a rival's band
on Overview (REQ-096 criterion 5, JN-005) put the same word in front of the same
customer in one session meaning two different things. Nothing in this derivation
moved: the boundaries below are still §7's own two expressions, applied to rivals
and to targets identically, and REQ-096's handles `near`/`middle`/`far` are
placeholders for words the owner has yet to write (REQ-096 criterion 9), not a
second scale. A future reader should not read the distinct names as evidence of
distinct arithmetic — the bands are cut at the same two counts for both, and the
argument for those counts, below, is one argument serving both.

Transposition rather than invention is the point. §7's `max(500, 5× customer's)`
is the product's one existing statement of *what is displaceable by this
customer*. A rival is exactly the kind of object that bar was written about — the
domains it screens are the ones sitting in a target's top ten, which is where
tracked rivals live. Inventing a second, unrelated scale for rivals would mean the
product held two contradictory opinions about the same domain: a rival banded
"catchable" whose pages are simultaneously banded Not-yet as targets. Reusing the
bar makes the two consistent by construction.

The tighter bar is 100 and not 500 for the reason REQ-047's second `rests-on` row
already records for targets: at 500 the two expressions are equal for every
customer ranking below 250 searches, so every qualifying rival would band `near`
and the `middle` band would be unreachable for the cold-start founder — the one
customer this requirement exists for. The same collapse applies here identically,
because the expressions are the same.

Cold-start check (§6.6's law, and criterion 8's test): at C = 0 the floors carry
the whole expression — `near` ≤ 100, `middle` 101–500, `far` > 500. All three
bands are assignable. At any C > 0 the multipliers differ (2× < 5×), so the two
bars never coincide and all three stay reachable. There is no customer count at
which a band becomes impossible.

## Why this costs no new vendor call

The measurement already exists in the closed dataset list. §6.3's paid deep scan
buys `ranked_keywords`@100 per rival, monthly, and `DATA-COSTS.md` P2 names its
feeds as "Keyword gap, format gap, 'how far ahead' ratios" at 7.2¢. The rival
count REQ-096 bands on is the same count the how-far-ahead ratio (REQ-041 criteria
8 to 11) already divides by, and the customer's own count is P1's
`ranked_keywords`@300. So the requirement reads two measurements the product
already takes and adds no spend site — which is what §6.4's law ("Nothing is
fetched that no rendered surface reads") and `00-project.md`'s "A new spend site
needs its own price-book row before it ships" would otherwise require.

Two consequences of that same fact bound where the behaviour can bite, and they
are what fixed the requirement's shape rather than its numbers:

- **Not at setup.** §4.3 runs setup while the deep pass runs and releases the
  founder however it ends. The sizing does not exist yet at the moment the rivals
  are picked, so the promise is picked-unsized-then-told, never a warning on the
  setup screen.
- **Not on the free report.** §6.4's never-list forbids per-rival
  `ranked_keywords` on the free path outright, and the arithmetic agrees
  independently: 5 rivals × `RANKED_RIVAL_ROWS` at 2.4¢ (§6.1) is 12¢, the whole
  of `CAP_FREE`. The free report is untouched by this requirement, and REQ-096
  says so as a non-goal rather than leaving it to be inferred.

## The row cap is not the count (rule 1.2)

P2 fetches 100 rows per rival, so a naive reading would cap every measured rival
at 100 and collapse the Reach and Not-yet bands into one. That reading is
inconsistent with what the source already promises: §6.6's paid reveal states a
rival at "12,400" and §4.5's rival module renders `78×` against `was 276×`, and
neither number is expressible from a 100-row ceiling. The product therefore
already depends on a rival's *total* count being available beside the rows it
fetches. That dependency is not this requirement's to assert as fact — no
implementation has verified it, and §16 milestone 2 is the first point that can —
so it is carried as an open `rests-on` row on REQ-096 rather than stated in a
criterion. If it is refuted, REQ-041's ratio is refuted with it, and the fix is
one shared with that requirement rather than one this band scheme owns.

## Cost of reversing

**Low, for both counts.** Each is one pinned constant in
`src/lib/config/constants.ts` (`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` |
**Every** pinned number in the specification", asserted by `tests/pins.test.ts`);
nothing implements them today. Neither is stored on a record: a band is derived
from two counts the product already holds with their dates, so moving a boundary
re-derives correctly for every account and every past measurement without a
migration, a re-measurement or a vendor call.

Three consequences are not free, and each is a reason to move the numbers before
the behaviour is built on rather than after:

- Moving either boundary moves it for **targets too, or splits them.** The counts
  are shared with REQ-047 criteria 9 and 10 by deliberate transposition; changing
  the rival copy alone re-introduces exactly the contradiction the transposition
  was chosen to avoid. The 2026-08-31 ruling separated the *names*, not these
  counts, so this stays true: reversal is cheap in code and is a decision about
  both requirements, not one.
- Criterion 6's line, and now the three band terms criterion 9 requires, are copy
  the owner owns and none of it exists yet. It should not be written against a
  boundary still expected to move: the sentence a customer reads about a `far`
  rival is only true for as long as `far` means what it means today.
- Widening `far` makes the line more frequent, which is the direction that
  degrades it fastest. A warning a customer sees against most of their set stops
  being read as information about a rival and starts being read as noise, and no
  boundary chosen here is defended by a measurement of how often it will fire —
  that is measurable only once real customer and rival counts exist, at §16
  milestone 6 ("Real supply fills the calendar; empty days honest") and after.
