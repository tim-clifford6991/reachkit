# Evidence — REQ-011 (why nurture is per domain, non-concurrent, and suppressed address-wide)

> REQ-011 was superseded by REQ-010 on 2026-08-30. This evidence now supports
> REQ-010 criteria 9 to 13 and is cited from REQ-010's rationale under this
> filename, which is kept so the citation does not rot.

Source: `BUILD.md` v1.1, 2026-08-28 (in this repo, at `/BUILD.md`).

- §4.2, verbatim: "Nurture: max 3 mails (24h/72h/168h), stops on conversion."
- §11, jobs table, verbatim: "`lead/nurture` | event + delays | Draft email,
  then ≤3 touches, stops on convert".

BUILD fixes the cadence of one sequence and says nothing about a lead who scans
a second domain. The owner ruled on 2026-08-30 that each distinct domain a lead
scans gets its own sequence of up to three touches.

Deliverability reasoning for the two bounds that ruling forces (recorded under
rule 1.1; the owner may overrule either):

- **Volume.** With one sequence per address the ceiling of unsolicited
  commercial mail one address can receive is three. Per-domain re-entry makes it
  three per domain scanned, with no ceiling of its own — a lead who scans six
  domains could receive eighteen mails from a single email address they gave
  once. The opt-out therefore cannot be scoped to the sequence that carried it:
  it is the only bound on the total, so it suppresses the address across every
  domain and every future scan (criterion 3). An opt-out that a later scan can
  reset is not an opt-out, and treating it as one is what turns a nurture
  sequence into a complaint rate.
- **Concurrency.** Two sequences running to one address at once put six mails
  into a 168-hour window instead of three, arriving in interleaved pairs. That
  reads as a mailing list rather than a follow-up to a thing the founder asked
  for, and it is the pattern that draws spam reports and bulk-sender
  throttling. A second domain's sequence therefore starts only once the first
  has ended or been stopped (criterion 4), which also keeps every mail
  attributable to one domain the founder actually scanned.

Why this sits here and not in the requirement: the volume arithmetic and the
sender-reputation argument are reasoning for a choice, not behaviour a customer
can observe (rule 2.1). REQ-011 states only the observable promises — three
touches per domain, no concurrent sequences, no second sequence for a domain
already sequenced, and an opt-out that ends all future follow-up to that
address.
