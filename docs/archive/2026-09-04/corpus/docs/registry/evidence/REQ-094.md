# Evidence — REQ-094 (why the coherence check needs three measured searches)

Source: `BUILD.md` v1.1, 2026-08-28 (in this repo, at `/BUILD.md`).

The cost bound behind "one correction per free scan" is in
`registry/evidence/REQ-014.md`, kept under that filename so the citation does
not rot. This file carries one thing REQ-014's does not: the floor of three
measured searches in REQ-094 criterion 2, chosen by the system under rule 1.1
(the decision-rights table gives floors to the system) and recorded here rather
than in the requirement (rule 2.1 — a REQ carries no argument and no rejected
alternatives).

## The source clause the floor is derived from

- §6.7, Step 5 — Coherence check + correction, verbatim: "After the 12 SERPs
  return: if no domain appears in ≥3 of the 12 top-10s, the market set is likely
  wrong (disjoint SERPs = incoherent market)."

## Why three (derived, rule 1.1)

The source states its condition over twelve measured top-10s and fixes no
behaviour for a report that measured fewer, which a scan stopping early at the
90-second ceiling or the free path's spend ceiling can produce (REQ-003 criteria
5 and 11, surfaced to the reader by REQ-001 criterion 14). The floor is the
count below which the source's own condition stops carrying information.

Below three measured searches the condition "no domain appears in ≥3 of the
top-10s" is satisfied by arithmetic rather than by evidence: with zero, one or
two measured top-10s, no domain can appear in three of them whatever those
top-10s contain, so the condition holds for every market alike — a coherent one
and an incoherent one produce the identical result. The check has no input, and
a line saying the market may have been read wrongly would be a statement about
how many searches were measured, presented to the reader as a finding about
their business. Three is therefore the smallest count at which the condition is
falsifiable — at exactly three, a single domain appearing in all three refutes
it — which is why the floor equals the source's own threshold rather than a
number chosen beside it. REQ-094 criterion 2 states both sides of that floor:
the incoherence line at three or more, and, below three, a written line saying
coherence could not be judged, pointing at the same correction control.

## The rejected alternative: a floor of twelve

Reading the source literally — the condition is written over "the 12 top-10s" —
would set the floor at twelve, running the check only on a report that measured
a full twelve searches. Rejected: REQ-006 criterion 1 makes a market that yields
fewer than twelve a legal *complete* result, not a shortfall, and the source
supplies both the reason and the mechanisms.

- §6.6, the cold-start law, verbatim: "**The cold-start law: every derivation in
  the product must work for a domain that ranks for nothing.** Cold start is the
  default customer — the product exists to build their foundation — so no
  dataset, module, or sentence may assume presence."
- §6.7, composition constraints, verbatim: "near-duplicates collapse (same
  content-token stem set → keep highest volume), so the 12 SERPs are 12
  different questions — a leanness rule as much as a quality one."

A floor of twelve would therefore silence the correction prompt exactly on thin
markets — few searches, sparse or collapsed candidates — which are the markets
a homepage inference is most likely to have read wrongly, and the readers with
most to correct would be the only ones never told the market might be wrong. The
floor of three keeps the check running wherever it can carry evidence, and names
its own absence where it cannot.

## Cost of reversing it

Low. The floor is one pinned constant in `src/lib/config/constants.ts`
(`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` | **Every** pinned number in
the specification", asserted by `tests/pins.test.ts`); the repo is greenfield
and carries no `src/` yet, so nothing implements it today. No stored data or
schema depends on it: the check is computed at render time from measurements a
report already holds, so changing the number changes which of REQ-094 criterion
2's two written lines a reader sees, and re-renders correctly against every
report already stored — no migration, no re-measurement, no vendor call.

## The threshold rescales with the denominator (derived, rule 1.1, 2026-08-31)

> Amends "Why three" above. That section derived the floor while criterion 2 kept
> the source's *absolute* threshold of three appearances at every denominator;
> criterion 2 no longer does, and the ground for the floor of three has moved
> with it. The floor is still three. Read this section for why.

The defect the amendment fixes: an absolute threshold of three, measured against
however many searches the report actually measured, is not the same test at two
denominators. REQ-006 criterion 1 makes a market that yields three to five
searches a legal *complete* result, so on such a report three appearances demands
a domain present in all or nearly all of the top-tens — a bar twelve searches
never faced — and a perfectly coherent thin market is told its market may have
been read wrongly. The source's condition is a share of the market, not a count:
three of twelve is one quarter.

The threshold is therefore `max(2, ceil(n / 4))`, where `n` is the number of
searches the report measured (REQ-006 criterion 1's denominator, the only
denominator the report states).

- **One quarter** is the source's own proportion, read off §6.7 step 5's "≥3 of
  the 12 top-10s". At `n = 12` the formula returns 3, so the amendment is exact
  at the denominator the source wrote for and changes no behaviour there.
- **Never fewer than two** because coherence is *overlap*: a domain appearing in
  one top-ten evidences nothing about whether two searches belong to one market.
  Below two appearances the test cannot distinguish a coherent market from an
  incoherent one, whatever the denominator — the same vacuity the floor exists to
  refuse.

**Why the floor stays three.** With the threshold at `max(2, …)`, the test is
falsifiable from `n = 2`. It is not *comparable* there: at `n = 2` the threshold
equals the whole denominator, so "coherent" means both top-tens share a domain —
unanimity, a bar no other denominator imposes, which is the same non-comparability
this amendment exists to remove. Three is the smallest `n` at which the threshold
is strictly less than the number measured. Below it, criterion 2's second branch
says coherence could not be judged rather than reporting a verdict the arithmetic
fixed in advance.

Cost of reversing: unchanged from the section above — one pinned expression in
`src/lib/config/constants.ts` (`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` |
**Every** pinned number in the specification", asserted by `tests/pins.test.ts`),
computed at render time from measurements a report already holds. No stored data,
no schema, no vendor call, and no customer-visible string changes: the two lines
criterion 2 binds are the same two.

## One retry for a correction that produced no report (owner ruling, 2026-08-31)

The ruling, as the owner gave it: **a failed correction may be retried once;
after that the report stands.** The reader is told the correction could not be
completed, the report keeps its original category, and the correction control is
not offered again for that report.

What it settles: an earlier draft of criterion 7 left the correction allowance
unspent whenever a correction produced no report, so attempts were unbounded and
criterion 6's promise — that a second correction always costs a second scan —
was false on the failure path. With the retry bounded at one, criterion 6's
promise is true again past that retry, and criterion 7 now names both ends: what
spends the retry, and what the reader sees once it is spent.

Derived under this ruling, rule 1.1:

- **The retry is spent by being taken, not by succeeding** (criterion 6). Spending
  it on the outcome instead — retry again while it keeps failing — is the
  unbounded loop the ruling closes, and it is not decidable from what the product
  records at the moment the control is drawn.
- **A correction refused before it ran spends neither attempt** (criterion 7).
  The ruling governs a *failed* correction: one that ran and produced no report.
  A refusal is the product declining to start — nothing measured, nothing spent,
  no vendor call — so charging an attempt for it would take the reader's
  correction away to pay for our own pause. Repetition is bounded by what does the
  refusing (REQ-003 criteria 7 and 8), not by this allowance.
- **The retry costs no money that was not already committed.** REQ-003 criterion
  11 binds a scan and every correction it runs to one spend ceiling, so a retry
  that would exceed it stops early and gives back an incomplete corrected report
  (criterion 7's last sentence) rather than spending past the cap. The bound the
  ruling adds is therefore about a report reaching a state a reader can rely on,
  not about cost: on the price book in `registry/evidence/REQ-014.md` a scan plus
  one correction is 10.5¢ worst case against a 12¢ cap, and a second full
  correction has no headroom to run in.

Cost of reversing: low. "How many correction attempts this report has taken" is
one stored counter on a report the product already stores, read at render time to
decide whether the control is drawn. Reversal changes no measurement, no stored
figure and no schema beyond that counter, and re-renders correctly against every
report already stored. It does change customer-visible copy: the line criterion 7
requires once the retry is spent is the owner's to write, and does not exist yet.

## What refuses a correction before it runs (derived, rule 1.3)

Criterion 7's third branch names conditions that already exist rather than
minting one. REQ-003 criterion 6 excepts a correction's re-measurement from the
hourly per-network allowance *only* — "except a re-measurement after a market
correction, which belongs to the scan it corrects and consumes nothing further"
— and states no exception anywhere else. REQ-003 criterion 1 counts "a
re-measurement after a market correction" among the ways a scan runs, so REQ-003
criterion 7 (a scan already running from that network) and criterion 8 (the day's
free-scan ceiling reached, or scanning switched off) reach a correction by their
own terms, and each already requires the visitor to be told in writing which
limit was met. Nothing was derived here that those criteria do not already say;
criterion 7 cites them rather than restating them (rule 2.4).

REQ-001 criterion 16's 24-hour cooldown is **not** among them. It bars a scan
starting by itself and offers a manual retry control in the same breath, and the
correction retry is that manual control — which is why criterion 7 identifies the
retry as the one control REQ-001 criterion 16 admits, and REQ-001 criterion 16
names it (the one edit this pass made outside REQ-094).

## The correction offer ends at 7 days (derived, rule 1.1, 2026-08-31)

Criterion 1 offers the correction control only while the stored report is less
than 7 days old. Derivation:

- A correction does not re-read the domain's own pages (criterion 3, from §6.7
  step 5's "re-runs steps 2–5"), so the corrected report carries Foundations and
  Answerability readings older than the measurement date it shows. Unbounded, that
  gap is unbounded: a report can be corrected at any age, and REQ-004 criterion 1
  shows one date for the whole report.
- 7 days is the age at which this product already declares a free report no longer
  current — REQ-001 criterion 13 withholds a re-scan below it and criterion 15
  grants one at it. Bounding the offer by the same number means a carried-forward
  reading is never older, at the moment a correction starts, than the report's own
  currency window; no separate number is introduced, and none needs pinning that
  is not pinned already.
- The reader is never left without a route. At 7 days REQ-001 criterion 15 offers
  a re-scan, and criterion 6 gives that re-scan's report a correction of its own,
  so "correct this" becomes "measure it again and correct that" rather than
  nothing.

Cost of reversing: low. The bound is a comparison against a constant the product
already pins (`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` | **Every** pinned
number in the specification"), evaluated at render time; lifting or lowering it
changes only whether the control is drawn, and no stored report changes. One
consequence is not free to reverse in the other direction: dropping the bound
re-opens the unbounded gap between a corrected report's date and the readings it
carries forward, which criterion 3's last clause depends on.
