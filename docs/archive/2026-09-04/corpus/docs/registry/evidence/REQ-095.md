# Evidence — REQ-095 (supply depth: the month's target, the told threshold, and why neither is a promised page count)

Source: `BUILD.md` v1.1, 2026-08-28 (in this repo, at `/BUILD.md`).

REQ-095 carries two numbers — 30 unused qualifying opportunities as what the
onboarding deep pass pursues (criterion 1), and 7 as the count below which the
customer is told supply is short (criterion 5). Both are floors, which the
decision-rights table (§1) puts in the system's column, so both were chosen by the
system under rule 1.1 and are derived here rather than in the requirement (rule
2.1 — a REQ carries no argument).

## The source clauses this requirement is cut against

- §7, verbatim: "**Supply is the cap:** never invent an opportunity to fill a day."
- §4.6, verbatim: "one event per day, every day filled while supply lasts, weekends
  included" and "when opportunities run out, future days are empty and the empty
  state says so — the calendar is never padded".
- §2.5, verbatim: "an empty queue is a success state ("Nothing worth publishing
  today")".
- §4.3, verbatim: "A degraded pass still releases setup (zero proposals is legal,
  never faked)."
- §11, verbatim: "`weekly/refresh` | Mon 06:00 UTC | Weekly scan per active site →
  re-derive → verdicts → movement email".
- §16, milestone 6, verbatim: "Real supply fills the calendar; empty days honest".

The first three fix the *filling* obligation and its honesty. None of them, and no
requirement transcribed from them, obliges supply to be deep enough to keep the
promise the subscription is sold on: a build producing three opportunities and
rendering twenty-seven correctly worded empty days satisfies every one of them.
§4.3 is the clause that decides the *form* of the fix — depth cannot be a gate on
setup, because the specification releases a founder whose pass produced nothing at
all — so the obligation lands as a disclosure at the moment the shortfall is known.

## Why 30 (derived, rule 1.1)

30 is one page per day for one month, read off the promise the product is sold
under — §4.6's head, verbatim: "One page a day. Every day." — and the monthly
billing period it is sold in (€49/mo, `00-project.md`). It is the smallest target
that makes the deep pass responsible for the period the customer has just paid for;
anything lower makes onboarding responsible for part of a month and leaves the
remainder to a replenishment that has not run yet.

30 rather than 31: a month is 28 to 31 days, and the target is a floor the pass
pursues, not a ceiling it stops the calendar at. Choosing the modal month length
keeps the number readable as "a month" without minting a per-month variable that
would have to be recomputed at every onboarding date.

## Why 7 (derived, rule 1.1)

Supply is drawn down at one opportunity per day (REQ-043 criterion 1) and is
replenished by exactly one recurring event — the weekly re-measurement, due on the
customer's Monday (REQ-065 criterion 1, from §11's `weekly/refresh`). Nothing else
adds supply. So the notice period a customer can be given is bounded by the
replenishment interval: with 7 unused opportunities in hand, the calendar cannot
empty before the next re-measurement has had its chance to top it up; with fewer,
it can. 7 is therefore the largest count at which "supply is short" is still news
the customer can act on rather than a report of something already lost, and the
smallest at which the telling is not noise — a healthy customer replenished each
Monday sits above it as a matter of course, which is what keeps criterion 5's line
rare and therefore legible.

The two numbers do not compete. 30 is what onboarding pursues once; 7 is the
standing state thereafter. Criterion 5's last clause resolves the one moment both
would speak (a deep pass that produced fewer than 7) to a single statement.

## No expected yield is derivable (rule 1.2)

An obvious way to justify 30 would be to compute how many opportunities a deep pass
*should* produce from §7's eight types against the 12 questions. That computation
cannot be done honestly from anything in this repo, and asserting a yield would be
inventing a measurement.

- §6.1's numbers are input-row caps, not opportunity yields: `QUESTIONS` 12,
  `TARGET_SERPS_MAX` 13, `MEASURED_PAGES_MAX` 25, `RANKED_PAID_ROWS` 300,
  `RANKED_RIVAL_ROWS` 100 × `COMPETITORS_MAX` 5, `SUGGESTIONS_COST` "1.8¢ / call @
  50 rows" × 2. They bound how much evidence is read, not how much of it triggers a
  type.
- Every trigger in §7's table is conditional on what the evidence turns out to say —
  "AI answer for a question names rivals, not customer", "Rival top-20 for a query
  ≥10/mo; customer absent", "Customer ranks 4–30, page thin", "Ranking page stale vs
  rivals'". The conversion rate from rows to triggers is a property of a real
  market, and no market has been measured: the repo is greenfield
  (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No source code on disk yet").
- The winnability gate (REQ-047 criteria 9 and 10, from §7's "a Write target
  qualifies only if its top-10 contains at least one domain whose ranked count ≤
  max(500, 5× customer's)") removes an unknown share of whatever does trigger.
- The three Improve types are keyed on pages the customer already ranks with, and
  §6.6's cold-start law makes "ranks for nothing" the default customer, for whom
  that family yields nothing. The customer most exposed to a shallow queue is
  exactly the one for whom the least is derivable.

Hence REQ-095 promises the customer a *telling*, not a page count: criterion 1 binds
the pass to pursue 30 and to stop only when its evidence is spent, and criteria 3,
5 and 6 bind what is said when it falls short. The claim that 30 is reachable at all
for a cold-start customer is carried as an open `rests-on` row on REQ-095, not as a
promise: §16 milestone 6 ("Real supply fills the calendar; empty days honest") is
the first point in the build order at which real yield can be measured, and M6 is
where both numbers are validated or moved.

## Cost of reversing

**Low, for both numbers.** Each is one pinned constant in
`src/lib/config/constants.ts` (`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` |
**Every** pinned number in the specification", asserted by `tests/pins.test.ts`);
nothing implements them today. Neither is stored on a record, so changing either
re-evaluates correctly against every account already in the product: 30 changes when
the deep pass stops deriving, 7 changes when a standing line is drawn. No migration,
no re-measurement and no additional vendor call follows from moving either.

Two consequences are *not* free, and both are reasons to move the numbers early
rather than late:

- Raising 30 after M6 — the likely direction if cold-start yield disappoints — is
  cheap in code and not cheap in money: the only way to deepen supply is more
  evidence, and more evidence is spend against `CAP_DEEP` 150¢ and the ~€2.50/month
  COGS the €49 price rests on. That trade changes what the product promises and is
  the owner's, not the system's.
- Lowering 7 to quieten the line is a change to how often a customer is warned
  before their calendar empties. The copy criteria 3, 5 and 6 require is the owner's
  and does not exist yet; it should not be written to a number still expected to
  move.
