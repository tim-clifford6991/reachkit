## The score's name and scale, checked

The screenshot says "Discoverability Score" and "/100". Grep of the whole corpus
for `Discoverability|findability score` returns no requirement, no blueprint and
no journey giving the number a customer-visible name. What the corpus does fix:

- REQ-004 criterion 1, verbatim: "the first module shows a single score on a
  0–100 scale, its band as one of the four written words — Invisible, Hard to
  find, Findable, Dominant".
- `BUILD.md` §5, verbatim: "Bands: 0–24 Invisible · 25–49 Hard to find · 50–74
  Findable · 75–100 Dominant".

So the **scale agrees** ("/100" is REQ-004's 0–100), the **band word agrees**
("Hard to find" is one of the four, and 47 falls in 25–49), and the specimen is
internally consistent with the corpus. Only the **name** is new, and a name a
customer reads is the owner's, not the system's — hence REQ-098's first open
question rather than a resolution here.

## Why criterion 5 is derivable and the specimen's provenance is not

`00-project.md` standing law 5, verbatim, with the source's own emphasis and no
other: "**One claim, one home.** Every constant — price, cap, limit, copy string —
is defined once. Every UI number traces to a stored measurement with a date."
REQ-004 criterion 12, verbatim: "nothing stands in its place: no estimate, no
interpolated value, no default … Every number the report shows and the product
stores comes from the scan whose measurement date the report carries." Between
them, a figure on any ReachKit surface is either a measurement with its date or
must not read as one — that is criterion 5, derived, not asked. Which of the
owner's options the panel actually is (real scan · fixed example) is a
customer-visible claim about a named domain, so it is asked.

**Whether an example figure may stand on a public surface at all is the owner's,
not derivable.** Standing law 5 and REQ-093 criterion 4, read as written, admit
no figure that is not a stored measurement; the owner endorsed a page whose card
carries "47" and "+6 pts est." with no provenance recorded. The two cannot both
hold, and which gives way is a public claim (§1, decision-rights table). It is
asked once, as REQ-099's fourth open question — the landing page is the larger
surface and its criterion 8 is the rule's one home (rule 2.4) — and REQ-098's
second open question is the dependent choice for this card.

Two things in criterion 5 are chosen rather than transcribed, under rule 1.1:

1. **The example rule is cited from REQ-099 criterion 8, not restated.** The
   register (RFC 2606's reserved names), the line's placement (on the same card,
   wherever and whenever the figure renders) and what the line says are fixed
   there once. The earlier clause "resolves to no real site" is withdrawn: it was
   a live-DNS property rather than a property of the string, and `example.com`
   does in fact resolve. Cost of reversing: one clause.
2. **No identifiable customer's domain, score or band on this screen at all.**
   Derived from REQ-020 criterion 5 — "nothing is said about whether any account
   or payment exists until an address has been given" — applied to the panel
   rather than only to the form: a screen that renders with no session cannot
   carry one customer's measurements where any stranger reads them. This is a
   privacy floor, not a promise change, so it is derived rather than asked (rule
   1.3).

The pill "+6 pts est." fits neither branch as transcribed, and that is the whole
of open question 2: an estimated gain is not a stored measurement and is not
offered as fiction. The requirement does not resolve it, because which of the two
things gives way — the pill, or the real scan — is a customer-visible claim.

## Criterion 3's timing-and-allowance clause, withdrawn

Round one added to criterion 3 that "the answer takes the same time and the same
number of requests is allowed whichever [address] it was". It is withdrawn
under the altitude bar (`skills/prd-writing/SKILL.md`): REQ-020 criterion 4 is
approved and requires the three answers to differ *in words* — a link sent, a
payment held, no account — so whoever submits an address already reads whether
it has an account. Equal timing and equal allowance after that protect nothing a
person cannot already read, so nothing breaks for a customer if they stop being
true; they are BP-032's mechanism and stay there. What the customer is promised
stays: nothing before a submission reveals anything (REQ-020 criterion 5), and
abuse controls are a declared non-goal.

Downstream impact, recorded for the backward pass (rule 6) and not this
requirement's to fix: WO-127's file plan reads "neither latency nor wording
distinguishes them" and its test is named "the three branches … are
indistinguishable in latency and shape". The wording half contradicts REQ-020
criterion 4 and WO-127's own interface, which returns a different `lineKey` per
branch. The planner owns the correction.

## Criterion 1 and BP-001 — the address is the promise, the redirect is not

The owner endorsed a page at `https://reachkit.app/login`: a sign-in screen with
its own address is the endorsement. BP-001's `## Error & edge behavior` answers a
signed-out `/setup` or `/app` request in place ("asks for an address and says
nothing about whether an account or a payment exists"), and REQ-020 criterion 5
permits either a redirect or an in-place render. Which one is structure — the
architect's (§4) — so criterion 1 promises only that the screen the person meets
is this one, "whether they are brought to its address or shown it where they
are". BP-001's public route table gains the sign-in route on expansion; that is
an addition, not a conflict.

## Criterion 8, deleted — one link works, and the promise already has a home

Round one added a criterion that "the most recently requested link signs them in
and any earlier one is answered as criterion 7 requires". The first half is a
promise about the link, not this screen, and the corpus already homes it: WO-136's
test-plan row for REQ-024 criterion 4 reads "this module's half: a fresh link
supersedes the old one, so the newest link is the one that works". Restating it
here was the second copy rule 2.4 forbids, and BP-061's "at most one live token
per `(user_id, purpose)`" is the shape under that promise, not a promise of its
own. The second half — what this screen says about the older link — is criterion
7's, which now names "replaced by a later link they asked for" as a cause so that
BP-061's single `reason: 'spent'` is covered whichever way it arose.

## The destination of "Start a free scan →", derived not transcribed

The transcription records only "the second half an accent link"; the owner
endorsed the string, not a target. Criterion 2 states the landing page as the
destination, and that is this analyst's derivation under rule 1.1, recorded here
rather than presented as the owner's: REQ-001 criterion 1 puts the one text input
that starts a free scan on the landing page and nowhere else, so a link labelled
"Start a free scan" has exactly one place in this product to reach. Cost of
reversing: one clause in criterion 2.
