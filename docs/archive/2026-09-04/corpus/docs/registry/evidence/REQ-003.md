Why this sits here and not in the requirement: the vendor tier, the per-call

## The 90-second hard ceiling (derived, rule 1.1)

REQ-003 criterion 5 stops measuring at 90 seconds. `BUILD.md` fixes 60 seconds
and no ceiling above it, so the ceiling is a derived parameter, not a
transcribed one.

Derivation: 60 seconds is the promise a visitor is held to (§11, "Free ≈60s
live"), and a ceiling equal to the promise would cut off any run that overran by
a single slow vendor call — turning a report that was one call from complete
into a truncated one. A ceiling of 90 seconds is a 50% overrun: it leaves room
for exactly one vendor call to answer slowly past the promise while still
bounding what any visitor waits, and it is short enough that the wait stays
inside the "abandons an unlabelled wait" window the requirement is written
against. The upper bound is behavioural, not technical — every second past the
promise is a second the visitor already believes the page is stuck.

Cost of reversing it: low. The ceiling is one pinned constant
(`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` | **Every** pinned number in
the specification", asserted by `tests/pins.test.ts`), and moving it changes one
criterion's number and one pin — no schema, no vendor contract, no
customer-visible string beyond the written line REQ-003 criterion 5 already
requires.

## The window criterion 2 counts over (derived, rule 1.1)

`BUILD.md` §11 fixes "Free ≈60s live" and names no population or window, so the
sample criterion 2 is judged over is a derived parameter. Chosen: any 100
consecutive free scans of reachable domains, in start order, with a budget of 5
overruns. Derivation: 100 consecutive scans is the smallest window in which a
5% budget is a whole number of scans, so a verdict never turns on rounding; it
is counted in start order rather than per calendar period so a low-traffic day
cannot pass the criterion on a sample of three scans, and a launch build can be
judged the moment it has served 100 scans rather than waiting out a month.
"Reachable" excludes the domains criterion 4's failure path owns, whose duration
is bounded by criterion 5 and not by this one.

Cost of reversing it: low — the window and the budget are two pinned constants
in the test harness, not product behaviour; no customer-visible string, schema
or vendor contract moves with them.

## Why not 10 seconds (owner considered and ruled, 2026-08-31)

The owner asked whether the free scan could promise a report in 10 seconds
rather than 60. It cannot, for the complete report — not because any call is
known to be slow (no such measurement exists; see "No timing evidence" below)
but because of the *shape* of the free path's dependency chain.

### The chain is serial, and each link waits on the one before it

`DATA-COSTS.md` §2 ("What the FREE report needs") and `BUILD.md` §6.3
("Datasets — the closed list") describe the same four-link chain, and each link
consumes the previous link's output:

| Link | Waits on | The dependency, in the source's own words |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** — home document + detected pricing page | the scanned domain's server | `DATA-COSTS.md` §2, verbatim: "Home document + detected pricing page, raw HTML \| … \| Own fetch \| 0¢" |
| **F5** — category label + market seed terms | F1 | `DATA-COSTS.md` §2, verbatim: "Nano ×1 over **F1's homepage text** — never over rankings, so it works at zero presence" |
| **F6** — the market's search set + volumes | F5 | `DATA-COSTS.md` §2, verbatim: "`keyword_suggestions` **seeded from F5's category terms** — never from the customer's rankings" |
| **F7** — 12 organic SERPs | F6, via the selection of the 12 | `DATA-COSTS.md` §2, verbatim: "Top-10 + **AI Overview** (with cited domains) for each of the 12 questions' source searches \| … \| **12 organic SERPs, live**" |

Between F6 and F7 sits the selection of the 12 questions, which adds no network
wait — `BUILD.md` §6.7, verbatim: "The chain is five steps; **selection is
deterministic and auditable end to end — the LLM touches vocabulary, never
selection.**"

The two remaining paid or fetched datasets are parallel branches, not links:
F2 (`robots.txt`, own fetch) and F4 (`ranked_keywords`@50) depend on nothing in
the chain and so do not extend it. F3 is a parse of F1 and F8 a count over F7 —
`DATA-COSTS.md` §2, verbatim: "Counting over F7."

So the critical path is four serial network round-trips — the last of them a
twelve-way live SERP batch — and the first is a fetch of a third-party server
whose response time nothing in this specification bounds. Ten seconds has to
cover all four in sequence. That is the finding: it rests on the shape of the
chain and on the specification's own budget, not on any latency figure.

### `BUILD.md` budgets 60 seconds in three separate places

- §4.1 (Free report `/scan/{domain}` — public), verbatim: "Purpose: a stranger
  sees, in under a minute, that AI recommends rivals and not them — and leaves
  with a finished page."
- §11 (Jobs), verbatim: "`scan/run` | on demand | The one pipeline; tier is a
  parameter. Free ≈60s live; deep live; weekly standard" (also recorded at the
  head of this file, as the source of the sixty-second figure itself).
- §16 (milestone 3), verbatim: "Free scan pipeline + report + share + cooldown |
  Real domain → real report <60s, ≤12¢ ledgered".

Corroborating from the cost side: `BUILD.md` §6.1 prices `SERP_LIVE / SERP_STD`
at "0.2¢ · 0.06¢", and §6.4 justifies paying the premium, verbatim: "Live mode
only where a human is waiting (free scan, onboarding pass). Everything scheduled
= standard queue." Live mode is bought for the sixty seconds; it is not evidence
that ten is reachable. `DATA-COSTS.md` §6 makes the same argument as a cost
danger, verbatim: "**Live mode creep.** Live SERP/scraper calls cost 3–7×
standard. Live is justified exactly once: the free report's 60-second promise.
Everything scheduled runs standard queue."

### No timing evidence exists in this repo

`DATA-COSTS.md` prices calls; it times none. It carries no latency figure, no
p50/p95, no measured duration for any vendor endpoint — its only mentions of
time are the sixty-second promise quoted above and the scheduling split between
live and standard. `BUILD.md` states the 60-second budget but no measurement
behind it. No vendor latency figure has been invented here, and none may be
(rule 1.2): the first real measurement of this chain is milestone 3's own check.

### The ruling

The owner ruled to **keep the 60-second promise** and to make 10 seconds a
**target that `BUILD.md` §16 milestone 3 measures against** when it first runs
the pipeline on a real domain. No acceptance criterion changes: REQ-003
criterion 2's p95 form and criterion 5's 90-second ceiling stand exactly as
they were. This is recorded in REQ-003's first `rests-on` row, which stays
`open` until M3 measures.

### What would have to change for 10 seconds to become reachable

Only a **staged promise** — one that separates *first content on screen* from
*complete report* — could put a ten-second number in front of a visitor, since
the complete report cannot outrun its own chain. That was offered and not taken.
It was not taken cheaply: a staged promise needs a third rendering state for a
measurement that is *still being taken*, and the report's rendering vocabulary
is deliberately binary — REQ-004 criteria 6, 7 and 9 admit exactly two readings
of any value, unmeasurable and measured-and-empty, and criterion 9 already
assigns the unmeasurable reading to work the scan never attempted. A partially
rendered report would need a third reading distinct from both, in REQ-004, and
that is a change to what the report promises, not a presentation detail. Should
M3's measurement reopen this, that is the change to price first.
