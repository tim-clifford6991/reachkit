Why this sits here and not in the requirement: the cache windows, the per-call
spend and the guardrail's justification are cost-control and policy facts (rule
2.1 — a REQ carries no vendor evidence and no argument). REQ-001 states only the
observable promise: what a report address serves, to whom, and when a re-scan
control appears beside it.

## The incomplete-report re-scan exception (owner ruling, 2026-08-31)

REQ-001 criterion 14 offers a re-scan control on an incomplete report at any
age. `BUILD.md` §6.4 does not say this; read literally, its 7-day window applies
to every stored report alike, which left a visitor whose scan stopped early — at
the 90-second ceiling (REQ-003 criterion 5) or the free path's spend ceiling
(REQ-003 criterion 11) — told the report is incomplete and offered nothing for
seven days.

Grounds the owner ruled on: §6.4 justifies its restriction in its own words —
"no new spend" — and what it refuses is redundant spend on a report that already
answered the question. A report that never finished did not answer it, so the
restriction's own justification does not reach the case. The exception is
therefore narrow by construction: it fires on incompleteness, not on age, and it
leaves the complete report's 7-day window exactly as §6.4 states it (REQ-001
criteria 13 and 15).

## The exception does not chain (derived, rule 1.1)

`BUILD.md` fixes no bound for an exception it does not contain, and the owner
ruled the control is offered "once" without naming what spends it, so the
spending condition is a derived parameter.

Chosen: the offer belongs to an incomplete stored report that was not itself
produced by a re-scan taken from this offer. Derivation — three candidates were
available and only this one is both bounded and testable:

- *Per visitor* fails: the free path has no account, no session and no cookie
  (REQ-001 criterion 6), so there is no visitor to count against.
- *Per report, renewing* fails: a domain that stops early every time — a slow
  server, or one whose battery always reaches the spend ceiling — would offer a
  re-scan after every re-scan, an unbounded loop of new spend, which is the
  exact harm §6.4 exists to prevent.
- *Per stored report, not chaining* holds: it bounds the exception at one extra
  scan per domain per 7-day window, it is decidable from what the product
  already records (which measurement produced the stored report), and it never
  dead-ends a visitor, because criterion 15's age rule reaches every stored
  report at 7 days whatever its completeness.

A re-scan that produced no report at all leaves the offer standing, because it
gave the visitor nothing; that case is bounded instead by the 24-hour failure
cooldown (REQ-001 criterion 16) and matches the settled treatment of an
allowance whose attempt produced nothing (REQ-094 criterion 7).

Cost of reversing it: low. The 7-day window and the failure cooldown are pinned
constants (`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` | **Every** pinned
number in the specification", asserted by `tests/pins.test.ts`); the chaining
rule is one stored fact about the measurement that produced a report. No schema
beyond that fact, no vendor contract, and no customer-visible string beyond the
written line criterion 14 already requires.

## Reports are `noindex` (owner ruling, 2026-08-31)

REQ-001 criterion 8 has no source clause and does not claim one. `BUILD.md`
calls the report "public, permanent, shareable" (§3) and carries `noindex` only
for the customer's own home document (§5) and for `*.reachkit.app` preview pages
(§9, §14.6). That a free report address is `noindex` and absent from our
sitemaps is the owner's ruling: a report is an auto-generated judgement about a
third party's site, and indexing one is the pattern §14's guardrails exist to
refuse.
