## Resuming: removed from criterion 1, and why

The first draft routed resuming to Stripe with the rest, on the owner's "all
billing … entirely". Reversed at review, on two verified facts rather than a
preference:

- **The portal cannot do it.** BP-060's `PORTAL_FEATURES` sets
  `subscription_update: { enabled: false }`, and a resume after the paid-through
  date needs a new subscription rather than a portal edit. Of the six things the
  first draft routed to Stripe, five are covered by the four enabled keys —
  `payment_method_update`, `customer_update` with
  `allowed_updates: ['address', 'tax_id', 'email']`, `invoice_history` and
  `subscription_cancel` — and resuming is the only one unreached. The `rests-on`
  row's first draft said "four of the six", which read as two unreached and named
  neither; it now names the coverage key by key.
- **Resuming is not only a Stripe act.** BP-060's `resumeSubscription` performs
  ReachKit-side effects Stripe's surface cannot: it "advances `paid_through`,
  clears `hosted_serving_ends_at` and both notice stamps". Moving the control
  without moving those effects would leave REQ-076 c6's self-service promise with
  no surface and BP-060's own function with no caller.

So resuming stays where approved REQ-076 c6, BP-055's `ACTIONS` member `'resume'`
and JN-004's last step already put it, and REQ-097 records it as a non-goal. Cost
of reversing: one clause in criterion 1 and one non-goal line. Consequences: the
`rests-on` row no longer carries a resume leg; `resumeSubscription` is no longer
contradicted; and JN-004 step 5 needs no resume clause, which is why the step is
left as it stands (rule 5.7 is met by the step that already exercises REQ-076).

**What round two added: the resume's payment.** Leaving resuming out of criterion
1 left the one money-moving act no criterion reached: BP-060's
`resumeSubscription` "creates a new Stripe subscription against the existing
customer", and a build could take that payment on a ReachKit-rendered card form
without failing anything. Criterion 3 now covers it. The owner's statement is
the derivation — "Checkout is COMPLETELY handled by stripe checkout and nothing we
should build" — and the criterion is written so that a resume charged to the card
already on file (nothing asked of the customer) passes, and a resume that needs a
new card sends the customer to Stripe's checkout or to the billing surface rather
than to a ReachKit form. Cost of reversing: one Given clause.

## The rests-on row, rescoped at round two

The round-one row called `tax_id` "the one leg this corpus has not verified" and
priced its fallback against one refutation. BP-060 holds **two** open rows on the
portal, and both are legs of criterion 1: the tax ID (REQ-076's row, carried
forward) and the billing address invoices and receipts are sent to. The row now
names both, prices either — REQ-076 criterion 9 for the VAT number, REQ-076
criterion 2 for the address, each left with no surface in the product if its leg
is refuted — and stops restating `PORTAL_FEATURES` key by key, which is BP-060's
to assert (rules 2.4, 2.5). Disposition stays `open`: both legs are dischargeable
by the validator of the first work order that configures the portal and observes
it (rule 2.3b), so `undischargeable` would be false. BP-060's third open row — that
`cancel_at_period_end` leaves `current_period_end` unchanged — is REQ-076
criterion 3's arithmetic, not a destination of criterion 1, and is not carried
here.

## "The plan": removed from criterion 1, and why

The first draft listed the plan among the things one control reaches on Stripe's
surface. `PORTAL_FEATURES` sets `subscription_update: { enabled: false }` and
REQ-076's non-goal fixes "one plan at €49/mo, with no upgrades, downgrades, annual
billing, seats or add-ons", so that control led to a surface on which nothing
about the plan could be done. Criterion 1 no longer routes it; the plan and its
price stay readable in ReachKit under this requirement's own price non-goal, and
open question 1 now asks about the three withheld values only, not about the plan
and price it keeps.

## REQ-070 is not contradicted, and the edge moved

The first draft carried `blocked-by: REQ-070` on the reading that its criteria 1
and 2 "list billing details, invoices, and cancelling or resuming among what
Settings offers". They do not require them. Both are **ceilings**: c1 — "nothing
beyond this list is offered for them to change"; c2 — "nothing beyond this list is
offered". Criterion 1 offers strictly less than either permits: a merged control
reaching invoices and cancelling offers items that are *on* c2's list, not items
beyond it, and rendering no field for billing details satisfies a ceiling on what
may be changed. REQ-070 therefore moves to `depends-on`. Cost of reversing: one
front-matter entry. The contradiction the first draft mislabelled as REQ-070's is
really BP-055's `ACTIONS` tuple, which the front-matter now names directly.

## One control, on Settings

Criterion 1's first draft read "exactly one control is offered for all of it",
which contradicted a clause requiring the control "on the screen where they
looked": on the instance reading a second screen needed a second instance the
first clause forbade, and no test passed both. Round one moved to **one
destination and no per-item control** and left the control's placement to "the
screen where they looked" — which names no screen, so the requirement's only
positive obligation was the one no test could fail (round two's finding).

The screen is derivable, not owner-shaped (rule 1.3). Every approved artifact
that places billing places it on Settings: REQ-076 c1, "Given a customer on
Settings, when they open billing"; REQ-070 c2, Settings does "reaching their
invoices, cancelling or resuming the plan"; BP-060, "the Settings billing card is
BP-001's surface"; `BUILD.md` §4.7's Billing card, quoted above. Criterion 1 now
states the positive obligation there — the control is offered on Settings where
billing is opened — and criterion 5 offers it "in their place" on the same
screen. Mechanically checkable: Settings renders a control whose destination
resolves to the portal session; no other route renders a billing field, form or
consequence screen; a build offering the control only on a deep sub-page fails
criterion 1. Cost of reversing: one Given clause.

## The return, and where it is promised

Criterion 1's first draft moved cancelling to Stripe and said nothing about the
customer's return. REQ-076 c2 carries the corpus's only return promise — "reach
Stripe's own customer portal and return to Settings afterwards still signed in" —
and scopes it to its own four acts, which do not include cancelling. JN-004 step 5
already promises it for cancelling: "leaving ReachKit for Stripe's own billing
surface to do any of the three, and coming back still signed in". Criterion 2 is
that promise, widened from four acts to five. Round one wrote the destination as
"the screen they left from", which differed from c2's on every screen but
Settings; with the control homed on Settings (section above) the two coincide,
and criterion 2 now carries c2's destination verbatim — Settings — so no build
satisfies this requirement while breaking an approved one. Derived from the
journey and REQ-076 c2 rather than asked (rule 1.3); reversal is one criterion.

## The boundary criterion 4 draws: a closed list of Stripe's events, with the exceptions named

One test is in force. Four were tried; the corpus has an approved case that splits
each of the other three, and this section records only that so the reason each
went is on file once (rule 2.4).

- **"Anything that happens to a customer's money."** Refuted by REQ-024 c3: a
  founder whose second purchase was charged is told, in an `account` mail, that it
  bought no second subscription. Money moved; Stripe cannot say that sentence.
- **"Stripe holds no fact about it."** Refuted by REQ-076 c3: Stripe holds the
  period-end date, and no money moves at the moment the customer is told it.
- **"The mail's subject"** (round one's rule). Refuted at round one: a subject is
  fixed by nothing mechanical, so REQ-076 c11's notice on a lapsed renewal reads
  as the payment to one reader and as the hosting to another, and a test would
  need a human ruling per mail; and it governed the subject only, so a body
  stating the next invoice amount passed it.
- **"The occasion's producer"** (round one's replacement — "a mail sent because
  Stripe recorded an event against the customer's billing record is Stripe's").
  Refuted at round two by approved REQ-024: c1's sign-in link "within 60 seconds"
  of a completed payment, c3's double-purchase `account` mail, c5's backstop that
  "says the payment succeeded" and c6's 24-hour link are all sent on an occasion
  Stripe recorded against the billing record, and all four are ReachKit's by an
  approved requirement. The test made them Stripe's by implication, with no
  `blocked-by` edge and no non-goal.

**The rule in force.** Criterion 4 names a closed list of the billing events
Stripe records — a charge taken, an invoice issued or upcoming, a renewal paid or
failed, a card expiring or changed, a refund, a chargeback, a cancellation — and
promises ReachKit sends nothing on any of them, then names the two exceptions by
their approved home: REQ-024's mails on a completed payment, and REQ-076 c11's
two hosting notices, whose occasion is `paid_through` passing on ReachKit's own
clock (BP-060, verbatim: "`hasActiveAccess` is `paid_through > now()`, full
stop") and not a Stripe event, even where the two moments coincide. Its second
sentence binds subject and body to a closed list of withheld values and verdicts,
and admits the one payment fact REQ-024 c5 requires — "says the payment
succeeded". Both halves are mechanical: a fixture per Stripe event asserts no
ReachKit send outside the two named exceptions, and a fixture per ReachKit mail
kind asserts none of the listed values in subject or body; adding a receipt send
or an amount line fails the test (§8, mutation). A build that sends no mail at all
passes both halves, as a prohibition should.

**Settled from approved text, not escalated.** Which mails ReachKit may send about
a payment was owner-shaped only if REQ-024 and REQ-076 left it open. They do not:
REQ-024 states the four payment-adjacent mails ReachKit sends and REQ-064's
approved non-goal marks "receipts, failed-payment notices and card-expiry
warnings" as "not ReachKit speaking". Criterion 4 names REQ-024 and lists the
class REQ-064's non-goal illustrates; REQ-097's non-goal records that REQ-064's
three examples illustrate the list rather than bounding it. Open question 2's
option of an `account` mail after cancellation is the one thing still open, and
the question now says that ruling it in would add a further named exception —
so approving criterion 4 as written does not rule the question by default.

## The mail register, checked

The owner asked which mail kinds disappear and whether the count changes. **None,
and no.** The register is not REQ-064's: REQ-064's non-goal assigns it to "one
table the mail blueprint holds", and BP-016 decision 1 makes it a typed constant
(`blueprints/BP-016.md`, `MAIL_KINDS`). REQ-064's non-goal names eight kinds; the
ninth, `first-page-unavailable`, is BP-016 decision 2's. **REQ-097 states neither
the count nor the list anywhere** — BP-016's own open-questions note records this
same restatement being cleaned out of REQ-079, REQ-074 and REQ-077, and it is not
re-minted here.

What the check found, recorded once, here: no row in `MAIL_KINDS` is sent on an
occasion criterion 4 lists, outside its two named exceptions. The rows the
statement comes near are `magic-link` (`occasionsFrom: 'REQ-024, REQ-077'`) and
`account` (`occasionsFrom: 'REQ-024, REQ-074, REQ-076, REQ-077, REQ-079'`). Their
REQ-024 occasions — the sign-in link on a completed payment (c1, c6), a second
purchase that bought no subscription (c3), the 15-minute backstop (c5) — are
criterion 4's first exception. Their REQ-076 occasion — the two hosting notices
(c11) — is the second, on ReachKit's clock. The rest are a destination that
stopped working (REQ-074), a moved sign-in address (REQ-077 c3) and the last mail
after erasure (REQ-079), none of which is an event on criterion 4's list. The
register is unchanged, which is why REQ-097 carries it as a non-goal rather than a
criterion.

## Criterion 6: a refused session, whatever refused it

Round one's Given read "Stripe cannot be reached — criterion 1's billing surface
refuses to open a session". BP-060's `portalLink` refuses on two grounds,
`'no_customer' | 'vendor'`, and only the second is Stripe unreachable. A customer
whose record carries no Stripe customer presses the one control this requirement
allows and, on the narrow reading, is told nothing. The Given now reads "Stripe
unreachable, or a session refused for any other reason". Because "try again"
cannot clear the first ground, the line the customer is told gains one way to
reach a person — the pattern approved REQ-024 c3 and c5 already use ("naming one
way to reach a person") for a paying founder stuck on the far side of a payment.
Rule 1.1: chosen, not asked; the words are BP-019's; cost of reversing is one
clause.

## The price, checked rather than asked

Criterion 5 enumerates what no ReachKit surface may state and the plan's price is
not in it. Derivation (rule 1.3, so it is not a question): €49/mo is a public
product fact stated on every price surface — REQ-022 criterion 1 binds "any
surface that states the price" and REQ-021 places the offer — not a value Stripe
holds about one customer, and it cannot go stale against Stripe the way a next
invoice date can. A signed-in customer may therefore still read €49 in ReachKit.
Cost of reversing: one clause in criterion 5 and one non-goal.
