The four custom surfaces

One sheet, two work orders: WO-033 (Sidebar, AiDotMatrix) and WO-034 (CalendarGrid, DayPanel). These are the only four places BUILD.md §2.2 admits custom CSS, alongside chart SVGs. Everything else on this sheet is a registered primitive from previews/WO-031.html.

They are drawn first among the surfaces because CalendarGrid and DayPanel are the only consumers of the three proposed layout tokens, and because the day panel carries an unresolved slot — §4 gap 7 — that a drawing settles faster than an argument.

4 · The ways-through slot — what §4 gap 7 was missing

REQ-043 criterion 12, verbatim on the shape: a delivered page offers “one way through to the page as a visitor sees it, at its public address” and, for a page on the customer’s own WordPress, “a second way through offered separately from the first, whose address is inside their own WordPress”; and where ReachKit’s record already says a way through leads nowhere, “the detail says so in place of that way rather than offering it.”

The registered contract held one undifferentiated actions slot, which can express neither an ordered pair nor a per-route withheld state. Below is what does.

4.1  Three cases per route, not two

Drawing it surfaced a case the widened contract did not name. A route can be offered, withheld with its account, or not applicable at all — and the third is not the second. A page delivered to the hosted blog has no WordPress route to withhold; rendering “this leads nowhere” for a route that never existed states a fact about a destination the customer never chose. It renders as nothing, and the list is one item long.

4.2  A page ReachKit unpublished — both keys visible at once

The hardest case, and the one that shows why the address label is a pair and not a sentence. REQ-056 c6: the address is offered as the one the page is publicly readable at only while it still stands published; from the moment it moves to unpublished the record presents the same address as the one the page was published at and never as one it is readable at now. Same string, two keys, and which key is used is a state, not a choice.

7 · Loading, empty, error — a proposal, not a contract

proposed components.md §6 records the hole: no registered primitive carries a loading state, Table has no error state, and its “never a skeleton” rules out the usual answer without naming a replacement. Rule 7.3 requires every data view to specify all three, so a surface sheet cannot stay silent. What follows is drawn as a proposal and is not law until you approve it. It is registered in components.md §6 as proposed, not written into any component’s States cell.

7.1  The proposal, in one rule

A surface that is re-measuring keeps showing the last measurement, and says which one it is showing. Never a skeleton, never a spinner, never an indeterminate bar (REQ-003 c1 forbids the last two by name). The provenance line §2.5 already requires is the carrier: it is always visible and always quiet, so making it say which measurement is on screen costs a surface nothing it was not already spending.

Where there is no previous measurement, there is nothing to keep showing, and the proposal splits: if the stages are known, the surface renders them through Steps, every stage named; if they are not, it renders one written line and no motion at all. That second branch is §4 gap 4 unchanged — this proposal narrows the hole, it does not close it.

7.2  A measurement that failed

§2.5: “A measurement that failed says so in one written sentence.” Proposed tone: warn, never bad. A measurement ReachKit could not take is ReachKit’s problem, and §2.5 reserves red for the customer’s own problem shown to them. The surface keeps its frame; the failed section is absent with one line in its place, which is exactly §4.1’s registered degraded behaviour applied to a custom surface.
