# ADR-086 — A credential that cannot publish is its own occasion inside the failing state: a distinct line and an action that leads to a different account — amends ADR-084 Decision 3

- Date: 2026-09-01
- **Amends ADR-084 Decision 3 without superseding ADR-084.** ADR-084 stands in
  full: its ruling, its warning block, its five decisions and its arm-inversion
  table are unchanged and are not restated here (rule 2.4). One clause of its
  Decision 3 is now false, and *Instrument* below states which and why the
  instrument is an amendment rather than a supersession — the same test ADR-084
  itself applied to ADR-083.
- **Binds** REQ-060 criterion 7 and REQ-057 criterion 9, both `in-review`.

## Context

ADR-084 Decision 3 ruled that a WordPress credential which can create posts but
cannot publish them is a **destination state**, never a silently drafted post,
and put that state at `failing` — one of the three REQ-074 criterion 1 already
closes over. That ruling stands entire, and the probe (`canPublish`) it names is
built as written.

The clause that is now false, verbatim from ADR-084 Decision 3:

> "and a destination that cannot publish reads as **`failing`** — one of the
> three states REQ-074 criterion 1 already closes over, with the Reconnect
> action criterion 2 already offers. No new destination state and no change to
> REQ-074."

Three assertions, of which one survives and two do not.

**"No new destination state" survives.** `failing` is still the state, and
`DestinationHealth` still has exactly `BUILD.md` §10's three members.

**"With the Reconnect action criterion 2 already offers" is false.** REQ-060
criterion 7, as the analyst folded it on 2026-09-01, reads:

> "what the customer is told there says that the site is connected but the
> account ReachKit uses on it cannot publish, and never that pages are being
> held and nothing has been lost; the action it offers leads to giving ReachKit
> a credential for an account that can publish there, and re-entering the same
> credential is never offered as the remedy."

REQ-074 criterion 2's Reconnect is the expired-credential remedy, and the line
that travels with it says pages are being held and nothing has been lost. Both
halves are wrong here, and wrong in a way that costs the customer real time:

- The credential is **valid**. Re-entering it is the one action guaranteed to
  change nothing, and it is the action a customer offered "Reconnect" will take
  first. A capability is not restored by re-authenticating as the same user;
  it is restored by a different WordPress user, or by that user's role changing
  in a screen ReachKit does not own.
- The page has **already failed**, not been held. ADR-084 Decision 3 itself says
  so — the delivery "fails for a reason no repeated attempt could clear, so the
  page comes to rest needing the customer (REQ-056 criterion 4)". Telling that
  customer "nothing has been lost" over a page in `needs_attention` is a false
  statement about their own content.

**"No change to REQ-074" is false.** REQ-060 criterion 7's fold narrowed its own
non-goal — which had closed the destinations list to REQ-074 criteria 1 **and**
2 — down to criterion 1 alone, precisely because criterion 2 no longer covers
this occasion. REQ-074 today covers only the expired-credential occasion and
owes a criterion for this one. That is the analyst's to write, not this file's;
its absence is recorded as a `rests-on` row above rather than as a `blocked-by`,
because nothing here is in conflict with REQ-074 — REQ-074 is silent, and
silence is a gap, not a contradiction (rule 2.3).

## Instrument — why ADR-084 is amended and not superseded

ADR-084's own test: the instrument is chosen by whether the *ruling* changed,
not by whether a sentence did.

- **ADR-084 Decision 3's ruling did not change.** A credential that cannot
  publish is a destination state and never a drafted post; it reads `failing`;
  the probe runs at connect and at each health check; a delivery against it
  fails non-retryably; no publication row is ever `delivered` with a draft
  behind it. Every one of those survives and is built as written.
- **What changed is what the state *offers and says*** — one line and one
  action, inside a state ADR-084 fixed and this file does not move. That is the
  ADR-083 side of ADR-084's own line, not the ADR-082 side: ADR-084's warning
  block is untouched and still points at the right arm, so nothing a future
  reader trusts in that file is now misleading. Rule 2.2a: record the
  assessment, move nothing.
- **It still earns a file.** Rule 2.2's second test — it amends another decision
  — is met on its own, and rule 2.2b forbids editing an `accepted` ADR in place.
  It also meets the first test: the occasion spans BP-058's probe and
  destination view, BP-046's telling, BP-045's failure reason and BP-048's error
  mapping, and no one of those owns the others.
- **Discoverability**, the gap ADR-084 closed for ADR-083 by overlapping
  `decides-for`: this file's `decides-for` is a subset of ADR-084's, so every
  node that carries this decision carries ADR-084 too, and BP-058 — the node
  where the wrong action would actually be built — carries both.

## Decision

**Decision 1 — `cannot_publish` is a `HealthReason`, not a fourth
`DestinationHealth`.**

BP-058's `HealthReason` union gains `cannot_publish`; `DestinationHealth` stays
`'ok' | 'expired' | 'error'`, and a destination in this occasion is
`error`/`cannot_publish` — the pair the customer reads as **failing**. This is
BP-058 decision 2's existing mechanism ("keep the three states and add a reason
that selects the copy key and the action") applied to a fourth occasion, not a
new one; REQ-060 criterion 7's own non-goal says it "supplies one further
occasion for the failing state, adds no state".

**Decision 2 — the action is `reconnect_other_account`, and it is a distinct
member of `DestinationView.action`.**

`action` becomes `'none' | 'reconnect' | 'reconnect_other_account' | 'set_dns'`.
It is not `reconnect` with a different copy key. The distinction is in the
action union rather than in the sentence because a copy key is a string a
surface may route around and a union member is not: REQ-060 criterion 7's "re-
entering the same credential is never offered as the remedy" is then a property
of the type, and a surface that offers the ordinary Reconnect control in this
state fails to typecheck rather than fails a copy review.

Whether the two actions lead to the same form is a surface question and is
BP-001's; what this file fixes is that the destination read model states which
of the two is owed, and that the *ordinary* one is not.

**Decision 3 — the line is its own copy key, and the held-pages count is not
rendered in this occasion.**

`DestinationView.copy.line` carries a key of this occasion's own, distinct from
the expired-credential line. `heldPages` stays on the view — it is a count, and
the count is genuinely zero-or-more — but this occasion's line may not be
written from it, because the page that failed here is in `needs_attention` and
is not a held page (BP-045: holding is the absence of an edge; `needs_attention`
is a state a page came to rest in). The sentence is owner-owed and this file
mints none; the escalation is carried forward from ADR-084's own Open questions
and is not re-opened.

**Decision 4 — the probe's result is read at compose time by the telling, not
only at delivery.**

REQ-057 criterion 9 gained an arm for this occasion: where at the moment the
telling is sent ReachKit already reads that site as one it cannot publish to,
the telling says the page cannot go live there as things stand and what must
change, without suppressing the date criteria 1, 7 and 8 have it name and
without suppressing the stop action. So BP-046's `tellingFor` reads the
destination's current health, not only `drafts.told`. That is a new dependency
edge — BP-046 → BP-058 — and it is deliberate: the alternative is a fourth
`Telling` arm decided at generation time from a fact that can change before the
mail is composed, which is exactly the stale second copy rule 2.4 forbids.

**Decision 5 — a delivery attempted against such a credential fails with
`credentials_invalid`, not a new `FailureReason` and not
`credentials_expired`.**

`credentials_invalid` is already in BP-045's non-retryable set and already means
"this credential will not do what we need and no repetition will change that",
which is exactly true here. `credentials_expired` is rejected because BP-048's
error mapping already routes 401/403 to it and BP-058 marks the destination
`expired` from it — which would put this occasion in the *wrong one* of REQ-074
criterion 1's three states and hand the customer the Reconnect action Decision 2
exists to withhold. The health state and the reason are BP-058's, decided by the
probe; the delivery's failure is BP-045's, decided by the adapter; they must
agree, and this is the pairing that makes them agree without a fifth reason.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Leave ADR-084 Decision 3's clause standing and let BP-058 quietly render a
   different line** — rejected. The clause is the one a future reader of ADR-084
   will trust when they wire the destinations list, and a blueprint that
   contradicts an `accepted` decision with no file saying why is the divergence
   rule 2.4 exists to prevent.
2. **Supersede ADR-084** — rejected under *Instrument*. Its ruling survives
   entire, its landmine warning is still correct, and superseding a landmine
   file to correct one clause means the survivor must restate the whole warning
   block or lose it. Rule 2.2a forbids the restructuring, and ADR-084 itself
   took the same decision about ADR-083.
3. **A fourth `DestinationHealth` member, `cannot_publish`** — rejected. It
   changes a column `BUILD.md` §10 specifies and makes REQ-074 criterion 1's
   three customer-read states a mapping every surface has to know. BP-058
   decision 2 already rejected exactly this for exactly this reason.
4. **One `reconnect` action with a different sentence** — rejected under
   Decision 2. The promise "re-entering the same credential is never offered as
   the remedy" is then enforced by nobody: the control is the same control, and
   a surface renders it from `action === 'reconnect'` without ever reading the
   line.
5. **Block the delivery before it is attempted, so no page ever fails here** —
   rejected. BP-045's `destination_working` guard already holds pages against a
   destination that is not `ok`, so in the ordinary case no delivery is
   attempted; but the probe can be stale by one health-check interval, and
   REQ-060 criterion 7 requires the *delivery* to fail non-retryably when it
   does happen. Relying on the guard alone leaves the race unspecified and the
   page in `publishing`.
6. **Record this inside BP-058's `## Decisions`** — rejected under rule 2.2
   twice: it amends an `accepted` decision, and it settles a reason and an action
   in BP-058, a telling and a dependency edge in BP-046, a failure reason in
   BP-045 and an error mapping in BP-048.

## Consequences

- BP-046 gains `depends-on: BP-058`. At **file** granularity the new edge is
  `publishable/telling.ts → destinations/health/`, and `destinations/` imports
  from `publish/switch/` and from nothing else under `src/lib/publish/` — so the
  import graph stays acyclic and structure.md rule 6 holds.
  At **node** granularity it is not so clean, and it is worth saying rather than
  glossing: `BP-045 ↔ BP-058` is already a mutual `depends-on` pair in this
  corpus (BP-045's `attempt/` calls the registry and reads health; BP-058's
  `reconnect` reads BP-045's `switch/`), so this edge joins an existing node-level
  cycle and makes it a triangle. That cycle is an artifact of node granularity —
  BP-045 owns two directories that sit on opposite sides of BP-058 — and not a
  build cycle; it predates this file and both rulings, and rule 2.2a says record
  the assessment and move nothing. Splitting `switch/` off BP-045 to break it is
  restructuring with no user-visible value; if it is ever done, it is its own
  pass and its own decision.
- The destinations list has four reasons inside three states. Every one of them
  is a distinct sentence the owner owes; none is derived from another.
- **Cost of reversing.** Decision 2 is one union member and reverses by deletion
  — which is the deletion that reads as removing a redundant action and hands
  every affected customer the one remedy that cannot work. Decision 5 reverses
  by pointing the mapping at `credentials_expired`, which reads as more accurate
  and silently moves the destination into the wrong state. Both are cheap in
  code and expensive in what the customer is told to do.

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-09-01, minted at `accepted` on the
precedent ADR-082, ADR-083 and ADR-084 set in this corpus. Rule 3.2 makes the
status this agent's assertion, audited by the librarian afterwards; rule 3.4
states that a decision takes no owner-approval round.

**Grounds.** The behaviour is REQ-060 criterion 7's and REQ-057 criterion 9's
own text; the mechanism — which union carries the discriminator, which reason
the adapter returns, where the telling reads health — is boundaries and names,
which rule 1.1 places with this agent. The sentence the customer reads is
owner-owed and is carried forward, not minted.

**What is not a ground, and why it does not block.** REQ-060 and REQ-057 are
`status: in-review` pending the owner's signature, with zero open `REVIEW(...)`
lines after two full review rounds (rule 3.4). §3's gate binds a *blueprint* to
an *approved* requirement; this file does not claim the gate is met. REQ-074
owes a criterion for this occasion and does not yet carry one — recorded as a
`rests-on` row above, `open`, because a silent requirement is a gap and not a
conflict (rule 2.3).
