# ADR-094 — LANDMINE — the free report's AI matrix sets `load_async_ai_overview`; `BUILD.md`'s own "never set it" line is now wrong

> **Read this before "fixing" `serpOrganic`'s free-matrix call back to
> `loadAsyncAiOverview: false`.** `BUILD.md` §6.2 says "Never set
> `load_async_ai_overview`" and §6.4's never-list names it explicitly, in
> writing. Restoring that literal reads as specification fidelity. It silently
> reintroduces the exact undercount the owner ruled against: an AI answer
> Google served asynchronously renders as "no AI answer," and the free
> report's stated denominator becomes false again.
>
> **And read this before "fixing" the other asymmetry.** Since the owner's
> ruling of 2026-09-03 (`OWNER-QUESTIONS.md` item 9) the *correction*'s twelve
> SERPs pass `false` while the initial scan's pass `true` — Decision 5 below.
> Two call sites of one function disagreeing on one boolean reads like an
> oversight. It is the lever the owner chose in place of raising `CAPS.FREE_C`:
> making them agree spends the free report's one allowance twice as fast and
> stops the correction mid-flight, which is the outcome the ruling exists to
> avoid.

- Date: 2026-09-03

## Context
`registry/evidence/RESEARCH-dataforseo-endpoints.md` §2.3, read against `BUILD.md`
§6.2 and §6.4 and against BP-008's own enforcement of the never-list: DataForSEO's
`ai_overview` item carries `asynchronous_ai_overview`, and its docs state
verbatim (quoted in full in the evidence file) that Google "frequently" serves an
AI Overview this way, that the default request only returns a cached one, and
that an asynchronous one is returned as `"ai_overview": null` unless
`load_async_ai_overview: true` is set on the request — at a cost of one extra
base price, refunded when the flag turns out to be unused.

`BUILD.md` §6.2 states, unconditionally: "Never set `load_async_ai_overview`."
§6.4's never-list repeats it as one of the calls BP-008's client may not make.
BP-008 enforced this by absence — no function on its surface could set the
flag — and REQ-006 criterion 2 ("that cell states that no AI answer appeared")
was accordingly silently false on any search where Google served an
asynchronous overview the product never fetched: an AI answer had appeared and
was rendered as though none had.

`OWNER-QUESTIONS.md` item 1 put the choice to the owner: pay the surcharge so
the card counts Google's actual AI answers, or keep the 0¢ rule and add a
disclosure that the card counts only answers Google had cached. The owner's
ruling, verbatim: "the free report loads asynchronous AI Overviews
(`load_async_ai_overview`), the cost stays inside the 12¢ cap; the card counts
Google's actual AI answers."

The worst-case cost is bounded and self-refunding (evidence §2.3, derived):
12 live SERPs at the surcharge rate is 4.8¢ instead of 2.4¢, taking the free
report's own total from BP-025's ~6.6¢ (decision 2, the two-nano-call reading)
to **~7.2¢ worst case** for that node's spend, comfortably inside `CAPS.FREE_C`
12¢. Nothing here is a promise the product cannot afford.

This spans three nodes and none of them owns it alone:
- **BP-008** enforces the never-list by absence and is the only place a flag
  can be admitted at all.
- **BP-007**'s `recordFetch` contract takes a fixed `costCents` per call, known
  before `run()` executes — a design that assumes every endpoint's price is
  fixed. This is the one endpoint whose actual vendor charge is data-dependent
  (1x or 2x the base price, decided by the vendor after the call), and the
  contract is not to change for it.
- **BP-025** is the only caller permitted to set the flag, and its NFR budget
  states the free report's cost envelope.

And it amends a written rule (`BUILD.md` §6.2/§6.4) rather than merely adding
to one, so under rule 2.2's sharpened form and the constitution's landmine
clause this is a standalone file, not an entry inside any of the three.

## Decision
1. **`serpOrganic`'s `loadAsyncAiOverview` parameter is required, never
   defaulted** — the same pattern BP-008 decision 2 already applies to `mode`,
   so a call site that has not decided is a compile error rather than an
   inherited default.
2. **Exactly one call site passes `true`**: BP-025's twelve-question free-matrix
   call (`phrase.ts`'s SERP fan-out, `src/lib/market/questions/`). Every other
   `serpOrganic` call site — the paid weekly/deep target-SERP battery (BP-050,
   BP-036) — passes `false` explicitly, unchanged. **This ruling is scoped to
   the free report only**, per the owner's own words ("the free report loads
   asynchronous AI Overviews"); extending it to the paid battery is a separate
   question this file does not decide.
3. **The cost seam ledgers the surcharge-inclusive price on every flagged
   call, unconditionally** — `recordFetch`'s `costCents` is set to the
   surcharge rate (2x the base price) whenever `loadAsyncAiOverview: true` is
   passed, whether or not DataForSEO's own response later refunds the
   difference. BP-007's interface is untouched: this is a choice BP-008's
   call site makes about what value to pass, not a new capability the seam
   needs. See the `rests-on` row above for why this cannot under-count spend.
3a. **The surcharge is *reserved* before the call and *settled* from the
   response** — Status: accepted (amends decision 3; seam shape and parameter,
   rule 1.1; taken 2026-09-03, after the item-9 ruling)
   - Context: decision 3 ledgered the surcharge-inclusive price on every flagged
     call and accepted the consequence in terms — BP-007's own Error & edge
     bullet reads "a free scan can therefore degrade at a ceiling it did not, in
     the vendor's own accounting, actually reach". That was harmless while the
     free path had one spender: the phantom 2.4¢ sat inside 3.0¢ of headroom
     nothing else wanted. **Decision 5 ends that.** Under the owner's item-9
     ruling the correction runs inside the same ceiling, needs 4.5¢ and finds
     3.0¢ — so on an uncached scan it is the phantom charge, not the money, that
     truncates the corrected report. The fact that justifies re-opening a twice-
     accepted convention is the second spender, which did not exist when
     decision 3 or BP-007's bullet was written.
   - Decision: `recordFetch` **reserves** `costCents` before the call exactly as
     today — the cap check is unchanged and no call is made against money the
     context has not got — and, where the call site supplies one, **settles** the
     ledgered figure from the response afterwards:
     `settleCents?: (payload: P) => number`, with `settled ≤ reserved` an
     invariant the seam enforces. BP-008's `serpOrganic` supplies the closure for
     the one flagged endpoint and applies the vendor's own documented rule — base
     price where the `ai_overview` element is absent or carries
     `asynchronous_ai_overview: false`, the surcharge rate otherwise. Vendor
     docs, verbatim (`registry/evidence/RESEARCH-dataforseo-endpoints.md` §2.3):
     "if the element is absent or contains \"asynchronous_ai_overview\": false,
     all extra charges will be returned to your account balance".
   - **Why this is not the alternative decision 3 rejected.** Alternative 5 below
     proposed widening `costCents` itself to `number | ((payload: P) => number)`,
     which removes the pre-call figure the cap check needs and changes a required
     field at all twelve call sites. This adds one *optional* field, leaves the
     eleven other call sites byte-identical, and keeps the reservation
     conservative: cap-safety and ledger accuracy together, rather than one
     traded for the other. The stated ground for the rejection — "a seam eleven
     other functions already rely on being simple" — does not reach this shape.
   - **What it costs if the vendor's documented refund does not land:** the
     ledger under-counts by at most 0.2¢ per flagged SERP, 2.4¢ per scan, and
     1.5¢ of real overspend per corrected report. The `rests-on` row added above
     names the reconciliation that discharges it. The confirmed row stands
     unchanged: the vendor mechanics it states are the same ones this decision
     reads in the other direction.
   - Consequences: BP-007 gains the optional argument and the invariant (its
     "accepted, not held as a refundable balance" bullet is replaced); BP-008
     supplies the closure; BP-025 and BP-028 state reserved and settled
     separately. A corrected report completes inside 12¢ whenever Google served
     asynchronous overviews on **at most four** of the twelve searches, instead
     of never on an uncached scan. `CAPS.FREE_C` does not move and no number a
     customer reads changes.
   - Cost of reversing: delete one optional argument — after which the ledger is
     conservative again and the correction truncates systematically again. Loud
     in the corpus, silent in production, which is why it is written here.
4. **`no_answer` now means what it says.** With the flag set, a `null`
   `ai_overview` on the free matrix's SERPs is a confirmed absence, not an
   unfetched asynchronous one — closing the gap the evidence file's §2.3
   identified in REQ-006 criterion 2. BP-025's decision 4 records the call-site
   change and the NFR consequence. **On a correction's pass the flag is not set**
   (Decision 5), and there `no_answer` is not reachable at all: an un-flagged
   payload cannot tell "no AI answer" from "an asynchronous one nobody
   fetched", so a null is `unmeasured / undeterminable` and the corrected card
   discloses what it could read. BP-025 decision 6 owns that cell rule; this
   file owns only the flag.
5. **A correction's twelve SERPs pass `false`** — added 2026-09-03, owner
   ruling on `OWNER-QUESTIONS.md` item 9, which amends Decision 2's "exactly
   one call site passes `true`" to *exactly one pass* of that call site. The
   owner's words, verbatim, on the cap this protects: "12c is already at the
   top end of what im willing to spend therefore would keep the limit here for
   the current time. this is a lead magnet but I am not yet confident what a
   user wants from us therefore wasting money on it is a crime." Item 9 offered
   three levers and the owner took the second — *don't pay on the second pass*:
   `CAPS.FREE_C` does not move (BP-005), and the correction's re-run of the
   free matrix declines the surcharge instead.
   - **Where the distinction travels:** nowhere new. Decision 1 already makes
     `loadAsyncAiOverview` a required parameter with no default, and BP-012's
     `runScan({ correctionOf })` already knows which pass it is running; the
     value the free-matrix fan-out is passed is `correctionOf === undefined`.
     No field, no flag on `CostContext`, no new interface, and BP-028's
     `CORRECTION_SCOPE` is unchanged.
   - **What it costs the reader, and where that is disclosed:** a corrected
     card counts only the AI answers Google had cached — the limitation the
     owner declined for the first pass, now applying to the report a reader
     asked us to fix. The corrected card therefore says so. The **words** are
     customer-visible copy and the owner's (constitution §1): REQ-094 carries
     the disclosure criterion, BP-019's registry holds the string, and
     `AiAnswersCard` carries only the *state* the surface renders it from
     (BP-025 decision 6). No sentence is written in this file.
   - **What it does not fix:** the pair still does not fit the cap in the worst
     case. The correction's own ledgered spend falls from 6.9¢ to 4.5¢, so the
     worst-case pair falls from 15.9¢ to 13.5¢ against 12¢ — better, not
     inside. BP-028 decision 4 owns that arithmetic and the residual it leaves;
     it is not restated here (rule 2.4).

## Alternatives considered
1. **Keep the 0¢ rule and disclose the limitation** (`OWNER-QUESTIONS.md`
   item 1's other branch) — this would have required a new customer-visible
   caveat on the card (a string, the owner's to write) stating that the count
   excludes answers Google had not cached. The owner did not take this branch;
   no REQ-006 text changes as a result of this decision.
2. **Set the flag on every `serpOrganic` call, free and paid alike** — rejected
   as broader than what was ruled. The owner's words name the free report; the
   paid weekly battery's own cost and accuracy trade-off (REQ-063's page
   verdicts read AI-citation tests off these same SERPs) is not this file's to
   decide and is recorded as an open question on BP-051 instead.
3. **Set the flag on the correction's pass too and raise `CAPS.FREE_C` to
   ~18¢ to pay for it** (`OWNER-QUESTIONS.md` item 9's first lever) — the
   corrected card would keep the first pass's promise exactly. The owner
   declined it in the words quoted in Decision 5; what would have moved is the
   free report's stated cost envelope and the worst-case daily exposure at 200
   free scans a day.
4. **Change nothing and let the correction degrade at the cap** (item 9's third
   lever) — rejected by the same ruling. It is the cheapest, and it fails
   hardest on exactly the reports whose market was read wrongly: a flagged
   correction exhausts the remaining allowance inside its first few SERPs.
5. **Widen `recordFetch`'s `costCents` to accept a function of the response**
   (`costCents: number | ((payload: P) => number)`) so the ledger could record
   the vendor's actual, possibly-refunded charge — rejected: it changes BP-007's
   contract for every endpoint to accommodate the one endpoint whose price is
   data-dependent, and the conservative fixed-price ledgering in Decision 3
   achieves the same cap-safety without touching a seam eleven other functions
   already rely on being simple.

## Consequences
- BP-025 decision 6 records the correction pass's `false` and the disclosure
  state on `AiAnswersCard`; BP-028 decision 4 and its NFR budget carry the
  two-spender arithmetic. BP-008 is unaffected by Decision 5 — the required
  parameter it already declares is the whole mechanism.
- BP-008's `serpOrganic` signature and one Error & edge behavior bullet change;
  its Decisions section gains one entry citing this file.
- BP-025's NFR budget and one Error & edge behavior bullet change to state the
  new worst case and the closed gap; its Decisions section gains one entry
  citing this file.
- BP-007 is not edited. Its fixed-cost-per-call contract stands, and this file
  is the record of why the one data-dependent endpoint does not need it
  changed.
- `BUILD.md` §6.2 and §6.4 are not edited here; the divergence from their
  written text is recorded in this file, which is where a reader restoring
  "spec fidelity" will find out why not to.
- **Cost of reversing:** trivial in code — one boolean at one call site — and
  that cheapness is the danger, exactly as ADR-060 states for its own landmine.
  Reversing it does not fail loudly: the free report keeps rendering, the cap
  is still respected, and the only symptom is that some fraction of "no AI
  answer" cells quietly go back to meaning "we did not check" on searches
  where Google served an answer nobody read.
