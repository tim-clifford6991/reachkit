# ADR-052 — LANDMINE — the €49 price object is tax-inclusive and Stripe Tax stays off; switching tax on must never raise a customer's bill

- Date: 2026-08-31

> **Read this before enabling Stripe Tax, before creating a replacement Price, and
> before "fixing" the missing VAT line on an invoice.** Enabling tax is the right
> thing to do eventually and the deferral is deliberate. Enabling it against a
> price that was created tax-exclusive raises every existing customer's bill by up
> to 27%, and nobody will notice until the invoices go out.

## Context
Three facts sit on top of each other here, and only the first is obvious.

**One: tax is deferred, on the record.** `BUILD.md` §13: "**Tax handling is deferred
(owner ruling, 28 Aug): no Stripe Tax at launch** — charge €49, collect the buyer's
country (Stripe does automatically) and VAT ID field on, so the records exist when
registration is set up. This is a known, accepted compliance debt from customer one;
revisit before meaningful EU B2C volume." REQ-022's non-goals repeat the deferral,
and REQ-076's non-goals repeat it again.

**Two: the customer's bill may never rise when that changes.** REQ-022 criterion 4:

> then it is €49 in euro … with no tax amount added to it or taken from it, any VAT
> due being contained within the €49, **so no customer's bill rises because tax
> registration was set up**.

That is a promise about a future the product has not reached yet. It is not
satisfied by anything the code does at launch, because at launch no tax is
calculated at all. It is satisfied — or broken — by one field on a Stripe Price
object created before the first customer pays.

**Three: the field is invisible while tax is off.** A Price created with
`tax_behavior: 'exclusive'` and a Price created `inclusive` charge exactly the same
€49 while `automatic_tax` is disabled. Every test passes. Every invoice reads €49.
The difference appears only on the day somebody enables Stripe Tax, at which point
the exclusive price becomes €49 + VAT — €61.74 for a German consumer — and the
inclusive price stays €49 with the VAT derived from inside it.

**Why this reads as a bug today.** An engineer or an accountant looking at a
European SaaS with EU customers, no VAT on the invoices, and a `tax_id_collection`
field that is collected and never used, is looking at a compliance problem. The
obvious fix is one dashboard toggle. The deferral is not written anywhere they will
look, and the consequence of the toggle is not visible until the next billing cycle.

**Why it spans blueprints.** BP-030 creates the Checkout Session against the price.
BP-060 renews against it and shows it in the billing summary and the portal. BP-005
pins the number and asserts it. The Price object itself lives in Stripe, outside
every `code:` glob in this corpus, which means no blueprint's file is where somebody
would find this. That is the case rule 2.2 reserves a file for.

## Decision
**The Stripe Price backing `STRIPE_PRICE_ID` is created once, in EUR, recurring
monthly, at 4900 minor units, with `tax_behavior: 'inclusive'`. `automatic_tax` is
`false` everywhere. Both are asserted in tests, not left to the dashboard.**

1. `PRICE_EUR_CENTS = 4900`, `PRICE_CURRENCY = 'eur'`, `PRICE_INTERVAL = 'month'`
   are pins in `src/lib/config/constants.ts` (BP-005), asserted in
   `tests/pins.test.ts`.
2. `CHECKOUT_PARAMS.automatic_tax` is `{ enabled: false }` and
   `CHECKOUT_PARAMS.currency` is `'eur'` (BP-030), both asserted field-by-field.
3. A startup check reads the live Price behind `STRIPE_PRICE_ID` and refuses to boot
   if its `unit_amount`, `currency`, `recurring.interval` or `tax_behavior` differ
   from the pins. A price object that drifts from the pin is a production incident,
   not a config detail, and the environment variable is the only thing standing
   between a correct price and a wrong one.
4. `tax_id_collection` stays enabled and the VAT number is recorded verbatim
   (REQ-022 criteria 6 and 7, BP-030), with no VIES check and no country blocked.
   Collecting it while not using it is the point of the deferral: the records exist
   when registration is set up.
5. No surface displays, calculates or implies a tax amount. The only sentences about
   the price are BP-019's `price.amount`, `price.vat_included` and `price.interval`
   copy keys, and their content is the owner's.
6. **When tax is enabled** — the revisit named in `BUILD.md` §13 — the change is
   `automatic_tax: { enabled: true }` and nothing else. Because the price is
   inclusive, the gross stays €49 and VAT is derived from within it, which is
   REQ-022 criterion 4 holding. Any change to the *amount* is a change to what the
   product promises and is the owner's alone (constitution §1's rights table).

## Alternatives considered
1. **Create the price tax-exclusive, since no tax is charged at launch anyway** —
   rejected: it is invisible now and breaks REQ-022 criterion 4 the moment tax is
   enabled, by raising the bill of every customer already on it. Correcting it later
   means a new Price and a migration of every live subscription.
2. **Enable Stripe Tax at launch and be done with it** — rejected: it contradicts an
   owner ruling on the record (`BUILD.md` §13, 28 Aug), and with an inclusive price
   it also changes what ReachKit nets per customer by country, which is a business
   decision, not an engineering one.
3. **Charge €49 + VAT and say so on the pricing card** — rejected: it is a change to
   the price the product promises (REQ-022 criterion 1, "it reads €49 per month and
   says that VAT is included"), which only the owner may make.
4. **Leave `tax_behavior` unset and rely on the account default** — rejected: the
   default is not `inclusive` in every configuration, and an unset field is exactly
   the invisible state this decision exists to remove.

## Consequences
- ReachKit's net revenue per customer varies by the buyer's country once tax is
  enabled, from €49 down to roughly €38.58 at 27%. That is the cost of the promise
  in REQ-022 criterion 4 and it is accepted here, not discovered later.
- Until tax is enabled, invoices carry no VAT line and the compliance debt on the
  record stands. This file does not discharge it; it makes discharging it safe.
- Reversal is genuinely expensive: `tax_behavior` is fixed at price creation, so an
  exclusive price must be replaced and every existing subscription moved to the new
  one, with the customer's amount held constant through the move. That asymmetry —
  cheap now, expensive after the first customer — is why this is written before any
  price object exists.
- Revisit trigger, verbatim from `BUILD.md` §13: "before meaningful EU B2C volume".
