# ADR-061 — LANDMINE — "nothing worth publishing" is a proven arm, never the fallback; an unattributed empty day is ReachKit's own stop

> **Read this before simplifying the empty-day resolver.** Collapsing its last
> two arms into one `else` that says the market offered nothing is the obvious
> cleanup: it removes a branch, it removes a state nobody can name, and
> `BUILD.md` §2.5 calls the empty queue "a success state". It makes the product
> lie to a customer about their own market, which is the single thing REQ-043
> criterion 3 was written to prevent.

- Date: 2026-08-31

## Context
A date on the calendar with no page must carry **exactly one** account of itself
and that account must be true. REQ-043 splits the space in two:

- **criterion 3** — the date is empty *because the supply of opportunities was
  exhausted and nothing in the customer's market was worth publishing*. It gets
  the exhausted-supply line. And then, decisively: "**No date emptied by any
  other cause, whether or not a requirement names that cause, ever carries that
  line.**"
- **criterion 4** — the date is empty for *"a page that can no longer go live, a
  day ReachKit did not do the work, a change the customer saved that holds pages
  back, or **any cause whatever**"*. It gets exactly one written line, and that
  line "says which cause emptied it".

Criterion 4's "any cause whatever" and criterion 3's "whether or not a
requirement names that cause" are the same instruction read from both ends: the
resolver must be **total**, and its total-ness must not be achieved by letting
the exhausted-supply line absorb the remainder.

Three facts make the wrong shape attractive:

1. **It is the natural `else`.** Every other arm has a positive test — an
   outstanding instruction exists, a kill switch is engaged, a draft is
   `skipped`, publishing is off. "Nothing was worth publishing" reads like the
   absence of all of those, which is precisely what an `else` is for.
2. **`BUILD.md` §2.5 blesses it.** "an empty queue is a success state ('Nothing
   worth publishing today')" — a designer reading that will make it the default
   empty state for the whole grid.
3. **The failing case is invisible.** An unattributed empty date is, by
   definition, one the product could not explain. Nobody notices it in review; it
   appears in production for the customer whose job crashed in a way nobody
   enumerated.

And the harm is not cosmetic. The customer bought a subscription whose premise is
that their market contains work worth doing. A date reading *nothing in your
market was worth publishing* on a day the product simply failed tells them, in
the product's own voice, that their market is empty — the most damaging false
statement this product can make, and the one a churning customer quotes back.
REQ-092 exists entirely to keep the two apart: criterion 1 says what a customer
reads on such a day "tells them it was ReachKit that did not do the work, and
**never** that there was nothing worth publishing in their market".

The same resolver is read by two surfaces — the calendar grid and day panel
(BP-039) and Overview, which states a stopped day on the screen the customer
lands on without their opening a calendar day (REQ-092 criterion 3, BP-038). It
therefore spans two nodes and neither owns it, which is rule 2.2's file test; and
it is a decision that reads as wrong and is load-bearing, which is the landmine
rule.

## Decision
**The empty-date resolver is a first-match over a fixed precedence, the
exhausted-supply arm requires positive proof, and the fallback arm is ReachKit's
own stop.**

```
EMPTY_PRECEDENCE = [
  'instruction',                  // REQ-047 c5 — outstanding, so it is the date's one account
  'reachkit_stopped',             // REQ-092 c1 — cap, halt, or a step that failed
  'page_cannot_go_live',          // REQ-043 c4 — skipped / unpublished
  'customer_change_holds_pages',  // REQ-043 c4 — publishing off, destination disconnected
  'supply_exhausted',             // REQ-043 c3 — PROVEN ONLY
  'unattributed',                 // the fallback — renders ReachKit's stopped-work line
]
```

1. **`supply_exhausted` is returned only when `supplyDepth().unused === 0` is read
   and is zero.** It is never reached by elimination. If the depth cannot be read,
   the arm does not fire.
2. **`unattributed` is the last arm and it renders BP-019's `stoppedWorkLine`** —
   the same line `reachkit_stopped` renders. This is defensible on the merits,
   not merely as a safe default: REQ-092 criterion 1 already covers a day on
   which the product "had a step fail", and a date the product cannot explain is
   a date on which something of the product's failed. It carries a resumption
   date or an explicit no-time-promised (REQ-092 criterion 4) and names no
   internal cause (criterion 8).
3. **`instruction` outranks everything**, including `reachkit_stopped`. REQ-043
   criterion 5 is explicit: while an instruction stands against a date, that
   instruction is the date's one account and the date is never presented as there
   having been nothing worth publishing.
4. **`reachkit_stopped` outranks the customer's own causes**, because REQ-092
   criterion 7 requires ReachKit's stop to be the reason given whenever it holds,
   "whether or not that other reason is also true of the account at that moment".
   This mirrors BP-037's `NO_PUBLISH_PRECEDENCE`, and the two orders agree by
   construction.
5. **`unattributed` is instrumented as a defect signal**, not as a normal state:
   BP-039 emits a counter every time the resolver reaches it. A non-zero count
   means a cause exists that nobody has enumerated, and the fix is a new arm, not
   a widened one.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Make `supply_exhausted` the `else`** — rejected. It is the shape the code
   wants to take and it is the exact statement REQ-043 criterion 3's second
   sentence forbids. It converts every unenumerated failure into a false claim
   about the customer's market, silently, on the surface the customer trusts most.
2. **Make the fallback a generic "no page today" line with no cause** — rejected:
   criterion 4 requires the one line to say *which* cause emptied the date, and a
   line that names none satisfies neither criterion 3 nor criterion 4. It also
   hides the defect signal that arm exists to raise.
3. **Throw on an unresolved date and render nothing** — rejected: REQ-043
   criterion 5 requires every empty date to carry an account, and `BUILD.md` §2.5
   forbids a blank ("Empty/degraded states are designed, never blank"). A thrown
   error becomes a blank cell or a broken month.
4. **Enumerate every cause exhaustively and assert the resolver is total at
   compile time** — attempted and rejected as sufficient on its own: the cause
   set is open by construction ("any cause whatever"), because it includes
   failures of parts of the system that do not yet exist. Exhaustiveness over the
   *known* causes is still enforced; `unattributed` is what covers the rest.
5. **Let Overview derive its own account** — rejected: two derivations of "why is
   this day empty" are two chances to disagree on the one screen a customer
   compares them on, and rule 7.1 puts one capability in one node. Overview reads
   BP-039's resolver.
6. **Record this inside BP-039's `## Decisions`** — rejected under rule 2.2's file
   test and the landmine rule: it binds BP-038 as well as BP-039, and it is
   precisely a decision whose reversal looks like a cleanup.

## Consequences
- The resolver has six arms where five would compile, and one of them is expected
  never to fire in production. That redundancy is the decision.
- `supplyDepth()` becomes a required read on every month render for a site with
  any empty future date. BP-013 already makes it an indexed count, not a counter.
- A test asserts, discriminatingly, that with supply depth unreadable and no other
  cause matching, the resolver returns `unattributed` and **not**
  `supply_exhausted` — a test that survives deletion of the feature only if the
  fallback is wrong, which is the point.
- **Cost of reversing:** two lines — delete the `unattributed` arm, move
  `supply_exhausted` to the end. Nothing fails, no test that only checks totality
  goes red, and the product begins telling an unknown number of customers that
  their market has nothing in it on days it merely broke. Anyone who finds the
  sixth arm and thinks "this is unreachable, delete it" should read this file
  first, which is the entire reason it is a file.
