# ADR-042 — LANDMINE — address-wide suppression and the per-kind toggles are two mechanisms and must never be merged

- Date: 2026-08-31

> **Read this before unifying "unsubscribe".** There are two stores of what a
> person does not want mailed, they overlap in no row, and merging them — the
> obvious cleanup, one concept in one place — takes a paying customer's sign-in
> link away.

## Context
Two requirements each give a person a way to stop mail, and they look like the
same feature.

REQ-010 criterion 11 gives a **lead** an opt-out that is address-wide and
cross-domain: it stops the running sequence, every other domain's sequence, and
any sequence a future delivery would start. It is keyed by email address, because
a lead has no account — there is nothing else to key it by — and it must bind
addresses that have not been captured yet. It lives in `email_suppressions`,
which BP-002 justified as a table rather than a column for exactly that reason.

REQ-075 gives a **paying customer** three independent switches over the three
recurring mails, keyed by user, stored in `users.notify`, and deliberately
reaching nothing else: criterion 3 says that with every notification off, the
sign-in link, the account and subscription mail, and the unsuppressible
autopilot announcement all still arrive.

Seen from the code they are one concept: "does this person want this mail". Seen
from a schema review they are a normalisation defect: two stores, one question,
and one of them keyed by a string. The unification is obvious, cheap, and reads
like an improvement in every direction — one `suppressions` table keyed by
address with an optional kind column, one `isSuppressed()` helper, one
unsubscribe link.

It cannot be built. ReachKit authenticates by magic link (`BUILD.md` §13, the
webhook "send magic link → `/setup`"), so the sign-in mail *is* the credential.
REQ-075's rationale states the harm in one sentence: "on magic-link auth,
unsubscribing from everything costs them the sign-in link itself". A merged
mechanism has one obvious default — an address-keyed suppression suppresses that
address — and the first customer who clicks unsubscribe on a Monday movement mail
loses the ability to sign in to the product they are paying for. The failure is
silent from the product's side: the mail is suppressed exactly as recorded, no
error is raised, and the customer's account looks healthy right up until they
try to get back in and cannot, with the only recovery channel switched off.

The reverse merge is no better: making the lead opt-out per-kind and per-user
gives it nothing to key on for a lead who has no user row, and REQ-010 criterion
11's whole point is binding sequences that do not exist yet.

## Decision
**Two mechanisms, two stores, two consult points, and no code path from one to
the other.**

| | Address-wide suppression | Notification toggles |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement | REQ-010 criterion 11 | REQ-075 |
| Subject | an email address | a user |
| Store | `email_suppressions` (BP-029) | `users.notify` (BP-059) |
| Reaches | `stoppable: 'opt-out'` kinds — the nurture sequence | `stoppable: 'toggle'` kinds — `draft-ready`, `published`, `weekly` |
| Never reaches | the page a founder asked for, or any customer mail | sign-in, account, lifecycle, setup-reminder, or REQ-057 criterion 7's send |
| Entry point | `suppressAddress()` / `applyOptOutToken()` | `setNotifyPref()` / `applyUnsubscribeToken()` |
| Link in the mail | `/opt-out/{token}`, signed on the address | `/unsubscribe/{token}`, signed on `(user, kind)` |

The register is what keeps them apart: `MAIL_KINDS[kind].stoppable` is
`false | 'toggle' | 'opt-out'`, and BP-053's send seam consults **exactly one**
store per send, chosen by that field. A kind marked `false` consults neither.
There is no call that asks both, and `applyUnsubscribeToken()` never writes
`email_suppressions` while `suppressAddress()` never writes `users.notify`.

## Alternatives considered
1. **One suppressions table with a nullable `kind` column** — rejected: it has a
   natural, wrong default (null kind = all kinds), and that default is what
   removes the magic-link mail. A constraint forbidding a null kind for a user's
   row would encode this ADR as a check nobody can read.
2. **One table plus a hard-coded list of kinds suppression may never reach** —
   rejected: the exception list is the register, and the register already exists
   with `stoppable` as its field. A second list of exempt kinds is rule 2.4's
   second copy, and the copy that goes stale is the one that lets a mail through
   or holds one back.
3. **Suppress at the account level and re-send the magic link out-of-band on
   request** — rejected: the out-of-band channel is the same inbox.
4. **Merge later, once auth is not magic-link** — rejected as a plan rather than
   a decision. It is recorded here so that the day auth changes, this file is
   what has to be superseded and REQ-075's rationale is what has to be re-read.
5. **An inline decision in BP-059** — rejected. The two mechanisms sit in three
   nodes (BP-029, BP-053, BP-059) plus the node that stamps a subscribe
   (BP-017), and the merge would be proposed by whoever is holding the schema,
   who is reading none of them.

## Consequences
- Two stores, two token formats and two unsubscribe routes, permanently. This is
  the intended cost.
- The register's `stoppable` field is load-bearing: it is the dispatch, not
  documentation. Changing a kind's stoppability changes which store binds it.
- A customer who is also a lead can hold both states at once — suppressed as an
  address, all three toggles on as a user — and that is coherent: they want no
  follow-up about a domain they scanned and every mail about the product they
  bought.
- Cost of reversing: a schema merge is a day's work and is why it will be
  proposed. The failure it causes is a locked-out paying customer with no
  recovery channel, discovered only when they complain, and it is not visible in
  any test written from the merged schema. Any change here supersedes this ADR
  and must cite REQ-075 criterion 3.
