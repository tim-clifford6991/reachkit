# ADR-083 — LANDMINE — a ReachKit post in a customer's WordPress carries two marks, not one: the invisible idempotency marker and a visible findability stamp, and neither may be made to do the other's job

- Date: 2026-09-01

> **Read this before merging the two things BP-048 writes onto a post it
> creates.** A post ReachKit creates in a customer's WordPress carries (1)
> ADR-080's marker, which is post meta naming the draft id, invisible in
> wp-admin, and (2) this file's stamp, which is a term the customer can list by
> and is visible to them. To a reader they are one idea written twice, and the
> obvious cleanup is to use one for both — read the stamp as the idempotency
> guard, or reshape the marker until it is visible. **Both directions break
> something and neither fails a test.** Reshaping the marker is what ADR-080
> decision 5 forbids without a fallback that finds the old shape first: a post
> created under the old shape becomes invisible to the search that prevents a
> duplicate, and the failure appears as a second article on a paying customer's
> blog. Reading the stamp as the guard is worse: a customer may rename, merge or
> delete a term in their own site at any time — it is *theirs* — and the moment
> they do, the at-most-once guarantee is gone with no error anywhere. The
> marker is ours and unreadable to them by design; the stamp is theirs to see
> and therefore theirs to break. Those are different jobs. See Decision 3.

## Context
REQ-060 gained criterion 6 in the round-two review fold of 2026-09-01
(`requirements/history/REQ-056-057-060-079-2026-09-01-review-round-two-folded.md`):

> Given pages ReachKit has delivered to the customer's WordPress, when the
> customer looks for them in their own WordPress, following the one place
> ReachKit is able to tell them they are (REQ-079 criterion 6), then every page
> ReachKit created in that site comes up together in one list there, without
> their having to open posts one at a time or already know a page's title, and
> posts they wrote themselves are not in that list.
> Whatever makes this true is put on a post only by ReachKit creating it, and
> never by a later write into their site.

It exists because REQ-079 criterion 6 rests on it: the account-deletion mail is
now "a count and a place, not a list" — it says how many posts ReachKit created,
that they stand there as drafts, and "the one place in their own WordPress that
brings all of them up together (REQ-060 criterion 6) — never a list of them,
whatever their number". Without a place, that mail has nothing to name.

The corpus already writes one thing onto a post ReachKit creates: ADR-080
decision 5's marker, "a marker naming the draft on every post it creates", which
BP-048 searches for before creating one. The marker is **post meta**. Post meta
is not shown in the wp-admin posts list, is not a filter there, and is not
something a customer sees at all without a plugin or the REST API. It answers
"is this the post for draft X?" for *us*, one post at a time. It does not answer
"show me every post ReachKit created" for *them*.

The requirements-analyst was explicit that criterion 6 cannot be folded into the
marker, and recorded an assumption it could not discharge: that every connectable
install permits such an attribute under the application password the customer
gave. **That assumption is carried forward here `open` and is not discharged.**
Nothing in this repository has spoken to a WordPress install; this corpus carries
no `src/` (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No source code on disk yet"), so there
is no evidence to discharge it with, and inventing one would be rule 1.2's
fabricated measurement.

## Decision

**Decision 1 — two marks, and the stamp is a term in the `post_tag` taxonomy.**
Every post BP-048 creates carries the ADR-080 marker *and* one term, assigned in
the same create call. The term is the "one place" REQ-060 criterion 6 promises
and REQ-079 criterion 6's mail names: the customer opens their own Posts list
filtered to that term and every post ReachKit created comes up together, without
opening posts one at a time and without knowing a title.

The choice of `post_tag` over the two other means REQ-060 leaves to the
architect (rule 1.1 — chosen here, with the derivation and the reversal cost,
not escalated):

- **Author** — rejected. Posts created through an application password are
  authored by the user that password belongs to, and wp-admin already filters by
  author, so it would cost nothing to write. It fails criterion 6's second half —
  "posts they wrote themselves are not in that list" — in the ordinary case
  where the customer gave the application password of *their own* account rather
  than creating a dedicated user for ReachKit. Then ReachKit's posts and theirs
  share an author and the list is not separable. Making it work would require
  ReachKit to create a WordPress user, which needs `create_users` — far beyond
  the `edit_posts` BP-048 assumes — and would be ReachKit administering a site it
  does not own.
- **Category** — rejected. A category answers the question as well as a tag
  does, and wp-admin's posts list even filters by category out of the box. But
  creating a category term is `manage_categories` in WordPress's default role
  map, which an author-level credential does not hold, and assigning one that
  does not exist yet is therefore not available at the capability BP-048 assumes.
  A tag is the taxonomy an author-level credential can both assign and create.
  It is also the less intrusive of the two: a category is structural in most
  themes and appears in navigation, a tag is not.
- **Tag** — taken, on the capability argument above and on one more: a tag is the
  cheapest thing for a customer to remove if they dislike it, and it is their
  site. Criterion 6 asks that a place exist while ReachKit is telling them about
  it; it does not ask that ReachKit hold a customer's own site to a shape.

**Decision 2 — the stamp is written at creation and never afterwards.** Criterion
6 says so in terms ("put on a post only by ReachKit creating it, and never by a
later write into their site"), and it is what keeps ADR-082 Decision 4 — the
never-live unpublish path writes nothing at all — true without an exception. In
particular: **no backfill.** A post created before the stamp existed never
receives one; adding it later is a later write, which criterion 6 forbids.

That is affordable only because of when this lands. This corpus is greenfield —
no WordPress post has ever been created by this product — so the population of
un-stamped posts is empty by construction, and the stamp ships in the same work
order as the first WordPress delivery or in one that precedes it going live. If
it ever ships *after* WordPress delivery is live, criterion 6 cannot be met for
the posts already there and that is a question for the requirement, not a licence
to backfill. **The work-order sequence is therefore load-bearing**, which is not
a sentence a blueprint usually gets to write and is the reason it is written
here.

**Decision 3 — the marker and the stamp are separate, permanently, and neither
substitutes for the other.** This is the landmine and the reason this is a file.

| | ADR-080's marker | This file's stamp |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | post meta naming the draft id | one `post_tag` term |
| Who reads it | ReachKit, before creating a post | the customer, in their own wp-admin |
| Question it answers | "is this the post for draft X?" | "show me every post ReachKit created" |
| Grain | one post | all of them |
| Whose it is | ours; the customer cannot see or edit it in wp-admin | theirs; they may rename, merge or delete the term at will |
| What breaks if it is lost | REQ-056 c5 — a duplicate article on a paying customer's blog | REQ-060 c6 and REQ-079 c6 — the mail names a place that is not there |

The asymmetry in the last two rows is the whole argument. The marker is
load-bearing precisely because it is *not* the customer's to touch; the stamp is
useful precisely because it *is*. A guard the customer can delete from their own
admin screen is not a guard. So: BP-048 searches for the marker and never for the
stamp when deciding whether a post already exists, and a test asserts that a post
whose stamp has been removed is still found by the idempotency search.

**Decision 4 — a stamp that cannot be applied is not a failed delivery.** Where
the credential or the install will not permit the term, the page is still
delivered — it is in their site, and saying otherwise would be false — and the
delivery result records `stampApplied: false`. This is the same shape BP-048
decision 2 already takes for a missing SEO plugin, and for the same reason.

What follows from it is stated rather than hidden: for those posts REQ-060
criterion 6 is not met and REQ-079 criterion 6's mail has no place to name.
REQ-060's own `rests-on` row anticipates exactly this ("where a connected site
permits none, criterion 6 cannot be met there and REQ-079 criterion 6's mail has
no place to name"), so this is the requirement's stated consequence and not a
gap invented here. BP-058 probes for the capability when the destination is
connected and at each health check, so the state is known before a customer is
told about a place, rather than discovered by a customer following a mail to a
filter that returns nothing.

**Decision 5 — the term's name is the owner's, not this file's.** The slug is a
pin (`WORDPRESS_STAMP_SLUG`, BP-005) so it lives in one place and reversal is one
constant; its **value** and the term's display name are customer-visible strings
and are owner-owed (§1's rights table). This file mints neither.

They are owner-owed for a stronger reason than the general rule: the term is
visible on the customer's own site to anyone, once the customer publishes the
draft themselves. ReachKit never makes a WordPress post live (REQ-060 criterion
1), so the term is private while the post is a draft — but the whole point of the
delivery is that the customer publishes it, and from that moment the term ReachKit
chose appears on their public site and in its tag archive. **That is a
customer-visible consequence and is escalated rather than decided** (§1, rule
1.1's limit).

## Alternatives considered
1. **Reshape ADR-080's marker so it is visible and do the job with one mark.**
   Rejected, and this is the rejection the warning block exists for. ADR-080
   decision 5 permits a change to the marker's shape only with "a fallback that
   finds the old shape first", because the marker is the at-most-once guarantee
   at a destination whose database we cannot index. A visible marker is also a
   marker the customer can delete, which converts an invariant into a preference.
2. **Read the stamp as the idempotency guard and drop the marker.** Rejected on
   the same asymmetry: the term is the customer's to rename, merge or delete, and
   the failure mode is silent — a retry whose outcome we never recorded then
   creates a second post, which is exactly REQ-056 criterion 5's promise and
   ADR-080's whole subject.
3. **Give the customer a link into their wp-admin filtered to our posts, and
   store nothing on the post.** Rejected: a filter needs something on the post to
   filter *by*. Without an attribute the link is a search by title, which
   criterion 6 rules out in terms ("without their having to … already know a
   page's title").
4. **List the posts on a ReachKit surface instead.** Rejected: it answers a
   different requirement. REQ-060 criterion 6 asks for the list "in their own
   WordPress", and REQ-079 criterion 6's mail exists precisely because after
   deletion there is no ReachKit surface left to read.
5. **A dedicated WordPress user for ReachKit.** Rejected under Decision 1:
   `create_users` is far beyond the capability BP-048 assumes, and it makes the
   product administer a site it does not own.
6. **Record this as a `## Decisions` entry inside BP-048.** Rejected under rule
   2.2 twice over. It spans BP-048's create path, BP-058's connect and health
   probe, BP-005's pin and BP-063's deletion mail — four nodes, none of which
   owns the others. And it is a landmine under the blueprint-writing skill's own
   test: the counterintuitive thing (two marks on one post, one of them
   deliberately invisible) reads as duplication, the merge reads as a cleanup,
   and neither merge direction fails a test.

## Consequences
- **BP-048** writes one term alongside the marker at creation, records
  `stampApplied` on the delivery result, and searches only for the marker when
  deciding whether a post exists. One more field on `WordPressDelivery`.
- **BP-058** gains a capability probe at connect and at health check, so a site
  that will not take the stamp is a known destination state rather than a
  surprise in a mail. It mints no sentence.
- **BP-005** gains `WORDPRESS_STAMP_SLUG`. Its value is owner-owed.
- **BP-063**'s deletion mail names the place. It counts the posts left in the
  customer's WordPress — ADR-082's `already_gone` posts are not among them — and
  where the stamp was never applied there is no place to name and the mail cannot
  make one up. What that mail says in that case is a sentence, and sentences are
  the owner's.
- **The work-order sequence is a constraint, not a preference** (Decision 2): the
  stamp cannot land after WordPress delivery is live without leaving posts
  criterion 6 cannot reach and no permitted way to reach them.
- **Cost of reversing:** changing the mechanism (tag → category, or → author) is
  one pin and one call while the population is empty, and becomes a migration of
  somebody else's site the moment it is not — the same trap ADR-080 decision 5
  names for the marker, arriving by a different road. Removing the stamp
  altogether is one deleted call, and it silently withdraws the place REQ-079
  criterion 6's mail names, on a surface no ReachKit test can see.

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-09-01, minted at `accepted`. Rule 3.2 makes
the status this agent's assertion, audited by the librarian afterwards; rule
2.2b's ADR grammar is `proposed → accepted → superseded`.

**Grounds.** The behaviour is REQ-060 criterion 6, which the requirements-analyst
folded and which REQ-079 criterion 6 already depends on; the mechanism is a
parameter, which rule 1.1 places with this agent; the one part that is not a
parameter — the term's visible name — is escalated in Decision 5 rather than
chosen. The blueprint that carries the behaviour, BP-048, already `satisfies`
REQ-060 and needs no new node (rule 7.1: one capability, one BP node, one
module — the stamp is written by the same `deliver` call in the same module).

**What is not a ground.** REQ-060 is `status: in-review` pending the owner's
signature on the pass that added criterion 6. §3 makes decisions derived and
self-certified, and rule 3.4 exempts `/decide` from the second round; the gate
that binds a *blueprint* to an approved requirement is a separate question and
is answered in BP-048's own status grounds. Three `rests-on` rows above are
`open` and are not discharged here: this repository has never spoken to a
WordPress install, and there is nothing in it to discharge them with.
