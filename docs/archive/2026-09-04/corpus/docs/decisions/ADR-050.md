# ADR-050 — LANDMINE — active access is the paid-through date alone; `plan_status` is recorded and never read by the gate

- Date: 2026-08-31

> **Read this before adding a term to `hasActiveAccess`.** The obvious
> improvement — "and the subscription is actually active" — is the bug.

## Context
`BUILD.md` §10 gives `users` a column `plan_status(active/past_due/canceled)`. A
column with those three values, sitting next to an access check, reads as the
access check's input. It is not.

REQ-076 criterion 8 states the gate verbatim:

> Given any site, when the weekly re-measurement and the daily generation and
> publishing loops ask whether it has active access (REQ-065 criterion 1), then it
> has active access while its paid-through date has not passed and none once that
> date has passed, **whether or not the subscription was cancelled and whether or
> not its latest renewal was paid**.

So a customer whose `plan_status` is `canceled` **has access**, until the date. A
customer whose `plan_status` is `past_due` **has access**, until the date. Only the
date decides.

Three separate subsystems ask the question — BP-003's schedulers, BP-012's scan
pipeline and BP-015's publishing — and BP-060 owns the answer. No one of those four
blueprints owns the invariant that binds the other three, which is what puts this
in a file rather than in a `## Decisions` section (rule 2.2).

**Why it looks wrong.** Every instinct says a cancelled subscription should stop
service, and a `past_due` one certainly should. An engineer reading

```ts
return user.paid_through > new Date()
```

next to a `plan_status` column will read it as a check somebody forgot to finish,
and the fix is one clause long.

**Why it is load-bearing.** That clause breaks a public promise on the pricing card.
`BUILD.md` §4.1 sells the product with "Cancel in one click", and §4.7's billing card
says "cancelling keeps everything running until {date}". REQ-076's rationale is
explicit that "a cancellation that … cuts service off mid-period is a broken public
promise rather than a billing detail". ANDing `plan_status = 'active'` into the gate
would stop measurement, generation and publishing the instant a customer clicks
cancel — for a period they have already paid for — and the customer would never
report it as a bug, because they cancelled.

The `past_due` half is worse in a quieter way. Refunds, chargebacks and failed
renewals are deferred post-launch by owner ruling (REQ-076 and REQ-024 non-goals),
so **no requirement in this corpus states what a `past_due` customer experiences**.
An engineer who adds `past_due` to the gate has not implemented a deferred
requirement; they have invented one, and the first person to find out is a paying
customer whose card was replaced and whose site went quiet.

## Decision
**`hasActiveAccess(siteId)` is `users.paid_through > now()`, and nothing else.**

1. `plan_status` is kept as the record of what Stripe last said. It is written by
   BP-060's `onSubscriptionEvent` and read by `billingSummary` for display. It is
   read by **no** gate, guard, job filter or query that decides whether work runs.
2. `cancelled_at` is likewise a record, read only to render REQ-076 criterion 3's
   date. It gates nothing.
3. There is exactly one implementation of the gate, in `src/lib/account/billing/`.
   BP-003, BP-012, BP-015 and BP-063 call it and compute no access predicate of
   their own — a second predicate is the rule 7.1 violation, and this is the
   capability most likely to grow one.
4. `tests/account/billing/` asserts, as three separate cases that each fail if the
   clause is added: a cancelled subscription with a future `paid_through` has
   access; a `past_due` subscription with a future `paid_through` has access; a
   subscription Stripe still calls `active` with a past `paid_through` does not.
   Deleting the gate must fail all three (mutation discipline, constitution §8).
5. Ending access early is done by moving the date, never by a status. Deletion
   (REQ-079 criterion 6) sets `paid_through = now()`; that is the whole mechanism.

## Alternatives considered
1. **`paid_through > now() AND plan_status = 'active'`** — rejected: it cuts service
   off at the moment of cancellation, breaking the "cancel keeps everything running
   until {date}" promise the product is sold on.
2. **`paid_through > now() AND plan_status <> 'past_due'`** — rejected: no requirement
   states what a `past_due` customer experiences, because the case is deferred by
   owner ruling. Building a behaviour for it is inventing a requirement.
3. **Derive access from Stripe at read time** — rejected in BP-060 decision 2 on
   latency and availability grounds; it also makes a vendor outage indistinguishable
   from a cancellation, which is this same failure with a different trigger.
4. **Drop `plan_status` entirely, so it cannot be misread** — rejected: `BUILD.md`
   §10 defines it and REQ-076 criterion 1's billing card needs something to show. The
   column stays; this file is why it is inert.

## Consequences
- A customer who cancels on day 2 of a paid month keeps measurement, generation and
  publishing for the rest of it. Intended, and sold.
- A customer whose renewal fails keeps working service until the paid-through date
  passes, at which point access ends like any other lapse. There is no dunning
  behaviour, by owner ruling, and none may be added without a requirement.
- Reversing this is not a refactor: the clause is one line to add and its effect is
  invisible in every test that uses an active subscription. That is exactly why it is
  written here and asserted in three discriminating tests rather than left as a
  comment.
- Revisit trigger: the same one the deferred refund and tax debts carry — before
  meaningful EU B2C volume. A requirement stating what a `past_due` customer
  experiences amends this decision and supersedes this file; it does not edit it.
