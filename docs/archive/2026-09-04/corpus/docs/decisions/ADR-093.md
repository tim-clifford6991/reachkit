# ADR-093 — LANDMINE — content fits its box or the box changes: one layout law over three named bands, text is never shrunk to fit, and no type step below the floor may be minted

- Date: 2026-09-02
- **Amended 2026-09-03, in-body, while `proposed`:** Decision 6's viewport
  gains a height. Now `accepted`, any further change is a superseding or
  amending ADR, never an edit here (rule 2.2b; the instrument ADR-082 and
  ADR-086 record).

## Context

The owner ruled twice in one statement on 2026-09-02: that no container or
component may cut off its own content, citing `RivalSparkline`'s `was 276×`
label; and that the whole system must work at every screen size, including
mobile, "throughout the app".

**This is one decision, not two, and the ruling's own example is why.** The
`was 276×` badge is simultaneously the instance of both comments: it is a box
sized for one width, read at another. Neither half states itself without the
other. "Sized correctly with appropriate buffer" has no truth value until the
widths it must hold at are named — correct at 1440 and clipped at 375 is the
defect being reported, not a pass. And "works at every screen size" has no
truth value until *works* is given a failure condition — which is exactly
"nothing is cut off". Each half is the other's acceptance criterion. Split
across two files, neither could be read alone (rule 2.1's seam cost), and the
width set would have two homes (rule 2.4). One ruling, one ADR, per
`decide.md`.

Four facts about this corpus, all verified rather than assumed, shape what can
be decided here:

1. **There is no breakpoint anywhere in the design system.** `BUILD.md` names
   no width. `tokens.md` §7 records the absence as a gap
   (*"`previews/app/` uses Tailwind's own named breakpoints for structure only
   — a borrowed vocabulary, not a ruled one"*). The running preview app spends
   Tailwind's `lg:` prefix eleven times and uses no other breakpoint prefix and
   no `@media (min-width:)` rule at all — its only `@media` blocks are
   `prefers-color-scheme`. So the corpus has **one borrowed boundary and zero
   owned ones**, and a ruling that the system works at every screen size is,
   as stated, unbuildable.

2. **Only one node in sixty-three has a narrow-viewport arm.** BP-037 carries
   one (*"Narrow viewport: the sidebar is replaced by top tabs …"*). Grepped
   across every blueprint on 2026-09-02, no other node mentions a viewport, a
   breakpoint, mobile or a column count. Twelve surfaces are specified at one
   width.

3. **The two places `BUILD.md` does speak are a specification, and they are
   incomplete in the same spot.** §4.4: *"Mobile: sidebar hidden, top tabs."*
   §4.6: *"Day panel (290px, sticky, beside the grid — not a drawer)."* §4.6
   also fixes the grid as `repeat(7,minmax(0,1fr))` and says *"the minmax is
   load-bearing"* — which is this ruling's own rule, already written down once,
   for one grid: `minmax(0,…)` exists so a long title cannot blow its column
   out. Nothing states at what width "mobile" begins, and nothing states what
   the day panel does when there is no *beside* left.

4. **The type scale has a hole exactly where the clipping is.** `--t-sm` 13px
   and `--t-xs` 12px are proposed and unregistered, and both surface sheets and
   the preview app reached for untokened 12/13 "to draw at all". Below that,
   `previews/WO-033.html` and `previews/WO-035.html` reach `font-size:10px` and
   `font-size:9.5px` in the calendar day cell and the week strip — below
   `--t-eyebrow-size`, where the scale proposes nothing. `tokens.md` §7 hands
   the question here in terms: *"Either the cell gets more room or the scale
   gets a floor — a layout question for the surface blueprint."*

And the mechanism of the cited defect, read off the running app: `RivalSparkline`
renders `<svg width={120} …>` inside a `.between` flex row alongside the name,
the endpoint value and the delta badge, and `globals.css` sets
`.rk-chart { width: 100% }`. The chart's flex base therefore resolves to the
full row and the text siblings absorb the shortfall. **The customer's number is
compressed so that a decoration can keep its width.** That inversion is what
Decision 4 forbids in general.

## Decision

### 1. Three bands, two boundaries, and the set is closed

The product has exactly **three layout bands** — `compact`, `medium`, `wide` —
and every surface has an arm in each. A fourth band is an ADR, not a media
query.

Three is derived, not preferred. `BUILD.md` implies exactly one boundary — the
one §4.4 calls "Mobile" and §4.6 calls "on mobile" — and the 290px day panel
forces a second, because sidebar (222) + a seven-column grid + panel (290)
cannot coexist at the width where two-column cards first fit. Two boundaries
give three bands. Nothing in `BUILD.md` §4 asks for a fourth.

Each band is defined by a **structural fact**, not by a device:

| Band | Sidebar | Calendar | Day panel | Card grids |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `compact` | hidden; top tabs (§4.4) | dated list, one row per date | below, in flow | one column |
| `medium` | shown | seven-column grid | below, in flow | two columns |
| `wide` | shown | seven-column grid | **beside** the grid (§4.6) | two, or three where §4 names three |

**The token names and the two pixel values are `design/`'s to mint, not this
ADR's** (the design-guardian owns tokens). This ADR fixes the set, its
cardinality, what each band means structurally, and three constraints the
values must satisfy:

- `wide`'s lower bound is the width at which `--w-sidebar` (222px) +
  `--w-day-panel` (290px) + the seven-column grid all fit with every day cell
  rendering its stage word at or above the type floor of Decision 3, at named
  spacing steps. That width is a **measurement taken from a drawn sheet**, and
  taking it belongs to whoever draws it (rule 1.2 — this ADR does not invent
  it).
- `medium`'s lower bound is the width at which a two-column card grid fits with
  each column at or above its content minimum.
- Below `medium` is `compact`, whose floor is fixed by Decision 2.

A surface may not name a width that is not one of the two boundaries. A
`max-width`, a `minmax()` floor or a `min-width` on a flex child is a *content*
dimension, not a breakpoint, and is a named token like any other length.

### 2. The supported range is 320 CSS px and up, and that number is chosen

"Every screen size" is unbuildable read literally — there is always a narrower
one, and a design with a 290px panel and a 222px sidebar has a width below
which no arrangement of it is legible. The product therefore commits to
**320 CSS px and above**, with no upper bound (above `wide`, content is centred
within a named maximum, which `design/` owes).

Rule 1.1 derivation and reversal cost, recorded so this is never read as a
measured device statistic: 320 is the smallest round width at which the
`compact` arm of every band-1 surface in the table above still fits its content
at the type floor, and it is the conventional narrowest CSS viewport. **It is a
chosen parameter, not a device census** — nothing in this corpus measured
anything about customers' phones and this ADR does not pretend otherwise.
Reversing it costs one token value and one parameter in the suite of Decision 6;
lowering it costs a redesign of the `compact` arms, which is why it is stated
before they are drawn rather than discovered by one of them.

### 3. LANDMINE — text is never shrunk to fit, and no step below the floor may be minted

**The floor for rendered text is the bottom of `--t-eyebrow-size`'s range
(§2.3, `10.5–11px`). No type step below it is minted, now or later.**

The `10px` and `9.5px` marks in `previews/WO-033.html` and
`previews/WO-035.html` do not ship. They are the symptom of a cell that is too
narrow, and the fix is the cell, never the type.

This is the landmine. The one-line diff that makes every failing cell pass is
`--t-2xs: 10px`, and it will read as completing a scale that obviously has a
hole in it. It does not complete a scale; it deletes the only thing standing
between a 41px calendar cell and an illegible one, and it does it in a commit
that looks like tidying. `tokens.md` §7 already refused to guess it and handed
the question here. **The answer is that the question was a layout question all
along: a cell that cannot carry its label at the floor renders a different
registered state — fewer elements, or a narrower registered variant — never the
same elements smaller.**

Two exemptions, both already stated elsewhere and not widened here:

- Text inside an SVG `viewBox` is coordinate space, not a design value
  (`tokens.md` §7, verbatim). The floor does not apply to it; the *rendered*
  size after the viewBox scales does.
- `--t-sm` (13px) and `--t-xs` (12px) stand as proposed and are what the app
  shell's ordinary text is set at. They are `design/`'s to register; this ADR
  depends on them existing and mints neither.

### 4. LANDMINE — the value is rigid and the decoration is flexible, never the reverse

In any row that pairs a chart, an icon or a rule with a number the customer
reads, **the text is `flex: 0 0 auto` and the graphic is the flexible element,
with its own named minimum.** When the graphic's minimum plus the text no
longer fits, the row wraps; the number is never compressed and the graphic is
never squeezed below its minimum.

`RivalSparkline` is the instance and the reason this reads as counterintuitive:
`.rk-chart { width: 100% }` is the natural way to make a chart responsive, and
applied to a flex child it makes the chart's base the whole row, so the
customer's `78×` and their `was 276×` pay for it. A chart that gets narrower is
still a chart; a number that gets narrower is a lie or an ellipsis. The rival
row therefore wraps to two lines at `compact` rather than compressing, and the
sparkline keeps a declared minimum width.

**No value is ever truncated, at any width.** The set is the one `BUILD.md`
§2.3 already routes through the mono numeral utility — numerals, dates, URLs,
search queries and code-like strings. A *name* may truncate where the truncation
is declared and registered (the corpus already has two: `.rk-day .t` and
`.rk-matrix-name`); a value may not, and there is no allow-list entry that can
grant it one.

**Buffer is a named step, and which step is not this ADR's to say.** The inset
between content and its box edge is always one of `--s-1`…`--s-7`, never a raw
value; that much is mechanically checkable. Whether `--s-3` or `--s-4` is the
right inset for a given card is a judgement, and it is held by rule 7.3's
preview gate, which is where an inconsistent rhythm is visible — the same
division `tokens.md` already draws for the spacing scale. **This ADR does not
invent a check that could rule on it.**

### 5. The calendar's `compact` arm is a dated list, and the day panel is never a drawer

`BUILD.md` §4.6 states the grid and the panel at one width and stops. Both arms
below `wide` are settled here because both are load-bearing for REQ-043 and
neither can be left to a work order.

**The seven-column grid does not survive `compact`, and the reason is a
criterion, not taste.** The arithmetic, stated so it reads as arithmetic and
not as a measurement: 320 less two `--s-4` insets is 288, less six `--s-1`
gaps is 264, over seven columns is **under 38px a cell**.
REQ-043 criterion 2 requires every date carrying a page to *state* its stage —
Live, Your review, Scheduled, Planned, Needs you — and criteria 3, 4 and 5
require every empty date to carry exactly one written line. Neither fits 41px
at the floor of Decision 3, and Decision 3 forbids the alternative. So at
`compact` the month renders as a **full-width dated list, one row per date, in
date order, weekends included** — every criterion of REQ-043 holds unchanged,
and the row has the width the written line needs. At `medium` and `wide` it is
`BUILD.md`'s grid. The list is an arm of `CalendarGrid`, not a sixth custom
surface — the same shape `RivalSparkline`'s `absolute`/`ratio`/`gapped` arms
already have.

**The day panel moves below the grid in normal flow at `compact` and `medium`,
full width, and is still not a drawer.** §4.6's "not a drawer" is a constraint
on *modality*, not on position: §4.6 also says *"today selected on open"*, so
the panel has content before the customer selects anything, and a drawer that
is open by default over the grid is a modal the customer must dismiss to see
the calendar at all. Below the grid it stays non-modal, keeps its default
selection, and leaves the grid visible above it. It is never hidden: REQ-043
criterion 12's two ways through to a delivered page, criterion 9's
stage-appropriate actions and REQ-056 criterion 7's unpublish all live in the
panel and nowhere else, so hiding it at `compact` removes the only route to
them on a phone.

**The draft view is two columns at `medium` and above, tabbed at `compact`** —
`BUILD.md` §4.6 already ruled this (*"two columns on desktop, tabbed on
mobile"*); it is recorded here only because it was in no blueprint.

**A card grid collapses one step per band down**: three-up → two-up → one. Stated
once here rather than in each of BP-022, BP-027, BP-033, BP-038 and BP-055
(rule 2.4).

### 6. The check is a conformance suite over the route tree, and it carries a canary

The enforcement idiom is **ADR-010's**, unchanged: a path-glob suite whose
enumerator is the route tree, so a surface added later is in scope by
construction. It lives at `tests/ui/layout/**` — inside BP-018's existing
`tests/ui/**` glob, so no new owner, no new top-level directory and no
`structure.md` rule change. BP-018 owns it because BP-018 owns the vocabulary
the law is stated in (bands, spacing steps, the type floor), the same reason
ADR-010 put the cold-start sweep in BP-021 rather than in the container whose
tree it sweeps. The *runner* — a real browser driver as a dev dependency and a
script — is BP-001's root-toolchain concern (BP-001 decision 2).

Each route is rendered at five widths: the floor (320), each of the three
bands, and **each boundary minus one pixel**, which is where the off-by-one
lives.

**Amended 2026-09-03 — the viewport carries a height.** Each of the five widths
is rendered at a viewport **480 CSS px tall**: 320 × 480 at the floor, and 480
tall at the other four. The promise this serves is REQ-099 criterion 1, which
fixes what a visitor meets on the landing's first screen *before they scroll*
at a floor viewport of 320 × 480 and at every wider or taller one; 480's
derivation is `registry/evidence/REQ-099.md`'s and is not restated here. As
written above, checks 1–4 read widths and an element's own box and never the
viewport's height, so a before-you-scroll promise had no viewport at which it
could fail. The height is a suite parameter here and not a limb of Decision 2:
Decision 2 commits a supported *width* range because layout reflows by width,
whereas a page scrolls by height, and a product-wide height floor would promise
that every surface fits 480 tall — a promise no requirement makes and this file
will not invent. The first-screen assertion the height exists for is per
surface — the landing's own node declares it against criterion 1 when REQ-099
is approved and expanded — never a sixth product-wide check. Cost of reversing:
one suite parameter in `tests/ui/layout/**`, and criterion 1 becomes unfailable
again.

1. **No horizontal document scroll.**
   `document.scrollingElement.scrollWidth <= innerWidth`.
2. **Containment.** Every element's border box lies inside its containing
   block's padding box, unless it is on the declared scroll-container
   allow-list — and a scroll container must render its affordance.
3. **No clipping and no truncation.** For every text-bearing element,
   `scrollWidth <= clientWidth` and `scrollHeight <= clientHeight`, unless it is
   on the declared truncation allow-list. **An allow-list entry does not save an
   element whose text is a value**: the suite reads the computed
   `font-family`, and the numeral utility is mechanical (`tokens.md` §4), so a
   truncated value is detectable without anyone maintaining a list of them.
4. **The floor.** Every text-bearing element's computed `font-size` ≥ the floor
   token, SVG-internal text exempted per Decision 3.
5. **The canary, and it is the point.** The suite ships one fixture route that
   overflows on purpose — a long value in a box too small for it — and asserts
   that checks 1–4 **fail** on it. This exists because of the specific way a
   layout assertion goes vacuous: run under a DOM with no layout engine, every
   metric is `0`, `0 <= 0` holds everywhere, and the suite reports a clean
   sweep over a corpus it never measured. §8 calls a test that survives deletion
   of its feature vacuous; this one survives deletion of the *entire layout
   engine* unless the canary is there to notice. **A green run in which the
   canary also passes is a failed run.**

**Declaration is type-level, so it cannot be skipped.** `Surface` is a layout
primitive in `src/ui/` — it renders no chrome and is not a widget, so
`BUILD.md` §2.2's closed set of five customs is untouched by it — and every
screen root is one. Its `arms` prop is required, has no default, and names what
the surface does in each of the three bands. A screen that declares nothing
fails to compile rather than failing a test. This crosses no boundary
`structure.md` rule 6 forbids: `src/app/` → `src/ui/` is the allowed direction,
which is precisely why ADR-010 point 3 could *not* use the type-level mechanism
and this can.

**What is not checkable is said plainly.** Checks 1–4 catch clipping,
truncation, overflow and illegible type — every failure mode the owner's
statement names, including both instances they cited. They cannot rule on
whether a chosen spacing step is the *right* one. That is the preview gate's,
and this ADR does not simulate a check for it.

### 7. The narrow-viewport nav is a composition, not a sixth custom component

`components.md` §4 gap 5 leaves `TabBar` open as "architect's boundary call".
Called: the tab bar is a **shell-local composition over registered `Tabs`**,
owned by BP-037's `src/app/(account)/app/_shell/**`, mapping the same
`DESTINATIONS` tuple BP-037 decision 1 already made structural. It is not a
sixth custom surface and needs no `components.md` row: what distinguishes it
from `Tabs` is sticky positioning and band visibility, which are layout under
Decision 1, not a widget. `design/` closes gap 5 by recording that resolution.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Two ADRs — a sizing discipline and a responsive contract** — rejected, and
   this was the closest call. The owner did state two comments. But the width
   set would then be declared in one and depended on by the other, giving one
   fact two homes (rule 2.4); the sizing file could not state a single testable
   assertion without naming widths from the other; and the owner's own example
   appears in both halves of their statement. `decide.md` forbids splitting a
   ruling across files, and here the ruling genuinely is one.
2. **Mint the breakpoint tokens and their values in this ADR** — rejected. The
   design-guardian owns `design/` and tokens are theirs (constitution §4), it
   is running concurrently on that directory, and `wide`'s lower bound is a
   measurement taken from a drawn sheet, which rule 1.2 forbids this file from
   inventing. The set, the cardinality and the three constraints are
   architecture and are fixed here; the numbers are named where they are
   measured.
3. **Adopt Tailwind's five breakpoints wholesale** — rejected. Five bands means
   five arms per surface across thirteen surfaces, and the corpus has one arm in
   total today. It also decides the boundaries by someone else's device census
   rather than by this product's two fixed dimensions (222 and 290), which are
   the only widths anything here actually depends on. The preview app's use of
   `lg:` stands as scaffolding and says so on screen; it is not adopted by
   reference.
4. **Add `--t-2xs: 10px` and let the small cells shrink** — rejected, and
   Decision 3 exists to keep it rejected. It is the cheapest possible fix, it
   turns every failing check green, and it makes the product's densest surfaces
   illegible on the devices this ruling is about. The `9.5px` already drawn in
   `previews/WO-035.html` is how far this goes when nothing stops it.
5. **Make the day panel a drawer at `compact`** — rejected. §4.6 forbids it in
   terms, and independently it breaks *"today selected on open"*: a
   default-open modal over the grid is something the customer must dismiss
   before they can see the month.
6. **Hide the day panel at `compact` and route to a separate day screen** —
   rejected. REQ-043 criterion 12 promises the delivered page's two ways
   through are reachable *"without opening any other screen"*, and a separate
   screen is the thing it names.
7. **Keep the seven-column grid at every width and let the cells be tiny** —
   rejected by REQ-043 criteria 2–5, not by preference: a 41px cell cannot
   state a stage word or a written line at the floor, and Decision 3 forbids
   going below the floor.
8. **A stylesheet lint on raw dimensional values as the primary check** —
   rejected as primary, kept as a companion. It catches the ~16 raw `1px`
   borders and the untokened `12`/`13` — real findings — but a fully tokened
   layout still clips. It cannot fire on the owner's actual complaint, so it
   cannot be the check that answers it.
9. **Declare the ruling met by eye at the preview gate** — rejected for
   checks 1–4, which mechanise cleanly, and accepted for buffer alone, which
   does not. Saying "the preview gate holds all of it" would have been the
   comfortable answer and would have left thirteen surfaces with no failure
   condition at all.
10. **Write the responsive arm into all thirteen bound nodes now** — rejected.
    Five have determinate answers and are edited; the other eight would receive
    a restatement of Decision 1's table, which is rule 2.2a restructuring and
    rule 2.4 duplication. Decision 6's required `arms` prop is what makes their
    silence fail, and it fails at compile time rather than in a review.

## Consequences

- Thirteen nodes are bound. Five are edited in this pass — BP-018 (vocabulary,
  the suite, `Surface`, the floor), BP-037 (the named band and the `TabBar`
  boundary), BP-038 (the rival row's rigid-value rule), BP-039 (the `compact`
  list arm and the panel's position) and BP-044 (the two-column/tabbed arms).
  The other eight — BP-001, BP-004, BP-022, BP-027, BP-033, BP-036, BP-047,
  BP-055 — take Decision 5's collapse rule and Decision 6's required
  declaration and are not edited, deliberately (alternative 10).
- **`design/` owes five things**, none of which this ADR writes: the two
  breakpoint tokens and their values against Decision 1's three constraints; a
  border-width token (the ~16 raw `1px` in `previews/app/src/app/globals.css`
  are the only raw values left in it); registration of `--t-sm` and `--t-xs`,
  which every band depends on; a maximum content width for above `wide`; and a
  `components.md` row for `Surface` plus the closure of §4 gap 5 per Decision 7.
- **What the analyst owes.** No approved requirement promises the product works
  at every screen size (`rests-on` row 3). REQ-040 criterion 5 is the only
  narrow-viewport clause in the corpus and it promises one thing about one
  surface — that the three destinations stay reachable. `BUILD.md` §4.4's
  "Mobile: sidebar hidden, top tabs" is a specification line, not a promise.
  This ADR therefore rests on an owner ruling with no requirement under it, and
  the gap is the analyst's to close with a criterion stating what the customer
  is promised at a small screen. **No requirement moved to `in-review`, because
  none of them changed**: this ruling adds a promise the corpus does not carry
  rather than altering one it does. When the criterion is written, whether
  REQ-040 criterion 5 becomes its second home (rule 2.4) is the analyst's call,
  not this file's.
- Every `ui: yes` work order is affected: each needs its `compact` arm in scope
  and each preview sign-off (rule 7.3) now covers three bands rather than one.
- **Cost of reversing.** Decision 1's cardinality: adding a fourth band costs
  one arm on every surface and is why it is an ADR. Decision 2's floor: one
  token and one suite parameter upward; a redesign downward. Decision 3: one
  CSS line to reverse and the product's densest surfaces to lose — the reason
  it carries the landmine marker. Decision 5's list arm: one component arm, but
  reversing it re-breaks REQ-043 criteria 2–5 at `compact` silently, because a
  41px cell renders *something*. Decision 6's canary: deleting it costs nothing
  visible and buys a suite that passes forever.

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-09-03 by the architect; minted `proposed`
on 2026-09-02 (commit `c040ce5`). Rule 3.2 makes the status this agent's
assertion, audited by the librarian afterwards; rule 3.4 states that a decision
takes no owner-approval round. Decision 6's amendment above landed in the same
pass, before the status moved, so it is in-body rather than a second file.

**Grounds.**
- **The ruling is the owner's.** Context records the owner's statement of
  2026-09-02 — no container cuts off its content; the system works at every
  screen size — and nothing decided here changes what it promises.
- **Architecturally significant, reversal cost stated.** It binds thirteen
  nodes no single blueprint owns (rule 2.2's file test) and Consequences states
  the cost of reversing each of Decisions 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6.
- **The five edited nodes carry the edits** — checked 2026-09-03 by symbol and
  quote, not line (rule 5.3): BP-018 `## Public interface` exports `Surface`
  with its required `arms` and `## NFR budget` carries the suite and *"the
  bottom of `--t-eyebrow-size`'s range"*; BP-037 *"`compact` (ADR-093
  decision 1): the sidebar is replaced by top tabs"* and the `TabBar` boundary
  (decision 7); BP-038 *"the row wraps to two lines rather than
  compressing"* (decision 4) and the floor at every band; BP-039 *"At
  `compact` the month is a full-width dated list"* and the panel *"beside the
  grid only at `wide`"* (decision 5); BP-044 *"two columns at `medium` and
  `wide`, and tabbed at `compact`"*.
- **The consequences are already bound.** WO-269 (`c044896`) is cut from
  Decision 6 and BP-018; REQ-099 criterion 1 (`3db58b6`) is written in this
  file's bands and floor; `design/tokens.md` §2b mints the breakpoint tokens
  against Decision 1's constraints.
- **`## Open questions` is `None`**, and was at minting.
