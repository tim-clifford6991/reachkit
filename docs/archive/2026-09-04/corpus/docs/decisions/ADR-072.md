# ADR-072 — LANDMINE — four ways a page has no ordinary verdict, none of which may be merged, and all four of which are now terminal: nothing restores a page to judgement

- Date: 2026-09-01
- **Supersedes ADR-071** (rule 2.2b: an `accepted` ADR is never edited in
  place). ADR-071's Decisions 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 are carried forward **unchanged
  and un-re-argued** (rule 2.4) and are restated below only as a list of what
  survives; its Decision 5 is replaced. *Instrument* states why this is a
  supersession and not an amendment.
- **Binds** REQ-063 criteria 1 and 6, which are `in-review`.

> **Read this before collapsing two verdict states that render alike, before
> giving `not_measured` a stored row, before making `not_judgeable` a single
> reversible flag — and now, before adding the code that judges a page again
> once its search comes back into the tracked set.** ADR-071's warning block
> named the first three; this file adds the fourth, and the fourth is the one
> that will read as a *bug report* rather than a tidy-up. Somebody will notice
> that a page went `not_judgeable` because its target search left the tracked
> set, that the search is back, that the page is still live, and that ReachKit
> is not judging it. That is not a defect. REQ-063 criterion 6 says in terms:
> *"A page marked no longer judgeable stays so, and nothing restores it: no
> verdict is produced for it in any later week, and no line ever says it is
> judged again."* The restore that looks like a fix reinstates a claim ReachKit
> cannot stand behind — see *Why terminality*, below.

## Context

ADR-071 put a page in one of four positions in any given week and forbade every
merge between them. That part is unchanged and is not re-argued here; read
ADR-071 for the merge argument, which stands verbatim.

What changed is the **"does it lift?" column**. ADR-071's Decision 5 read:

> "`domain_changed` is terminal; the other three are conditional. Once a
> `domain_changed` row exists for a page, every later week is `not_judgeable`
> for that page (REQ-071 c14). `search_untracked`, `unpublished` and
> `question_left_set` lift the moment their condition lifts, and the page is
> judged again from that week with no intervention."

Two rulings of 2026-09-01 make every clause after the first false.

**The owner's ruling that ReachKit never looks at a published page again after
its 24-hour check.** REQ-063 criterion 7 — the resumption path — was **withdrawn
outright** in the round-two fold, and its number was deliberately not reused, so
anything still citing "REQ-063 criterion 7" dangles visibly. Criterion 6 gained
the permanence its withdrawn sibling used to qualify, and criterion 1 now states
the accepted consequence in the requirement's own words: "a page the customer
removes after the 24-hour check goes on being judged week after week as though
it were still there, and ReachKit never learns otherwise."

**A fifth cause, and the sharpening of a second.** REQ-063 criterion 6's causes
now include "ReachKit's one check at 24 hours found no page at its live address
(REQ-062 criterion 4)" — a cause BP-049 records once and BP-051 reads, never
re-derives (ADR-085). And the `unpublished` cause was already terminal in fact
and nobody had noticed: REQ-056 criterion 3 makes `unpublished` a terminal
*state* — "`skipped` and `unpublished` are the only states from which no further
change is possible" — so ADR-071 Decision 5's "a page being republished" named a
transition REQ-056 criterion 2's fifteen edges do not contain. That clause was
never reachable; it merely read as though it were.

## Why terminality, and why it is a landmine rather than a shrug

A conditional cause promises a future observation. Under the ruling there is no
future observation of a published page at all — the only one there ever was is
REQ-062's, at 24 hours, and it runs once. So "lift the moment their condition
lifts" would mean, in practice:

- `page_not_found` lifting: impossible. Nothing looks again, by construction.
- `unpublished` lifting: impossible. The state is terminal.
- `question_left_set` and `search_untracked` lifting: **possible, and wrong.**
  The market re-derivation can put a question or a search back into the tracked
  set at any week, and a page judged again from that week would be judged
  against a test it was given for a market position ReachKit stopped measuring
  and has since re-derived. The resumed verdict reads as continuous with the
  ones before the gap and is not: nothing tells the customer that the page was
  unjudged for eleven weeks, and criterion 6's line — which already told them
  the page was no longer judgeable and when it was last judged — is contradicted
  by a later line saying it is judged again, which criterion 6 now forbids in
  terms.

The restore is a landmine precisely because it presents as the compassionate
fix. The page is live, the search is back, the customer is paying, and the
screen says "no longer judgeable" — every instinct says judge it. The reason not
to is one sentence in one criterion, and the code that would restore it fails no
test that exists.

## Decision

**Decisions 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 of ADR-071 are carried forward unchanged**, in
substance and in wording, and are not restated (rule 2.4). In summary, so a
reader of this file knows what it does *not* change: four shapes not four enum
members; `not_measured` and `no_week` represented by the absence of a row;
`no_week` derived once per site per week and rendered once; `not_judgeable`
always carrying its cause and always carrying `lastJudgedWeek` including the
`null` that means never judged; and three different written lines for the three
"cannot tell you" standings, in the owner's words. ADR-071's whole merge
argument stands as written there.

**Decision 5 (replacing ADR-071 Decision 5) — every `not_judgeable` cause is
terminal. A page marked no longer judgeable is never judged again, by any
route.**

Once a `not_judgeable` row exists for a page, every later week is
`not_judgeable` for that page with the same cause and the same
`lastJudgedWeek`, whatever happens to the search, the question, the tracked set,
the destination or the site. There is no lift condition, no re-entry path, and
no code that computes one. BP-051's `judgeWeek` reads whether a prior
`not_judgeable` row exists for the publication **before** it evaluates anything
else, and short-circuits.

**Decision 5a — the causes are five, and `page_not_found` is one of them.**
`NotJudgeableCause` is `search_untracked | unpublished | page_not_found |
question_left_set | domain_changed`. `page_not_found` is written from BP-049's
recorded outcome and from nothing else — never from a fetch of this node's own,
which REQ-063's non-goal forbids ("Any look at a published page's address").

**Decision 5b — `could_not_confirm` is not a cause and must never become one.**
A check that could not be confirmed leaves the page fully judgeable: REQ-063
criterion 1 shows that outcome "beside its verdict and never in place of one".
The two outcomes render as the same grey line and have opposite consequences
here; ADR-085 Decision 4 is the table, and this is the row of it that costs the
most, because Decision 5 makes the mistake unrecoverable.

**Decision 5c — terminality is enforced by the row, not by a flag.** There is no
`is_judgeable` column and no field cleared when a condition lifts. The
`page_verdicts` series is insert-only (ADR-071 Decision 2, carried forward), and
"has this page a `not_judgeable` row" is one indexed read. A flag would have a
writer that clears it, and the whole of this decision is that no such writer
exists.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep ADR-071 Decision 5 and treat the withdrawal of REQ-063 criterion 7 as
   removing only the *stated* resumption** — rejected. Criterion 6 does not
   merely omit resumption; it forbids it ("nothing restores it … no line ever
   says it is judged again"). A decision that contradicts an acceptance
   criterion is not a decision.
2. **Amend ADR-071 without superseding it, as ADR-084 did to ADR-083** —
   rejected under *Instrument* below.
3. **Make `search_untracked` and `question_left_set` alone conditional, since
   only they are observable** — rejected, and it is the most tempting option
   because it is the only one that is even implementable. It produces two
   classes of `not_judgeable` that render identically and behave oppositely,
   which is the merge hazard ADR-071 exists to prevent, arriving from the other
   direction. And it is the case *Why terminality* argues is substantively
   wrong, not merely unimplementable.
4. **Record a `resumed` standing so the customer can see the gap** — rejected.
   It is a fifth shape and a new promise; REQ-063 asks for four and forbids the
   fifth's occasion. If the owner later wants resumption, it arrives as a
   criterion and then as a superseding ADR, not as a shape added underneath one.
5. **Record this inside BP-051's `## Decisions`** — rejected for the reason
   ADR-071 alternative 1 gave, which is unchanged: the merge and now the restore
   will be proposed by whoever is writing the rendering (BP-019) or the weekly
   mail (BP-016), looking at a grey row and a live page. A decision buried in the
   verdict engine is invisible from there. It also amends another decision, which
   is rule 2.2's second file test on its own.

## Instrument — why ADR-071 is superseded and not amended

ADR-084's test, applied here: the instrument turns on whether the *ruling*
changed or a supporting sentence did.

- **ADR-071 Decision 5 is a ruling, not a rationale sentence**, and it inverts:
  three of four causes move from conditional to terminal. An implementer
  following it would build a resumption path REQ-063 criterion 6 now forbids in
  terms — the same failure ADR-084 named in ADR-082, "an instruction that would
  now destroy the wrong thing".
- ADR-071's warning block, unlike ADR-082's, is still **correct** — but it is
  now **incomplete**, and the case it omits is the one most likely to be
  proposed. A landmine file that is missing its live mine is not a file that can
  be left cited-and-standing.
- **Rule 2.2a is respected.** Nothing is restructured: five of six decisions are
  carried by citation and not rewritten, and one blueprint (BP-051) carries every
  prose citation of ADR-071 in the corpus, so the supersession costs one file's
  citations and no reorganisation. BP-019 and BP-016 reference ADR-071 only
  through its `decides-for` edge, which this file's own `decides-for` reproduces
  and extends with BP-049.

## Consequences

- BP-051's "`not_judgeable` is not a state a page recovers from silently … three
  of the four causes lift on their own terms" is false and is corrected in the
  same pass as this file.
- `NotJudgeableCause` gains a member (`page_not_found`), and `judgeWeek` gains a
  short-circuit read. Neither is a migration: `page_verdicts.cause` is `text`.
- A customer whose search returns to the tracked set sees the page they
  published for it stay "no longer judgeable" indefinitely. That is accepted,
  stated in REQ-063 criterion 6, and is not re-argued downstream (rule 4.2).
- **Cost of reversing.** In code: one short-circuit and one union member — an
  afternoon. In effect: a resumed verdict is presented to a customer as
  continuous with verdicts taken before the gap, against a test written for a
  market position ReachKit stopped measuring, and the criterion's own line
  saying the page is no longer judged is contradicted on the same screen. The
  reversal reads as restoring a feature and is the withdrawal of a promise.

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-09-01, minted at `accepted` rather than
`proposed`, on the precedent ADR-071, ADR-082, ADR-083 and ADR-084 set in this
corpus. ADR-071 is set to `superseded` in the same pass, because leaving it
`accepted` while its Decision 5 instructs a reader to build a resumption path
the requirement forbids is the authored index that lies (rule 5.1).

**Grounds.** The behaviour is REQ-063 criteria 1 and 6's own text and the
owner's ruling of 2026-09-01; the mechanism — a short-circuit read rather than a
flag, and which union carries `page_not_found` — is shape and boundaries, which
rule 1.1 places with this agent. The three "cannot tell you" lines stay the
owner's words and are carried forward outstanding (ADR-071 Decision 6).

**What is not a ground, and why it does not block.** REQ-063 is `status:
in-review` pending the owner's signature, carrying zero open `REVIEW(...)` lines
after two full review rounds (rule 3.4). §3's gate binds a *blueprint* to an
*approved* requirement; this file does not claim the gate is met and asserts the
status on the design. If the owner's signature restores a resumption path, that
reaches this file as a further superseding ADR — never as an edit here.
