# ADR-095 — The report blob's `market` section is the leaf's shape, and `coherence` has a member

- Date: 2026-09-04

> Standalone file because the subject spans four nodes and no single blueprint
> owns it (rule 2.2): BP-012 declares the blob, BP-025 writes the section,
> BP-011 names a fifth section the blob has nowhere to hold, and BP-056 reads a
> field none of the declared shapes exposes. Self-certified `accepted` per rule
> 3.2: it decides structure and type members only — internal names and module
> boundaries, the system's under §1 — changes no customer-visible string,
> number or verdict, and is taken at the moment reversal is cheapest (no
> `StoredReport` exists anywhere under `src/`, verified 2026-09-04). The
> librarian may refuse it retroactively.

## Context

Four nodes describe one `jsonb` value four ways.

| Node | What it says about `report.market` |
|---|---|
| **BP-012** | `market: Measured<SuggestionRow[]>` — a member of `StoredReport`, in its `## Public interface` |
| **BP-025** | `report.market = { profile, suggestions, totalVolume }` — in its `## Data model delta`, as prose, never as a declared type |
| **BP-056** | reads `report.market.category` — reachable under neither of the above |
| **BP-011** | writes a `coherence` section; `StoredReport` has no member that can hold one |

WO-286 followed BP-012 on rule 2.4 grounds and reported the disagreement back
rather than resolving it, which was right: its two `rests-on` rows name this
decision's occasion and say in terms that "resolving the three-way disagreement
is the architect's" and that "widening `StoredReport` is BP-012's author's, not
this work order's". WO-286 is `approved` and `blocked-by` eight orders, and no
`StoredReport` exists on disk, so nothing has been built on either reading.

The root cause is not that anyone chose wrongly. BP-025 never declared the
section as a **type** — it described the composition in a `## Data model delta`
sentence — so BP-012, composing the blob, reached for the nearest declared
thing, which was `deriveMarketSet`'s return type. That is the function's
result, not the section's shape: the section also carries the profile the
market was seeded from and the total volume behind it.

## Decision

1. **`MarketSet` is a declared type, owned by BP-025**, beside `SuggestionRow`
   in `src/lib/market/questions/market-set.ts` — the leaf cut from REQ-006,
   which owns `src/lib/market/questions/**` under `structure.md` rule 2. It
   composes exactly what BP-025's `## Data model delta` already writes: the
   `Profile` the market was seeded from, the suggestion rows, and their total
   volume. A section shape is declared by the node that writes the section.

2. **`StoredReport.market` is `Measured<MarketSet>`.** One `Measured` wrapper,
   not two: BP-025's own error behaviour states that "`deriveProfile` returning
   `unmeasured` (unreadable home page) yields no market", so the profile and
   the suggestions are unmeasured together and never apart. This keeps BP-012's
   own stated rule intact — "a section that could not be measured is present
   with its `Measured` arms saying so; a section is never omitted".

3. **`StoredReport` gains `coherence: CoherenceVerdict`**, BP-028's type
   (`src/lib/market/coherence/check.ts`), unwrapped. Not `Measured<…>`: the
   union is already total over the failure it has — its third arm,
   `{ verdict: 'unjudgeable'; measuredCount: number }`, is BP-011's "a report
   that measured fewer than three searches cannot judge coherence and must say
   so", so a `Measured` wrapper would be a second way to say the same thing and
   a consumer would have two `unknown`s to branch on. Same treatment as
   `verdict: Verdict` and `correctionState: CorrectionState`, which are
   likewise total and likewise unwrapped.

4. **BP-056 reads `report.market.profile.category`** — one hop deeper than the
   line it carries today. The category it needs is a field of `Profile`, and
   under decision 1 `Profile` is where it stays; the blob does not gain a
   second copy of it at the top level for one reader's convenience (rule 2.4).

5. **The general rule, restated once here because this is now its third
   instance.** Where a composing node and the leaf that writes a section
   declare the same thing differently, **the leaf's declaration stands and the
   composer's line is corrected to it** — the leaf is the one bound to the
   requirement. BP-010's `computeScore` and BP-011's `CoherenceVerdict` were
   both settled this way, each recording it as its own local precedent; it is
   written down here so the fourth instance cites a rule instead of a habit.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep `market: Measured<SuggestionRow[]>` and leave BP-025 and BP-056 to
   follow it** — rejected. It is the only reading under which the corpus loses
   information rather than moves it: `Profile` would have no home in the blob
   at all, so BP-056's measured `category` could not be read back from the
   current report and REQ-071's declared-versus-measured difference — which is
   the whole of ADR-030's pending-change mechanism — would have nothing to diff
   against. `totalVolume` would be homeless too. WO-286's own note records the
   consequence plainly ("**the blob carries no `Profile`**") and flags it as
   the fact WO-183's stop condition turns on.

2. **Add a separate `profile: Measured<Profile>` section beside `market`** —
   rejected. It invents a fourth shape where three already disagree, and it
   splits two things that are measured together and fail together into two
   members a consumer must check independently. BP-025 writes one section; the
   blob should hold one.

3. **Promote `category` to the top of `market`** (i.e. `market.category`,
   satisfying BP-056's line as written) — rejected. It is the second copy rule
   2.4 exists to prevent: the category would live at `market.category` and at
   `market.profile.category`, and the day they diverge a customer is shown one
   category and measured against the other.

4. **Leave all four as they are and let each work order pick** — rejected, and
   this is the alternative the corpus was actually running. WO-286 picked
   correctly *and defensibly* and still ended up with a blob that cannot hold a
   `Profile` or a `CoherenceVerdict`; the next order would pick differently on
   equally good grounds. Rule 7.1 gives one capability one owner, and the
   report blob's shape is one capability.

## Consequences

- BP-012's `## Public interface` changes on two lines: `market` gains its type
  and `coherence` gains a member. BP-025 declares `MarketSet`. BP-056's
  `## Data model delta` reads one field deeper. BP-011 is cited, not edited —
  its `coherence` section now has somewhere to go.
- **Reversal cost is at its floor and will not be this low again.** No
  `StoredReport`, no `MarketSet`, no `CoherenceVerdict` and no
  `src/lib/market/` exist anywhere under `src/` (verified 2026-09-04 — the same
  sweep WO-280's implementer and WO-286's planner each ran independently). This
  is a change to four documents and zero lines of code. Once the blob is
  written by a live scan, reversing it is a `version` bump on the blob and a
  reader that branches on both shapes.
- **WO-286 is not edited by this decision and does not need to be.** It is
  `blocked-by` eight orders that own the section types, none of which has
  landed, so no file it plans has been written. Its file plan says the type is
  taken "verbatim from BP-012's `## Public interface`", which is now this
  shape; its two reporting rows are answered by this file. The planner is told
  in this pass's return; the work order is the planner's to touch, not the
  architect's.
- The `blocked-by` edge WO-286 speculated about — "if either ships under a
  different name, this is a `blocked-by` on that work order added at that
  moment" — is unaffected: `OnPageFacts` and `SerpResult` are still declared by
  work orders and by no blueprint, which is a separate gap and not this
  decision's to close.
