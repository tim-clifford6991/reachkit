# ADR-002 — LANDMINE — report pages are `noindex` forever, and v1 report removal has no authentication by construction

- Date: 2026-08-31

> **Read this before removing a `noindex`, adding a report sitemap, or building
> a self-service takedown.** Every one of those reads like an obvious
> improvement. Each of them breaks a promise the corpus makes.

## Context
Three indexing policies coexist in this product and two of them are the opposite
of what an SEO-minded engineer expects from a company whose product is findability.

**What `BUILD.md` actually says.** Grep is the check, not memory. `BUILD.md`
mentions `noindex` three times: §5's scoring rule ("generic noindex on the home
document ⇒ 0"), §9's preview guardrail ("Preview at `{slug}.reachkit.app` is
`noindex` **forever** (site-reputation-abuse guardrail — customer content never
ranks on our domain)") and §14.6 restating it. It says **nothing anywhere** about
whether `/scan/{domain}` report pages are indexed, and it contains **no removal,
takedown or de-listing path of any kind** — the words do not appear.

So two of the three policies are owner rulings that arrived after the
specification, not transcriptions of it:

| Surface | Policy | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Hosted customer pages, `content.{domain}` | **Indexable**, sitemapped, canonical on the customer's domain, AI crawlers named and allowed | `BUILD.md` §9 — the product's whole point |
| Preview, `{slug}.reachkit.app` | **`noindex` forever**, no setting can change it | `BUILD.md` §9 — site-reputation-abuse guardrail |
| Free report, `/scan/{domain}` | **`noindex`, and listed in no sitemap the product publishes; still reachable by link to anyone holding it** | REQ-001 criterion 8 — an owner ruling of 2026-08-31, stated by no line of `BUILD.md` |

And REQ-002, the removal path, is likewise a requirement with no source clause
behind it: "`BUILD.md` states no removal path anywhere; this requirement, not the
specification, is the authority for it."

**Why the report `noindex` looks wrong.** A free report is the product's best
public content: unique, data-rich, one page per domain, permanently addressable.
Indexing it is the obvious growth move, and the first person to notice it is
`noindex`ed will read it as an oversight — especially since the adjacent hosted
surface is deliberately, elaborately indexable.

**Why it is load-bearing.** A report is a public verdict about somebody else's
business, published without their asking, produced from a scan a stranger can
start with one field and no account (REQ-001 criteria 1, 6 and 9). Indexing it
would mean: a permanent, search-discoverable page asserting a named third party
is "Invisible", scored, banded, with rivals named beside them — reachable by
anyone searching that business's name, created by anyone at all, about a business
that never consented.

The only route that business has to end it is REQ-002: a written request to an
address printed on the page, judged by a person, honoured within five working
days. In v1 there is **no proof of control at all** — no DNS TXT record, no
confirmation link to a mailbox at the domain, no verified-owner session; REQ-002's
non-goals defer all of it to v1.1 "and everything that would rest on it with it".

Those two facts are a matched pair. An unindexed page a stranger must be handed
the link to, whose subject can have it removed by asking, is a defensible v1. An
**indexed** page with the same unauthenticated, manual, five-day removal path is a
reputational and legal exposure that scales with every scan anyone runs. The
`noindex` is what makes the missing authentication survivable.

## Decision
1. **Every report address is served `noindex`** — meta robots and `X-Robots-Tag`
   — and appears in **no** sitemap the product publishes. It stays reachable by
   its link to anyone who has it, permanently, unbound to the visitor who first
   opened it (REQ-001 criterion 11). BP-001 owns this.
2. **`{slug}.reachkit.app` previews are `noindex` forever and no setting can
   change it** (REQ-059 criterion 5). BP-004 owns this.
3. **Hosted customer pages are the one surface built to be indexed**, with the
   sitemap, canonical and named-crawler robots policy of `BUILD.md` §9. BP-004
   owns this too, and serves two opposite robots policies from two hosts — which
   is why it is a separate container from BP-001.
4. **v1 report removal is manual, unauthenticated, and stated as such.** The
   product ships no self-service takedown, no proof-of-control check, and no
   verified-owner correction. What ships is: the address on every report, a
   `domain_blocks` row a person writes (BP-002), and a report address that
   afterwards serves the removal line to everyone and starts no scan for anyone,
   permanently, until a request to the same address asks for it back (REQ-002
   criterion 3, REQ-001 criterion 18). Removal is never taken at ReachKit's own
   initiative (REQ-002 criterion 4).
5. **These three policies move together.** Indexing report pages is not a
   configuration change; it is a change to REQ-001 and requires the v1.1
   proof-of-control mechanism REQ-002 defers, because it is that mechanism the
   exposure rests on.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Index report pages for organic growth** — rejected: it converts an
   unconsented public verdict about a named third party into a search result for
   that party's own name, while the only remedy is an unauthenticated manual
   request. The growth is real; the exposure is uncapped and grows with every
   scan a stranger runs.
2. **Index report pages and build proof of control first** — not rejected on the
   merits, but out of scope: REQ-002's non-goals defer the mechanism to v1.1, and
   nothing in v1 may depend on it. This is the path a future reversal must take,
   and it is a requirement change, not a header change.
3. **Self-service takedown in v1** — rejected: with no proof of control, a
   self-service control lets anyone remove a report about anyone, which is a
   worse failure than the one it fixes. REQ-002's non-goals say so directly.
4. **`noindex` on previews only, leaving reports indexable** — rejected: it
   protects ReachKit's domain reputation and leaves the third party unprotected,
   which is the wrong way round.
5. **Record this inside BP-001's `## Decisions`** — rejected under rule 2.2's file
   test and the skill's landmine rule: the subject spans BP-001 (report route),
   BP-004 (two opposite robots policies) and BP-002 (the removal record), no one
   of which owns it — and it is precisely a decision that "reads as wrong but is
   load-bearing", where the reversal would look like a cleanup.

## Consequences
- The free funnel grows by sharing, paid acquisition and word of mouth, never by
  ranking its own reports. Any growth plan resting on indexed reports is invalid
  until the v1.1 proof-of-control work lands.
- Two robots policies exist and must never be merged into one helper; BP-004's
  separateness from BP-001 is partly this decision's consequence.
- Removal is operational work with a five-working-day promise and no product
  surface behind it. It is a `rests-on` above, open: nothing in the corpus
  establishes the volume that promise survives.
- **Cost of reversing:** low in code — delete a meta tag, add a sitemap entry —
  and that cheapness is the danger. The true cost is a requirement change to
  REQ-001, the v1.1 authentication mechanism REQ-002 defers, and the exposure
  described above for every report already published. Anyone who finds the
  `noindex` and thinks "this is a bug" should read this file first, which is the
  entire reason it is a file.
