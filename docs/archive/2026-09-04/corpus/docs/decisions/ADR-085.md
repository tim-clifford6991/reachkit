# ADR-085 — LANDMINE — the one check has three outcomes, and "could not be confirmed" asserts nothing, is final, and must never be merged with "no page found"

- Date: 2026-09-01
- **Binds** REQ-062 criteria 4, 6 and 7, REQ-063 criteria 1 and 6, and REQ-043
  criterion 12 — all `in-review` on the owner's signature (see *Status grounds*).
- Depends on the owner's ruling of 2026-09-01, recorded in the round-two fold
  (`requirements/history/REQ-043-062-063-079-2026-09-01-review-rounds-one-and-two-folded.md`):
  the 24-hour check is the only look ReachKit ever takes at a published page,
  and on that check a page counts as gone on an unambiguous 404 or 410 only.

> **Read this before adding a retry to a check that "could not be confirmed",
> before folding `could_not_confirm` into `reachable: false`, and before adding
> `could_not_confirm` to `NotJudgeableCause` beside `page_not_found`.** All
> three read as tidying up an obviously incomplete union. All three pass every
> test that exists. Each one of them makes the product say something about a
> customer's page that ReachKit never observed:
>
> - **The retry** is the one that looks most like a bug fix. A transport error
>   at 24 hours *is* a flake, and retrying flakes is what good code does. It is
>   forbidden here because there is no second look — REQ-062's non-goal states
>   it in terms ("not retried, rescheduled or repeated; 'not confirmed' is a
>   final outcome for that page, not a pending one") — and a retry that
>   succeeds an hour later records a *different day's* fact under the check's
>   own date.
> - **The merge with `reachable: false`** turns ReachKit's own network problem
>   into an accusation against the customer's page, which REQ-062 criterion 4
>   forbids ("nothing is stated as a failure of this page").
> - **The merge into the verdict causes** is the expensive one. `page_not_found`
>   retires a page from judgement forever (REQ-063 criterion 6, and ADR-072:
>   nothing restores it). `could_not_confirm` does not and must not — REQ-063
>   criterion 1 shows it *beside* a verdict, "never in place of one". The two
>   render as the same grey line and have opposite consequences, and the page
>   that gets retired by the merge stops being judged silently, week after week,
>   with every screen still correct.

## Context

Until 2026-09-01 the 24-hour check had two shapes in the corpus: four
`Measured<boolean>` outcomes, or a disposition saying the check would never run
(`taken_down_first`, `no_live_address`). REQ-062 criterion 4 now names three
things that can come back from the one fetch, and BP-049's `VerifyDisposition`
had an arm for none of them:

1. **found** — the address answered and the four checks were measured;
2. **no page found** — "a 404 or a 410, and no other answer";
3. **could not be confirmed** — "the site could not be reached, the address
   returned a server error, it redirected away from the page, or what came back
   was not the page ReachKit published".

The third is not a degraded second. Criterion 4 says of it that "neither the
four outcomes nor the page's absence is asserted": it is an outcome whose whole
content is *ReachKit does not know*, carrying the date it was tried.

Two further rulings landed on the same text and are what make this a file rather
than a union:

- **Criterion 6 no longer covers reachability.** It kept only the sitemap and
  the robots policy, and it now records a condition of a site "only from an
  answer the site gave". Any suppression keyed on "the site could not be
  reached" is wrong twice: the case has moved to criterion 4, and criterion 6's
  suppression may not fire on a non-answer at all.
- **Criterion 7 is new and is a law over surfaces, not a field.** Wherever any
  surface states a page's state, it carries the recorded outcome and its date
  alongside, and "no surface states as a fact about the page today anything
  beyond what that one check recorded on that date".

No single blueprint owns all of that. The union and the fetch are BP-049's; the
stored disposition and the page record are BP-045's; the day panel is BP-039's;
the verdict cause is BP-051's; the `published` mail is BP-053's. Criterion 7 in
particular is a constraint on four of them at once.

## Decision

**Decision 1 — the outcome of the one check is a four-arm union, and the two
"we did not measure" arms are distinct from each other.**

BP-049's `VerifyDisposition` gains `page_not_found` and `could_not_confirm` as
*outcomes of a check that ran*, beside the existing `never` arms for a check
that could not run. `VerifyResult` — the four `Measured<boolean>` — is produced
in the `found` arm and in no other. A surface that wants to render "no verdict
this week" must destructure, the same mechanism ADR-071 chose for
`WeekStanding` and ADR-072 carries forward.

**Decision 2 — a 404 or a 410 and nothing else is `page_not_found`.**

Every other answer — 5xx, 3xx away from the page, a 200 whose body is not the
page ReachKit published, a DNS failure, a reset, a timeout — is
`could_not_confirm`. The mapping is in one place, BP-049's `verify.ts`, and it
is stated as a **whitelist of two statuses**, never as a blacklist of failures:
a blacklist admits an unclassified answer into `page_not_found` by default, and
the default arm must be the one that asserts nothing.

**Decision 3 — neither outcome is retried, and neither schedules anything.**

`dueNow` selects only rows with no recorded outcome. A recorded
`could_not_confirm` is recorded, and the row is not due again. There is no
backoff, no reschedule, no "pending" render and no second look for any reason.

**Decision 4 — the two outcomes have opposite consequences downstream, and each
consequence is stated where it is read.**

| | `page_not_found` | `could_not_confirm` |
|---|---|---|
| The four checks | not produced | not produced |
| Stated as a failure of the page | never | never |
| Cause named | never (REQ-062 c4: "names no cause and never says who removed the page") | never |
| The page's publish state | stops being shown as live (REQ-060 c2) | **unchanged** — still shown as it was |
| Weekly verdict | `not_judgeable`, terminal (REQ-063 c6, ADR-072) | ordinary verdict continues; the outcome is shown **beside** it (REQ-063 c1) |
| The day panel's way through | the detail says the way leads nowhere (REQ-043 c12) | the way is offered; the detail claims only where the page was put |
| The `published` mail | carries this in place of the four outcomes (REQ-062 c5) | carries this in place of the four outcomes (REQ-062 c5) |

The middle three rows are the whole reason this is a file. They are the rows a
merge deletes, and none of them is asserted by a test that exists before the
merge is proposed.

**Decision 5 — criterion 7 is enforced by there being one place to read a page's
observed state, not by a rule each surface follows.**

BP-045 exposes the page record's read model (`record/`), and it returns the
recorded outcome and its date **inside the same value** as the page's state. A
surface cannot obtain the state without the outcome, because they are one
object and neither field is optional. That is the same mechanism BP-019's
`Measured<T>` uses for REQ-004 and BP-054's `WorkStop` uses for REQ-092: the
promise holds on surfaces that do not exist yet, because the type will not let
them render without it.

**Decision 6 — criterion 6's site condition is recorded only from an answer, and
it suppresses nothing that was not answered.**

BP-049 records a condition of a site (`no_sitemap`, `robots_blocks_site`) only
where the site returned something to read. Where the sitemap or the robots
document could not be reached or could not be parsed, that one check is
`unmeasured` for this page alone, no site condition is written, nothing is
stated as the customer's to act on, and **no later page's check is suppressed**.
The suppression that exists is keyed on a recorded condition, never on a
non-answer.

## Alternatives considered

1. **A boolean `confirmed` beside the existing outcome** — rejected. It puts
   the discriminator outside the union, so the four `Measured<boolean>` values
   exist in a shape where they are meaningless, and the first consumer that
   forgets the boolean renders them.
2. **Reuse `Measured<T>`'s `unmeasured` arm for the whole check** — rejected.
   `Measured` says *this quantity was not measured*; criterion 4's third outcome
   says *the check itself did not settle*, which is a fact about the page's
   record and carries its own date and its own sentence. Folding them makes
   "the page has no sitemap entry because we could not fetch the sitemap"
   indistinguishable from "we never reached the page at all".
3. **Retry `could_not_confirm` once, immediately** — rejected. It is the most
   defensible version of the forbidden thing and is why the warning block leads
   with it. A retry inside the same job tick is still a second look, still
   records a later moment under the check's stated date, and still needs a
   stopping rule the requirement does not give. REQ-062's non-goal closes it.
4. **Let `could_not_confirm` make a page `not_judgeable`, matching
   `page_not_found`** — rejected under Decision 4. It retires a page from
   judgement forever on the strength of ReachKit's own transport failure, and
   ADR-072 makes that retirement permanent, so the mistake is unrecoverable by
   design.
5. **Keep reachability in REQ-062 criterion 6 and treat an unreachable hosted
   page as a site condition** — rejected, and it is what the requirement itself
   now forbids: criterion 6 is scoped to sites ReachKit does not serve, so a
   hosted page whose CNAME broke would be blamed for a condition of a site
   ReachKit runs, and the suppression would silently disable the check for every
   later page on that site.
6. **Record this as a `## Decisions` section inside BP-049** — rejected under
   rule 2.2's first file test. Decision 4's table is read by BP-051 (the verdict
   cause), BP-039 (the way through), BP-053 (the mail's payload) and BP-045 (the
   page record), and Decision 5 is a constraint on all four at once. The merge
   this file exists to stop will be proposed by whoever is looking at two grey
   lines in a day panel, and a decision buried in the verification engine is
   invisible from there — the same argument ADR-071 alternative 1 made, for the
   same reason.

## Consequences

- Two copy keys exist where a reviewer will see one concept, and two verdict
  paths where a reviewer will see one grey row. That redundancy is the decision.
- `publications` gains no column for the outcome: it is the shape of the
  existing `verify jsonb`, which BP-049 already owns (BP-045's migration).
- A page whose check could not be confirmed goes on being judged and goes on
  being shown as it was, indefinitely, and ReachKit never learns otherwise. That
  is accepted, stated in REQ-063 criterion 1 in the requirement's own words, and
  is not re-argued downstream (rule 4.2).
- **Cost of reversing.** Trivial in code: one arm, one branch, one key. The cost
  in effect is a page retired from judgement by a network blip, or a customer
  told their page is gone when ReachKit hit a 502 — neither of which fails a
  test, and both of which read as the tidier design.

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-09-01, minted at `accepted` rather than
`proposed`, on the precedent ADR-082, ADR-083 and ADR-084 set in this corpus.
Rule 3.2 makes the status this agent's assertion; rule 3.4 states that a
decision is derived and takes no owner-approval round.

**Grounds.** The behaviour is REQ-062's and REQ-063's own criterion text and the
owner's ruling quoted above; the mechanism — a four-arm union, a two-status
whitelist, one read model carrying the outcome with the state — is boundaries
and shape, which rule 1.1 places with this agent. Every sentence the customer
reads is a copy key here and is owed by the owner, not minted.

**What is not a ground, and why it does not block.** REQ-062, REQ-063 and
REQ-043 are `status: in-review` pending the owner's signature. They carry zero
open `REVIEW(...)` lines after two full review rounds (rule 3.4), so the text is
settled — but §3's gate binds a *blueprint* to an *approved* requirement, and
this file does not pretend the gate is met. It is asserted on the design. If the
owner's signature changes what any of these criteria promise, that reaches this
file as a further superseding ADR — never as an edit here (rule 2.2b), and never
as the deletion of an arm.
