# ADR-060 — LANDMINE — the weekly measurement is triggered hourly and gated on the site's own Monday; `Mon 06:00 UTC` is not the trigger

> **Read this before changing `weekly/refresh` to a weekly cron.** `BUILD.md`
> §11 says `Mon 06:00 UTC` in a table, in writing, and moving the schedule back
> to it will read like restoring fidelity to the specification. It silently
> breaks the weekly measurement for every customer west of UTC−6.

- Date: 2026-08-31

## Context
`BUILD.md` §11 pins the job table row verbatim:

> `weekly/refresh` | Mon 06:00 UTC | Weekly scan per active site → re-derive →
> verdicts → movement email

REQ-065 criterion 1, approved, states the promise that row is supposed to
deliver:

> Given a site with active access, when a week beginning on Monday **in the time
> zone the customer set** arrives, then its market is re-measured exactly once
> within that week and **never before it begins**

The two are not compatible. Monday 06:00 UTC is:

| Site zone | Local time at Mon 06:00 UTC | Inside the customer's Monday? |
|---|---|---|
| UTC+13 (Auckland) | Mon 19:00 | yes |
| UTC+0 (London, winter) | Mon 06:00 | yes |
| UTC−6 (Mexico City, winter) | Mon 00:00 | yes, exactly at the boundary |
| UTC−7 (Denver) | **Sun 23:00** | **no — the previous week** |
| UTC−8 (Los Angeles) | **Sun 22:00** | **no — the previous week** |
| UTC−11 (Niue) | **Sun 19:00** | **no — the previous week** |

Everything west of UTC−6 is measured in the wrong week. The failure is not a
late measurement, which would be harmless; it is a measurement filed under a week
that has not started, which makes REQ-065 criterion 2 ("no value from an earlier
week is presented as this week's") false for a third of the world's populated
offsets, and makes REQ-040 criterion 6's week count — "never larger than the
number of weeks in which the site was measured" — count a week the site was not
measured in.

A second, quieter failure: even for zones where 06:00 UTC lands inside Monday,
a **single** weekly fire has no retry inside the customer's own week. BP-003's
NFR line already asserts the stronger property — "`weekly/refresh` must complete
every active site inside the customer's own Monday (REQ-065 criterion 1)" — which
one instant per week cannot guarantee for a site whose run fails at 06:05.

REQ-065's own non-goals hand the resolution to the design rather than to the
owner:

> The clock the job runs on: what must hold is one measurement inside each
> customer's own Monday-to-Sunday week, whatever schedule delivers it.

So this is a parameter choice under rule 1.1, not an escalation — but it spans
BP-003 (the schedule and the job's stated NFR), BP-005 (the pin), BP-012 (the
tier) and BP-050 (the promise), and no one of those owns it. Hence a file rather
than a `## Decisions` entry (rule 2.2), and a landmine one, because the wrong
value is the one written in the specification.

## Decision
1. **`weekly/refresh` fires hourly**, not weekly. The cron expression is a
   fan-out tick, not the semantic schedule.
2. **Each tick selects due sites by the site's own local clock.** A site is due
   when: its local weekday is Monday or later within the current site-local week,
   its local hour has reached `WEEKLY_DUE_HOUR_LOCAL` (**6**, the hour
   `BUILD.md` §11 chose — kept, with its zone corrected), it has active access
   (BP-017's `hasActiveAccess`), and no `scans` row exists for
   `(site_id, week_start)` with `tier = 'weekly'`.
3. **`week_start` is the site-local Monday**, stored as a `date` at insert
   (BP-050 decision 2), and `(site_id, week_start) where tier = 'weekly'` carries
   a partial unique index. That index — not the schedule — is what makes "exactly
   once within that week" true; the hourly tick is merely how the run gets a
   chance to happen inside every zone's Monday.
4. **The hour, not the day, is the tunable.** `WEEKLY_DUE_HOUR_LOCAL = 6` is
   pinned in `src/lib/config/constants.ts` and asserted in `tests/pins.test.ts`.
   The Monday-start is REQ-065's and is not a pin.
5. **Retry is free.** A site whose run failed has no row, so the next hourly tick
   inside the same site-local week retries it. A site whose run completed —
   `done` or `degraded` — has a row and is not retried, because criterion 1
   permits one measurement, not one success.

An hourly tick is fine enough for every real offset, including the quarter-hour
ones (UTC+5:45, UTC+8:45, UTC+12:45): the worst case is a measurement taken up to
45 minutes after the local due hour, which no criterion constrains.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Keep `Mon 06:00 UTC` and shift the *reported* date into the customer's zone**
   — rejected, and this is the tempting one. It makes the displayed date look
   right while the measurement genuinely belongs to the previous week: a Denver
   customer's "Monday" figures would be Sunday-evening figures relabelled, which
   is exactly the "last week's wearing today's date" REQ-065's user story names as
   the harm.
2. **Twenty-four weekly crons, one per whole-hour offset** — rejected: it does not
   cover quarter- and half-hour offsets, it multiplies the schedule surface by 24,
   and it still gives each site one instant per week with no retry inside its own
   week.
3. **Fire daily instead of hourly** — rejected for retry granularity: a run that
   fails gets one more attempt 24 hours later, and a site whose local Monday is
   nearly over then misses its week entirely. Hourly costs one indexed query per
   hour and buys twenty-four attempts per Monday.
4. **Fire every 15 minutes** — rejected as unnecessary: nothing in the corpus
   constrains the measurement to a quarter-hour, and the query runs 96 times a day
   for no promise it makes truer.
5. **Store the trigger as a per-site scheduled job created at signup** — rejected:
   a zone change would require rescheduling every affected job, and a customer who
   changes zone mid-week could be measured twice or not at all. The due-query plus
   the unique index has no such state to keep in sync.
6. **Record this inside BP-050's `## Decisions`** — rejected under rule 2.2's file
   test: it corrects a schedule BP-003 states, a pin BP-005 holds, and a promise
   BP-050 makes, and a reader arriving at BP-003 to "fix the cron back to what
   `BUILD.md` says" would find nothing telling them why it is not that.

## Consequences
- BP-003's `weekly/refresh` row is an hourly tick that calls BP-050's
  `dueSites`; the job's idempotency key stays `(site_id, week_start)`, exactly as
  BP-003 already states.
- BP-003's NFR line — "the schedule fires at 06:00 UTC and fans out per site" —
  is superseded by this decision. This ADR does not edit BP-003; the correction is
  reported for that node's owner to fold in.
- `BUILD.md` §11's cell is now a value the product deliberately does not
  implement literally. `BUILD.md` is not edited here either; the divergence is
  recorded in this file, which is the one place a reader will look.
- The measurement may be taken up to an hour (or 1h45 in a quarter-hour zone)
  after the site-local due hour. No approved criterion constrains the hour.
- **Cost of reversing:** trivial in code — one cron expression and one predicate —
  and that cheapness is the danger. The true cost is that every customer west of
  UTC−6 is measured in the wrong week, silently: nothing fails, no error is
  logged, and the only symptom is a week count and a set of weekly figures that
  are off by one week for some customers and correct for others. Anyone who finds
  the hourly tick and thinks "this should be a weekly cron, the spec says so"
  should read this file before changing it.
