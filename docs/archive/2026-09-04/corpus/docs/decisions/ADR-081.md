# ADR-081 — Unpublish is scoped by liveness: a page ReachKit published to the customer's WordPress is returned to draft there; a page it created but never made live is named, and not touched

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-01 by ADR-082 — read that file, not this one.** The
> ruling (scope by liveness) is carried forward there unchanged. Two things in
> this file are wrong and are corrected there: the sentence *"a WordPress post
> is never made live by us"*, presented below as a quotation of REQ-060
> criterion 1, is a paraphrase and not that criterion's text; and Decision 3's
> three-arm `UnpublishResult`, together with Decision 5's mapping of a
> since-deleted post onto `named_for_removal`, is refuted by REQ-056 criterion
> 16's current four outcomes. The verbatim quotations of REQ-056 criterion 16
> and REQ-079 criterion 4 in the Context below are those criteria as they read
> on this file's own date; both have since been rewritten. Nothing below is
> edited — an `accepted` decision is amended by supersession (rule 2.2b), and
> the body is left as the record of what was ruled and why.

> **Read this before deleting the `returned_to_draft` arm or the
> `made_live_by_us` column as dead code.** With REQ-060 criterion 1 standing —
> "a WordPress post is never made live by us" — that arm is **unreachable for
> WordPress today**, and every WordPress unpublish takes `named_for_removal`.
> It is not dead code; it is the branch REQ-079 criterion 4 promises, held open
> against the day the product gains the ability to publish into a customer's
> WordPress live. Removing it looks like a cleanup with a passing test suite,
> and the day that ability arrives the promise silently reverts to "touch
> nothing". See Consequences.

- Date: 2026-08-31
- **Binds** REQ-056 criterion 16 and REQ-079 criterion 4. Both requirements move

## Context
Two approved requirements described what ReachKit does inside a customer's own
WordPress when a page stops being one of their live pages, and they described it
differently.

REQ-056 criterion 16, verbatim: "Given a page delivered to WordPress, when the
customer chooses to unpublish it, then the product stops treating it as one of
their live pages and names the post in their own site for them to remove, since
ReachKit never made it live."

REQ-079 criterion 4, verbatim: "Given 'unpublish everything' completes, when
anyone visits a page that was live, then the hosted CMS no longer serves it and a
page ReachKit published to WordPress is returned to draft there; where a
destination cannot be reached, its pages are listed as still live with one
written line saying so."

Read as one population, they are incompatible: one says touch nothing, the other
says write. BP-048 carried `blocked-by: [REQ-079]` and refused to implement
either limb; BP-063 carried `blocked-by: [REQ-056, BP-015]`; BP-015 carried the
open question. One BP-048 work order — the unpublish write path — was
deliberately never cut, and has no id.

## Decision
**Scope by liveness.** There are two populations, not one, and each criterion is
true of its own.

1. **A page ReachKit actually published to the customer's WordPress is returned
   to draft there** on unpublish and on unpublish-everything. This is REQ-079
   criterion 4's behaviour and it is a write into the customer's site: exactly
   one field of exactly one post ReachKit itself created and itself made live.
2. **A page ReachKit created but never made live is named for the customer, and
   not touched.** This is REQ-056 criterion 16's behaviour, and criterion 16's
   own subordinate clause — "since ReachKit never made it live" — is what makes
   the scoping legible rather than invented: the criterion states its own
   population in the sentence that states its behaviour.
3. **Neither criterion is wrong and neither is narrowed by the other.** Criterion
   16 governs the never-live population and criterion 4 governs the was-live one;
   the requirements-analyst's job on the way back through the gate is to make
   each say which population it speaks for, not to delete either.

### What this fixes in the interfaces

**Decision 2 — the discriminator is a stored fact on the publication row, never
a re-read of the customer's site.** BP-048's data-model delta sets
`live_url` **null** for every WordPress delivery, so `live_url != null` cannot
separate the two populations. BP-045's `publications` topic gains
`made_live_by_us boolean not null default false`, and `DeliveryResult` gains
`madeLive: boolean` — the adapter that did or did not make the page live says
so, and `publish()` stores what it said. BP-048 narrows its own to `false` by
type, so REQ-060 criterion 1 is upheld by there being no value that adapter
could return rather than by a rule somebody has to keep. Note that `madeLive`
is not `servesPublicly`: the second is a property of the destination, the first
of what this call did to this page, and only the first is a fact about
ReachKit's own act. Nothing else may be consulted: reading the customer's site at
unpublish time would make the answer depend on what the customer did in the
meantime, and a customer who published our draft themselves is not a page
ReachKit made live.

**Decision 3 — `UnpublishResult` states which of three things happened.**
BP-045's `unpublish` returns `{ ok: true; removedFromDestination: boolean }`
today, and a boolean cannot carry three outcomes. It becomes a closed union:

- `removed` — the hosted destination stopped serving it (REQ-056 c15);
- `returned_to_draft` — the WordPress post ReachKit made live is now a draft
  again (REQ-079 c4);
- `named_for_removal` — the WordPress post ReachKit never made live; nothing was
  written, and the post is named for the customer (REQ-056 c16).

A fourth arm, `unreachable`, is **not** added here: a destination that cannot be
reached is already REQ-079 criterion 4's second clause and is BP-063's
`stillLive` list, which exists.

**Decision 4 — the never-live path still writes nothing at all.** Not the post
status, not a tag, not a comment, not a removal of the marker ADR-080 forbids
removing. "Names the post" is a line ReachKit renders on its own surface, naming
a post in a site it does not own. REQ-060 criterion 1 ("never made live by us")
and REQ-060's non-goals are untouched by this ruling for that population.

**Decision 5 — the was-live path writes exactly one field, and is idempotent.**
Setting a post that is already `draft` to `draft` is a no-op and returns
`returned_to_draft`; a post the customer has since deleted returns
`named_for_removal` rather than failing, because there is nothing left to return
and nothing was lost. The marker ADR-080 requires stays on the post in both
cases — ADR-080 decision 5 is not weakened by this file, and a "cleanup" that
removes the marker while returning the post to draft is the same landmine.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Take criterion 16 everywhere: never write into WordPress at all.** Rejected.
   It silently discards REQ-079 criterion 4's promise for the population it was
   written about — a customer who asked ReachKit to take every page down, and
   whose pages ReachKit itself put up, would find them still live. That is the
   failure REQ-079's user story names first ("leaving is a decision I can
   survive").
2. **Take criterion 4 everywhere: always return the post to draft.** Rejected. It
   writes into a site we do not own on a page we never made live, which
   contradicts REQ-060 criterion 1 — a third approved requirement neither
   criterion is in conflict with — and REQ-056's own non-goal ("Deleting or
   editing posts inside the customer's own WordPress").
3. **Ask the customer at unpublish time which they want.** Rejected: it puts a
   question in the one place REQ-079 promises a decision the customer can
   survive, and it makes the product's behaviour inside somebody else's site a
   thing the customer must get right rather than a thing ReachKit knows.
4. **Discriminate on `publications.live_url`.** Rejected on the evidence above —
   it is null for every WordPress row by design, so the predicate would classify
   every WordPress page as never-live and quietly reduce to alternative 1.
5. **Discriminate by re-reading the post's status at unpublish time.** Rejected:
   a customer who published our draft themselves would then be treated as a page
   ReachKit made live, and ReachKit would write into a post the customer chose to
   publish. The stored fact is about what *we* did; the site's state is about
   what *they* did, and only the first is ours to act on.
6. **Record this as a `## Decisions` entry inside BP-048.** Rejected under rule
   2.2's file test. It settles one clause in BP-048's adapter, one column and one
   return union in BP-045, one branch of BP-063's `UnpublishAllResult`, and the
   `DestinationAdapter.unpublish` contract in BP-015 — four nodes, none of which
   owns the others. It is also the resolution of a conflict two requirements
   carried, which is the artifact a `/decide` ruling is.

## Consequences
- BP-048's `blocked-by: [REQ-079]` is discharged, and the fourth BP-048 work
  order — the unpublish write path, never cut — can now be cut. It is the only
  work order this ruling creates.
- BP-063's `blocked-by: [REQ-056, BP-015]` is discharged. Its criterion 4
  WordPress branch is now specified; criteria 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 were never
  blocked and are unchanged.
- BP-015's open question is answered and removed.
- BP-045 gains one column and one union arm-set. Its existing statement — that
  the edge `published → unpublished` is the same edge under either ruling —
  stands: no transition changes.
- Two written lines are now needed where BP-048 named one: the was-live line and
  the never-live line. They are copy keys under BP-019/BP-020 and their strings
  are still the owner's; this file mints no sentence.
- **The `returned_to_draft` arm is empty for WordPress under the corpus as it
  stands, and that is stated here rather than discovered later.** REQ-060
  criterion 1 promises "a WordPress post is never made live by us", and BP-048
  has no code path that sets any status other than draft, on creation or
  afterwards. So `made_live_by_us` is `false` for every WordPress publication
  this product can currently create, and every WordPress unpublish takes
  `named_for_removal`. REQ-079 criterion 4's WordPress clause therefore describes
  a population that is empty today.
  - That is not an argument against the ruling. It is what "each criterion is
    true of a different population" *means* when one of the two populations
    happens to have no members yet: the scoping is still the correct reading of
    two criteria that each state their own scope, and the alternative — deciding
    that criterion 4's WordPress clause was simply wrong — throws away a promise
    the owner has not withdrawn.
  - It does change what the branch is *for*. It is not a behaviour that ships
    working on day one; it is the shape that makes the promise survive the change
    that would otherwise silently break it, which is why it is a named arm of a
    union rather than a boolean and a comment.
  - The `hosted` destination is where liveness is non-empty today: ReachKit does
    make hosted pages live and `removed` is the arm they take, which is
    criterion 4's first clause.
  - Reported to the owner in the return that carries this file. If the owner's
    intent was that criterion 4's WordPress clause be withdrawn rather than
    scoped, that is a change to REQ-079 and reaches this file as a superseding
    ADR — not as a deletion of the arm.
- **Cost of reversing:** moderate and asymmetric. Reversing toward alternative 1
  is one deleted branch and one dead column, and it silently breaks REQ-079's
  promise for every page ReachKit published. Reversing toward alternative 2 is
  one deleted branch and it starts writing into posts on sites we do not own.
  Both read as simplifications — "why are there two paths here?" — which is why
  the two populations are named in the type (Decision 3) rather than left to a
  boolean and a comment.

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-08-31, moved from `proposed`. Rule 2.2b's
ADR grammar is `proposed → accepted → superseded` and rule 3.2 makes the status
this agent's assertion, audited by the librarian afterwards, not a queue.

**Grounds.** Four blueprints already record this decision's consequences as
landed, and none of them is `draft`: BP-045 carries `made_live_by_us` in its
data-model delta as "the whole discriminator" and `UnpublishResult` in its public
interface; BP-048 and BP-063 each carry a `## Blocked-by — discharged 2026-08-31
by ADR-081` section, and BP-063's front-matter `blocked-by` is `[]` because of it;
BP-015 cites it at three points. Nine work orders name `UnpublishResult` or the
adapter it sits on. A decision the corpus has already built on, whose status
still reads `proposed`, is the authored index that lies (rule 5.1) — it says
undecided where every artifact downstream says decided. It was the only
non-template ADR of twenty-one still `proposed`.

**What is *not* a ground, and why it does not block.** REQ-056 and REQ-079 are
both `status: in-review`, and REQ-056 still carries the open `REVIEW(conflict
with REQ-079 criterion 4)` line this file was written to resolve. That is the
requirements-analyst's fold and the owner's approval — the constitution's first
owner gate — and it is downstream of this file, not upstream of it: §3 makes
decisions "derived and self-certified by their authoring agent", and rule 3.4
says in terms that `/decide` "produce[s] no such draft" and takes no round-two
gate. The gate that binds a *blueprint* to an approved requirement is unaffected;
BP-063's own `## Status grounds` already states that dependency and what it would
do if the rewording changes behaviour rather than wording.

**The reversal path is unchanged and is named in Consequences:** if the owner's
intent was that REQ-079 criterion 4's WordPress clause be withdrawn rather than
scoped, that reaches this file as a **superseding ADR**, never as an edit here
and never as a deletion of the `returned_to_draft` arm. `accepted → superseded`
is the grammar for exactly that, and it is the transition this file's warning
block exists to make visible.
