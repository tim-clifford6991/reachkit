# ADR-084 — LANDMINE — every CMS is published live in one call, and the two booleans that used to be one must never be merged back: the empty unpublish arm has inverted

- Date: 2026-09-01
- **Supersedes ADR-082** (rule 2.2b: an `accepted` ADR is never edited in
  place). Reasoning for supersession rather than a premise note is in
  *Instrument* below.
- **Amends a premise of ADR-083 without superseding it.** ADR-083 stands in
  full; Decision 5 below states which sentence of its Decision 5 is now false
  and what stands in its place.
- **Binds** REQ-056 criteria 6 and 16, REQ-057 criterion 9, REQ-060 criteria 1
  and 2, REQ-062, REQ-063 and REQ-079 criteria 4 and 6 — all seven of which go
  back through the requirements gate in the same pass.

> **Read this before deleting the `named_for_removal` arm, before dropping
> `publications.made_live_by_us` as a column that is always `true`, and before
> merging `servesPublicly` and `hostedByUs` back into the one boolean they were
> until this file.** ADR-082's warning block told a future reader to protect
> `returned_to_draft` because it was the empty arm. **That warning now points at
> the wrong arm.** With ReachKit publishing every WordPress post live,
> `returned_to_draft` is the ordinary WordPress unpublish and
> `named_for_removal` is the arm with no members — so the arm that reads as dead
> code has swapped ends, and the deletion that looks like a cleanup, passes every
> test and silently withdraws a promise is now the other one. `made_live_by_us`
> becomes constant `true` for every WordPress row and reads exactly like a column
> nobody needs; it is the discriminator REQ-056 criterion 16 rests on and the
> reason `unpublish` never writes into a post ReachKit did not make live. And
> `servesPublicly` and `hostedByUs` had the same value at every destination until
> today, which is why they were one field; they now differ at WordPress, and
> collapsing them either stops verifying live customer pages or points
> `unpublish` at a delete call against a site we do not own. See Decisions 2, 3
> and 4.

## Context

### The ruling
The owner ruled on 2026-09-01 that content is published complete, not as a
draft, at every destination — "This must be true for all CMSs, including
Workpress and Reachkit CMS" — that the customer's supervision is the window in
which they may edit or veto and the ability to unpublish later, and that every
page is tracked in the calendar with its status, a link to their CMS and an
unpublish action.

### What it overturns, and why the specification could be read either way
REQ-060 criterion 1 reads, verbatim:

> "Given a customer connects their WordPress site with an application password,
> when a page is published to that destination, then it appears in their
> WordPress site as a draft and is never made live by us."

That criterion was faithfully transcribed. **`BUILD.md` contradicts itself on
this point, and both readings are in the file.** For draft:

> "**WordPress:** REST + application password, posts as draft +
> Yoast/RankMath meta when detected; credentials encrypted at rest, never
> logged, revoked on disconnect."
> — `BUILD.md` §9 "Publishing and autopilot", lines 569–571

> "| 10 | WordPress + settings/billing complete | Connect → draft lands with
> meta; token expiry = state not error |"
> — `BUILD.md` §16 "Build order", line 673

For live, in the specification's own opening paragraph, before any section:

> "From that we derive a ranked list of pages worth publishing, write one per
> day, and publish it to a blog on their own domain (or their WordPress) after
> a 24-hour veto window."
> — `BUILD.md` "The one-paragraph product", lines 20–22

and twice more in the mechanics, where nothing is conditioned on destination:

> "Every published page records: opportunity id, target query, measurement
> date, approved-vs-autopilot, live URL."
> — `BUILD.md` §9, line 560

> "| `publications` | id, draft_id, site_id, destination, live_url,
> published_at, mode(approved/autopilot), verify jsonb(reachable/indexable/
> sitemap/ai_readable/checked_at), verdict(working/too_early/not_working),
> unpublished_at |"
> — `BUILD.md` §10 "Data model", line 589

The analyst took the specific clause over the summary, which is the ordinary
rule and was defensible. The owner has now ruled the summary's reading is the
product. **`BUILD.md` is the incoming specification and is not edited by this
file**; a future reader who greps it will find "posts as draft" in two places
and must read it as ruled against here, not as missed.

One line in the same section is **not** a third vote for drafts and must not be
read as one: "**Draft-by-default everywhere.** Autopilot = auto-approve when the
veto window (default 24h, settable 0–7d) expires without a veto" (`BUILD.md`
§9, line 556) is about ReachKit's own `in_review` state and the veto window
(REQ-057), not about a post's status inside WordPress.

### What it does to the standing decisions
ADR-082's Decision 1 (carried from ADR-081) scopes unpublish by liveness, and
its warning block rests that scoping on REQ-060 criterion 1 in terms. With that
criterion overturned the *ruling* survives — liveness is still the right
discriminator, and it is now the discriminator that actually does work — but
**both populations invert**, and the file's warning block, which exists
precisely to be trusted by a reader about to delete a branch, now names the
wrong branch. That is what makes this a supersession rather than a note.

## Instrument — why ADR-082 is superseded and ADR-083 is not
Both files are `accepted`, and rule 2.2b makes supersession the only instrument
for either. The instrument is chosen by whether the *ruling* changed, not by
whether a sentence did.

- **ADR-082's ruling changed.** Its Decision 3 union is unchanged in shape but
  every arm's population moves; its Decision 6 column becomes constant at one
  destination; and its warning block gives a future reader an instruction that
  would now destroy the wrong thing. A file whose load-bearing paragraph is
  actively misleading cannot be left standing as the record (rule 5.1 — the
  authored index that lies). Superseded.
- **ADR-083's ruling did not change.** Every one of its five decisions survives
  intact — two marks not one, a `post_tag`, written at creation only, never
  substituted for the marker, a stamp that cannot be applied is not a failed
  delivery, and the term's name is the owner's. One supporting sentence in its
  Decision 5 rationale is now false, and the falsity *strengthens* the decision
  it supports rather than changing it (Decision 5 below). Rule 2.2a: record the
  assessment, move nothing. To close the discoverability gap that leaves — a
  reader of ADR-083 has no pointer to this correction — this file's
  `decides-for` deliberately covers every node ADR-083 also decides for
  (BP-048, BP-058, BP-005, BP-063), so no blueprint carries one without the
  other.

## Decision

**Decision 1 — a page delivered to any destination is created live there, in
one call, and there is no draft state ReachKit intends to leave behind.**
For BP-048 that is a single WordPress REST create carrying `status: 'publish'`
and the SEO fields and ADR-083's stamp in the same request. There is no
create-then-publish two-step. The one-call shape is not a performance choice: a
two-step opens a window in which a post exists in the customer's site as a
draft ReachKit meant to publish, and a crash inside that window re-creates by
accident exactly the population this ruling removes — while ADR-080's marker
search, which answers "is this the post for draft X?", would then have to also
answer "and did we finish?", which is the second job ADR-083 Decision 3 forbids
loading onto a mark.

BP-047's hosted edge already published live and is untouched (rule 2.2a).

**Decision 2 — the delivery records a real live address, and the adapter
contract splits one boolean into two.**

`WordPressDelivery` now carries `liveUrl: string` (the permalink the create
response returns), `madeLive: true`, and `remoteId` as before. BP-045 writes
`publications.live_url` and sets `verify_due_at` for WordPress rows exactly as
it does for hosted ones.

The adapter's single `servesPublicly` boolean was true for hosted and false for
WordPress, and carried two different facts that happened to agree at every
destination that existed. They no longer agree, so BP-015's `DestinationAdapter`
carries both:

```ts
export interface DestinationAdapter {
  readonly kind: 'hosted' | 'wordpress'

  /** The destination makes the page publicly readable at an address we can
   *  fetch. Governs whether `publications.live_url` is set, whether the page
   *  is verified at 24 hours (REQ-062 c1) and whether it receives a weekly
   *  verdict (REQ-063 c1). TRUE FOR BOTH KINDS since ADR-084. */
  readonly servesPublicly: boolean

  /** ReachKit runs this destination and can take the page off it. Governs
   *  REQ-056 c15's `removed` arm ("a destination ReachKit serves … it is
   *  removed from that destination") versus c16's `returned_to_draft`.
   *  TRUE FOR HOSTED ONLY. Never merge this with `servesPublicly`. */
  readonly hostedByUs: boolean
  // deliver, unpublish, health unchanged
}
```

Two things follow that cost nothing, and one that costs a rename.

- **REQ-062's verification and REQ-063's verdicts reach WordPress with no new
  destination rule.** Both paths were already written against the *address*
  rather than the *kind*: BP-049 decides its `never: 'no_live_address'`
  disposition from `live_url is null`, explicitly "not from `destination.kind`
  … without a second rule being written". A WordPress row that now carries a
  `live_url` becomes due for verification by the rule already there. The seam
  holds; what breaks is the assertions written against the old constant, not
  the shape.
- **REQ-056 criterion 15's `removed` arm stays hosted-only**, because it now
  reads `hostedByUs` rather than a boolean that also means "has a public
  address". Without the split, the obvious reading of `servesPublicly === true`
  at WordPress is "so we can remove it", and `unpublish` would reach for a
  delete call against a site ReachKit does not own — which REQ-060's non-goal
  forbids in terms and which no ReachKit test can observe.

**Decision 3 — a credential that cannot publish is a destination state, never a
silently drafted post.**
WordPress's default role map gives `edit_posts` to Contributor and
`publish_posts` only from Author upward, so an application password from a
Contributor-level user can create a post and have WordPress quietly hold it at
`draft` regardless of what the request asked for. Left unhandled, that is the
one route by which the owner's ruling could produce, unnoticed, precisely the
outcome it removes: a page ReachKit reports as published sitting invisible in a
drafts folder.

So BP-058 gains `canPublish(cfg)` beside ADR-083's `canStamp(cfg)`, probed at
connect and at each health check, and a destination that cannot publish reads as
**`failing`** — one of the three states REQ-074 criterion 1 already closes over,
with the Reconnect action criterion 2 already offers. No new destination state
and no change to REQ-074. A delivery attempted against such a credential is a
failed attempt whose reason needs the customer (REQ-056 criterion 4), so the
page comes to rest in `needs_attention` and no publication row is ever marked
`delivered` with a draft behind it.

*Chosen, not escalated (rule 1.1).* The alternative — deliver as a draft, tell
the customer, and carry on — is rejected in Alternatives 5 because it reinstates
the two populations, the two tellings and the two verification rules that ADR-081
and ADR-082 spent two files separating, to serve a case the ruling exists to
abolish. **Cost of reversing: one probe and one arm**, while no site is
connected; it becomes a question about live customer sites the moment one is.
The sentence the customer reads when their credential cannot publish is
owner-owed and is named in Open questions; this file mints none.

**Decision 4 — the unpublish arms invert, and not one of them is deleted.**

ADR-082's `UnpublishResult` union is **carried forward with all five arms and no
change of shape**, and its Decision 6 column `publications.unpublish_outcome`,
its `retryUnpublishWrite` entry point, its "no attempt cap, no expiry", and its
`already_gone` / `unreachable` separation all stand and are not re-argued
(rule 2.4). What changes is which arms have members:

| Arm | Population before | Population now |
|---|---|---|
| `removed` | hosted | hosted — unchanged |
| `returned_to_draft` | **empty** — REQ-060 c1 | **every WordPress unpublish that reaches the site** |
| `named_for_removal` | every WordPress unpublish | **empty** — nothing ReachKit creates there is left un-live |
| `already_gone` | WordPress, post deleted by the customer | unchanged |
| `unreachable` | WordPress, site not reached | unchanged |

`named_for_removal` is kept for the same reason ADR-082 kept `returned_to_draft`:
it is the arm REQ-056 criterion 16's second outcome promises, held open against a
page ReachKit created but did not make live. Its copy key,
`publish.wordpress.namedForRemoval`, is kept minted and unreached beside it — a
deleted key is how the arm becomes unrenderable and therefore deletable next.

`publications.made_live_by_us` is **retained and still written from
`DeliveryResult.madeLive` and from nothing else.** It is now `true` for every
WordPress row, which makes it look like a column with one value. It is not:
it is what keeps criterion 16's second outcome renderable at all, and the wrong
derivation has merely changed sign. ADR-081 alternative 4 rejected
`live_url != null` because it was null for every WordPress row and would have
classified every WordPress page as never-made-live; it is now non-null for every
WordPress row and would classify every WordPress page as made-live-by-us —
including any page a future draft-delivery option produced, whose post ReachKit
would then write into. Same substitution, opposite failure, both wrong.

**Decision 5 — ADR-083 stands in full; the stamp is more load-bearing under
this ruling, not less, and one sentence of its Decision 5 is corrected.**

The question this ruling raises against ADR-083 is fair: the `post_tag` stamp
exists because "posts sit unfindable in a drafts folder", and a live post has a
permalink ReachKit can hand the customer. The answer is that the stamp never
answered the per-page question. REQ-060 criterion 6 is a *bulk* promise —
"every page ReachKit created in that site comes up together in one list there,
without their having to open posts one at a time or already know a page's
title, and posts they wrote themselves are not in that list" — and a permalink
answers it one page at a time, which the criterion rules out in terms. Nothing
about a live post separates it from a post the customer wrote; the stamp is
still the only thing at `edit_posts` that does.

REQ-079 criterion 6 makes it *more* load-bearing. That mail exists because after
account deletion no ReachKit surface is left to read. Before this ruling the
posts it names were drafts: unfound meant invisible and harmless. Now they are
live pages published under the customer's own name on their own public domain,
and unfound means the customer cannot review, keep or remove content that is on
the web in their name with no product left to tell them where. **The stamp is
kept, unchanged in mechanism.**

The sentence of ADR-083 Decision 5 that is now false, verbatim:

> "ReachKit never makes a WordPress post live (REQ-060 criterion 1), so the term
> is private while the post is a draft — but the whole point of the delivery is
> that the customer publishes it, and from that moment the term ReachKit chose
> appears on their public site and in its tag archive."

In its place: **the term is public from the moment of delivery, and it is
ReachKit's act rather than the customer's that makes it so.** The escalation
ADR-083 Decision 5 draws from that sentence — the term's display name and
`WORDPRESS_STAMP_SLUG`'s value are customer-visible and are the owner's, not the
architect's — is unchanged in substance and stronger in ground: it was a
consequence the customer opted into by publishing, and is now one ReachKit
imposes. The pin does not cease to exist; it is owed sooner, and the tag archive
it produces on the customer's own domain (`/tag/<slug>/`) is a public page
ReachKit causes to exist there. Named as an accepted consequence, not decided
here.

ADR-083's `rests-on` rows, its Decision 4 (`stampApplied: false` is a delivered
page, not a failed one), and its Decision 2 constraint that the work-order
sequence is load-bearing all stand exactly as written.

## Accepted consequence, recorded once
Publishing into a customer's live site over an application password is a heavier
act than dropping a draft there: a wrong credential or a wrong page is
immediately public on their own domain, and the veto window (REQ-057) plus
unpublish (REQ-056 criteria 7 and 16) are the whole of the mitigation. This was
put to the owner before the ruling and the owner ruled with it in front of them.
It is recorded here as accepted and is not re-argued anywhere downstream
(rule 4.2).

## Alternatives considered
1. **Leave REQ-060 criterion 1 standing and treat the ruling as applying to the
   hosted blog only.** Rejected: the ruling says "This must be true for all
   CMSs, including Workpress and Reachkit CMS", and the hosted blog already
   published live, so that reading makes the ruling a no-op.
2. **Create as a draft, then publish in a second call.** Rejected under
   Decision 1: it opens a window whose crash outcome is the drafted post this
   ruling abolishes, and it forces ADR-080's marker to carry a second question.
3. **Make draft-versus-live a customer setting.** Rejected: "the goal of this is
   automation, the user should not care unless they want to supervise", and the
   supervision the ruling names is the veto window and unpublish, both of which
   already exist. A setting would also restore two populations at every one of
   the seven binding criteria above — the cost ADR-081 and ADR-082 exist to
   record.
4. **Keep `servesPublicly` as one boolean and read the unpublish arm from
   `kind === 'hosted'`.** Rejected: a `kind` test is the rule BP-049 deliberately
   avoided writing, and it re-adds a destination-kind switch every future
   destination must be threaded through. The split names the two facts instead.
5. **Where the credential cannot publish, deliver as a draft and tell the
   customer.** Rejected under Decision 3: it makes the abolished population
   reachable again by an accident of a role assignment, and every surface,
   telling and verification rule this ruling simplifies would have to keep both
   branches for it. Failing the destination is one arm; keeping the draft branch
   is seven criteria.
6. **Edit ADR-082 in place.** Rejected: rule 2.2b, `proposed → accepted →
   superseded`.
7. **Supersede ADR-083 as well.** Rejected under *Instrument*: its ruling
   survives entire and rule 2.2a forbids the restructuring; the premise
   correction is Decision 5 and the discoverability gap is closed by
   `decides-for`.
8. **Record this as a `## Decisions` section inside BP-048.** Rejected under
   rule 2.2 twice over: it settles the adapter contract in BP-015, two columns
   and an arm map in BP-045, the create call in BP-048, a probe in BP-058, a
   disposition in BP-049, verdict scope in BP-051, the telling in BP-046, the
   mail occasion in BP-053, the day panel in BP-039, two copy keys in BP-019,
   the deletion mail in BP-063 and a pin's escalation in BP-005 — twelve nodes,
   none of which owns the others. And it amends another decision, which is rule
   2.2's second file test on its own.

## Consequences

**Requirements sent back through the gate** (five moved to `in-review` in this
pass; REQ-060 and REQ-079 were already there). What each must now say
differently is the analyst's to write; this file names only what is false:

- **REQ-060** — criterion 1 inverts; criterion 2's "no live address", its
  waiting-in-WordPress line and its "sends the customer no mail of its own" all
  go; the user story ("land in my own site as drafts"), the rationale's
  transcription of `BUILD.md` §9, and the non-goals "Publishing a page live on
  WordPress on the customer's behalf" and "The 24-hour liveness verification,
  which needs a live address a WordPress draft does not have" are all false.
  Criterion 6 and its `rests-on` row stand. Criterion 3, 4 and 5 stand.
- **REQ-056** — criterion 6's second arm ("where the destination received the
  page for the customer to publish themselves, a written line saying so in place
  of an address") has no destination left to describe, since REQ-074's non-goal
  closes destinations at hosted and WordPress. Criterion 16 stays true and its
  second outcome becomes unreachable in practice; the criterion should not be
  narrowed to today's population (ADR-082's lesson, inverted). Criterion 1's ten
  states and criterion 2's fifteen transitions are **unchanged** — see
  *Already true* below.
- **REQ-057** — criterion 9 is about a destination "that receives it as a draft
  for the customer to publish themselves (REQ-060 criterion 1)" and now
  describes nobody; the telling must say the page will be published live at that
  destination. Criterion 7's zero-window mail already says "going live" and needs
  no change.
- **REQ-062** — the non-goal "Verifying a page delivered to a destination as a
  draft the customer must publish themselves — it has no live address (REQ-060)"
  is false; criterion 1's scope now includes WordPress pages, whose failures are
  facts about the customer's own site and are stated plainly under criterion 3.
- **REQ-063** — the non-goal "one delivered to a destination as a draft they
  never made live (REQ-060) — neither is live to be judged" is false; WordPress
  pages now receive weekly verdicts under criterion 1.
- **REQ-079** — criterion 4's two WordPress populations invert; criterion 6's
  mail counts posts that stood live until the action returned them to draft,
  which is a different sentence from counting posts that were never live.
- **REQ-043** — the ruling asserts a promise the approved text does not carry:
  that the calendar is where every page is found again with its status, a link
  to the customer's CMS and an unpublish action. Criterion 2 carries the status
  and criterion 9 permits the action; neither requires the day panel to be that
  place, and no requirement promises a per-page link into the customer's own
  WordPress as distinct from the page's public address.

**Journeys.** JN-003 step 6 reads "Finds the page live the next morning on their
own domain, **or waiting as a draft in their own WordPress** — or, where a check
still stood against it, finds it unpublished and still theirs to fix, with what
held it named". The second clause is now false. Journeys are the
requirements-analyst's directory and the owner's gate; this file does not touch
JN-003 and records that it is owed.

**Blueprints.** No blueprint is edited by this file. Every node below derives
from a requirement now back at `in-review`, and §3's gate — "No blueprint from a
non-approved requirement" — binds the edit, not the decision. What each owes:

- **BP-015** — `DestinationAdapter` gains `hostedByUs`; `servesPublicly` keeps
  its name and changes value at WordPress (Decision 2). The re-exported
  `UnpublishResult` is unchanged in shape.
- **BP-045** — `made_live_by_us` and `unpublish_outcome` retained unchanged;
  `live_url` and `verify_due_at` now written for WordPress rows; the `removed`
  versus `returned_to_draft` branch reads `hostedByUs`. No state, no transition,
  no guard changes.
- **BP-048** — one create call carrying `status: 'publish'`, returning
  `liveUrl` and `madeLive: true`; `liveUrl: undefined` and `madeLive: false` are
  no longer narrowed by type. `unpublish`'s ordinary arm becomes

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-09-01, minted at `accepted` rather than
`proposed`, on the precedent ADR-082 and ADR-083 set in this corpus. Rule 3.2
makes the status this agent's assertion, audited by the librarian afterwards;
rule 2.2b's ADR grammar is `proposed → accepted → superseded`; rule 3.4 states
that `/decide` produces no draft the owner is asked to approve and takes no
second review round.

**Grounds.** The behaviour is the owner's own ruling of 2026-09-01, quoted in
Context; the mechanism (one call, the boolean split, the capability probe, the
arm map) is parameters and boundaries, which rule 1.1 places with this agent;
the parts that are not parameters — every sentence the customer reads — are
escalated in Open questions rather than chosen. ADR-082 is set to `superseded`
in the same pass, because leaving it `accepted` while its warning block points a
future reader at the wrong arm is the authored index that lies (rule 5.1).

**What is not a ground, and why it does not block.** Seven requirements this
file binds are `in-review` pending the owner's signature — five of them moved
there by this pass. That is the requirements gate; §3 makes decisions derived
and self-certified, and the gate that binds a *blueprint* to an approved
requirement is a separate question, answered in each blueprint's own status
grounds and deliberately not pre-empted here. Five `rests-on` rows above are
`open`: this repository has never spoken to a WordPress install and there is
nothing in it to discharge them with. If the owner's approval changes what any
of the seven criteria promise, that reaches this file as a further superseding
ADR — never as an edit here, and never as the deletion of an arm.
