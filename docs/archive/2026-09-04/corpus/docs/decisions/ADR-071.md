# ADR-071 — LANDMINE — four ways a page has no ordinary verdict, and why none of them may be merged

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-01 by ADR-072 — read that file, not this one.** Decisions
> 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 below are carried forward there **unchanged**, and the merge
> argument in *Context* stands verbatim; ADR-072 does not repeat it. What is
> wrong below is **Decision 5 and the "Does it lift?" column of the table in
> Context**: the owner ruled on 2026-09-01 that ReachKit never looks at a
> published page again after its 24-hour check, REQ-063 criterion 7's resumption
> path was withdrawn outright, and criterion 6 now reads "A page marked no longer
> judgeable stays so, and nothing restores it." **All four causes are terminal,
> not one**, and a fifth cause (`page_not_found`, from REQ-062 criterion 4) has
> joined them. A reader following Decision 5 would build the resumption the
> requirement forbids, which is why this file is superseded rather than
> annotated.

- Date: 2026-08-31

> **Read this before collapsing two verdict states that render alike, before
> giving `not_measured` a stored row, and before making `not_judgeable` a single
> reversible flag.** Every one of those is a tidy-up. Every one of them makes the
> product tell a customer something false about a page they paid for.

## Context
REQ-063 puts a page in one of four positions in any given week, and three of them
say some version of "we cannot tell you". They are computed by BP-051, worded by
BP-019, and mailed by BP-016 — no one of which owns all three.

| Standing | Where it comes from | What it means | Does it lift? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `verdict` — working / too early / not working | REQ-063 c1, c2 | judged this week against the recorded test | n/a |
| `no_week` | REQ-063 c5 first half, on REQ-065 c3 | **the week itself** was not measured, for anyone; no verdict is produced for any page | yes, when a measurement for that week completes |
| `not_measured` | REQ-063 c5 second half, on REQ-065 c4 | the week ran but this page's test could not be evaluated **from what that week measured** | yes, next week, with nothing else changing |
| `not_judgeable` | REQ-063 c6 | the recorded test can no longer be evaluated **at all** — search untracked, page unpublished, question left the set, or the domain changed | three of the four causes lift; `domain_changed` never does |

And `not_judgeable` has a fifth distinction inside it: `lastJudgedWeek` is a date
where the page was judged before, and **null** where it never was — c6 requires
the customer to be told "that it was never judged", which is a different sentence
from a date.

**Why merging reads as right.** On a screen these render as three grey rows that
all say some form of "no verdict this week". In a mail they are three lines that
sound alike. In an enum they are three members with no data of their own except
`not_judgeable`'s cause. A reviewer looking at

```
'working' | 'too_early' | 'not_working' | 'not_measured' | 'not_judgeable'
```

will see two members that mean "unknown" and propose one. It is a smaller enum,
a shorter switch, one copy string instead of three.

**Why merging is the failure.** REQ-063 c6 does not merely label a page; it
**stops** it: "no working, too early or not working verdict is produced for it
that week **or afterwards** while that holds". A page that missed one week for a
transient reason (`not_measured`) and is written down as `not_judgeable` is
thereby retired from judgement — permanently in the `domain_changed` reading, and
for as long as nobody notices in the others. The customer keeps publishing, keeps
paying, and their pages stop being judged. Nothing fails. The screen looks
correct: it says what it always said.

The reverse merge fails too. Rendering `not_judgeable` as `not_measured` promises
a verdict next week that will never come, and c6 requires the customer to be told
*which* of the four things happened and *when* the page was last judged — a
sentence a transient state has no data to fill.

And `no_week` is not a property of the page at all. It is a property of the
week, stated once for the whole site (REQ-065 c3's one written line), not once
per page. Materialising it per page produces N rows saying a thing that is true
once, and the first customer to publish twenty pages reads twenty copies of it.

## Decision

1. **Four shapes, not four enum members.** BP-051's `WeekStanding` is a
   discriminated union — `verdict`, `not_judgeable`, `not_measured`, `no_week` —
   and each carries exactly the data its own sentence needs and no more. A
   consumer that wants to render "no verdict" must destructure, so the three
   cases are unavoidable at every call site rather than optional at one.
2. **`not_measured` and `no_week` are represented by the absence of a row.**
   `page_verdicts` holds only judged and not-judgeable weeks. A transient miss
   therefore cannot be written down as a permanent state, because there is
   nowhere to write it.
3. **`no_week` is derived once per site per week**, from the REQ-065 node's own
   statement that the week was not measured, and is stated once. BP-051 returns
   it per page for uniformity of type; BP-019 and BP-016 must render it once per
   week, never once per page.
4. **`not_judgeable` always carries its cause and always carries
   `lastJudgedWeek`, including the `null` that means never judged.** Neither
   field is optional in the type.
5. **`domain_changed` is terminal; the other three are conditional.** Once a
   `domain_changed` row exists for a page, every later week is `not_judgeable`
   for that page (REQ-071 c14). `search_untracked`, `unpublished` and
   `question_left_set` lift the moment their condition lifts, and the page is
   judged again from that week with no intervention.
6. **The three "cannot tell you" standings render as three different written
   lines** in BP-019's copy registry and in BP-016's weekly mail. They are the
   owner's words and they are outstanding; until they exist, no surface renders
   any of the three.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Record this inside BP-051's `## Decisions`** — rejected under rule 2.2's
   file test *and* under the landmine rule. The merge that breaks it is most
   likely to be proposed by whoever is writing the rendering (BP-019) or the mail
   (BP-016), looking at three grey rows and one copy key; a decision buried in
   the engine node is invisible from there. The subject spans three nodes and
   depends on a fourth (REQ-065's).
2. **One `unknown` state with a reason enum spanning all six causes** — rejected.
   It makes the permanent and the transient the same type, so the code that stops
   judging a page and the code that skips it for one week are one code path, and
   the difference survives only in a string. That is the merge this file exists
   to prevent, dressed as a design.
3. **Store a row for `not_measured` so the series is complete** — rejected. A
   complete series is not a promise anything makes; c5 says only that no earlier
   week's verdict is shown as this week's, which reading by `week_start` already
   gives. The rows would be indistinguishable in shape from `not_judgeable` rows
   and would make decision 2's safety property unavailable.
4. **Make `not_judgeable` a boolean flag on the publication, cleared when the
   condition lifts** — rejected. It has no week, so c6's "the date of the last
   verdict it did receive" has no source, and clearing a flag loses the record
   that the page was unjudgeable at all — which is exactly what a customer asking
   "why did this page stop being judged" needs.

## Consequences
- Three copy keys and three mail lines exist where a reviewer will see one
  concept. That redundancy is the decision, not an oversight.
- `page_verdicts` is sparse: a page has rows only for weeks in which something
  was decided about it. Any consumer computing a streak or a count must read the
  week, not the row count.
- A page that becomes judgeable again resumes with no migration and no backfill,
  because nothing recorded its absence.
- **Cost of reversing:** trivial in code — one enum, one switch arm, one copy
  key. The cost in effect is that a page can be permanently retired from
  judgement by a single missed measurement, silently, with every screen still
  rendering correctly. It is precisely the reversal that looks like a cleanup and
  breaks what REQ-063 promises, which is why it is a file.
