# ADR-001 — One band-label registry owns all six band words, and their disjointness is asserted once

- Date: 2026-08-31

## Context
Two approved requirements each define a three-way band, and one of them
constrains the other.

REQ-047 criterion 10 bands a **target's winnability** — internal handles
`winnable`, `reach`, `not-yet` — from the ranked counts in its top ten.
REQ-096 criterion 2 bands a **rival's size** — internal handles `near`,
`middle`, `far` — from that rival's own ranked count. REQ-096 criterion 9 then
says: each of the three renders as its own distinct term, the same term on every
surface, "and no term rendered for a rival's band is a term rendered for a
target's winnability band … so no single word names both how big a rival is and
how winnable a target is."

That last clause is a property of the **union** of two sets. Neither requirement
owns the union: REQ-047 does not know REQ-096's words exist, and REQ-096's
non-goal explicitly declines to change REQ-047's ("criterion 9 asks only that a
rival's three share none of them"). The two bands are also computed by two
different components — winnability by BP-013, rival size by BP-011 — and rendered
by a third, BP-019, while the counts behind them are pins in BP-005.

Left alone, the disjointness check is implemented in whichever surface a
developer happens to notice it in, or in neither, and drifts the first time
either set gains, loses or reworders a term. It is not a rendering detail: a
customer reading "Reach" against a rival and "Reach" against a target in the same
session is being told two different things by one word, which is the specific harm
REQ-096 criterion 9 was written to prevent.

## Decision
**BP-019 owns a single band-label registry, `BAND_LABELS`, declaring both sets in
one place; `tests/pins.test.ts` (BP-005) asserts the disjointness once.**

Concretely:

1. BP-011 and BP-013 produce **internal handles only** — `near | middle | far`
   and `winnable | reach | not-yet`. Neither engine knows a customer-facing word.
2. BP-019's `BAND_LABELS` maps both handle sets to `CopyKey`s in the copy
   registry. The words are customer-visible strings and therefore the owner's
   (constitution §1's rights table); the registry is where the owner's words
   land, not where the system chooses them.
3. `tests/pins.test.ts` asserts, over `BAND_LABELS`: the winnability set has three
   distinct terms, the rival-size set has three distinct terms, and the
   intersection of the two rendered sets is empty. One assertion, one failure
   message, one place to fix.
4. Adding a fourth term to either set — the case that would silently break the
   property — fails that assertion the moment it is added, because the assertion
   is over the sets rather than over six named strings.

## Alternatives considered
1. **A `## Decisions` entry inside BP-019** — rejected because the subject is not
   BP-019's alone. The handles are BP-011's and BP-013's, the assertion runs in
   BP-005's test file, and a reader arriving at BP-011 to add a fourth rival band
   would find nothing telling them a constraint exists. Rule 2.2's file test is
   exactly "spans nodes no single blueprint owns", and this spans four.
2. **Each engine owns its own words and a shared test compares them** — rejected:
   two owners for one vocabulary, and the comparison test has no home either. It
   also puts customer-visible strings inside engine modules, where the design
   system's no-default-strings rule (BP-018) cannot reach them.
3. **Enforce disjointness by construction — derive one set's words from the
   other's complement** — rejected as cleverness with a customer-visible
   consequence: the words are the owner's, and a derivation would let a change to
   one set silently reword the other.
4. **Do nothing; rely on review** — rejected. The property is invisible in either
   requirement read alone, and REQ-096's own non-goal is the evidence: it
   deliberately declines to constrain REQ-047, which means nobody reading REQ-047
   will ever learn the constraint exists.

## Consequences
- Six strings and their two boundary sets move together; a band rename touches
  the copy registry and nothing else.
- The pins test gains one assertion whose failure message names the two sets and
  the offending term, so the fix is obvious to whoever broke it.
- BP-011 and BP-013 stay free of customer-visible copy, which keeps REQ-093's
  "every sentence is a written string" enforceable in one place.
- Cost of reversing: low in code (move six strings), high in effect — reversing
  it re-opens the exact drift the decision exists to prevent, and the drift is
  silent, since nothing fails when two sets happen to share a word.
