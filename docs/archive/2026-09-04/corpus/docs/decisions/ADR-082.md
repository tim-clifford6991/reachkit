# ADR-082 — Unpublish is scoped by liveness, and states which of five things happened — the four outcomes REQ-056 criterion 16 now names, plus the hosted removal

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-01 by ADR-084 — read that file, not this one.** The
> ruling (scope by liveness) and the five-arm `UnpublishResult`, the
> `unpublish_outcome` column and `retryUnpublishWrite` are all carried forward
> there unchanged. What is wrong below is the **populations**: the owner ruled on
> 2026-09-01 that content is published complete at every destination, including
> the customer's own WordPress, so REQ-060 criterion 1 no longer stands. The
> warning block that follows tells a future reader to protect
> `returned_to_draft` as the empty arm. **That is now the ordinary WordPress
> outcome, and `named_for_removal` is the empty one** — the warning points at the
> wrong branch, which is why this file is superseded rather than annotated.
> Nothing below is edited: an `accepted` decision is amended by supersession
> (rule 2.2b), and the body is left as the record of what was ruled and why.

> **Read this before deleting the `returned_to_draft` arm or the
> `made_live_by_us` column as dead code.** With REQ-060 criterion 1 standing —
> a page published to a customer's connected WordPress "appears in their
> WordPress site as a draft and is never made live by us" — that arm is
> **unreachable for WordPress today**, and no WordPress unpublish takes it. It
> is not dead code; it is the branch REQ-079 criterion 4 promises, held open
> against the day the product gains the ability to publish into a customer's
> WordPress live. Removing it looks like a cleanup with a passing test suite,
> and the day that ability arrives the promise silently reverts to "touch
> nothing". See Consequences.

- Date: 2026-09-01
- **Supersedes ADR-081**, whose ruling this file carries forward. Two things
  made an amendment necessary rather than an edit, and rule 2.2b makes
  supersession the only instrument for an `accepted` ADR:
  1. ADR-081 presents *"a WordPress post is never made live by us"* as a
     quotation of REQ-060 criterion 1, in its warning block and again in
     Consequences. **It is not that criterion's text.** Verbatim, criterion 1
     reads: "Given a customer connects their WordPress site with an application
     password, when a page is published to that destination, then it appears in
     their WordPress site as a draft and is never made live by us." The sense
     survives the paraphrase; §8's rule does not care ("a paraphrase presented
     as a quotation is a defect"), and it sat in the one paragraph a future
     reader is told to trust before deleting a branch.
  2. REQ-056 criterion 16 has since gained a **fourth** outcome and an
     owner-ruled action, and its third outcome has become distinguishable from
     its second. ADR-081 Decision 3 declined a fourth union arm on a ground that
     no longer holds. See Decision 3 below.
- **Binds** REQ-056 criterion 16 and REQ-079 criteria 4 and 6.

## Context
ADR-081 settled a genuine conflict: two approved requirements described what
ReachKit does inside a customer's own WordPress when a page stops being one of
their live pages, and they described it differently. That settlement — *scope by
liveness*, two populations, each criterion true of its own — is **unchanged and
is not re-argued here** (rule 2.4). ADR-081 remains the record of the conflict,
of the five readings it rejected, and of the evidence for the stored
discriminator; its Context quotes REQ-056 criterion 16 and REQ-079 criterion 4
as they read on 2026-08-31, which is the day the file is dated and is no longer
the text of either criterion.

What has changed since is not the ruling but the shape of what the customer is
told. REQ-056 criterion 16 was rewritten whole in the round-two fold of
2026-09-01
(`requirements/history/REQ-056-057-060-079-2026-09-01-review-round-two-folded.md`).
It now names **four** outcomes of unpublishing a page delivered to a customer's
own WordPress, each with its own written line on that page's own record:

1. ReachKit made it live there and it now stands as a draft again;
2. ReachKit never made it live, "so nothing was written and removing the post is
   theirs to do";
3. "the post is no longer in their site, so there was nothing to return and
   nothing is theirs to remove";
4. "their site could not be reached, so the post may still be live there and
   nothing was written" — and in this outcome alone the record carries an action
   to ask for the return to draft again, "offered for as long as the page
   exists".

ADR-081 Decision 3 gave the union three arms and mapped two of those four onto
one of them.

## Decision

**Decisions 1, 2, 4 and 5 of ADR-081 are carried forward without change**: scope
by liveness; the discriminator is `publications.made_live_by_us`, never a re-read
of the customer's site; the never-live path writes nothing at all; the was-live
path writes exactly one field and is idempotent. Their reasoning is ADR-081's and
is not restated (rule 2.4). Only ADR-081's Decision 3, and the misquotation
above, are amended.

**Decision 3 (amended) — `UnpublishResult` states which of five things happened,
and the fourth WordPress outcome is a success, not a failure.**

```ts
export type UnpublishResult =
  | { ok: true;  outcome: 'removed' }             // hosted — REQ-056 c15, 410 at BP-047
  | { ok: true;  outcome: 'returned_to_draft' }   // WordPress, made live by us — c16 outcome 1
  | { ok: true;  outcome: 'named_for_removal' }   // WordPress, never made live, post still there — c16 outcome 2
  | { ok: true;  outcome: 'already_gone' }        // WordPress, post no longer in their site — c16 outcome 3
  | { ok: true;  outcome: 'unreachable'; retryOffered: true }
                                                  // WordPress, site not reached, nothing written — c16 outcome 4
  | { ok: false; reason: FailureReason }
```

Three things follow, and each is a promise the union now carries that a boolean
or a three-arm union could not.

**(a) `unreachable` is `ok: true`.** Criterion 16's opening binds all four
outcomes alike: on the customer's choosing to unpublish, "the product stops
treating it as one of their live pages". The unreachable case is one of the four
things that *did* happen, not a failure of the action — the page reaches
`unpublished`, the record carries its line, and the customer is not asked to try
the unpublish again. `ok: false` would mean the page is still one of their live
pages, which is the opposite of what the criterion promises.

**(b) `already_gone` is its own arm, and ADR-081 Decision 5's mapping of it onto
`named_for_removal` is withdrawn.** ADR-081 Decision 5 said "a post the customer
has since deleted returns `named_for_removal` rather than failing, because there
is nothing left to return and nothing was lost". That was correct about the
*behaviour* — nothing is written either way — and wrong about the *telling*, and
the telling is what criterion 16 promises. The two lines say opposite things
about what the customer must now do: outcome 2 says "removing the post is theirs
to do", outcome 3 says "nothing is theirs to remove". One arm cannot render both,
and a customer told to go and delete a post that is not there is sent looking for
something that does not exist. The behaviour ADR-081 fixed is unchanged: neither
arm writes anything, and `already_gone` is still not a failure.

**(c) The retry is a call, not a transition, and it does not reuse `unpublish`.**
Criterion 16's fourth outcome carries "an action to ask for the return to draft
again, offered for as long as the page exists; asking leaves the page's state
unchanged (criterion 3), and when an ask succeeds the record shows the first
outcome in its place". `unpublished` is terminal (criterion 3) and the page is
already in it, so no edge exists for a second `unpublish()` to take and calling
it again would either be refused by the state machine or would have to be given
one. BP-045 gains a second entry point instead:

```ts
/** REQ-056 c16 outcome 4's action. Retries only the write into the customer's
 *  site; takes no transition and reads no guard, because the page is already in
 *  the terminal `unpublished` state and criterion 3 must stay true of it.
 *  Refuses unless the last recorded outcome for this publication is
 *  'unreachable' — the criterion offers the action in that outcome alone. */
export function retryUnpublishWrite(a: { draftId: string; by: Actor }): Promise<
  | UnpublishResult
  | { ok: false; reason: 'not_offered' }
>
```

No attempt cap and no expiry: the criterion says "offered for as long as the page
exists", and REQ-056 criterion 4's three-attempt ceiling is a rule about
*publish* attempts, not about this. A customer-initiated write into a site we do
not own has a human deciding whether to press it, which is the bound that makes
an automatic ceiling unnecessary.

**Decision 6 — the outcome is stored, because it cannot be derived.**
`publications` gains `unpublish_outcome text null`, on BP-045's `publications`
topic. `made_live_by_us` and `unpublished_at` together cannot separate outcomes
2, 3 and 4: those are facts about what the last call *found* at a destination we
do not own, and nothing already stored records them. The column is what the
record's written line is rendered from and what `retryUnpublishWrite` reads to
decide whether the action is offered at all. A successful retry overwrites it
with `returned_to_draft`, which is criterion 16's "when an ask succeeds the
record shows the first outcome in its place" — one field, one home.

This adds a column; it deletes nothing. **ADR-080 is untouched.** Its Decision 2
("a publication row is never deleted to reflect an outcome … outcomes are
recorded by updating `delivery_state`, `failure_reason` and `unpublished_at`")
names the fields that existed when it was written; recording a fourth outcome
field is the shape that decision prescribes, not an exception to it. Its
Decision 5 — the marker on the post is never removed — holds in every one of the
five arms, including `already_gone`, where there is no post left to hold it.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Edit ADR-081 in place.** Rejected: it is `accepted`, and rule 2.2b's ADR
   grammar is `proposed → accepted → superseded`. ADR-081's own Status grounds
   name supersession as the instrument for exactly this and its warning block
   exists to make the transition visible.
2. **Keep three arms and carry the fourth outcome as a `FailureReason`.**
   Rejected on (a) above: a `FailureReason` means the action did not happen, and
   criterion 16 says it did. It would also put the unreachable case on the same
   footing as `credentials_expired`, which brings the page to rest needing the
   customer with no unpublish recorded at all.
3. **Reuse ADR-081's ground: the unreachable case is REQ-079 criterion 4's
   second clause and BP-063's `stillLive` list.** Rejected, and this is the
   ground that has failed. `stillLive` is a field on `UnpublishAllResult` — it
   exists on the *unpublish-everything* path, and REQ-079 criterion 4's clause
   ("the pages at any destination that could not be reached are listed as still
   live with one written line saying so") is about that action. REQ-056
   criterion 16 is one customer unpublishing one page from that page's own
   record; there is no list there, no second page to list beside it, and no
   `UnpublishAllResult` in the call at all. The two are the same *fact* on two
   different surfaces, which is why criterion 16 as folded adds "Where another
   requirement puts the same naming on another ReachKit surface, it names the
   same outcome as the line on this record and never a different one" — a
   sentence that only makes sense if the single-page path can reach the outcome
   in the first place.
4. **Derive the outcome at render time by re-reading the customer's site.**
   Rejected for the reason ADR-081 rejected it for the liveness discriminator,
   and again here: the line would change under the customer without anything
   having happened, and a customer who deleted the post themselves after reading
   "removing the post is theirs to do" would find the line silently rewritten as
   though ReachKit had acted.
5. **Offer the retry as a `unpublished → unpublished` self-edge on the state
   machine.** Rejected: it makes `unpublished` non-terminal in the transition
   table, which is the table REQ-056 criterion 2 enumerates and criterion 3 rests
   on, and a sixteenth edge is a change to the requirement (BP-045 decision 1).
   The retry touches the destination and the outcome column, never the state.
6. **Record this as a `## Decisions` entry inside BP-045.** Rejected under rule
   2.2's file test, on the same grounds ADR-081 was: it settles one column and
   one return union in BP-045, two arms and an error mapping in BP-048, the
   adapter contract in BP-015, and the relationship between the per-page outcome
   and BP-063's `stillLive`. Four nodes, none of which owns the others. It also
   amends another decision, which is rule 2.2's second file test on its own.

## Consequences
- **BP-045** gains two union arms, one column (`unpublish_outcome`) and one
  exported function (`retryUnpublishWrite`). No state, no transition and no guard
  changes — the same thing ADR-081 was able to say, and the reason this ruling
  is cheap.
- **BP-048** gains two arms in its `unpublish` and one distinction in
  `errors.ts`: a 404 for a post id we hold is `already_gone`; a DNS failure,
  connection reset, timeout or 5xx at the site is `unreachable`. That distinction
  is the third `rests-on` above and is **not verified in this corpus** — if it is
  refuted, the two arms collapse and one of criterion 16's outcomes becomes
  unreachable, which is a change to the requirement and not a workaround here.
- **BP-063** keeps `UnpublishAllResult.stillLive` unchanged. It is now the
  projection over the per-page `unreachable` outcomes rather than an independent
  computation, and BP-063 must read it that way rather than deciding
  reachability a second time (rule 2.4). REQ-079 criterion 6's mail counts the
  posts left in the customer's WordPress: an `already_gone` post is not one of
  them and is not counted, because there is nothing there to count.
- **BP-015**'s adapter contract re-export is unchanged in shape; the union it
  re-exports has five arms instead of three.
- **Two further written lines are now owed to the owner**, on top of the two
  ADR-081 already named: the `already_gone` line, the `unreachable` line, and the
  label of the retry action itself. They are copy keys under BP-019/BP-020 and
  their strings are the owner's; this file mints no sentence.
- **The `returned_to_draft` arm is still empty for WordPress**, for the reason
  ADR-081 gave and this file's warning block repeats. Nothing about the two new
  arms changes that: they are both never-made-live outcomes.
- **Cost of reversing:** low in code and high in meaning, in the same asymmetry
  ADR-081 recorded. Collapsing `already_gone` back into `named_for_removal` is
  one deleted arm and one deleted line, and it reads as a simplification —
  "these both mean we wrote nothing" — while sending customers to delete posts
  that are not there. Collapsing `unreachable` into `ok: false` is one deleted
  arm and it silently withdraws the retry action the owner ruled for.

## Status grounds (rule 3.2)
Self-certified **`accepted`** on 2026-09-01, minted at `accepted` rather than
`proposed`. Rule 3.2 makes the status this agent's assertion, audited by the
librarian afterwards; rule 2.2b's ADR grammar is `proposed → accepted →
superseded`.

**Grounds.** This file corrects a §8 defect in an `accepted` decision the corpus
has already built on — nine work orders name `UnpublishResult` or the adapter it
sits on — and it does so by the only instrument rule 2.2b provides. Leaving
ADR-081 `accepted` while its Decision 3 is refuted by REQ-056 criterion 16's
current text is the authored index that lies (rule 5.1): it says decided where
the requirement says otherwise. ADR-081 is set to `superseded` in the same pass.

**What is not a ground, and why it does not block.** REQ-056 and REQ-060 are both
`status: in-review` pending the owner's signature. That is the requirements
gate; §3 makes decisions "derived and self-certified by their authoring agent",
and rule 3.4 says in terms that `/decide` "produce[s] no such draft" and takes no
round-two gate. The gate that binds a *blueprint* to an approved requirement is
unaffected. If the owner's approval changes criterion 16's four outcomes rather
than ratifying them, that reaches this file as a further superseding ADR — never
as an edit here, and never as a deletion of an arm.
