# ADR-021 — LANDMINE — the spend ceiling outranks the verdict: a cut-off factor nulls the score, and no partial score is ever computed

- Date: 2026-08-31

> **Read this before raising a cap "just to finish the last call", before
> computing a score from the factors that were measured, and before treating a
> `—` score on a scan that spent 6¢ of a 12¢ budget as a bug.** All three read
> like obvious fixes. Each of them breaks a promise a customer can be shown.

## Context
A free scan carries two hard bounds: 90 seconds (REQ-003 criterion 5) and
`CAP_FREE`, 12¢ (`BUILD.md` §6.1, REQ-003 criterion 11). It also carries a
verdict at the top of the report: one score, one band, three factors (REQ-004).

The two collide in one specific way. The last stage of the free battery is
twelve live organic SERPs (`BUILD.md` §6.3), and those SERPs are the only source
of `top10share` and of the whole AI-Overview matrix — so they feed **Presence**,
one of the three factors the score is composed of. If the ceiling is reached
part-way through that stage, the scan has read the domain, measured Foundations
and Answerability, and has no Presence. The score is then `—`.

**Why that reads as wrong.**

1. The expected free-scan spend is about 6.3¢ against a 12¢ cap (`BUILD.md`
   §6.3): the first person to meet this in production sees a report with no
   score and roughly half the budget apparently unspent, and concludes the cap
   check is miscounting.
2. The remaining work is cheap. Finishing the twelve SERPs costs 0.2¢ each
   (`BUILD.md` §6.1). "Let it run 0.4¢ over so the customer gets a score" is a
   two-character change and looks like kindness.
3. Two of the three factors are in hand. `∛(F × A × P)` with a known F and A and
   an unknown P invites an average, a substitution, a "provisional" score, or a
   band derived from the two measured factors "with a caveat". Each is one line.
4. The visitor waited up to 90 seconds and the thing they came for is the number.
   Showing a dash after a minute feels like the product failing rather than the
   product being honest.

**Why it is load-bearing.**

- REQ-003 criterion 11 forecloses the first two, in terms that name this exact
  case: "the ceiling is never exceeded to finish any outstanding measurement,
  **whether or not a driver of the score depends on it**". REQ-003's rationale
  states why in one line: "the money is a hard bound and the verdict is not." A
  cap that yields to a good reason is not a cap; it is a default, and the
  reasons to exceed it are unbounded because every scan has a last call.
- REQ-004 criteria 3 and 9 foreclose the third and fourth, also in terms that
  name this case: where a factor "was among the work cut off — including a driver
  whose measurement was still outstanding when the spend ceiling was reached —
  the score itself is shown as '—' … never computed, banded or estimated from the
  drivers that were measured. No band is stated, implied, or guessed."
- The harm is not aesthetic. A free report is a public verdict about somebody
  else's business, published without their asking (ADR-002), permanently
  addressable and shareable. A score computed from two of three factors would
  band a real, named company — plausibly as *Invisible* — on a measurement that
  was never taken. REQ-004's rationale calls that what it is: "rendering an
  outage as 0 invents a customer fact and makes a false public accusation about
  someone's site." Rule 1.2 forbids inventing a measurement; a partial score is
  an invented measurement wearing a caveat.
- The cost side is not theoretical either. `BUILD.md` §0's fourth law is lean
  data, and §6.5 fixes the mechanism: "Caps degrade (skip remaining optional
  work, mark scan `degraded`), never throw." At €49/month against ~$2.71 of
  monthly cost, the free funnel is the one uncapped surface a stranger can point
  at any number of domains. The cap is what makes a public, accountless,
  one-field scan safe to offer at all.

Neither prohibition lives in one blueprint. BP-007 holds `capHit()` and the
degrade-never-throw rule; BP-023 holds the deadline and the `Ending`; BP-024
holds `computeScore` and the partition. The "fix" would most naturally be
applied in BP-024, by someone who has never read BP-023, or in BP-023, by
someone who has never read BP-024.

## Decision
1. **The ceiling is absolute.** `capHit()` and the 90-second deadline are
   re-checked between stages and between calls inside any multi-call stage. When
   either fires, all remaining work is skipped. No call is made "to finish", no
   cap is raised at runtime, and there is no override parameter, environment
   variable or per-scan exemption anywhere in the codebase.
2. **Skipped work is `unmeasured / not_attempted`**, never absent and never 0
   (REQ-004 criterion 9). The `Measured<T>` arm carries the reason, so the
   written line the visitor reads is the "we stopped early" one and not the
   "we could not determine it" one.
3. **`computeScore` returns `unmeasured` if any of the three factors is
   `unmeasured`**, and it takes no flag that would let a caller ask for a partial
   result. `ScoreFactors` has no arm that could hold a substituted value.
4. **A ceiling always produces a stored report** (REQ-003 criterion 11: "never an
   error, a hang, or a refusal"). The report says it stopped early, names what
   was not measured, and — because it is incomplete — carries the one re-scan
   control REQ-001 criterion 14 offers, at any age. That control is the answer to
   the visitor who wanted a score: they may have one for the cost of a second
   scan, at a moment when the daily budget is not exhausted.
5. **The three prohibitions are asserted as tests, not as review comments**: a
   scan whose cost context is at the cap makes no further vendor call; a
   `ScoreFactors` with one `unmeasured` factor yields an `unmeasured` score for
   every combination of the other two; and a ceiling-stopped scan always yields
   `Ending.kind === 'report'`.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Allow a bounded overrun — say 20% — to finish outstanding score work** —
   rejected. REQ-003 criterion 11 forbids it in terms that name the score
   dependency explicitly, and an overrun budget is a cap with a second cap behind
   it, which is the shape that has no last member.
2. **Order the pipeline so the score's inputs are always bought first** — not
   rejected on the merits, and partly already true: the six stages run own
   fetches before vendor calls. But it cannot discharge the case, because the
   twelve SERPs are both the largest spend and a score input, so any ceiling
   reached at all is reached inside score work. Ordering reduces the frequency;
   it does not change what the report must say when it happens.
3. **Compute a partial score from the measured factors and label it provisional**
   — rejected. REQ-004 criterion 3 forbids a band "stated, implied, or guessed
   from the drivers that were measured", and a labelled number is still the
   number people quote. This is the specific fix this file exists to stop.
4. **Fail the scan instead, so no half-report exists** — rejected. REQ-003
   criterion 11 requires the visitor be shown everything measured up to that
   point, and REQ-004 criterion 11 requires a partially failed scan to render
   every part that was measured. Throwing away a minute of the visitor's wait and
   the money already spent is worse on every axis.
5. **Record this inside BP-024's `## Decisions`** — rejected under rule 2.2's
   file test and the blueprint-writing skill's landmine rule. The mechanism spans
   BP-007 (the cap), BP-023 (the deadline and the ending) and BP-024 (the score),
   no one of which owns it, and it is precisely a decision that "reads as wrong
   but is load-bearing", where the reversal would look like a cleanup.

## Consequences
- Some free reports carry no score. That is the designed outcome, and REQ-001
  criterion 14's once-at-any-age re-scan control is the compensating affordance —
  it exists in the corpus for this case.
- Three tests, in three modules, are the enforcement. If one is deleted the
  property is gone, so each names this ADR in its failure message.
- The free path's cost is bounded per scan by construction, which is what makes
  an accountless public scan offerable. Any future work that widens the free
  battery must fit under 12¢ or change the pin — never the check.
- **Cost of reversing:** trivial in code — one comparison, one flag, one
  substitution — and that cheapness is the danger. The true cost is a
  requirement change to REQ-003 and REQ-004, both approved, plus every free
  report already published carrying a band that was never measured. Anyone who
  finds a `—` score over an unspent budget and thinks "this is a bug" should
  read this file first, which is the entire reason it is a file.
