# REQ-095 — Supply runs a month deep, is topped up every week, and a customer whose supply is short is told when we know it — not when the calendar empties

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer buying one page a day, I want the product to go looking for a
month of pages at the start and to keep topping that up every week, and to tell me
itself the moment it holds fewer than it needs, so that I learn how much work is
coming from ReachKit rather than from a calendar that has quietly gone empty.

## Rationale
The subscription is sold as one page a day, and every promise around it until now
has bound only what happens *while supply lasts* — so a customer could be told the
truth on every screen and still receive three pages in a month. What must be true
is that the product pursues a month of pages at onboarding, replenishes weekly, and
says so at the moment it knows it holds less, rather than leaving the customer to
discover it weeks later from a date that renders empty. Depth is a disclosure, never
a gate: `BUILD.md` §4.3 states verbatim "A degraded pass still releases setup (zero
proposals is legal, never faked)", so a shortfall is told at arrival and never
delays or conditions entry (REQ-029 criteria 3 and 5). An exhausted queue keeps its
honest wording on the day (REQ-043 criterion 3) and is nonetheless a state the
product must raise and keep raising, because a customer paying while nothing is
written for them is being failed accurately. The month's supply the pass pursues and
the count below which the customer is told are floors the system chose (rule 1.1);
their derivation, reversal cost and the milestone at which they can first be
measured are in `registry/evidence/REQ-095.md`. No expected opportunity yield is
derivable from the pinned battery, so what is promised to the customer here is the
telling, not a number of pages. Placement: JN-003 step 8, and JN-002 step 9 for the
told-at-setup half — the moment the founder arrives in the app. No implementation
exists to touch: this repo carries no `src/` (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No
source code on disk yet").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer's onboarding deep pass, when it derives opportunities, then it
   does not stop while fewer than 30 unused qualifying opportunities exist for that
   customer and evidence it has already measured remains unassessed; it stops when
   30 exist, when that evidence is spent, or when the pass itself ends early.
2. Given a deep pass that ended holding fewer than 30 unused qualifying
   opportunities, when it ends, then nothing about that shortfall holds the customer
   at setup, delays their arrival in the app, or changes what they are released
   into.
3. Given that shortfall, when the customer first arrives in the app after that pass,
   then one written line tells them there how many days of pages the product holds
   for them and that it looks for more every week; they are told there, and are
   never first told of it by a date rendering empty.
4. Given a weekly re-measurement completes for a customer, when it produces
   qualifying opportunities no page has been created from, then those are added to
   that customer's unused supply within the same week and without the customer
   asking.
5. Given a customer's unused supply falls below 7, when they open the app, then the
   screen they land on carries one written line saying supply is running short and
   how many days of pages remain; that line stands until unused supply is 7 or more
   and stops when it is; and where criterion 3's telling would also apply, the
   customer reads one statement of supply, not two.
6. Given a customer's unused supply reaches zero, when they open the app, then the
   screen they land on states that the product holds nothing worth writing for them
   and since which date that has been true; the statement stands while it remains
   true, is restated after each weekly re-measurement that adds no supply, and stops
   when supply returns. A date rendering empty is never the only place a customer is
   told their supply is exhausted.

## Non-goals
- A guaranteed number of pages in a month, or any promise of supply the evidence
  does not support — criterion 1 binds what the deep pass must pursue, and criteria
  3, 5 and 6 bind what the customer is told when it falls short.
- Filling a short or empty calendar — REQ-043 criterion 1 and REQ-047 criterion 3
  hold whatever this requirement says; this requirement adds telling, never filling.
- The wording of any line this requirement requires; criteria 3, 5 and 6 fix that a
  line exists and what it must convey.
- The account an individual empty date carries — REQ-043 criteria 3, 4 and 5.
- Which datasets, row counts or spend the deep pass and the weekly re-measurement
  use to reach criterion 1's target, and any of it shown to the customer.
- Buying more data, raising a cap, or lowering the winnability bar (REQ-047
  criterion 9) to manufacture depth.
- A customer-adjustable supply target, threshold or notification setting.
- Refunds, credits or price changes for a month in which supply ran short.
