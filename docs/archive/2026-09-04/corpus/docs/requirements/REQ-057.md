# REQ-057 — Nothing publishes without an approval or an expired veto window

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer, I want every page to sit in review for a window I control
and to be told before it goes out, so that nothing is ever published in my name
that I did not have a fair chance to stop.

## Rationale
Draft-by-default is the promise that makes automated publishing acceptable: the
customer is always told what is about to happen, at what moment, and how to stop
it, and in copilot mode silence never publishes anything. It is the default the
customer may set away from, not an invariant — BUILD.md §4.7's stepper puts zero
inside the settable range — so at a window of zero the promise that survives is
the telling, not the interval. BUILD.md §9 fixes the behaviour —
"**Draft-by-default everywhere.** Autopilot = auto-approve when the veto window
(default 24h, settable 0–7d) expires without a veto. Copilot = explicit approve
only" — and BUILD.md §12 fixes what the customer is told whenever a draft enters
review: "`draft-ready` (daily: title, why-data, *publishes tomorrow 09:00
unless you say no*, one veto link)." Placement: JN-003 step 5 ("Approves it,
vetoes it, or does nothing at all and lets the veto window run out") and step 6
("Finds the page live the next morning on their own domain or in their own
WordPress").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a page planned for a date — under copilot at any veto window, or under
   autopilot at a window longer than zero — when it is generated on the evening
   before that date, then it enters review, its veto window starts, and the
   customer is told which page it is, why it was chosen, and either the exact
   date and time it will publish unless they act, under autopilot, or that it
   will not publish until they approve it, under copilot — in the time zone they
   set — and is given a single action to stop it, all of it in the
   `draft-ready` mail (REQ-064). Where the customer has
   switched that mail off (REQ-075 criterion 1), the interval to stop the page
   and the draft in review stand whatever they chose (REQ-075 criterion 2) and
   this telling is not repeated on another channel.
2. Given a page in review, when it publishes, then it publishes under this rule
   and no other: a page becomes publishable when its veto window has expired or
   it was explicitly approved, and no edit is unsaved and no claim re-check is
   outstanding (REQ-053 criterion 5, which fixes when one stands outstanding on
   a draft and how it clears); it publishes at the first publish time at or
   after it becomes publishable, and never before.
3. Given copilot mode, when the veto window expires without an explicit
   approval, then expiry does not make the page publishable — only an explicit
   approval does — so the page does not publish and remains in review.
4. Given autopilot mode, when the veto window expires without a veto, then that
   expiry makes the page publishable and it publishes under criterion 2; and when
   the customer vetoes inside the window, it never publishes.
5. Given a page in review, when its veto window is evaluated, then the window
   that governs it is the one the customer's publishing settings hold at that
   moment (REQ-073), at whatever length REQ-073 then puts in force for a page
   already in review; this requirement fixes only what the window's expiry does,
   never its permitted lengths, its default, or when a saved change to it takes
   effect.
6. Given copilot mode and a page still in review after its window expired, when
   later days arrive, then it stays in review and approvable until the
   customer's access ends, it never publishes of its own accord, and it does not
   stop the next day's page being generated; at the end of the paid-through
   period the approve action is no longer offered and the draft stays exportable
   (REQ-076, REQ-078).
7. Given autopilot mode and a veto window the customer set to zero, when a page
   is generated, then it becomes publishable at once and there is no interval in
   which the customer can stop it; the `draft-ready` mail (REQ-064) names the
   page, why it was chosen, and the date and time it publishes, states plainly
   that no window to stop it exists because they set none, and offers taking the
   page down (REQ-056 criterion 7) in place of an action to stop it. That mail
   reaches the customer no later than the moment the page publishes, no
   notification setting the customer holds suppresses it (REQ-075), and it
   carries no
   unsubscribe link, because no link would stop it (REQ-075 criterion 5) —
   unlike the same mail at any longer window, which criterion 1 lets them switch
   off. Each of those three holds on its own, whether this mail is the first
   telling the customer has had about the page or a further one criterion 8
   requires.
8. Given a draft in review, when the pair of publishing mode and veto window
   governing it changes (REQ-073 criterion 4) so that what the customer was last
   told about whether and when it publishes has stopped being true, then before
   it publishes they are told again which pair now governs it and, under that
   pair, either the exact date and time it will publish unless they act or that
   it will not publish until they approve it — on criterion 1's terms where the
   new pair leaves them an interval in which to act, and on criterion 7's where
   it leaves none. No page publishes on a pair the customer was never told
   about, and no page publishes at all without their having been told, on the
   pair it actually publishes under, either the interval they have to stop it or
   that no interval exists.
9. Given a page bound for the customer's own site rather than a destination
   ReachKit serves, when any telling criteria 1, 7 or 8 require about that page
   is sent, then it names that site and says that publishing the page puts it
   live there, readable by anyone, and it never states that the page will wait
   there for the customer to publish it themselves. Where that telling names a
   date and time at which the page publishes unless the customer acts, it says
   the page goes live there then; where it names none because the page will not
   go anywhere until the customer approves it, it says the page goes live there
   only once they approve, and never that it will be published and readable by
   anyone whether or not they do. Where at the moment that telling is sent
   ReachKit already reads that site as one it cannot publish to (REQ-060
   criterion 7), the telling says instead that the page cannot go live there as
   things stand and what the customer must change for it to; whatever date and
   time criteria 1, 7 or 8 have it name, it never says the page goes live there
   then, and it never says that approving it will make it live. What the
   customer is told about the interval in
   which they can stop it, that no such interval exists, or that nothing happens
   until they approve, stands as those criteria require and is not changed by the
   destination.

## Non-goals
- Multi-person approval, sign-off chains or comment threads.
- Publishing without the customer having been notified — criteria 1, 7 and 8
  carry that promise as behaviour, across the whole of a draft's life and not
  only at the moment it was generated; this line only points at it.
- The veto window setting itself — its permitted lengths, its default, when a
  saved change takes effect on a page already in review, and where it and the
  publish time are set — all REQ-073's; criterion 8 fixes only what the customer
  must be told when such a change lands on a draft in review.
- The publishing rate limits, which hold whatever the window — REQ-056
  criterion 8.
- Cancellation, the paid-through date itself and what else stops with it —
  REQ-076.
- When a claim re-check stands outstanding on a draft and how the customer
  clears it — REQ-053 criterion 5; criterion 2 states only that an outstanding
  one withholds publication.
- Which kinds of mail exist — REQ-064; what `draft-ready` says is this
  requirement's, in criteria 1, 7 and 9.
- What a page delivered to the customer's own WordPress does inside their site,
  and how it is recorded there and in the app — REQ-060; criterion 9 fixes only
  what the customer is told beforehand about a page bound for such a
  destination.
- Where the notification toggles live, how they are set and what the
  unsubscribe link reaches — REQ-075; which one mail they may never suppress is
  criterion 7's.
