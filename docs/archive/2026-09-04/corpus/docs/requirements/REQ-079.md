# REQ-079 — Nothing is destroyed before it is handed back

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a customer taking my pages off the web or closing my account, I want my
content in my hands before anything disappears, so that leaving is a decision I
can survive rather than one I have to get right first time.

## Rationale
These are the only two irreversible actions in the product, and both act on
pages published under the customer's own name on their own domain, so the
customer must have every page in their hands before any of it goes, must
understand which of the two they are choosing before they choose, and must not
find the product quietly undoing what they asked for (BUILD.md §4.7,
"**Danger zone** (unpublish all, delete account; 'pages are exported to you
first, never silently destroyed')"). Every page is live at its destination, the
customer's own WordPress included (the owner's ruling of 2026-09-01, ADR-084), so
most of what is left behind after either action is pages that were on the web in
the customer's own name, and the last thing the product says must state each
outcome it left them in separately, with its own count and — where there is
anywhere to look — its own place, never lumping two outcomes under one number
and never sending them to look where nothing is. What an export
contains is REQ-078's, and what cancelling a subscription keeps is REQ-076's. Placement: JN-004 step 8
("Pulls their pages off the web, or closes the account for good").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer on Settings, when they open the danger zone, then exactly
   two actions are offered — unpublish every page, and delete the account —
   each with one written sentence saying what it removes, what it keeps, and
   whether the account survives it.
2. Given the customer starts either action, when they have not explicitly
   confirmed it, then nothing is unpublished, deleted or cancelled.
3. Given the customer confirms either action, when it runs, then an export of
   their pages is produced and downloaded by them before anything is
   unpublished or deleted; if it cannot be produced, or they do not take it,
   the action does not proceed and says why.
4. Given "unpublish everything" completes, then three things are observable and
   each has its own moment: when anyone visits a page that was live at a
   destination ReachKit serves, it is no longer served; when anyone looks in the
   customer's own WordPress at a post ReachKit created there, a post ReachKit
   itself made live stands as a draft with nothing else about it changed, and a
   post ReachKit never made live stands exactly as it did before the action,
   with nothing written into it; and when the customer opens ReachKit afterwards
   — the account survives this action, criterion 5 — every post ReachKit created
   that is still in their own site is named there as REQ-056 criterion 16 names
   a single one, whichever of those two it is, and the pages at any destination
   that could not be reached are listed as still live with one written line
   saying so.
5. Given "unpublish everything" completes, when it does, then publishing is
   switched off — the switch on the closed settings list (REQ-070 criterion 1,
   "whether pages publish at all") — and stays off until the customer switches
   it on themselves, so that no page is published by the daily loop and none is
   released by a destination being reconnected (REQ-074 criterion 3, "where
   publishing is switched off … none of them publishes"); the screen states
   that publishing is off, and the account stays active with its content still
   listed and still exportable.
6. Given "delete account" completes, when it does, then the customer is signed
   out, a sign-in request at that address finds no account, the subscription
   ends at once with no further charge and no remaining paid access, and pages
   are taken down at their destinations as criterion 4 describes. Because
   criterion 7 leaves the customer no ReachKit surface to read afterwards, the
   naming criterion 4 puts on such a surface is carried instead by one `account`
   mail (REQ-064) sent to the address being deleted. Where ReachKit created posts
   in the customer's own WordPress, that mail gives each of REQ-056 criterion
   16's four outcomes one sentence of its own, carrying that outcome's own count
   and, where there is anywhere to look, its own place — and no sentence carries
   two outcomes or one count for both:
   - how many posts ReachKit made live in that site and has now returned to
     draft, and where those can be found;
   - how many posts ReachKit created there and never made live, which nobody
     published, which were never public, and into which nothing has been
     written, and where those can be found;
   - how many were no longer in that site when ReachKit went to return them —
     this sentence naming no place, because there is nothing there to find;
   - how many stand in a site ReachKit could not reach, which may still be live
     there and into which nothing was written, and where those can be found.

   An outcome holding no posts is not named at all rather than named with a
   count of none. The mail says of every post still in that site that it is
   theirs to keep or remove, and never lists any post, whatever the number. The
   place a sentence names is the one place in their own WordPress that brings
   them up together (REQ-060 criterion 6); where the site permits no such place,
   that sentence says instead that the posts are in that site and that ReachKit
   cannot point to them there, and it still carries its count. Where a
   page is still live at a destination that could not be reached, the mail names
   it and says what they must do about it. Where nothing of either kind is left
   behind, no such mail is sent.
7. Given an account was deleted, when the customer or anyone else reaches for
   its data through any product surface — sign-in, export, a published page's
   record — then none of it is reachable from the moment of deletion; and when
   30 days have passed, then the account and the sign-in address it was reached
   at, the site, its pages, drafts, measurements,
   destination credentials and every copy ReachKit holds of the customer's own
   page content — including the passages copied from their live pages and kept
   as a draft's grounding evidence (REQ-050 criterion 1, "the passage is kept
   unchanged from then on"), whose retention ends
   here — are no longer present in ReachKit's stored data at all.

## Non-goals
- Recovering a deleted account or restoring unpublished pages — the export is
  the only recovery path, which is why criterion 3 precedes both actions.
- Unpublishing a single page — REQ-056 criteria 7, 15 and 16 (BUILD §9,
  "published → unpublished (always available)").
- A pause or hibernate plan — cancelling already keeps service to the
  paid-through date (REQ-076).
- Legal-erasure timelines and data-subject request handling, which BUILD does
  not specify; criterion 7 states the product's own erasure window.
- Deleting anything from the customer's own destination, and touching content
  ReachKit did not create there at all — criterion 4 carries what does happen to
  the pages ReachKit created as behaviour.
- Whether the unused remainder of a paid period is refunded when an account is
  deleted — refunds are deferred post-launch by owner ruling (REQ-076).
- Whether a mail may be sent at all — REQ-064 criterion 6 states that rule, and
  the register of kinds is the table REQ-064's own non-goals place with the mail
  blueprint. Criterion 6 above supplies the occasion and `account` is the kind it
  carries; adding a kind changes that table, not this requirement.
- What switching publishing off does to pages already in flight, and how
  quickly — REQ-056 criteria 9 to 13.
