# REQ-053 — The customer's do-not-claim list is a hard filter on output, and a stopped draft has one recovery path

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer in a regulated or careful market, I want the things I must
never claim to be blocked from every page rather than discouraged, so that one
forbidden sentence can never reach my live site.

## Rationale
For customers with legal, medical or competitive constraints a claim that slips
through once is a serious harm, so the list is enforced as an output filter that
stops a page rather than as guidance to a model — and because a stopped draft is
the customer's day, every generation hard rule ends the same way: one
regeneration, then the item comes back to them with nothing published and the day
saying so, rather than looping or vanishing. Source: `BUILD.md` §8, hard rule 4 —
"Do-not-claim list: hard output filter (string/semantic match), failure =
regenerate, twice = needs-attention".

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given the customer has recorded claims the product must never make, when a
   draft is generated, then it is checked against that list before it can be
   queued for review, and a draft matches when it states a recorded claim either
   in the recorded words or in a paraphrase of them.
2. Given a generated draft that matches the list, when the check fails, then the
   draft is not queued for review and the item follows the recovery path in
   criterion 3, which bounds how often it may be regenerated.
3. Given a draft stopped by any generation hard rule — this list, or any of
   REQ-050 criteria 1 to 9 (grounding, invented people, the doorway and
   hidden-text rules, near-duplication) — when it is stopped, then it is
   regenerated once and no more; if the regenerated draft is stopped by any of
   those rules again, the item comes to rest in the state REQ-056 defines for an
   item needing the customer's attention (REQ-056 criterion 2), nothing is
   published for that day, and that day carries its line (REQ-043 criterion 4).
4. Given the customer changes the list, when a draft is generated afterwards,
   then it is checked against the updated list before it can be queued for
   review; and every page of theirs not yet handed to a destination is re-checked
   against the updated list, whatever stage it has reached — in review, approved
   and waiting for its publish time, held after a failed attempt, or waiting on
   the customer to act.
5. Given a page carrying an outstanding claim re-check against the changed list,
   when it would otherwise go to its destination — its veto window expiring, the
   customer approving it, its publish time arriving, an automatic retry falling
   due, or the customer starting one — then it is not handed over, whatever stage
   it sits in; no approval, schedule or retry can put a page carrying a forbidden
   claim on the customer's site. The re-check stands outstanding until one passes
   against the text as it then stands (REQ-057 criterion 2); the entry it matched
   is named to the customer; it is never regenerated — criterion 3's path reaches
   only a draft stopped before it entered review — and it goes to its destination
   only if a re-check later passes, or never, if the customer stops it.

## Non-goals
- Re-checking pages already live on the customer's site, or already handed to a
  destination, when an entry is added — criteria 4 and 5 reach every page short
  of that hand-off and no further.
- Regenerating any draft the customer already has in review, whoever wrote the
  matching text — an edit of their own that fails this check is returned to them
  (REQ-045 criterion 11).
- Legal review, compliance certification, or advice on what to add to the list.
- Where the customer records and changes the list — REQ-070's Settings card.
- Which publish state a draft held by a failed re-check occupies, and which
  calendar stage that state is shown as — REQ-056 and REQ-043; criterion 5 fixes
  only that it does not publish and that the customer is told what held it.
