# REQ-073 — The customer sets how and when pages go out

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer, I want to control whether pages publish on their own, how
long I get to stop one, and what time of day they go live, so that the product
publishes on my terms without me having to be present every day.

## Rationale
Publishing to the customer's own domain under their own name is the highest
consequence thing the product does, so the customer must own the settings
that govern it — whether pages go out on their own, how long they have to stop
one, what hour a page appears and which zone that hour is read in — must be able
to change them after setup, and must be told what each choice means before they
make it
(BUILD.md §4.7, "**Publishing** (mode toggle, veto window stepper 0–7d default
24h, publish time, ...)"; §9, "Autopilot = auto-approve when the veto window
… expires without a veto. Copilot = explicit approve only"). What either mode
then does to a draft at expiry is REQ-057's.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer on Settings, when they open publishing, then they can set
   the publishing mode to Autopilot or Copilot, set the veto window to any
   whole number of days from 0 to 7, set the time of day pages publish, and set
   the time zone that time of day is read in; where they have set nothing, the
   veto window is 24 hours and the time zone is the one their browser reported
   at first sign-in, which they may change here at any time afterwards.
2. Given a mode and a veto window are selected, when the screen renders them,
   then one written line states what that selected pair does to a draft the
   customer never acts on: for Autopilot with a window longer than 0 days, that
   the page publishes at the first publish time at or after the window expires
   (REQ-057 criterion 2); for Autopilot with a window of 0
   days, that there is no interval in which they can stop it and it publishes at
   their next publish time; and for Copilot at any window, that the page stays in
   review and never publishes on its own.
3. Given a publish time is set, when a page publishes, then it publishes at
   that hour in the customer's stated time zone — the one stored preference
   every customer-facing scheduled time and window in the product is stated in
   — and never at an hour other than the one the screen shows.
4. Given a change to mode, veto window, publish time or time zone is saved,
   when it takes effect, then it applies to every draft not yet published,
   including drafts already in review; a window already running is never
   shortened by it, a draft already in review whose window is lengthened keeps
   the later of the two expiry moments, and a draft whose window already expired
   under Copilot starts its window again at the moment of the change, so a
   switch to Autopilot never makes a held draft publishable (REQ-057 criterion
   4) without the interval this screen's settings state.
5. Given the publishing mode is changed anywhere in the product, when it is
   changed, then it is the same single setting this screen shows, and the
   change is the same change criterion 4 governs.
6. Given a change is saved, when the customer looks at the calendar, then the
   next publication's date and time reflect the new settings.

## Non-goals
- What either mode does to a draft when the veto window expires, and what the
  customer is told before it does — REQ-057's, not this screen's.
- Pause, and the switch that turns publishing off altogether: both are states of
  their own, the switch sitting on the closed settings list (REQ-070 criterion
  1) and behaving as REQ-056 criteria 9 to 14 describe, and
  neither is a third publishing mode.
- The autopilot hard limits — at most one publish a day and eight a week, which
  hold whatever these settings say (REQ-056 criterion 8), and fix-type tasks
  never automated (REQ-047) — promised there, not by this screen.
- Choosing mode or destination at setup (REQ-025).
- Per-draft scheduling, moving or skipping, which is the calendar's (BUILD
  §4.6).
- Any control over how many pages a day are written, or over generation
  cadence — engine parameters REQ-070 criterion 3 keeps off every screen
  (BUILD §4.7).
- Publishing windows, blackout dates, or per-day schedules.
