# REQ-045 — The customer reads the whole page, edits it as Markdown, and can copy it out for any destination

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer who is the publisher of record, I want to read the finished
page exactly as it would publish, see what it is grounded in and whether it
passed its checks, change a sentence I disagree with without losing my work, and
take the words away in a form I can paste anywhere, so that I can approve, fix or
stop a page on evidence rather than faith.

## Rationale
The customer's name goes on every page, so nothing may publish that they could
not have inspected in full and corrected in place: every word that would go out,
the fact it stands on with the page and date it was read from, and the result of
the check against what they forbid; and because most reviews end in a small
change rather than a veto, the edit path must be fast and lossless, must never
leave a stale check result attached to text the customer has since altered, and
must never throw away the customer's own words. The words are theirs whatever
destination they are bound for, so any draft or published page can be copied out
as Markdown or HTML — the path that keeps a customer on a destination ReachKit
does not serve from being locked out of their own content. The rule deciding when
a page becomes publishable stays REQ-057's, and what may be model-written
anywhere in the interface, and how it is identified, is REQ-093's. Placement:
JN-003 steps 4, 5, 7 and 9. Source: `BUILD.md` §4.6 (the draft view: full page
render, grounded-fact highlight with its source line, claim-check badge, Approve
/ Edit / Veto, and the "what happens if you do nothing" box; Markdown textarea
with a live preview pane, autosaved, no rich-text editor; an edited draft keeps
its grounding highlight if the fact survives the edit and drops the claim-check
badge until the check re-runs on save) and §9 ("Everything else = copy as
Markdown/HTML (always shown)"). No implementation exists to touch: this repo
carries no `src/` (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No source code on disk yet").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a draft awaiting review, when the customer opens it, then every word
   that would publish is rendered for them to read, with nothing withheld or
   summarised, and what ReachKit wrote is labelled here as that page's generated
   content, in the form REQ-093 criterion 2 requires of it wherever it appears.
   Where the customer has edited the draft, that label never presents their own
   words as text ReachKit generated (REQ-093's non-goal on text the customer
   writes themselves).
2. Given a draft is rendered for review, when the customer reads it, then the
   fact it is grounded in is marked within the text and shown with the URL it was
   read from and the date it was read.
3. Given a draft is rendered for review, when the customer reads it, then the
   outcome of the check against their do-not-claim list is stated — including
   where they have recorded no entries, in which case it states that there was
   nothing to check against.
4. Given a draft awaiting review, when the customer reads it, then they are told
   what will happen if they do nothing, and can approve, edit or veto without
   leaving the view.
5. Given a draft awaiting review, when the customer chooses to edit it, then they
   edit its Markdown and see the rendered result of their changes as they type.
6. Given an edit in progress, when the customer pauses or leaves the view, then
   the change is saved without an explicit save action and is present when they
   return; where two sessions edit the same draft, the most recently saved
   version is the draft.
7. Given a save does not land, when the customer is still in the view, then they
   are told the change is unsaved, and no text that failed to save is ever
   published.
8. Given an edited draft, when it is saved, then the grounding is still marked if
   the grounded fact survives the edit, and is no longer marked if the fact was
   removed.
9. Given an edited draft, when the edit is saved, then a claim check is run
   against the text as saved and its outcome is shown when it completes; until
   then no claim-check outcome is shown for that draft.
10. Given an edited draft, when its veto window expires, then it does not publish
    unless no edit is unsaved and a claim check has completed and passed against
    the text as it then stands — so text no check has run against never publishes;
    whether and when it publishes afterwards follows REQ-057 criterion 2's single
    rule for becoming publishable, and nothing here releases it earlier.
11. Given the check fails on an edited draft, whoever wrote the matching text,
    when the check completes, then a claim re-check stands outstanding on that
    draft until one passes against the text as it then stands, so the draft does
    not become publishable (REQ-057 criterion 2); it is never regenerated, and it
    is returned to the customer naming the entry it matched, with their own text
    intact, so no draft is ever held without the customer being told which entry
    held it.
12. Given any draft or published page, whatever its destination, when the
    customer opens it, then its Markdown and its HTML are available for them to
    copy.

## Non-goals
- Reproducing the appearance a page will take inside a customer's own WordPress
  theme (REQ-060) — this view promises the words, not a destination's rendering.
- Images or media in a draft, inserted by ReachKit or by the customer.
- Comments, suggestion mode, simultaneous editing by more than one person, or
  multi-person approval.
- Publishing directly from this view to a destination the customer has not
  connected.
- A rich-text or WYSIWYG editor.
- Merging two concurrent edits of the same draft.
- Version history browsing or diffing.
- Typography, highlight styling, badge tone, and two-column versus tabbed
  layout — design system.
- How an edited draft's label distinguishes the customer's words from ReachKit's
  — the blueprint's, from criterion 1's promise that it never claims theirs.
