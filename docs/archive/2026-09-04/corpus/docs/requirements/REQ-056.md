# REQ-056 — A page moves through defined publish states, a retry never publishes twice, and stopping is instant

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer who has handed over the keys, I want every page to be in
exactly one knowable state on its way to being live, a failure to be recoverable
without ever producing a duplicate post, and one action that stops everything at
once, so that I always know where a page stands, never have to clean up after the
system, and can take the keys back immediately.

## Rationale
Publishing is the moment the product touches the customer's own site, so a page
must always be in exactly one knowable state, a failure must be recoverable
without ever leaving a duplicate post behind, every page that went out must carry
the record of why it exists and where it landed, and the amount that can ever go
out without the customer is capped whatever their settings say. The stop is part
of the same machine: switching publishing off must take effect at the moment it
is recorded rather than next cycle, must lose no page while it is off, and must
tell the customer truthfully what the one attempt already in flight did. Two
moves the state machine leaves implicit are named here because other requirements
end in them: the customer skipping a page before it is written, and a generation
that produced no draft fit for review, whatever stopped it (REQ-053 criterion 3,
REQ-092 criterion 1). No move returns a draft in review to generation, whoever
wrote the text that failed a re-check: it stays in review for the customer to
edit or stop (REQ-053's non-goal). Placement: JN-003 step 5 ("Approves it,
vetoes it, or does nothing at all and lets the veto window run out"), step 6
("Finds the page live the next morning on their own domain or in their own
WordPress") and step 9 ("Finds a page again on the calendar … or unpublishing a
page they regret"), which is where criterion 16 is walked.
Evidence: `registry/evidence/REQ-056.md`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a page in the pipeline, when it is inspected at any moment, then it is
   in exactly one of `planned`, `generating`, `in_review`, `approved`,
   `publishing`, `published`, `skipped`, `failed`, `needs_attention` or
   `unpublished`, and no other state exists.
2. Given a page in any state, when its state changes, then the change is one of
   these fifteen and no other, and any other attempted change is refused with
   the page's state unchanged: `planned`→`generating`, `planned`→`skipped`,
   `generating`→`in_review`, `generating`→`needs_attention` (any generation that
   produced no draft fit for review, whatever stopped it — a generation hard rule
   for the second time, REQ-053 criterion 3, or a step that failed, REQ-092
   criterion 1 — so no failure leaves a page resting in `generating`),
   `in_review`→`approved`, `in_review`→`skipped` (a draft held by a failed claim
   re-check leaves review by one of these two and by no other, REQ-053 criterion
   5), `approved`→`publishing`,
   `publishing`→`published`, `publishing`→`failed`, `failed`→`publishing`,
   `failed`→`needs_attention`, `needs_attention`→`publishing` (open only to a
   page holding a draft that passed every generation hard rule, so a draft one
   of those rules stopped is never published by starting an attempt),
   `needs_attention`→`generating` (open only to a page whose draft never entered
   review, and only when the customer starts it, so REQ-053 criterion 3's bound
   on automatic regeneration is never raised),
   `needs_attention`→`skipped`, `published`→`unpublished`.
3. Given a page in `planned`, `in_review` or `needs_attention`, when the customer
   skips, vetoes or discards it, then it moves to `skipped` and never publishes.
   `skipped` and `unpublished` are the only states from which no further change is
   possible, so a page the customer stopped or took down is never returned to a
   destination by any later attempt, and no page — whichever route brought it to
   `needs_attention` — is left in a state it has no way out of.
4. Given a page in `publishing`, when the attempt fails, then it becomes `failed`
   with a written reason and is retried automatically only while that reason is
   one a repeated attempt could clear, and at most three times; when no such
   retry is due — because the reason needs the customer, or because the third
   retry failed — it moves to `needs_attention`, where no further automatic
   attempt is made and the customer can start one. No page rests in `failed`
   without either a further attempt scheduled or that move already made — except
   while publishing is switched off, when a retry that was due is withheld and
   the page is held rather than moved (criterion 11), and becomes due
   again when publishing resumes.
5. Given a page and a destination for which a publish has already been attempted,
   when a publish is attempted again — including a retry of an attempt whose
   outcome was never confirmed, and an attempt made after the page was
   unpublished — then at most one post for that page exists at that destination,
   and a page in `unpublished` gains no post.
6. Given a page has reached `published`, when the customer opens that page, then
   they are shown the opportunity it came from, the search it targets, the date
   of the measurement behind it, whether it was approved or published
   automatically, and the address ReachKit published it at, whichever
   destination that is. That address is offered as the address the page is
   publicly readable at only while the page still stands `published`; from the
   moment it moves to `unpublished` (criterion 7) the record presents the same
   address as the one the page was published at and never as one it is readable
   at now, and carries beside it the outcome criterion 15 or criterion 16 names
   for that page and no other account of what is at that address — ReachKit
   never goes back to look, so it never claims a page is live at an address it
   has itself stopped serving or asked the customer's site to stop serving.
   Where ReachKit never made the page live at its destination, no address is
   offered at all and the record says so in place of one.
7. Given a `published` page, when the customer unpublishes it, then it moves to
   `unpublished`, and this transition is available for as long as the page is
   published.
8. Given pages are publishing in either mode, when they publish, then no more
   than one publishes in any calendar day and no more than eight in any calendar
   week, counted in the time zone the customer set, whatever their settings or
   plan would otherwise allow; the two ceilings hold independently of each other.
9. Given the customer switches publishing off — the switch on the closed
   settings list, REQ-070 criterion 1 — when they do so, then it takes one
   action and no publish attempt begins for any page after the moment the switch
   is recorded — including for one already approved or already in review — until
   they switch it back on; the only page that can still reach a destination is
   one whose attempt was already under way at that moment, which criteria 11 and
   12 govern.
10. Given publishing is switched off, when a page that would otherwise have gone
    to a destination is held by it — one already approved, one whose veto window
    expired while the switch was off, one whose publish time arrived, or one
    whose retry fell due — then it stays in the state it holds, the move to
    `publishing` is not taken for it, and it is never skipped, discarded or moved
    to `needs_attention` on account of publishing being off; every such page is
    one of the held pages criterion 13 resumes.
11. Given a page whose publish attempt is already under way, or a page that failed
    and is due to be retried, when the customer switches publishing off, then no
    publish attempt begins for it after the moment the switch is recorded — not
    the retry it was due, nor any later automatic attempt — for as long as
    publishing is off, and it is held with the pages in criterion 13 rather than
    dropped, published, or brought to rest as needing the customer (criterion 4).
12. Given a publish attempt was already under way at the moment the switch was
    recorded, when its outcome is known, then the customer is shown that outcome:
    a page that did reach its destination is shown as published, with the record
    criterion 6 requires, and a page that did not is shown as held — so
    what the customer reads after switching off never says nothing was published
    while a page went out, and never says a page went out that did not.
13. Given pages were held while publishing was switched off, when the customer
    switches it back on, then those pages resume under the ceilings in criterion
    8, in the order they were held, and none is dropped.
14. Given publishing is switched off, when the customer opens the app, then that
    state, its effect on the schedule, and how many pages are held by it are
    visible without opening Settings.
15. Given a page published to a destination ReachKit serves, when the customer
    chooses to unpublish it, then it is removed from that destination, and this
    action is available for any such page at any time.
16. Given a page delivered to the customer's own WordPress, when the customer
    chooses to unpublish it, then the product stops treating it as one of their
    live pages, and the only thing ReachKit ever writes into their site is the
    return to draft of a post it made live there itself — a page the customer
    made live themselves is not a page ReachKit made live, and is never written
    into. ReachKit tells the customer which of the four outcomes below happened
    in one written line it renders on that page's own record, identifying the
    post well enough for them to find it in their own site; the line is rendered
    on ReachKit's own surface and no line is written into the customer's site.
    Where another requirement puts the same naming on another ReachKit surface,
    it names the same outcome as the line on this record and never a different
    one. The four outcomes:
    - ReachKit made it live there and it now stands as a draft again, with
      nothing else about the post changed;
    - ReachKit never made it live, so nothing was written and removing the post
      is theirs to do;
    - the post is no longer in their site, so there was nothing to return and
      nothing is theirs to remove;
    - their site could not be reached, so the post may still be live there and
      nothing was written. In this outcome alone the page's own record also
      carries an action to ask for the return to draft again, offered for as
      long as the page exists; asking leaves the page's state unchanged
      (criterion 3), and when an ask succeeds the record shows the first outcome
      in its place.

## Non-goals
- States beyond the ten named in criterion 1, and transitions beyond the
  fifteen named in criterion 2; adding one is a change to this requirement.
- Returning a draft the customer already has in review to generation — REQ-053's
  non-goal, and no transition here provides for it.
- How those states are grouped into the stages the customer is shown — REQ-043.
- Holding the queue for a destination that is not working — its credentials
  expired, its connection broke, or its DNS does not resolve — REQ-074.
- Republishing a page the customer unpublished, or restoring a skipped one —
  criteria 3 and 5 carry that promise as behaviour; this line only points at it.
- Deleting a post inside the customer's own WordPress, or editing any part of
  one other than returning a page ReachKit made live there to draft (REQ-060);
  criterion 16 carries what does happen to each of the two as behaviour.
- Retrying indefinitely, or silently skipping a failed page — criterion 4
  carries that promise as behaviour; this line only points at it.
- Whether a skipped page's opportunity returns to the supply — REQ-047.
- When a page is published, the veto window and the publish time — REQ-057's;
  criterion 8's ceilings hold whatever those say.
- Raising the ceilings by plan, by request or by setting — criterion 8 carries
  that promise as behaviour; this line only points at it.
- Catching up by publishing more than criterion 8 allows once publishing
  resumes — criterion 13 carries that promise as behaviour; this line only
  points at it.
- Where the switch that turns publishing off lives, and the rest of what
  Settings offers — REQ-070 criterion 1.
- The publishing mode, the veto window, the publish time and what the screen
  says about each — REQ-073.
- What else switching publishing off does: it stays off until the customer
  switches it on themselves after unpublishing everything (REQ-079 criterion 5),
  and a reconnected destination releases no held page while it is off (REQ-074
  criterion 3).
- Pages held because the product stopped its own work, rather than because the
  customer stopped it — REQ-092.
- Automating Fix-family items — that promise lives in REQ-047.
