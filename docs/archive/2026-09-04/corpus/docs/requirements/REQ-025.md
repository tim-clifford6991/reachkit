# REQ-025 — Setup is three decisions and one submit

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a founder who has just paid, I want to answer three things once, so that the
product starts working for me instead of asking me to configure it.

## Rationale
The founder bought an outcome, not a tool; anything they are asked to tune is
work the product should have derived, and every extra question is a chance to
answer it wrong. Setup therefore asks the three things the product cannot
derive — their market, their competitors, how and where pages publish — once,
in one screen, with nothing that tunes the engine present anywhere on it
(`BUILD.md` §4.3, §4.7). The site address alongside them is identity rather than
configuration — shown to confirm or change where a report measured it, asked
outright where none did (REQ-021) — so the screen admits it without
becoming a fourth decision. A customer who never submits keeps their
subscription and is asked back to finish it, each ask carrying a way in so the
reminder is reachable rather than rhetorical; they are neither paused nor
completed on their behalf. Being returned to setup is a return to the one screen
they left, never a lock on the account: a customer who has paid and does not
want to go on can reach Settings, cancel and export with setup unfinished, so
the one-click cancellation the pricing card promises (REQ-076 criterion 3) never
requires them to first finish the thing they are leaving.
The three decisions themselves are REQ-026's — the market and the competitors
that derive from it — and REQ-028's; this requirement owns the shape of the
screen and the fact that it happens once.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a founder arriving at setup, when the screen loads, then it asks for
   exactly three decisions — their market, their competitors, and how and where
   pages publish — and for nothing else, save the site address: shown for the
   founder to confirm or change where a report measured one (REQ-021 criterion
   6), and asked outright where the purchase had no report behind it (REQ-021
   criterion 7); and nothing on the screen states how long the deep pass, or the
   founder's first page, will take.
2. Given a founder who has answered the three, when they act to finish, then one
   action starts the product: no multi-page wizard, no second confirmation
   screen, no further questions.
3. Given a founder at setup, when they look for anything that tunes the engine —
   question counts, cadences, spend caps, scan depth, model choices — then none
   is present or offered.
4. Given a founder who has completed setup, when they return to it, then they
   are not asked the three decisions again and are taken onward (REQ-029
   criterion 6).
5. Given a founder who has not completed setup, when they open the app, then
   they are returned to setup — except that Settings stays reachable to them
   with setup unfinished, where cancelling or resuming the plan, exporting their
   content, reaching their invoices, signing out and deleting the account are
   every one of them available (REQ-070 criterion 2), and cancelling there
   behaves exactly as it does for a customer who finished setup (REQ-076
   criterion 3): no customer who has paid is ever required to complete setup in
   order to leave.
6. Given a customer who has paid and not submitted setup, when 24 hours have
   passed since their payment and an account is open against it (REQ-024
   criterion 6), then they are sent a `setup-reminder` mail — an occasion
   REQ-064 criterion 6 admits, and one their own account needs them to have
   (REQ-064 criterion 7) — asking them to finish it and carrying a working way
   back in: a sign-in link that lands them on the setup screen itself (REQ-024
   criterion 4). No reminder ever goes out before there is an account to sign in
   to, and there are up to three in all with the last no later than 7 days after
   the payment; and once they
   submit setup, or cancel, no further one is ever sent.

## Non-goals
- Publish time, veto-window length, brand voice, do-not-claim list, or
  notification preferences at setup — those live in Settings; criterion 1
  carries what the screen may ask.
- Any engine setting, at setup or anywhere else — criterion 3 carries it.
- Skipping into the product with the three unanswered, or a guided tour of the
  app before it. Criterion 5's exception opens Settings and the way out, never
  the product itself.
- Re-asking for payment details, name or company — criterion 1's "nothing else"
  covers them.
- Pausing, discounting or refunding a customer who has not finished setup, or
  completing it on their behalf — they are charged as REQ-022 criterion 2
  states, and their way out is to cancel (criterion 5).
- What Settings itself offers and how a cancellation is carried out — REQ-070's
  and REQ-076's. Criterion 5 states only that an unfinished setup bars none of
  it.
- Chasing someone who never bought: that is `nurture` to an unconverted lead
  (REQ-010), a different mail to a different person.
