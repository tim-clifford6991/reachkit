# REQ-075 — The customer chooses which recurring emails they receive

- Priority: Should

## User story
As a paying customer, I want to turn off the ReachKit emails I do not want, so
that a product that mails me daily does not become the reason I cancel it.

## Rationale
A product that mails a customer daily must let them quiet it without losing the
mail that carries their only key — on magic-link auth, unsubscribing from
everything costs them the sign-in link itself, so each recurring mail is
switched off on its own and the event it announced stays visible in the app
(BUILD.md §4.7, "**Notifications** (3 toggles)"). Which kinds of mail exist at
all is REQ-064's; of them, exactly three recur for a paying customer and are
this screen's to switch, and everything else the product sends is transactional
and reaches the customer whatever these toggles say — including the single
draft-ready send that is the whole of the telling because the page publishes on
its own with no window in which to stop it (REQ-057 criterion 7).

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer on Settings, when they open notifications, then they can
   turn each of the three recurring emails on or off independently — the daily
   draft-ready mail, the published-page mail, and the Monday movement mail —
   and the choice holds on any device they next open Settings on; the one
   draft-ready mail these toggles do not reach is the one sent under autopilot
   at a veto window of zero, which no setting here switches off (REQ-057
   criterion 7).
2. Given a notification is off, when the event that would have sent it occurs,
   then no mail is sent and the event is still visible in the app: a draft in
   review on the calendar, a published page on the calendar with its live URL,
   and the week's movement on Overview.
3. Given every notification is off, when the customer requests a sign-in link,
   when the product must tell them something about their account or the state
   of their subscription, or when a page generated under autopilot at a veto
   window of zero is announced, then that mail is still sent — these three
   toggles suppress no mail but their own, and the draft-ready toggle reaches
   every draft-ready mail but that one.
4. Given a notification is turned back on, when the next such event occurs,
   then the mail resumes, and none of the mails missed while it was off are
   sent afterwards.
5. Given the customer uses the unsubscribe link on one of these mails, when
   they do, then that one mail is switched off, the other two are unaffected,
   and no mail outside these three is suppressed.

## Non-goals
- Turning off sign-in, account or lifecycle mail: those are transactional, and
  no control over them is offered.
- Which mail the product may send at all — the rule that admits a mail and the
  registry of kinds are both REQ-064's.
- Digest, batching or frequency controls — a cadence is one of the engine
  parameters REQ-070 criterion 3 keeps off every screen (BUILD §4.7).
- Sending to a second address, a team, or a shared inbox: every mail ReachKit
  sends goes to the one address the account signs in with (REQ-077 criterion 3),
  and no control adds another.
- Any channel other than email: no SMS, Slack, push or webhooks.
- The content, wording or layout of the mails themselves (BUILD §12).
