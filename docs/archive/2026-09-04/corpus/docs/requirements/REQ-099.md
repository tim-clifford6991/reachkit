# REQ-099 — The landing page carries the whole pitch — the first screen, then the demo, then why · what · how to start

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a stranger who has just clicked a link to ReachKit, I want the first screen to
tell me what this is and show me what it looks like, and the page below it to walk
me from why I should care to what I do today, so that I can decide whether to type
my domain in instead of guessing what I would be typing it into.

## Rationale
The landing page is the first touchpoint anyone has with the product, and what
must be true is that a visitor can see, without scrolling, what ReachKit is and
what it looks like, and can then read themselves through why they should care,
what it does for them, and how to start. The owner's statement of 2026-09-02,
what it supersedes in `BUILD.md` §3, the two readings of REQ-001 criterion 1, and
every choice taken under rule 1.1 — including the floor viewport criterion 1 is
measured at — are in `registry/evidence/REQ-099.md`. This requirement declares
`blocked-by: [REQ-001]` (rule 2.3): that approved criterion reads "exactly one
text input and one submit control and no other input control of any kind", and
whether the ban is bounded by the scan-parameter list that follows it or reaches
every control on the page is the first open question below — criteria 3 and 5
say which reading each stands on. Criterion 1 is the small-screen criterion
ADR-093's Consequences record as owed by the analyst, written against that
decision's own bands and 320 px floor. Criterion 8 is the one home of the rule
for a figure on a public surface; REQ-098 criterion 5 cites it. Placement:
JN-001 gains a first step for it, and its domain-typing step gains this
requirement — JN-001 was `approved` and dropped to `in-review` in the same edit.
What it reaches: no code — this repo carries no `src/` — but two approved
blueprints publishing the landing contract, BP-022 (`src/app/(public)/page.tsx`,
"landing: one text input, one submit, nothing else") and BP-001 ("landing: one
field, one submit"), and ADR-093, whose conformance suite renders five widths and
no height; each is named with its clause in the evidence file.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a visitor arriving at the landing address in a viewport 320 CSS px wide
   and 480 CSS px tall — the floor — or any wider or taller one, when the page
   first paints and before they scroll, then the tagline, one subline beneath it,
   and REQ-001 criterion 1's one text input with its submit control are all
   entirely within the viewport, none of them clipped, truncated or set below
   ADR-093's type floor; at ADR-093's `medium` and `wide` bands one component
   showing what the product itself looks like is entirely within it as well; and
   at `compact` those three are within it and the component follows directly
   below, in normal flow. Given the same viewport after a submission REQ-001
   criterion 3 rejects, when the line naming what is wrong renders, then it is
   beside the field and both are within the viewport.
2. Given the tagline, when it renders, then it reads, verbatim: "See what AI
   tells buyers about your market — and write your way in."
3. Given the whole page, when a visitor counts what they can type into and what
   they can submit, then there is still exactly one of each (REQ-001 criterion
   1); and — on the bounded reading of open question 1 — every further call to
   action anywhere on the page brings that one field into view with the cursor
   in it, ready to type, without a page load and without adding a second input
   or a second submit control.
4. Given the component of criterion 1, when a visitor reads it, then it shows the
   product's own running interface — the screens a customer actually uses —
   rather than a stock illustration, a photograph, or a mock-up of a product that
   does not exist; and every word visible inside it is one the product itself
   renders on that screen (REQ-093 criterion 1), with nothing written for the
   illustration alone.
5. Given the demo video exists (open question 2) and open question 1's bounded
   reading admits its play control, when a visitor scrolls past the first
   screen, then the next thing on the page is that video showing the product
   being used — not a title card, a logo animation, a stock clip, or a sequence
   of still images.
6. Given the video does not play — absent, never produced, blocked, still
   loading, or a connection too slow for it — when the visitor continues, then the
   page still states everything criteria 1, 3, 7 and 8 require, a scan can still
   be started, and one written line stands where the video would be; no call to
   action and nothing on the first screen depends on the video having played.
7. Given the page below the first screen, when a visitor reads down it, then it
   answers three questions in this order, each in its own section under its own
   heading — why they should care, what ReachKit does for them, and what they do
   to start today — and that order holds unchanged at each of ADR-093's three
   bands. Where criterion 5's video is present it sits above those three sections;
   where it is not, they follow the first screen directly.
8. Given any number, score, band word, domain or brand name shown inside
   criterion 4's component or criterion 5's video, when a visitor reads it, then
   either it was read from a measurement the product stored for the domain shown
   and is shown with the date of the scan it came from (REQ-093 criterion 4); or
   — where open question 4 admits an example — it is about a domain RFC 2606
   reserves (`example.com`, `example.net`, `example.org`, or a name under
   `.example`, `.test` or `.invalid`) and a written line on the same component or
   frame, shown wherever and whenever the figure is, says that the figures are an
   example and measure no site. In neither case is any figure shown about the
   visitor's own site, which nothing has measured yet.

## Non-goals
- What starting a scan does, what a valid domain is, what address the visitor
  lands on and what they meet afterwards — REQ-001.
- Every customer-visible string on this page except the tagline: those are the
  owner's and are enumerated as owed below, not drafted here.
- Producing, hosting and captioning the demo video, and whether it autoplays —
  open below; criteria 6 and 7 fix only that the page works and keeps its order
  without it. Its words are owed, item 12 below.
- Whether any sentence on this page may be model-written — REQ-093 is the single
  home for that rule and it holds here unchanged, including the video's captions,
  on-screen text and narration: REQ-093 criterion 1 binds "every text the product
  renders or sends, on every surface it speaks on … including surfaces added
  after this requirement".
- The sign-in screen a returning customer needs — REQ-098.
- A price surface with no report behind it, and what any offer to subscribe must
  state — REQ-021 criteria 1 to 4, REQ-022 criterion 1.
- Breakpoint values, image treatment, which registered variant of each item
  renders at each band, and the height of the first screen above the floor —
  the design system's (`00-project.md`, standing law 1; ADR-093's three
  constraints). Criterion 1 fixes what must be in it at the floor, never how
  tall it is above it.
- A second landing variant, personalisation, or an A/B test.
- Marketing pages beyond this one: no blog, no case studies, no about page.

## Open questions
- [ ] REQ-001 criterion 1 reads "exactly one text input and one submit control
      and no other input control of any kind — no market, competitor, keyword or
      country field, selector or toggle". Two readings, and only the owner can say
      which was promised. **Bounded:** the list is the ban, so the promise is
      about the scan form — a video's play control and a repeated call to action
      are neither inputs nor submits and criteria 3 and 5 stand. **Whole page:**
      "of any kind" is the ban and the list illustrates it, so the promise is that
      the landing page carries no control but those two — criterion 5's video
      control and criterion 3's repeated calls to action break an approved
      promise and come out. BP-022 and BP-001 publish the same contract as the
      page's shape, so the answer moves them too. (owner: user)
- [ ] The demo video is an asset class this corpus has no home for: who produces
      it, where it is hosted, and whether it autoplays. No `BUILD.md` §16
      milestone names it and no cost-envelope row covers it. (owner: user)
- [ ] The customer-visible strings this page needs, none of which the system may
      write (§1, decision-rights table). The tagline in criterion 2 stands; these
      are owed:
      1. the subline beneath the tagline (criterion 1);
      2. the label on the submit control that starts the scan (criterion 1);
      3. the label on every further call to action lower down the page
         (criterion 3);
      4. the heading and body of the "why should they care" section (criterion 7);
      5. the heading and body of the "what it does for them" section
         (criterion 7);
      6. the heading and body of the "what they do to start today" section
         (criterion 7);
      7. any caption or label on the product component (criterion 4);
      8. any heading or caption on the video section (criterion 5);
      9. the line that stands where the video would be when it will not play
         (criterion 6);
      10. the interface copy visible inside criterion 4's component — the
          product's own registered strings, already the owner's (BP-019);
      11. where criterion 8's second branch is used, the line saying the figures
          are an example;
      12. the video's own words — its on-screen text and its narration script
          (criterion 5).
      (owner: user)
- [ ] Whether a figure no scan produced may stand on a ReachKit public surface as
      a declared example — criterion 8's second branch, and REQ-098 criterion 5
      rests on the same answer. Standing law 5 ("Every UI number traces to a
      stored measurement with a date") and REQ-093 criterion 4 ("never estimated,
      never recomputed for display") admit no example as written. **Admit it:**
      REQ-093 criterion 4 gains that one exemption as its single home — a figure
      on a domain RFC 2606 reserves, with a line on the same surface saying it is
      an example and measures nothing — and the second branches stand. **Refuse
      it:** every figure on the landing component, the video and the sign-in card
      is a stored measurement shown with its domain and scan date, the second
      branches go, and the sign-in card's transcribed "+6 pts est." goes with
      them. (owner: user)
