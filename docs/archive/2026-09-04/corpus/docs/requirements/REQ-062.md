# REQ-062 — A published page is verified a day later, and the result is shown

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer, I want the product to go and check that yesterday's page
is actually live, findable and readable by AI, so that "published" means the
page exists in the world and not just in a database.

## Rationale
A publish that reports success but produced an unreachable or unindexable page
is the failure the customer would otherwise discover weeks later, so the product
goes and fetches the page a day after it went out and shows every outcome,
failures included, rather than letting "published" mean only that a row was
written. Every page is now live at its destination, the customer's own WordPress
included (the owner's ruling of 2026-09-01, ADR-084), so all four checks run
everywhere — but at a site ReachKit does not serve it guarantees neither the
sitemap nor the robots policy that two of them read, and a result that blamed
this page for a condition of the site would mark every page on that site
permanently failed for something this requirement's own non-goal forbids
ReachKit fixing. So the result says what was measured and never blames the wrong
thing: a condition of the site is named as the site's, a page that is not at its
address is not reported as a publishing failure and is never blamed on the
customer, and nothing is asserted about a page from an answer that did not
settle the question. This one fetch is the only look ReachKit ever takes at a
published page — nothing revisits it afterwards — so what it records must be
what it actually saw, dated, and nothing more. BUILD.md §9 fixes the
check — "**Verification:** publish +24h → fetch the live URL, confirm
reachable/indexable/in-sitemap/AI-readable (chips in the day panel)" — and
BUILD.md §12 fixes what the customer is sent: "`published` (live URL + 24h
checks)". Placement: JN-003 step 7 ("Checks a day later that the page is
genuinely reachable, indexable and readable by AI assistants, and sees plainly
when one of those checks failed"). No implementation exists to touch: this repo
carries no `src/` (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No source code on disk yet").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a page published to a destination that serves it publicly, when 24
   hours have passed since it published, then its live address is fetched and
   the product records whether the page is reachable, is not marked against
   indexing, is present in the sitemap, and returns its whole page content to a
   crawler that runs no scripts and identifies as one the robots policy REQ-059
   serves permits.
2. Given verification has run, when the customer inspects that page, then each of
   those four outcomes is shown, with the date and time it was checked in the
   time zone the customer set.
3. Given a check the page itself did not pass, when the outcome is shown, then
   the failure is stated plainly rather than omitted or shown as pending; and in
   the 24 hours before the check runs, the four outcomes state that they have not
   been checked yet and when they will be, never that they passed or failed.
4. Given a page ReachKit's own record shows was unpublished or taken down before
   its check was due, when the customer inspects it, then no check is run and it
   states that the page was taken down before it could be verified. Given instead
   the check runs and the address answers, unambiguously, that there is no such
   page — a 404 or a 410, and no other answer — then this criterion governs the
   outcome and not criterion 3: it states that no page was found at that address
   when the check ran, carrying the date it was looked for; it names no cause and
   never says who removed the page or that the customer did; it is never stated
   as a failure of publishing or of any of the four checks; and no further check
   is run for that page. Given any other answer — the site could not be reached,
   the address returned a server error, it redirected away from the page, or what
   came back was not the page ReachKit published — then neither the four
   outcomes nor the page's absence is asserted: it states that the check could
   not be confirmed, carrying the date it was tried, the page is not recorded as
   no longer there, nothing is stated as a failure of this page, and no further
   check is run for it.
5. Given verification has completed, when the customer is told about that page,
   then one message carries its live address together with the outcome of all
   four checks, and it is sent on the same occasion whether every check passed
   or any failed — a failed check is never a reason to send nothing — naming
   which failed where any did and, where criterion 6 recorded a condition of the
   site, naming that separately from the page's own outcomes. Where criterion 4
   governed instead, that one message carries what criterion 4 recorded, with its
   date, in place of the four outcomes and never alongside them.
6. Given a check that did not pass at a destination ReachKit does not serve, when
   its outcome is shown, then it says whether what did not pass is this page or
   the site the page sits in. A site that answered that it publishes no sitemap,
   or whose own robots policy blocks the crawlers criterion 1 identifies as
   across that whole site, is recorded as a condition of that site — named,
   carrying the date it was found, and stated as the customer's own to act on —
   and no page on that site is marked as having failed a check for that cause,
   then or on any later page. A condition of the site is recorded only from an
   answer the site gave: where ReachKit could not reach what it went to read, or
   could not read what came back, no condition of that site is recorded, nothing
   about that site is stated as the customer's to act on, that one check is
   stated as not confirmed for this page alone, and no check is suppressed for
   any later page. A page absent from a sitemap the site does publish, or blocked
   where the rest of that site is not, is that page failing and criterion 3
   governs it.
7. Given criterion 4 recorded that no page was found at a page's address, or that
   its check could not be confirmed, when any product surface states that page's
   state, then that surface carries the recorded outcome and its date alongside,
   so no surface presents the page as live without also carrying what ReachKit
   saw when it looked; and no surface states as a fact about the page today
   anything beyond what that one check recorded on that date.

## Non-goals
- Repairing a failed check automatically.
- Changing anything inside a site ReachKit does not serve because a check
  failed there — criterion 6 names a condition of that site and leaves it with
  the customer.
- Going back to a site to see whether a condition criterion 6 recorded has been
  put right, outside the check criterion 1 runs for a page; and judging that
  site's sitemap or robots policy beyond what those four checks read.
- Watching a page for removal. Criterion 4 holds only for what the one check at
  24 hours found, and after it nothing goes looking again — not this
  requirement, not the weekly re-measurement (REQ-065), not the day panel
  (REQ-043 criterion 12). A page removed after that check goes on being shown and
  judged as it was, and ReachKit never learns of it.
- Saying why a page was not found, or who removed it: criterion 4 records an
  address and a date and attributes nothing. Telling a deletion from a plugin,
  a colleague or a misconfigured host is outside what one fetch can know.
- Looking again where criterion 4 could not confirm what it found. The check is
  not retried, rescheduled or repeated; "not confirmed" is a final outcome for
  that page, not a pending one.
- Traffic, ranking or conversion analytics for the page — the weekly verdict in
  REQ-063 is the only performance judgement.
- Re-verifying a page repeatedly after its first check.
- Whether the customer has switched the published-page mail off — REQ-075;
  criterion 5 fixes only that a failed check never changes whether it is sent.
