# REQ-098 — The sign-in screen — one address, one link, and no form that opens an account

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a returning customer on a device with no session, I want one screen that asks
for the address I pay with and sends me a link, and that tells me plainly when a
link I am holding no longer works, so that a product with no password never
leaves me with nowhere to go.

## Rationale
A returning customer on a new device, one who has just signed out, and one
holding a link that has expired or been spent must all have one screen to come to
that asks for the only credential this product has and says plainly that there is
no password and no signup — without it, `BUILD.md` §1's "magic-link auth" is a
promise with no surface behind it, and three approved criteria point at a screen
that does not exist: REQ-020 criteria 4 and 5, and REQ-077 criterion 5. The
owner's endorsement of 2026-09-02, the verbatim transcription every string in
criteria 2 and 4 is taken from, and the derivations taken under rule 1.1 are in
`registry/evidence/REQ-098.md`. The rule for a figure on a public surface — a
stored measurement with its date, or a declared example on a reserved domain —
has one home, REQ-099 criterion 8; criterion 5 cites it and restates none of it.
Placement: JN-004 gains one step for it, between "Moves the address they sign in
with, or signs out" and "Takes everything they have published away with them",
and its last step gains this requirement — JN-004 was already `in-review`, so no
approval was reopened. What it reaches: no code — this repo carries no `src/` —
but four approved blueprints and two work orders, each named with its clause in
the evidence file: BP-032 (`requestMagicLink` and its three answers), BP-061
(`redeemLink` and `signin.link_dead`), BP-001 (whose public route table declares
no sign-in route, and which WO-136 names as the route's owner) and BP-019 (the
copy registry holding every key the lines below are owed against).

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a person with no session, when they reach ReachKit's sign-in address,
   then the screen presents one email input and one submit control and nothing
   else to fill in: no password field, no social sign-in, and no control that
   opens an account. It is the screen anyone without a session meets — after
   signing out (REQ-077 criterion 5) and on any setup or app URL opened without
   a session (REQ-020 criterion 5) — whether they are brought to its address or
   shown it where they are.
2. Given the screen, when it renders, then it carries these strings verbatim: the
   heading "Welcome back"; the body "Enter the email you paid with and we'll send
   you a sign-in link. No password — accounts are created by payment, never by a
   signup form."; the email input's placeholder "you@company.com"; the submit
   control's label "Send my link"; and beneath that control the line "New to
   ReachKit? Start a free scan →", whose "Start a free scan →" is a link, and
   following it reaches the landing page where a free scan is started (REQ-001).
3. Given a person who submits an address, when they submit, then they are
   answered in writing on the same screen without being sent anywhere: an address
   with an open account is sent a link — including one whose plan lapsed long ago
   (REQ-076 criterion 5) — and an address with none is answered on REQ-020
   criterion 4's terms; and nothing on the screen before a submission reveals
   whether any address has an account (REQ-020 criterion 5).
4. Given the screen, when it renders, then alongside criterion 2's strings it
   carries these verbatim: the heading "One number tells you how findable you
   are."; and on one card beneath it the label "Discoverability Score", the pill
   "+6 pts est." and the line "Hard to find — and we'll show you the fixes that
   move it.". That card also shows a domain, a numeral, "/100" and a progress bar,
   and states no figure about a website beyond the numeral, the "/100", the pill
   and — where criterion 5's first case holds — the date the domain was measured.
5. Given every figure on that card — the numeral, the "/100", the pill, the bar
   and the band words — when a visitor reads them, then they meet REQ-099
   criterion 8's rule for a figure on a public surface: either every one of them
   was read from a measurement the product stored for the domain shown, and the
   card states that domain with the date of the scan they came from; or — where
   open question 2 makes the card an example — they are a declared example on the
   reserved domain and with the written line that criterion fixes. In neither
   case does this screen show a domain, score or band belonging to an
   identifiable customer: it renders to anyone, with no session.
6. Given a person who submits an empty value or one that is not a valid email
   address, when they submit, then no link is sent, one written line names what is
   wrong, and they stay on the screen with what they typed intact.
7. Given a person who opens a sign-in link that no longer works — expired; spent,
   because it was used once already or because a later link they asked for
   replaced it; or never issued by this product — when they open it, then they
   land on this screen and one written line tells them the link can no longer be
   used and that they may ask for another; that line and the time it takes are
   the same whatever the reason, so someone holding a link that is not theirs
   learns nothing about whether the address it was issued for has an account.

## Non-goals
- What the emailed link does when it is used, where it lands the customer, and
  which of several links a person asked for is the one that works — REQ-024
  criteria 1 and 4; criterion 7 owns only what this screen says when a link does
  nothing.
- Passwords, social sign-in, SSO, two-factor, or account recovery — REQ-077.
- What an address with no ReachKit account is told — REQ-020 criterion 4;
  criterion 3 routes to it and adds nothing to it.
- How long a link lives, how many may be requested, and what stops abuse of the
  request: criterion 7 owns only what the customer is told when a link no longer
  works.
- Moving the address an account signs in with — REQ-077.
- The landing page the "Start a free scan →" link reaches — REQ-099.
- What the score is, how it is computed, what its band words mean, and on what
  scale — REQ-004.
- Layout, panel widths, the gradient and the glass card, whether criteria 2 and
  4 render in one column or two at any of ADR-093's three bands, and whether a
  signed-out setup or app URL is redirected to this screen's address or shows
  it in place: the design system's and the blueprint's (`00-project.md`,
  standing law 1). Both sets of strings are carried at every band.

## Open questions
- [ ] Criterion 4's card is labelled "Discoverability Score". Nothing in this
      corpus gives the number a customer-visible name: REQ-004 criterion 1 fixes
      "a single score on a 0–100 scale" and the four band words, and names the
      number nowhere. Is "Discoverability Score" the product's name for it on
      every surface — the report header, Overview, the Monday mail — or copy
      local to this screen? (owner: user)
- [ ] What criterion 4's card is, once REQ-099's fourth open question — whether
      a declared example figure may stand on a public surface at all — is ruled.
      **A real scan:** a real business's score, domain and scan date on a page
      anyone can open, and the transcribed pill "+6 pts est." goes — an estimate
      is not a stored measurement (REQ-004 criterion 12). **A declared example**
      (available only if that ruling admits one): the pill stays, the transcribed
      domain becomes one RFC 2606 reserves, and the card carries one more written
      line saying it is an example. (owner: user)
- [ ] The written lines this screen owes, none of which the system may write (§1,
      decision-rights table). Criteria 2 and 4 are already the owner's,
      transcribed from the live page; these are written nowhere:
      1. what a person is told when a link has been sent (criterion 3);
      2. and 3. REQ-020 criterion 4's two lines — a payment held, and no account —
         already booked as owed by WO-127 against BP-019's `signin.payment_held`
         and `signin.no_account`;
      4. the line naming a missing or malformed address (criterion 6);
      5. the line for a link that no longer works (criterion 7), BP-019's
         `signin.link_dead`;
      6. where the question above makes the card an example, the line saying so
         (criterion 5).
      (owner: user)
