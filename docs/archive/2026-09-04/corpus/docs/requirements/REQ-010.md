# REQ-010 — A finished page is the trade for an email address, and follow-up stops at three per domain

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a founder who now knows I am missing from my market's biggest searches, I want
the first page you would write for me sent to my inbox, and a small, finite
number of follow-ups about that domain afterwards that stop the moment I
subscribe or ask them to, so that I leave with something publishable rather than
a diagnosis and a useful trade does not turn into a mailing list I regret.

## Rationale
A founder must leave with something publishable rather than a diagnosis, and the
exchange must be explicit in both directions before it is made: the card says
what is on offer — which page, which search it targets, which rival it takes
ground from, what shape it takes, and how many more were found behind it — and
the control says what the address will be used for, so a founder knows the whole
trade before they type. The page is written only once an
email address has been given, the rest of the report is never held back to force
the trade, an address once given is always honoured or the founder is told it was
not, and what follows is bounded — a small, finite number of follow-ups about
that one domain, never overlapping with follow-ups about another, stopping the
moment they subscribe or ask them to. Because a lead who scans several domains
may enter several sequences, the opt-out ends all of them and every one a later
delivery would otherwise start: it is the only bound on how much unsolicited mail
one address can receive. Why per-domain sequences must be non-concurrent and
suppressed address-wide is in `registry/evidence/REQ-011.md`. Placement: JN-001
steps 8 and 9. Sources: `BUILD.md` §4.1 module 5, the free page card — "title of
page 1 of N, target/beats/format rows, **"Email me the full page"**" — §4.2 (the
giveaway email, nurture cadence: "max 3 mails (24h/72h/168h), stops on
conversion") and §11 (`lead/nurture`). The rival and the format criterion 1 shows
are the opportunity's own evidence and type (§7, "trigger · evidence (query,
volume, rival, URL, position)" and the closed type enum). No implementation exists to
touch: this repo carries no `src/`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a completed report for which at least one page was found worth writing,
   when it renders, then it shows the title of the first page ReachKit would
   write, how many pages in total were found worth writing for that domain, the
   search that page targets, the rival that page would take ground from, the
   page's format, and one control to have the full page emailed — a control that
   asks for an email address and nothing else: no account, no password, no
   payment, no further field. Where the page was found without a rival to name,
   that row says so in writing rather than standing blank or naming an invented
   one (REQ-091 criterion 3).
2. Given no email address has been submitted for a report, when the report is
   viewed for any length of time, then no full page is written for it.
3. Given a visitor who submits their email address, when they submit, then the
   full page is written and delivered to that address, and the page is delivered
   to no other address. That delivery is a mail the founder cannot stop: it is
   the thing they asked for, sent once, in answer to their own submission.
4. Given the delivered email, when it is opened, then it contains the complete
   page in a copy-ready form, the target search with its monthly volume, and a
   line stating this is the first of the pages found for that domain.
5. Given a visitor who never submits an email address, when they use the report,
   then every other part of it remains fully readable.
6. Given a scan that derived no page worth writing, when the report renders,
   then it states in writing that no page is offered for this domain and shows
   no control to have one emailed; no page is invented to fill the slot.
7. Given an email address the report accepted, when the page cannot be written
   or the mail cannot be sent, then no confirmation of delivery is shown or sent
   for a page that was not sent, and the founder is told the page is not coming
   and why, in a written statement naming the cause: in writing where they
   submitted it if they are still on the report, and otherwise by one written
   message to the address they gave — a message they cannot stop, because it
   closes the request they made.
8. Given mail to the address the founder gave is itself what failed, when that
   failure is handled, then what is retried is whichever of the two things they
   are owed exists: the page itself where the page was written, and criterion
   7's message where it was not. Delivery to that address is retried for 24
   hours from the first attempt; if nothing has been delivered by then, no
   further attempt is made and nothing is shown or recorded as delivered.
9. Given a lead who received the free page for a domain and has not subscribed,
   when time passes, then at most three follow-up emails are sent for that
   domain, at 24, 72 and 168 hours after that domain's sequence begins
   (criterion 12), and none afterwards.
10. Given a lead who subscribes at any point, when they subscribe, then no
    further follow-up is sent to that address for any domain, and no later
    delivery starts one.
11. Given any of these emails, when it is opened, then it carries a working
    opt-out, and using it stops all future follow-up email to that address —
    every remaining touch of the running sequence, every sequence for every other
    domain, and any sequence a future delivery would otherwise start.
12. Given a domain's page has been delivered to an address, when that domain's
    sequence begins, then it begins no earlier than the delivery of that page and
    never while another sequence to that address is running or still waiting to
    run: at most one sequence to an address is ever running, and sequences to one
    address run in the order their pages were delivered, however many domains that
    address has. A sequence that has not begun within 7 days of its own page's
    delivery is dropped and never sent, so no follow-up about a domain ever
    reaches an address more than 14 days after that domain's page was delivered
    to it. A sequence ended by a subscribe (criterion 10) or an opt-out
    (criterion 11) releases nothing — those end the waiting sequences too, and no
    waiting sequence ever begins after either.
13. Given an address that has a sequence for a domain — running, waiting to
    begin, or already finished — when that domain's page is delivered to that
    address again, for any reason, then no second sequence for that domain is
    started and that domain's touches to that address stay capped at the three of
    criterion 9.
14. Given the control that asks for an email address, when it renders, then one
    written line beside it states, before the visitor submits anything, that the
    address will receive the page and up to three further emails about that
    domain and that they can stop those at any time. It is written text, not a
    further field or a thing to agree to (criterion 1), and it is neither
    conditional nor dismissible — a visitor never learns the size of the trade
    only from the first mail to arrive.

## Non-goals
- No account creation, password, or payment as part of the trade — stated as
  criterion 1, not left to this list.
- No second free page, page library, or regeneration of the page on request.
- No images in the delivered draft (`BUILD.md` §17).
- The set of causes a failure under criterion 7 can have, and which written line
  each gets: the blueprint's, derived from this requirement's promise that the
  founder is told both that the page is not coming and why.
- No follow-up beyond the three touches of a domain's sequence, and no second
  sequence for a domain that address already has one for, whether it is running,
  waiting, or finished.
- The scheduling rule that realises criterion 12 — what each sequence's start is
  anchored to, and how the sequences waiting behind a running one are held and
  released: the blueprint's, derived from this requirement's promise that one
  address never has two sequences running at once, never receives them out of
  the order their pages were delivered, and never receives a follow-up about a
  page delivered more than 14 days earlier.
- Whether any sentence in these emails may be model-written rather than written
  by us: REQ-093 is the single home for that rule.
- No behavioural targeting, open-based branching, or lead scoring.
