# REQ-092 — When the product stops its own work, it says so — never that there was nothing worth publishing

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer, I want to be told when ReachKit itself did not do the day's
work, so that I am never told there was nothing worth publishing when the truth
is that we stopped.

## Rationale
An empty day means one thing to the customer — that nothing in their market was
worth publishing (REQ-043) — so a day left empty because ReachKit reached a
spend cap, was halted, or had a step fail is a false statement about the
customer's own market. What must be true is that such a day carries the fact
that it was the product and not the market, the fact of whether anything is
needed from the customer, and the fact of when the work is expected back; that no
statement of when the next page publishes gives any reason for the absence but
ReachKit's stop, however true another reason may also be at that moment; that
work already prepared survives the stop; that a day whose work was cut short is
never presented as a complete one; and that none of what the customer reads hands
them the product's own internals in place of an account they can act on.
REQ-043 owns the wording of the line the calendar day carries and REQ-040 the
navigation's; this requirement owns the facts each must carry.
Source: `BUILD.md` §6.5 — "Caps degrade (skip remaining optional work, mark scan
`degraded`), never throw; money already spent is always ledgered"; §11 — "kill
switch env var stops scan+generate+publish"; §2.5 — "Empty/degraded states are
designed, never blank: a measurement that failed says so in one written
sentence; an empty queue is a success state ("Nothing worth publishing
today")".

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given no page was generated or published for a day because ReachKit reached a
   spend cap, was halted, or had a step fail — whether or not a page had been
   planned for that day before the stop — when the customer looks at that day,
   then what they read tells them it was ReachKit that did not do the work, and
   never that there was nothing worth publishing in their market.
2. Given such a day, when the customer reads it, then what they read also tells
   them whether anything is needed from them, and when nothing is, says so.
3. Given ReachKit has stopped its own work and today's page has not been
   produced, when the customer opens the app, then the screen they land on
   states it, without their having to open a calendar day or a settings screen,
   and it stops stating it once the work resumes.
4. Given the customer is told ReachKit did not do the work, when they read that
   statement, then it also tells them either the date the work is expected to
   resume or that no resumption time is promised — never neither.
5. Given ReachKit stopped its own work while pages were already prepared for the
   customer, when the work resumes, then every prepared page is still there and
   publishes on the normal schedule (REQ-056 criterion 8), and none is dropped or
   marked published.
6. Given ReachKit cut its own work short for a day and still produced a page,
   when the customer reads that day, then it states that part of the measurement
   behind that page was not done, and the day is never presented as a complete
   pass.
7. Given ReachKit has stopped its own work, when any statement of when the next
   page publishes renders — the navigation's among them, its line being REQ-040
   criterion 4's — then it names ReachKit's stop as the reason no publish is
   scheduled, and gives no other reason for it, whether or not that other reason
   is also true of the account at that moment.
8. Given the customer is told ReachKit did not do the work, when they read that
   statement wherever it renders, then it names no internal cause: no cap or
   spend amount, no error text and no system status detail appears anywhere in
   it.

## Non-goals
- The free scan's own bounds and refusals (REQ-003).
- The wording of the calendar day's line, which is REQ-043 criterion 4's; this
  requirement fixes the facts that line carries, not its sentence.
- Where the navigation's next-publish line sits, how it is worded, and which
  causes it may name when ReachKit has not stopped — the closed set is REQ-040
  criterion 4's — and Overview's at-most-two alerts for items that cannot
  proceed without the customer (REQ-041 criterion 5). Criterion 3 is neither of
  those, and criterion 7 fixes only what any statement of when the next page
  publishes must and must not say while ReachKit is stopped, wherever it renders
  — the navigation's line, the footer autopilot card's next-publish time and a
  mail's statement of what publishes next among them. Reconciling criterion 7's
  bar with REQ-040 criterion 4's set of causes belongs to the blueprint that
  builds that line.
- Pages held because a destination is disconnected or failing (REQ-074 criteria
  3 and 5); criterion 5 here reaches only pages held because ReachKit stopped.
- Which internal cause stopped the work: cap amounts, spend, error payloads and
  system status detail are never shown to the customer (criterion 8).
- Refunds or credits for a day the product did not work.
