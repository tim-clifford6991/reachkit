# REQ-011 — Follow-up stops at three emails per domain, and stops early on conversion or opt-out

> Superseded by REQ-010, which absorbed this requirement's acceptance criteria
> verbatim as its criteria 9–13 (2026-08-30; see
> `requirements/history/REQ-010-2026-08-30-requirement-cleanup.md`). Retired in
> place; cite REQ-010, not this file. The per-domain re-entry ruling (criterion 5
> → REQ-010 criterion 13) and the address-wide opt-out (criterion 3 → REQ-010
> criterion 11) are carried word for word.

- Priority: Should

## User story
As a founder who gave my email for one page, I want a small, finite number of
follow-ups about that domain — never two sets at once — that stop the moment I
subscribe or ask them to, so that a useful trade does not turn into a mailing
list I regret.

## Rationale
A founder who gave one email address for a page about one domain must receive a
small, finite number of follow-ups about that domain, never overlapping with
follow-ups about another, and stopping the moment they subscribe or ask them to
— an open-ended sequence misleads the lead who was promised a page, and a
customer who has already paid should never still be receiving the pitch.
Because a lead who scans several domains may enter several sequences, the
opt-out has to end all of them and every one a later delivery would otherwise
start: it is the only bound on how much unsolicited mail one address can
receive. Why per-domain sequences must be non-concurrent and address-wide
suppressed is in `registry/evidence/REQ-011.md`. Source: `BUILD.md` §4.2
(nurture cadence) and §11 (`lead/nurture`).

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a lead who received the free page for a domain and has not subscribed,
   when time passes, then at most three follow-up emails are sent for that
   domain, at 24, 72 and 168 hours after that domain's sequence begins
   (criterion 4), and none afterwards.
2. Given a lead who subscribes at any point, when they subscribe, then no
   further follow-up is sent to that address for any domain, and no later
   delivery starts one.
3. Given any of these emails, when it is opened, then it carries a working
   opt-out, and using it stops all future follow-up email to that address —
   every remaining touch of the running sequence, every sequence for every other
   domain, and any sequence a future delivery would otherwise start.
4. Given a domain's page has been delivered to an address, when that domain's
   sequence begins, then it begins no earlier than the delivery of that page and
   never while another sequence to that address is running or still waiting to
   run: at most one sequence to an address is ever running, and sequences to one
   address run in the order their pages were delivered, however many domains that
   address has. A sequence that has not begun within 7 days of its own page's
   delivery is dropped and never sent, so no follow-up about a domain ever
   reaches an address more than 14 days after that domain's page was delivered
   to it. A sequence ended by a subscribe (criterion 2) or an opt-out
   (criterion 3) releases nothing — those end the waiting sequences too, and no
   waiting sequence ever begins after either.
5. Given an address that has a sequence for a domain — running, waiting to
   begin, or already finished — when that domain's page is delivered to that
   address again, for any reason, then no second sequence for that domain is
   started and that domain's touches to that address stay capped at the three of
   criterion 1.

## Non-goals
- No follow-up beyond the three touches of a domain's sequence, and no second
  sequence for a domain that address already has one for, whether it is running,
  waiting, or finished.
- The scheduling rule that realises criterion 4 — what each sequence's start is
  anchored to, and how the sequences waiting behind a running one are held and
  released: the blueprint's, derived from this requirement's promise that one
  address never has two sequences running at once, never receives them out of
  the order their pages were delivered, and never receives a follow-up about a
  page delivered more than 14 days earlier.
- Whether any sentence in these emails may be model-written rather than written
  by us: REQ-093 is the single home for that rule.
- No behavioural targeting, open-based branching, or lead scoring.
