# REQ-060 — A customer on WordPress publishes to their existing site

- Priority: Should

## User story
As a paying customer who already runs WordPress, I want pages to be published on
my own site with their metadata filled in, so that I keep the blog I already
have instead of starting a second one.

## Rationale
Most customers with a site already have WordPress, and the promise is that they
keep the blog they already have: a page ReachKit delivers there is live on their
own site from the moment the delivery completes, with its SEO metadata already
filled in, readable at an address they can open, and theirs to take down at any
time. Source: the owner's ruling of 2026-09-01, recorded in ADR-084, that
content is published complete at every destination. Placement: JN-003 step 6
("Finds the page live the next morning on their own domain or in their own
WordPress") walks criteria 1 to 5, and walks criterion 7 on the morning the page
is not there — the destinations list the same criterion sends the customer to is
REQ-074's, walked at JN-004 step 3. Criterion 6 is walked by no step today:
it is the bulk promise the customer reaches when they go looking in their own
WordPress for everything ReachKit wrote there, which JN-003 step 6's one page on
delivery day does not cover, and JN-004 step 8 ("Pulls their pages off the web,
or closes the account for good") exercises REQ-079 alone. It is user-facing and
so takes a step rather than a rule 5.7 exemption: a step owed on JN-004 after
step 8, in which the customer opens their own WordPress after leaving and finds
every page ReachKit created there in one list, following the one place the
deletion mail named, with `exercises: [REQ-060, REQ-079]` — an edit that drops
JN-004 from `approved` to `in-review`. Neither journey is this pass's to edit. What any mail says about a
page bound here — including the one sent when the customer has left themselves no
window in which to stop it — is REQ-057's, in its criteria 7 and 9. No
implementation exists to touch: this repo carries no `src/` (`00-project.md`,
"Greenfield. No source code on disk yet").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer connects their WordPress site with an application password,
   when a page is published to that destination, then it is publicly readable on
   their site from the moment the delivery completes, at an address the customer
   can open, and no further action of theirs is needed to make it so.
2. Given a page delivered to the customer's WordPress, when the delivery
   completes, then the page reaches `published` — the same state, with the same
   meaning, as a page published to any other destination — its record names that
   WordPress site and the date of delivery, and on every product surface the
   page appears on it is shown as live until either the customer unpublishes it
   or ReachKit's one check finds no page at its address (REQ-062 criterion 4),
   whichever happens first. From the moment the customer unpublishes it, it is
   shown as unpublished, and what every surface says about the
   post inside the customer's own site is the outcome REQ-056 criterion 16 names
   for that page and never another: where the post was returned to draft, that it
   is no longer live there; where ReachKit did not reach the site, that it may
   still be live there, until a later ask succeeds and the surfaces follow. No
   surface says ReachKit returned the post to draft, or changed it in any other
   way, unless ReachKit wrote that into the site; a surface states that the post
   was not in the site only on the strength of a look ReachKit actually took and
   carries the date it looked, never as a claim about any later day, because
   ReachKit does not go back and look again; and no two surfaces say different
   things about the same page.
3. Given a WordPress site with Yoast or RankMath installed, when a page is
   published there, then the page's title and description are written into the
   fields of each of those plugins that is present.
4. Given a WordPress site with neither Yoast nor RankMath installed, when a page
   is published there, then it is still delivered, and one written line on that
   page's own record — the record criterion 2 describes, and on no other surface
   — says that no SEO plugin was found and that the title and description were
   not written into one.
5. Given a customer's WordPress application password, when it is stored, then it
   is encrypted at rest and never written to logs or shown back to anyone.
6. Given pages ReachKit has delivered to the customer's WordPress, when the
   customer looks for them in their own WordPress, following the one place
   ReachKit is able to tell them they are (REQ-079 criterion 6), then every page
   ReachKit created in that site comes up together in one list there, without
   their having to open posts one at a time or already know a page's title, and
   posts they wrote themselves are not in that list.
   Whatever makes this true is put on a post only by ReachKit creating it, and
   never by a later write into their site.
7. Given a connected WordPress site whose credential can create posts but cannot
   publish them, when that site is checked or a page would be delivered to it,
   then it reads as failing in the destinations list — the state REQ-074
   criterion 1 already carries — and what the customer is told there says that
   the site is connected but the account ReachKit uses on it cannot publish, and
   never that pages are being held and nothing has been lost; the action it
   offers leads to giving ReachKit a credential for an account that can publish
   there, and re-entering the same credential is never offered as the remedy. A
   delivery attempted against it fails for a
   reason no repeated attempt could clear, so the page comes to rest needing the
   customer (REQ-056 criterion 4) and is never shown as live, never carries a
   public address, and is never counted among the pages ReachKit published; and
   no post ReachKit reports as published is left standing as a draft in that
   site.

## Non-goals
- Webflow, Shopify, Ghost, Framer or Notion.
- SEO plugins other than Yoast and RankMath.
- Installing or configuring plugins on the customer's site.
- Uploading media, or editing any existing WordPress post — except returning to
  draft a post ReachKit itself made live there, when the customer unpublishes
  it (REQ-056 criterion 16), which changes nothing else about the post; a post
  ReachKit did not make live is never written into at all.
- A setting, at setup or in Settings, that delivers a page to WordPress for the
  customer to publish themselves rather than publishing it.
- Destroying credentials on disconnect — REQ-074.
- The destination states themselves and what the destinations list says in each
  of them — REQ-074 criterion 1; criterion 7 supplies one further occasion for
  the failing state, adds no state, and fixes only what must be true of what the
  customer is told and offered on that one occasion.
- The exact sentence the customer reads when the connected account cannot
  publish — owner-owed; criterion 7 fixes what it must and must not assert.
