# REQ-074 — A broken destination is a state the customer can fix, not an error loop

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer whose connection to my own site has stopped working — a
token that expired, or an account that is not allowed to publish there — I want
to see what has actually stopped and be offered the one action that can put it
right, so that the pages I paid for are recovered rather than lost and I am
never sent round an action that changes nothing.

## Rationale
The customer is paying for a page a day to reach their own site, so a
credential that expires must read to them as a state they can fix in one place,
with their pages waiting rather than lost — otherwise the product keeps
charging and produces nothing visible, which is the worst failure it can have
(BUILD.md §9, "expired credential is a **state** (reconnect prompt, queue
holds), not an error loop", credentials "encrypted at rest, never logged,
revoked on disconnect"; §4.7, "destinations list with health + Reconnect"). A
credential that has not expired but belongs to an account the customer's site
does not allow to publish breaks the same promise differently — the connection
is live and the page has already failed rather than been held — so it must read
as its own occasion inside the same failing state, be told truthfully, and be
offered an action that can change the outcome (criterion 8); its WordPress form,
and what becomes of the delivery itself, are REQ-060 criterion 7's. Placement:
JN-004 step 3 ("Changes how and when pages go out, and reconnects a destination
that stopped working"), which walks every criterion here. No implementation
exists to touch: this repo carries no `src/` (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No
source code on disk yet").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer on Settings, when they open the destinations list, then
   each connected destination shows its current state — working, needs
   reconnecting, or failing — and the date it was last checked, which is never
   more than 24 hours old whether or not a publish was attempted in between.
2. Given a destination whose credentials have expired, when the customer opens
   Settings, Overview or the calendar, then a Reconnect action is offered
   alongside one written line saying that pages are being held and nothing has
   been lost.
3. Given the customer completes a reconnect, when it succeeds, then the
   destination reads as working and the customer is told how many pages are
   waiting; where publishing is on, every held page publishes in the order it
   was held at the normal rate of at most one a day and none is dropped; where
   publishing is switched off (REQ-070 criterion 1), none of them publishes and
   they stay held, listed and exportable until the customer switches it on.
4. Given the customer disconnects a destination, when the disconnect completes,
   then its stored credentials are destroyed, pages already published to it stay
   live, and nothing further is sent to it.
5. Given every destination is disconnected or failing, when a page would have
   published, then the publish attempt fails and the page comes to rest in the
   state REQ-056 defines for an item needing the customer — reached by REQ-056
   criterion 4's limb for a reason no repeated attempt could clear, so no further
   automatic attempt is made — and the customer sees it under the Needs you stage
   (REQ-043 criterion 2); no page is silently dropped or marked published.
6. Given a destination has needed reconnecting for 24 hours and the customer
   has not signed in since it broke, when those 24 hours pass, then one
   `account` mail (REQ-064) is sent to them saying pages are being held, how
   many, and that reconnecting releases them; and no further mail is sent about
   that destination until it is reconnected and breaks again.
7. Given a destination's stored credentials, when the customer reads any screen,
   written line, mail or export ReachKit produces, then no part of the
   credential and no vendor error payload appears in it; a broken connection is
   stated as the state in criterion 1 and the action in criterion 2, and never
   as a technical message the customer is asked to interpret.
8. Given a destination whose stored credential is accepted but whose account is
   not allowed to publish there, when the customer opens Settings, Overview or
   the calendar, then the destination reads as failing (criterion 1) and what
   they are told says the connection is live and that the account ReachKit uses
   on it is not allowed to publish there; nothing they read on this occasion
   says that pages are being held or that nothing has been lost, because the
   page that could not go out is one criterion 5 brings to rest needing them.
   The action offered leads to giving ReachKit a credential for a different
   account that can publish there, and re-entering the credential already stored
   is never offered as the remedy; where the customer supplies that same
   credential anyway, the destination does not read as working, nothing is
   published to it, and they are told the account still cannot publish.

## Non-goals
- Connecting a destination for the first time, which is setup's and the
  publishing flow's (REQ-028, BUILD §9).
- Publishing idempotency and the retry state machine (BUILD §9).
- Adding destination types beyond the hosted blog and WordPress (BUILD §17,
  "Webflow/Shopify/Ghost/Framer/Notion").
- More than one destination of the same kind per site.
- Exposing credential storage, tokens or error payloads to the customer —
  criterion 7 carries that promise; how credentials are stored and logged is the
  blueprint's (BUILD §9).
- Publishing a backlog faster than the normal rate — a held queue drains at one
  page a day and is never caught up in a burst (REQ-056 criteria 8 and 13).
- Whether a mail may be sent at all — REQ-064 criterion 6 states that rule, and
  the register of kinds is the table REQ-064's own non-goals place with the mail
  blueprint. Criterion 6 above supplies the occasion and `account` is the kind it
  carries; adding a kind changes that table, not this requirement.
- Where the switch that turns publishing off lives and what turns it off —
  REQ-070 criterion 1 and REQ-079 criterion 5.
- The exact sentence the customer reads when a connected account is not allowed
  to publish, and the words on the action that leads them to a different
  account — both owner-owed; criterion 8 fixes what each must and must not do.
- Establishing whether an account is allowed to publish, and when that is
  looked for; criterion 8 fixes only what the customer reads and is offered
  once it is known.
