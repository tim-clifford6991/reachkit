# REQ-059 — Pages publish to the customer's own domain, and never rank on ours

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer without a blog, I want the pages ReachKit writes to live on
my own domain and be findable by search engines and AI assistants, so that the
value accrues to my business and never to ReachKit's.

## Rationale
The customer is the publisher of record: their pages must be served from their
own domain, be structurally findable and citable by search engines and AI
assistants, and never rank for ReachKit instead of for them — and a page must
never be reported as live at an address no visitor can reach. BUILD.md §9 fixes
the address and the policy — "**Hosted CMS:** `content.{customer-domain}` by
CNAME → our edge route serves static-rendered pages by Host header. Sitemap,
canonical, `FAQPage` schema where FAQ exists, and a robots.txt **we serve** that
allows GPTBot, ClaudeBot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot,
Google-Extended. Preview at `{slug}.reachkit.app` is `noindex` **forever**
(site-reputation-abuse guardrail — customer content never ranks on our domain)" —
and BUILD.md §14.6 fixes whose the content is: "Customer is publisher of record:
their domain, their identity." When that hosting ends, and what the customer is
told before it does, belongs with leaving rather than with publishing: REQ-076
criteria 10 and 11. Placement: JN-003 step 7.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given the customer has pointed `content.` on their own domain at us with the
   DNS record we give them, when a page publishes to the hosted destination,
   then it is served at that address on their domain.
2. Given the hosted destination's DNS record is unset, still propagating, or
   pointing elsewhere, when a page is due to publish there, then no page is
   published or recorded as live at that address, and the destination is not
   working in the sense of REQ-074 criterion 1 — which holds its pages, shows the
   customer that it is waiting on their DNS, and offers the record to set.
3. Given a published hosted page, when a search engine or AI crawler fetches it,
   then it is listed in a sitemap, declares its canonical address on the
   customer's own domain, and — where the page has a question-and-answer section
   — is marked up as such.
4. Given the robots policy we serve for the hosted destination, when any crawler
   reads it, then it blocks no general search engine crawler and permits by name
   GPTBot, ClaudeBot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and
   Google-Extended.
5. Given a page served at a ReachKit preview address (`{slug}.reachkit.app`),
   when any crawler fetches it, then it is marked as not to be indexed,
   permanently, and no setting can change that.

## Non-goals
- Per-customer page templates or theming in this version.
- Selling, aggregating or cross-linking customers' content on ReachKit
  properties.
- Custom domains, or a subdomain label other than `content.`.
- Which state a held page occupies and how a destination's health is shown —
  REQ-074, over REQ-056's states.
- Indexing and takedown of ReachKit's own public pages, including report pages —
  REQ-001 criterion 8 and REQ-002 criterion 3.
- Whether ranking data for the customer's root domain returns rows for pages
  served on `content.`: that assumption has one home, REQ-041's `rests-on`, where
  the surface it would go blind lives ("so pages ReachKit publishes register as
  the customer's own wins on the growth chart"). Nothing this requirement
  promises — the address, the canonical, the robots policy, the permanent
  `noindex` on preview — changes with its outcome.
- When hosted serving ends, what a visitor gets once it has, and who is told
  before it does — REQ-076 criteria 10 and 11, which own leaving.
- Keeping the customer's content available to them after serving stops — export
  remains available with no end date, REQ-078.
- Page typography and template layout — design system.
