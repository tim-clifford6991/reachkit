# REQ-043 — The calendar shows one page a day while supply lasts, is never padded, and selecting a day says why that page exists

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer with no SEO knowledge, I want a month view showing the one
page planned for each day and its stage, and selecting a day to tell me why that
page is on my calendar and offer only the actions that make sense for it right
now, so that I can see the whole plan at a glance, trust that every day tells me
the truth about why it is full or empty, and decide in seconds without learning a
discipline.

## Rationale
The promise sold is "one page a day", so every day carries one for as long as
there is anything worth writing about — while any qualifying opportunity remains
unused, not only when a new measurement produces one. The guardrails that keep
that promise honest are that a day with no evidence behind it stays empty rather
than being filled with a page nobody needed, that every date gives exactly one
account of itself, and that no account is false: a day emptied by any cause other
than exhausted supply never reads as the market having offered nothing, and an
unrecognised cause cannot inherit that line. The day panel is the other half of
the same screen — a customer who cannot say why a page is being written for them
cannot judge it, a page presented without its evidence reads as guesswork, and an
action offered that cannot succeed teaches them not to trust the panel. Which
cause produces which line, and which publish state belongs in which stage, are
the blueprint's to fix from the requirements that own each cause; this
requirement fixes only that every date has one account, that no account lies, and
that the panel repeats that account rather than minting a second. Criterion 3's
line is the honest account of a date whose supply ran out; it is not a verdict that
running out is fine, and what an exhausted supply raises for the customer beyond
the date itself is REQ-095's. The calendar is also where a page is found again
after it has gone out: pages ReachKit publishes are live on the customer's own
site without their having done anything, so the one place they can always come
back to — by the day the page went out — must tell them where it stands, take
them to it where it now lives — as a visitor reads it, and, for a page in their
own WordPress, inside their own WordPress — and let them
take it down. What the detail can honestly say about whether either way through
still opens anything is bounded by the only look ReachKit ever takes at a
published page: the single check at 24 hours (REQ-062 criterion 4). Placement:
JN-003 steps 1, 2 and 8, and step 9 ("Finds a page again on the calendar …"),
which is where both of criterion 12's ways through are walked. Source: `BUILD.md` §4.6 (one event per day, every day
filled while supply lasts, weekends included, stage = chip colour; "when
opportunities run out, future days are empty and the empty state says so — the
calendar is never padded"; day panel: "Why this page" — search / asked /
answered-today-by / you / done-when — plus stage-appropriate actions, one
provenance line, and today selected on open). No implementation exists to touch:
this repo carries no `src/` (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No source code on disk
yet").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a month view, when it renders, then every date it shows that is today or
   later carries a page while any opportunity a page can be written from remains
   that no page has yet been created from — a date is left empty only where that
   supply is exhausted, never because the day was not reached for; no date carries
   more than one page; any day of the week may carry one, weekends included; and
   no page is created to occupy a date that no such opportunity backs.
2. Given a date carrying a page, when it renders, then it states which stage that
   page is in — Live, Your review, Scheduled, Planned or Needs you — and no page
   renders without a stage.
3. Given no page is on a date because the supply of opportunities was exhausted
   and nothing in the customer's market was worth publishing, when that date
   renders — whether it is still to come or already past — then it carries no
   page, carries one written line saying there was nothing worth publishing then,
   and no page is created to occupy it. No date emptied by any other cause,
   whether or not a requirement names that cause, ever carries that line.
4. Given a date carrying no page for any cause other than criterion 3's exhausted
   supply — a page that can no longer go live, a day ReachKit did not do the work,
   a change the customer saved that holds pages back, or any cause whatever —
   when that date renders, then it carries no page and exactly one written line,
   and that line says which cause emptied it.
5. Given a date carrying no page, when the customer looks at it, then it carries
   exactly one account of itself and no second one; and where an instruction for
   the customer to carry out stands against that date (REQ-047 criterion 5), that
   instruction is the date's one account while it is outstanding and the date is
   never presented as there having been nothing worth publishing, and once they
   have carried it out the date carries the account criterion 3 or 4 gives it.
6. Given pages spread across several stages, when the customer selects a stage,
   then the view narrows to that stage, and every stage shows how many items it
   holds.
7. Given the customer opens the Calendar, when the day detail first renders, then
   today is the selected day.
8. Given a selected day holding a page, when the detail renders, then it states
   the search the page targets, who currently answers that search, where the
   customer stands on it, how winnable that search is judged to be, and what
   would count as the page having worked.
9. Given a selected day, when the detail renders, then every action it offers can
   be carried out in that page's current stage, and no action is offered that
   would be refused because of that stage.
10. Given the measured values shown in the detail, when it renders, then one
    written line states the date they were measured, and that date is not
    repeated separately beside each value.
11. Given a selected day holding no page, when the detail renders, then it states
    which kind of empty day it is, carrying the one account criterion 5
    gives that date and no other, whatever emptied it, and it offers no action that
    would publish or approve a page.
12. Given a page ReachKit has delivered to a destination, when the customer
    selects its day, then the detail is where they can, without opening any other
    screen: read which stage that page is in (criterion 2); take one way through
    to the page as a visitor sees it, at its public address; take, for a page
    delivered to their own WordPress, a second way through offered separately
    from the first, whose address is inside their own WordPress and opens that
    post there rather than at its public address; and, for as long as it is
    published, unpublish it (REQ-056 criterion 7). Where ReachKit's own record
    already says a way through leads nowhere — it unpublished the page itself
    (REQ-056 criterion 7), or its one check found no page at the public address
    (REQ-062 criterion 4), or no address inside that WordPress can be formed for
    that post — the detail says so in place of that way rather than offering it.
    Where the record says nothing against a page, the way through is offered and
    the detail claims only that this is where the page was put, never that it is
    there now. This holds for a page published on any earlier day, not only the
    most recent one.

## Non-goals
- More than one page on any day.
- Editing a published page's content, title or address at its destination from
  this view — criterion 12's second way through hands the customer over to their
  own WordPress, and what they may do to the post once they are there, under
  whichever WordPress user they are signed in as, is theirs and not ReachKit's
  to promise.
- Looking at a published page to find out whether it is still there. Opening the
  day detail fetches neither address, now or ever; criterion 12 speaks only from
  what ReachKit already recorded, and REQ-062 criterion 4's check at 24 hours is
  the only look at a published page there is.
- Which cause produces which of criterion 4's lines, and which publish state
  (REQ-056 criterion 1) is shown as which of criterion 2's stages — the exhaustive
  mapping is the blueprint's, drawn from the requirements that own each cause and
  each state; this requirement fixes only that every date has one account, that no
  account is false, and that no page renders without a stage.
- The wording of each line, and the facts a day's line must carry where the
  product did not do the work — REQ-092 owns those.
- Which empty date an outstanding instruction is placed on, where it comes from
  and what it asks — REQ-047's, and the placement rule is the blueprint's; this
  requirement fixes only that an instruction is a date's one account while it
  stands.
- The order in which unused supply fills future days — REQ-047 criterion 4;
  criterion 1 fixes only that no future date is left empty while supply remains.
- How deep supply must be, when a customer short of it is told, and what an
  exhausted supply raises beyond the date's own line — REQ-095's; criterion 3 fixes
  only the account an exhausted date carries.
- Editing the target search, slug or title of a planned page from this view.
- Approval workflows, comments, assignment or approval chains, assignees, or
  shared content calendars beyond this view.
- Grid track sizing, chip colours, panel widths, stickiness, badge tone,
  typography and today's ring — design system.
- Presenting the day detail as a drawer or modal is a design decision, not a
  requirement.
- Calendar export or subscription feeds.
