# REQ-080 — Changing the site's domain measures the new one, and never reads as movement

> Superseded by REQ-071, which absorbed this requirement's acceptance criteria
> verbatim (2026-08-30; see `requirements/history/REQ-071-2026-08-30-requirement-cleanup.md`).
> Retired in place; cite REQ-071, not this file. Its open REVIEW line moved with
> criterion 4 and now stands on REQ-071 criteria 12 and 13.

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer whose site has moved to a different address, I want ReachKit
to measure the new domain and to keep what it measured about the old one
separate, so that changing my address never reads as my business collapsing and
nothing is measured for a site that is no longer mine.

## Rationale
The domain is the subject of every measurement the product holds, so changing it
makes every earlier number a statement about a different website: what must be
true is that the customer is told before saving what starts again and when, that
the new domain is measured on the ordinary weekly cadence rather than on demand,
that nothing measured for the previous domain is presented as the new one's or as
movement across the change, and that pages already published stay the customer's.
The control itself is REQ-070 criterion 1's ("the domain the site is measured and
published under"), the
weekly event that carries the re-measurement out is REQ-065's, and the treatment
of history follows the cold-start law (`BUILD.md` §6.6, "every derivation in the
product must work for a domain that ranks for nothing") and the discipline
REQ-071 already applies to a rebuilt question set. Placement: JN-004 step 2,
beside the market and rival corrections it stands with as the changes on that
screen with teeth. No implementation exists to touch — this repo carries no
`src/` (`00-project.md`, "Greenfield. No source code on disk yet").

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer changing the domain their site is measured and published
   under, when they enter a new address, then it is not accepted unless the
   product can reach it (REQ-021 criterion 5, "does not accept it until an
   address it can reach is given"), and before they save, one written line states
   that measurement starts again against the new address at the next weekly
   re-measurement, names the date of that re-measurement, and states that what
   was measured for the previous address is kept and not carried across. The date
   that line names at the moment the customer saves is the date criterion 2 makes
   the change effective on: the customer is never shown one date and given
   another.
2. Given the customer saves a different domain, when the first weekly
   re-measurement that begins after the moment of the save runs, then the site is
   measured against the new domain from then on, and no measurement of the new
   domain is run before it.
3. Given a domain change is saved, when the days before that re-measurement
   pass, then no new page is generated from an opportunity derived for the
   previous domain, and each of those days carries one written line saying the
   domain change is why and naming the date that hold ends — never REQ-043
   criterion 3's "nothing worth publishing then". A draft already in review keeps
   the window it entered with, can still be stopped, and where it is not stopped
   it publishes under REQ-057's ordinary rule to the destination the site holds
   at that moment.
4. Given measurements taken for the previous domain, when the customer reads any
   number, chart or rival distance after the change, then each carries the domain
   it measured and the date it was taken, nothing measured for the previous
   domain is presented as a measurement of the new one, the difference across the
   change is not presented as movement — each affected number carrying its goal
   in place of a change (REQ-041 criterion 4, "either its change since the
   previous measurement or the goal it is moving toward") — any chart or series
   drawn across the change states that the domain changed on that date, and any
   count of weeks the product states for the site counts from the first
   measurement of the new domain.
5. Given pages published before the change, when the domain changes, then each
   stays published where it was published and stays exportable (REQ-078), keeps
   the last verdict it received with that verdict's date, and receives no further
   working, too early or not working verdict for as long as the site's domain is
   not the one that page's recorded test was written against: it is "marked as no
   longer judgeable" (REQ-063 criterion 6), with one written line saying the
   domain changed.

## Non-goals
- Re-measuring on demand or at the moment of the save — REQ-065's non-goal,
  "Re-measuring on demand, on save, or more than once in a week".
- A second proof that the customer controls the domain: the change is admitted
  by criterion 1's reachability check, and publishing to the new domain still
  waits on the customer's own DNS record (REQ-059 criteria 1 and 2). REQ-002
  criterion 2's proof governs the removal of a public report, and REQ-002's
  non-goals keep that criterion the single home for what proving control of a
  domain means.
- Moving, redirecting, re-publishing or taking down pages already published under
  the previous domain.
- Carrying the previous domain's measurements forward as the new domain's, under
  any option offered to the customer.
- Measuring or holding more than one domain per account (REQ-070).
- Where the control sits on Settings and what else that screen offers (REQ-070),
  and changing the address the customer signs in with (REQ-077).
- Whether a destination is ready to receive a page once criterion 3's hold ends:
  a hosted destination still waiting on the customer's DNS record holds its pages
  under REQ-059 criterion 2 and shows that state under REQ-074 criterion 1. This
  requirement states only when the domain change stops holding generation, never
  that nothing else can.
- Which pages carry no verdict at all across the change, and what a verdict line
  says for a page that never received one — REQ-063 criterion 6 owns the verdict
  and its written line; criterion 5 here states only the customer's promise that
  a page's old verdict is never restated as a judgement of the new domain.

## Open questions
- [ ] REVIEW(untestable: criterion 4): the limb "each affected number carrying its goal in place of a change" is scoped here to "any number, chart or rival distance", but the goal it cites (REQ-041 criterion 4) is defined only for a headline number on Overview, and REQ-041's non-goal pins that value per headline number. A rival distance or a point on a charted series therefore has no goal to carry, leaving the limb with no pass condition for most of the numbers criterion 4 covers.
