# REQ-050 — A generated draft passes every hard rule or is never queued

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer who is the publisher of record, I want every draft stopped
before it reaches me unless it is grounded in something true about my business,
honest about who wrote it, useful to the reader on arrival and not a repeat of a
page I already have, so that nothing published in my name is filler,
misrepresentation, or a page competing with my own.

## Rationale
One list of hard rules decides whether a generated draft may be put in front of
the customer at all: it stands on a statement read from one of their own live
pages and kept word for word; every figure and every person it reports is one
the reader can open a source for; it answers the search before it names the
brand, and carries nothing written for a machine rather than a reader; and it is
not a near-copy of a page the customer already has or already has waiting. A
draft failing any of them is never queued, and the item takes the one recovery
path REQ-053 criterion 3 defines for every rule on this list. None of these rules
is ever turned on words the customer writes themselves. Placement: JN-003 step 4
("Opens the full draft and reads it end to end"). Evidence:
`registry/evidence/REQ-050.md`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a draft is generated, when it is queued for review, then it carries at
   least one statement of fact drawn from a page on the customer's own live site,
   stored with that page's URL, the date it was read and the passage it was drawn
   from copied word for word from the content fetched that day; the passage is
   kept unchanged from then on, so the statement can still be read against it
   after the customer's own page has moved on.
2. Given a draft states any figure or claim about a competitor, when it is
   queued, then that statement links the public page it was read from, and no
   competitor figure appears that has no such page to link.
3. Given a draft is queued for review, when it is checked, then it states no
   figure that only ReachKit holds — a ranking position, a search volume, a
   visibility or answer count the product measured — so every figure a reader
   meets on the published page is one they can open a source for.
4. Given a draft is generated, when it is queued for review, then the only author
   it may attribute itself to is the customer — the business name or
   account-holder name recorded for their site — and it carries no other byline,
   no author biography and no persona; and every person it names or quotes other
   than the customer themselves is named in a public page the draft links. The
   customer's own attribution needs no such link.
5. Given a draft is generated, when it is queued for review, then it carries no
   testimonial, review, endorsement or reported customer remark, whether or not
   it names a speaker, unless the draft links a public page carrying that same
   remark.
6. Given a draft is generated, when it is checked, then the customer's brand — the
   business name and the domain recorded for their site — is not named, in any
   letter case, within the first 300 characters of the page as a reader reads it:
   the words the published page shows, not the Markdown syntax that produces them.
7. Given a draft is generated, when it is checked, then the text ReachKit would
   hand to the destination — the draft's Markdown and any markup embedded in it —
   carries nothing a reader of the published page would not see: no embedded
   markup positioning text off-screen, sizing it to zero or colouring it to match
   its background, no comment, and no alternative text, caption or title text
   carrying anything but a description of what it labels. A hyperlink and the
   address it points at are not text this criterion restricts — criteria 2 and 4
   both require a draft to carry links.
8. Given a draft is generated, when it is checked, then it carries no sentence
   addressed to a machine reader rather than a human one — no instruction,
   directive or assertion aimed at an assistant, a crawler or a ranking system
   about how to treat, cite, rank or recommend the page.
9. Given a candidate page, when it is compared against every page on the
   customer's own site the product has published or measured and against every
   draft already queued for them and not yet published, then it is queued only if
   it is less than 85 per cent similar to each of them.
10. Given a draft that fails any of criteria 1 to 9, when the pipeline finishes,
    then the draft is not queued for review, no part of it is published or handed
    to a destination, no page takes its day, and the item follows the hard-rule
    recovery path (REQ-053 criterion 3).
11. Given the site has no recorded business name or account-holder name to
    attribute to, when a page publishes, then it publishes with no author
    attribution rather than a generated one.
12. Given the customer edits a draft, when the edited text names their brand
    within its first 300 characters, then the edit is saved and neither held nor
    stopped for it: criteria 6 to 8 bind what ReachKit generates, not what the
    customer writes on their own site.
13. Given any two comparisons, when similarity is computed, then it is computed by
    the same single recorded measure, so the same pair of pages always returns the
    same verdict.
14. Given a candidate the gate rejected that the customer had already been shown
    on a day, when that day's line is produced (REQ-043 criterion 4), then the
    cause available to it is that the candidate duplicated an existing page,
    together with the page it duplicated, so the day never empties without an
    explanation.

## Non-goals
- Facts about the customer sourced from anywhere other than their own live
  pages.
- Requiring a different grounded fact for every draft.
- Displaying the grounded fact — that promise lives in REQ-045.
- Fact-checking claims a customer writes themselves during an edit.
- Author profiles, team pages or contributor management.
- Removing or rewriting a person, quotation or testimonial the customer
  themselves adds during an edit — criteria 4 and 5 bind what ReachKit generates.
- Where the customer's name is attached to a published page and how it renders —
  the blueprint's; criteria 4 and 11 fix only that it is theirs or nobody's.
- How a destination's own theme renders the page it was handed (REQ-060) — this
  check binds the text ReachKit produces, not the site it lands on.
- Enforcing a house structure or word count on the page.
- Making a page more likely to be cited by structuring it well, which the product
  does deliberately — criterion 8 bars only text that speaks to the machine
  instead of the reader.
- Comparing against pages published by anyone other than this customer.
- Rewriting a rejected candidate to slip under the threshold.
- A customer-adjustable similarity threshold.
- Surfacing candidates that never reached a calendar day.
- The wording of the day's line — REQ-043 owns the one line a day carries; this
  requirement owns only the cause it can carry for a near-duplicate.
