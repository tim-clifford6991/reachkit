# REQ-003 — The scan ends at a bounded moment — a report inside 60 seconds, or one written line naming the limit it met

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a founder waiting on a scan — of my own domain, of a few domains in one
sitting, or one that arrived while the free funnel was full — I want to see what
is being measured and get either the answer in under a minute or a plain
statement of which limit I met, so that I do not give up on a page that looks
stuck and do not read a bounded product as a broken one.

## Rationale
A stranger with no reason to trust us abandons an unlabelled wait, so the wait
must name what is being measured, end inside a minute, and — however slowly a
third party's server answers, and whichever of the free path's bounds it runs
into — end at a bounded moment in a report or a written line saying what
happened and how long it lasts, never in a hang, a crash, a half-built page, or
another visitor's scan. Every ceiling in this requirement gives back what was
measured rather than throwing away the wait, and the spend ceiling is the one
bound the product never trades away: reaching it degrades the scan even where
the work it cuts off is what the score is composed of, because the money is a
hard bound and the verdict is not. Evidence for the sixty-second
figure, the live mode it buys and the ninety-second ceiling derived from it is in
`registry/evidence/REQ-003.md`; the evidence that a spend ceiling degrades a scan
rather than failing it is in `registry/evidence/REQ-015.md`; research on the DataForSEO
endpoints and pricing is in `registry/evidence/RESEARCH-dataforseo-endpoints.md`. Placement: JN-001
step 2. Sources: `BUILD.md` §11 (the `scan/run` job, the free path's bounds and
the fail-open / fail-closed split), §4.1 States (scanning, degraded), §6.5
("Caps degrade (skip remaining optional work, mark scan `degraded`), never
throw") and §0's fourth law, lean data. No implementation exists to touch: this
repo carries no `src/`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a scan is running — however it started: a submission on the landing
   page, a first visit to a shared report address, a re-scan a visitor started
   from a stored report (REQ-001 criteria 14 and 15), or a re-measurement after a
   market correction — when the visitor watches the page, then it shows named
   stages that advance as work completes, never an unlabelled spinner or an
   indeterminate bar alone.
2. Given any 100 consecutive free scans of reachable domains, counted in the
   order they started and whichever of criterion 1's ways started each of them,
   when the time from each scan's start to a readable report is measured,
   then no more than 5 of those 100 take longer than 60 seconds, and none takes
   longer than the 90 seconds of criterion 5.
3. Given a scan that completes while the visitor is watching, when it finishes,
   then the report replaces the progress view without the visitor reloading.
4. Given a scan that cannot complete at all, when it ends, then the visitor is
   told in one written line that the measurement failed and is offered a manual
   retry (the retry window is REQ-001's).
5. Given a scan still running past 60 seconds — including when the delay is the
   scanned domain's own server answering slowly — when it reaches 90 seconds,
   then measuring stops and the visitor is shown the report of everything
   measured by that point, with the rest reported as unmeasured (REQ-004). No
   visitor waits on a running scan beyond 90 seconds.
6. Given a visitor who has already started five free scans from the same network
   within the last hour — every way a scan starts counting against that
   allowance (criterion 1), except a re-measurement after a market correction,
   which belongs to the scan it corrects and consumes nothing further — when
   another scan would start for them, then it does not start, they are refused
   with one written line saying how long until they may scan again, and the
   refused scan, having never run, produces no report of its own.
7. Given a scan is already running from the visitor's network, when another scan
   would start for them, then no second scan starts; if the running scan is of
   the domain they asked for they are returned to it, and if it is not they are
   refused in writing, told how long until they may scan, and are never shown a
   scan or a report of a domain they did not ask for.
8. Given the day's free-scan ceiling has been reached, or scanning has been
   switched off, when a visitor tries to scan, then one written line tells them
   free scanning is paused — stating how long until it resumes when the day's
   ceiling is the cause, and promising no time when scanning has been switched
   off — and every already-stored report stays readable.
9. Given the scan limiter itself is unavailable, when a visitor starts a scan,
   then the scan proceeds.
10. Given lead capture is unavailable, when a visitor submits their email address
    for the free page, then the submission is refused in writing and no
    confirmation of delivery is shown.
11. Given a free scan that reaches its spend ceiling while it is running, when it
    ends, then the ceiling is never exceeded to finish any outstanding
    measurement, whether or not a driver of the score depends on it: all
    remaining work is skipped, and the visitor is shown the report of everything
    measured up to that point, the skipped parts reading as not measured and the
    score reading as REQ-004 criterion 9 requires, with one written line stating
    the scan stopped early and the report is incomplete — never an error, a
    hang, or a refusal. That one ceiling bounds the scan and the re-measurement a
    market correction runs for it (REQ-094 criterion 3) together — a correction
    opens no second ceiling of its own — so a correction that reaches it ends the
    same way, in a corrected report that says it stopped early.
12. Given a visitor refused under criterion 6 or 7 at the report address of a
    domain with no stored report, when the address loads, then it shows that
    refusal in writing and starts no scan — never a blank page, a 404, or an
    error (REQ-001 criterion 5) — and once the limit has passed the same address
    starts a scan for them, unless that domain's report was removed at its
    owner's request (REQ-002 criterion 3), when it never does and what loads is
    what REQ-001 criterion 18 states. Given a visitor refused under criterion 6
    or 7 at the report address of a domain that does have a stored report — the
    address where the re-scan control of REQ-001 criteria 14 and 15 is used —
    when the address loads, then that stored report is shown there under its own
    measurement date (REQ-001 criterion 17) with the refusal in writing beside
    it, and no scan starts; once the limit has passed, using that same control
    starts a scan.

## Non-goals
- No queue position, percentage estimate, or countdown timer.
- No waitlist or captcha on the free scan.
- No emailed "your report is ready" notification for the free scan.
- No background continuation for a visitor who closes the page mid-scan.
- No account, sign-in, or paid tier that raises the free allowance.
- No spend, cost or cap figure shown to a visitor.
- No effect of these bounds on a paying customer's scans (`BUILD.md` §11 bounds
  are the free path's).
- The order in which a report address's removal, cooldown and limit checks are
  evaluated: the blueprint's, derived from this requirement's promise that a
  visitor who meets a limit is always told so in writing and from REQ-002
  criterion 3's, that a removed domain is never scanned again for anyone.
