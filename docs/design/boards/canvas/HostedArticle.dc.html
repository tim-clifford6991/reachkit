How to shut off water to a house in an emergency: 3 steps

The short answer

Find your main shut-off valve, usually where the water line enters the house or in a box near the street, and close it: turn a lever a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe, or a round wheel clockwise until it stops. Then open the lowest faucet to drain the pipes and switch off the water heater.

Key takeawaysSample

  • Most homes have one main valve, where the supply line enters: often by the water heater, in the garage, or in a box near the curb.
  • A lever valve is closed when the handle sits across the pipe; a wheel valve closes clockwise.
  • The valve inside the curb box beside the meter may belong to the water utility. If yours will not close, call the utility’s emergency number on your bill.
  • After the water is off, turn off the water heater’s power or gas so it does not heat an empty tank.

Where is the main water shut-off valve?

In most houses it sits where the water line comes into the house.

How do you turn off a lever valve and a wheel valve?

Turn a lever (ball) valve a quarter turn until the handle sits across the pipe. Turn a wheel (gate) valve clockwise until it stops; it can take several turns, and it should not be forced.

  1. 1Close the main valve.
  2. 2Open the lowest faucet in the house, and an outside tap, to drain the pipes.
  3. 3Turn off the water heater: switch off its breaker, or set the gas control to “pilot”.

What if the valve is stuck or you can’t find it?

Don’t force it with a wrench. An old gate valve can break off in the pipe. The meter-side valve in the curb box may need a meter key, and some utilities ask homeowners to call them instead. The emergency number is on your water bill (Austin Water).Sample source

Can you turn off water to just one fixture?

Yes. Most toilets and sinks have a small stop valve under them, which closes clockwise or with a quarter turn. That is enough for a running toilet or a leaking tap, but not for a burst pipe in a wall.

What should you do once the water is off?

Mop up standing water and move valuables. Take photos for your insurer before you clean up. Leave the valve closed until the leak is repaired.

When should you call an emergency plumber?

Call one straight away in any of these cases.

  • Water is coming through a ceiling or a wall
  • The main valve will not close
  • You smell gas near the water heater: leave the house first, then call the gas utility.
