A draft is the day's update of a live page where its meta has `fallbackFor` and either `mendOf` or `research: "stored"` (`becameUpdate`, `src/lib/publish/record/needs-you.ts`). (1) **No #1034 swap.** The owner has not ruled #1034, so the old wording stays and the two new mail keys follow the shipped `mail.weekly.setAside.*` family. (2) **No "why" line.** (3) **The mail's arm is a week-level fact.** No stored link pairs a set-aside page with the date it gave up, so the arm is read over the seven days the set-aside count reads. A gap is a date in that window whose `draft_days.outcome` is not `drafted`, or whose `becameUpdate` draft never went live (stopped or not published). u is the number of drafts scheduled in the window with `becameUpdate`, all of them published. Any gap, or rows that cannot be read, gives `unreplaced`. No gap and u = 0 gives `replaced`. No gap and u ≥ n gives `updated`. No gap and 0 < u < n gives `unreplaced`, so a mixed week never claims an update for pages a new page replaced. It is one line, never two, and supply depth is no longer read. (4) **The day row shows a change of plan only where the plan provably said "new page"**: the committed week's row for that date is of a Write or Earn family. Where that row cannot be read, or it was itself an update, no line shows. The strings are "Now an update", "This day now improves a live page instead of a new one. Nothing is needed from you." (present tense, ux-designer 2026-11-06: it also shows on days not live yet, and a page the customer stops must not have been claimed as done; the two mail lines speak of a past week and stay in the past tense), "One page didn’t pass our checks. We set it aside and improved a live page instead." and "{n} pages didn’t pass our checks. We set them aside and improved live pages instead." None of them promises a ranking, a reward or a crawl (seo-master's avoid list).
