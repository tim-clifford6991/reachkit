2026-10-19  **A page's index state is stated, and a page never seen gets a different fix** (issue 1538, from the SEO review of 2026-10-19, N5; conditions 1 and 2; the readings are drafted defaults the owner may correct). (1) *Seen.* A published page whose address — where it answers now, or answered before — is in any SERP or ranked row the site's scans stored, for any search and in any week, is `seen`. Otherwise it is `not_verified`: none of the results ReachKit read held it. It is never called "not indexed": Google says a `site:` search does not list every page it has indexed, and these rows are fewer than that. Rows that could not be read state nothing. The Monday mail's row for a page `not_verified` whose verdict is not *Working* says so beside its verdict (`mail.weekly.page.not_verified`). (2) *Unseen.* A live page of ours still `not_verified` 28 days after its version went live gets one link in from the strongest live page of its cluster — the seen page whose newest verdict holds the best place, the oldest among equals, passing over one the customer edited, stopped or took down and one younger than §6's floor — planned as that page's `link` refresh (#1391). Nothing is planned once a seen sibling links it, or where the page has no cluster or no such sibling. D13's check against pages written for other sites is run again on its live text; where it finds one as close as the near-duplicate bar, the page is due a refresh with cause `unseen`, whose writing runs D13 once more and holds it if it is still close. A page `not_verified` is not retargeted for D60's `unmet`: it was never seen to fail its search. 0¢: the rows are the ones the weekly passes stored, and no call is added. Whether a weekly `site:` SERP or Search Console feeds `seen` is condition 3, the owner's.
