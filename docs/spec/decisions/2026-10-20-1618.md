2026-10-20  **A search whose results are service or product pages gets an offering page** (owner, #1618: ruling B6 of 2026-10-19, *"a service/product page type when the search results show one"*, the SEO review's C1, an 8th type beside D42's seven). A new page — a Write row's keyword or answer page, or an Earn row's page — whose search has a stored top ten no more than 30 days old is written as an **offering** page, the business's own service or product page, where that results page asks for a provider's own page (the SEO shape ruling of 2026-10-20 on PR 1688). Each of its first ten results is read, from its stored address and title alone, as the first of five classes that fits: a job page, a public body or school, a third party that lists others (a directory, marketplace, social or forum page), an article, or else a provider's own page. It is an offering page where the SERP holds at least 6 results, at most 2 job pages, the search is not one to learn something (no how-to, guide, informational or insights words), and either 4 or more providers' own pages outnumber the articles, or the SERP shows a local pack, the business serves one place, the search has no list words (best, top, vs, alternatives, compare) and at most 3 results are articles. Readiness reads it and stores it on the row's evidence (`shape`), so the plan, the daily job and the page agree; a fresher results page that no longer shows it clears it, and a row with no fresh top ten is typed as before. A row already committed to a date is reshaped only by the week's full pass, never by the evening check. A comparison stays a comparison. An offering page is of the `product` page type. Its template: what it is and who it is for, what is included, its price or how it is priced, where and when (left out where the site states neither), how it works as steps, and why this business from its stated differences only; three to six buyer questions; 500–1,200 words; the pack must hold the business's own price (a figure, or "free") and four business facts on the topic, and needs no outside source. It buys no research: its questions are the "People also ask" the search's stored SERP already holds, and it costs the one write call, so a page costs no more than the type it replaces. On a list-shaped search its title, H1 and description leave out the search's best or top words unless the business's own pages state them. Until the week's own cap of two offering pages lands (issue 1610), D36's two pages of one type in seven days holds it. The hosted page draws it with the byline, the dates and the markup every page carries: a `Service` schema and a dates-only meta line (the SEO hosted-pages review's R3) wait for their own ruling. The thresholds (6 results, 2 job pages, 4 providers, 3 articles beside a pack), the section list and the length are drafted defaults the owner may correct. It adds no sentence to any screen.
