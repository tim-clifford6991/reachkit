2026-10-24  **A first renewal is refunded when no page went live in the first month** (owner, ruling D10, 2026-10-19, recorded for #1592; a second case beside D4 in REQ-076's "refunds deferred" non-goal). When `invoice.paid` reports the customer's first `subscription_cycle` invoice and none of the account's publications was delivered (`publications.delivery_state = 'delivered'`, `published_at` inside the period that invoice closes; drafts are never read, and a page taken down since still counts), ReachKit refunds that invoice in full through Stripe (idempotency key `refund:{invoice}`), records it once in `renewal_refunds`, and mails the customer why with the `account` kind. Access is not touched. A refund here neither ends access nor counts toward D4's once-per-web-address rule. A count that cannot be read refunds nothing. A refund Stripe refuses fails neither the webhook nor access; it is logged and the owner gets the `refund-refused` incident.

2026-10-24  **The D10 mail states the amount and the next day, and the two ledgers stay apart** (owner follows commercialization E1 and E2, 2026-10-24, recorded for #1957). E1: a D10 refund never counts toward D4's once-per-web-address rule, and a D4 refund never blocks D10; `refunds` and `renewal_refunds` are read only by their own rule. E2: the mail states the amount refunded (`invoice.amount_paid` in its currency), then either the day the next payment is taken (an active subscription not set to cancel) or the day ReachKit stops (cancelling or cancelled), as the subscription's period end in the site's zone, or UTC where none is stated; where the subscription cannot be read or is in any other state, the mail carries the refund sentence alone.
