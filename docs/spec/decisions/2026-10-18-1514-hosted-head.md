2026-10-18  **The content host states itself to crawlers: each index page is its own canonical, the icon is the customer's, the language is the site's, and the page is light** (owner on issue 1514: *"follow all recommendations from our seo master"*, and of the gap review's B6, *"B6 is adopted too"*; from the SEO master's review of 2026-10-18, rows 3, 5, 7 and 12, and the gap review's B6; every reading here is a drafted default the owner may correct). Each holds for every customer and every language (D66). (1) *Paging.* Each later page of the index, `?page=N`, declares itself as its canonical, as Google's guide to paging asks, so the articles only it lists stay reachable; a page past the last answers 404; a `?q=` search, and the first page, declare the index. (2) *Icon.* Every page of a site names an icon on the customer's own host (`icon.img`): the kept logo where it is a PNG, ICO, GIF or JPEG square of at least 48 px (any face of an ICO), the formats Google's favicon guidelines accept, else the first letter of the name on a 96 px tile, as the header draws it; an SVG or WebP logo draws the tile. It is never ReachKit's, and a host that serves a site never answers it 404; a host that serves none still names the empty icon (#1121, #1129 stand). (3) *Language.* `<html lang>` and the feed's `<language>` are the site's tag, the one `inLanguage` and `og:locale` state. For that, each route group has its own root layout: the two hosted routes draw a document of their own, and ReachKit's screens draw theirs from `(public)` and `(account)`, so a move between those two groups is a full page load, and an address no route matches is Next's global 404. hreflang is still never emitted. (4) *Pictures.* Every article and index head carries `max-image-preview:large`, and an article's structured data offers the share card at 16:9, 4:3 (1200×900) and 1:1 (1200×1200), the same card on a taller canvas. No image is added to the body: the "No images in the body" ruling stands. (5) *Weight (B6).* A hosted page preloads at most two fonts. Turbopack serves every route's font CSS as one chunk, so a face preloaded anywhere is preloaded on a customer's page too; every page in the product therefore preloads two, Jakarta 400 and 800 (owner on issue 1514, 2026-10-19: the whole app at two), and 600, 700 and JetBrains Mono swap in when used. A hosted page carries no theme script, no banner script and no client component. `scripts/smoke.sh` times the dogfood blog's newest article, the fastest of three answers after one that may fill the cache, against `HOSTED_TTFB_BUDGET_MS` (1,000 ms), and fails a page that preloads more than two fonts or states no language. *Not built:* "no `<script src>` and no RSC payload". Next's App Router emits its bootstrap scripts and the page's inline flight data on every page and has no setting to leave them out; only the Pages Router's `unstable_runtimeJS` does. On a cached article they run nothing, because it is served under `script-src 'none'`. Removing them is issue 1534. 0¢: no call is added.
