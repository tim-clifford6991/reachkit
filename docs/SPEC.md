# ReachKit — MVP Specification

The nine features below are the product. Anything not needed by one of them is out of scope (§11).
UI is `docs/DESIGN.md` — this file does not describe pixels. What is on each screen, in what order and with what wording, is that screen's board named there: structure and wording, never a pixel baseline.
Every customer-visible sentence is a key in `src/lib/presentation/copy/keys/`. A missing sentence is drafted and shipped, never `TODO(copy)`; the owner corrects the wording (owner, 2026-09-16, #759). A mail carrying the marker still sends nothing.
Pinned numbers live in `src/lib/config/constants.ts`. Each section states the rule that is current; a line a later ruling replaced is deleted, and git keeps the history.

## §0 Terms

**Goal (owner, 2026-09-16)** Users pay ReachKit to generate content that improves their SEO and GEO ranking. Each day the system decides the best content for that business — from what it knows of the business, the posts the site already has, and what its rivals rank for — and either publishes a new post or updates an existing page (§7). Everything is right-sized to the business’s own presence — its footprint (§6): never compete for searches outsized for the site. A new site that ranks for almost nothing — ReachKit itself — still gets winnable, small targets, not an empty calendar. Every change serves this goal.

**Current focus (owner, 2026-10-03, D35, #1210)** *"the sole goal we are currently trying to solve is that our users site measurement returns correct rivals, category, keywords to work towards and AI answers to optimize for. this context, together with the users own site information are used to build the daily content post."* So the work in hand is the measurement: a site's pass must return the correct rivals, market category, keywords to work towards and AI answers to optimise for (§6). That context and the site's own information are what the daily page is built from (§7). The Goal above stands; this names what is being solved now.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **SEO / GEO** | Google organic findability of the customer’s domain / whether ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews name them. |
| **Discoverability Score** | One 0–100 number. `Score = round(∛(Foundations × Answerability × Presence))`, `Presence = max(1, √(SearchPresence × AIPresence))`. Each factor 0–100. Bands: invisible 0 · hard-to-find 25 · findable 50 · dominant 75. An unmeasured factor nulls the whole score — no partial score. Shown on the free report only, as a one-off grade for strangers; the app's headline is §4's four numbers (owner, 2026-10-02, D22, #1172). |
| **Market** | Category the scan measures. It fixes the twelve questions, the rivals and the volumes. For a paying site ReachKit identifies and maintains it from evidence, re-checked each pass; the founder may override it but never has to (owner, 2026-10-02, D28, #1186). The free report's visitor may correct it once (§2). |
| **Site profile** | Page inventory (URL, title, h1, purpose), site name, products/claims, brand-voice summary. Up to 100 pages from sitemap + internal links. Free scan stores inventory + name; voice is paid-only, shown at setup (2026-09-12). |
| **Rival** | One of the 3–5 real competitors ReachKit identifies from evidence and re-checks each pass; the founder may override them but never has to (§5; owner, 2026-10-02, D28, #1186). A publisher, aggregator or platform in a top ten is a source, not a rival (owner, 2026-10-02, D24, #1161). |
| **Question** | One of the twelve buyer searches the scan measures: searches this business could answer, chosen by business fit first and demand second, right-sized per §6 (2026-09-16, 2026-09-29). They stay the Google searches; the AI measure asks tracked buyer prompts instead (owner, 2026-10-03, D19, §6). |
| **Tracked buyer prompt** | One of the about 25 prompts a buyer would put to an answer engine, generated from the site's structured market (D28) and asked every week. The AI measure is the share of their answers that name or link the site — a 4-week rolling mention rate with its margin, never an AI position (owner, 2026-10-03, D19, §6). |
| **Business fit** | 0–3, how much the business's own offering is the answer to a search: 3 it is the answer, 2 it plainly helps, 1 it can only be mentioned, 0 no relation. Judged against the knowledge base (§6, 2026-09-29). |
| **Knowledge base** | What the business sells and how: each offering with its features, prices and uses; who it serves; the problems it solves; how it differs from each rival; its proof (customers, results, testimonials); its buyers' common questions; integrations and places. Every item carries the page it was read from, or says the founder gave it (§5, 2026-09-29). |
| **Footprint** | How many keywords the site itself ranks for, and each rival’s count. The measure of a business’s presence that right-sizing scales with (§6, 2026-09-16) — not the score’s Presence factor. An internal right-sizing input only, never shown as a score or a lead (owner, 2026-10-02, D22, D24). |
| **Opportunity** | Write (missing page) · Improve (owned URL) · Fix (technical) · Earn (first-party citable asset on their domain — never outreach). |
| **Asset** | What one day publishes: new post, new page, or update to an existing page. At most one a day. |
| **Veto** | Finished draft waits, visible and stoppable, before it publishes. Default 24 h, range 1–7 days, never zero. |

## §2 Free scan

**User gets** A scan of any domain with no account. Named stages with elapsed times (no spinner, %, countdown). A permanent public report: score, band, limiting factor, AI-answers card, Google-search card, technical issues (§9), the DIY method, one first-page proposal. Full first page mailed on request.

**Rules**
- Cap 12¢ and 50 s (target 40 s) under a 60 s platform limit. The pass outlives the HTTP response.
- Same domain inside 7 days: stored report, spend nothing. Failed scan: 24 h cool-down. 5 scans/IP/hour, 1 in flight/IP, 200/day.
- Cut-off factor: "—" and no band word; one line per unmeasured driver; measured cards still render with “Retry this part”.
- Unreadable home page: no score, no rivals, no invented numbers; one line saying the home page could not be read.
- Reports are `noindex` forever, not in the sitemap, nothing blurred or paywalled.
- Measurement is US Google in English, stated in one always-visible line. Category may be corrected once within 7 days; correction reuses cached AI answers and says so.
- Twelve questions: search text only — no per-question volume.
- Driver mini-bars `n/10` live in the report header only.
- Scan builds the site profile (up to 100 pages) inside the same 12¢ cap; weekly pass refreshes it (2026-09-12).
- Fail closed (2026-09-16, #792): a free-scan bound or the day's spend that cannot be read refuses the scan with the paused line and logs the step; nothing is spent on a count nobody has. Every paid call reads the day's spend again first; an unreadable total skips the call and the pass holds.
- 2026-09-16  (#826, owner) The free first page is written once per report and stored with its scan. Every lead on that report is mailed the same stored page; a second lead costs nothing. A page the hard rules refused is recorded once, and later leads get the "no page to write" notice without another attempt.
- 2026-10-04  The free first page is written by §7's stages (D41, #1252; drafted default, the owner may correct it): its pack is the domain's own passages and its stored report, with no research and no knowledge base, and its one write call is sized to fit `CAPS.FREE_C` — there is no fix call. Where that pack is short of what the page's own type asks, the page is written as a Q&A, which the scan's measured questions and the domain's passages can hold; where it is short of that too, there is no page to write.
- 2026-09-18  Roll forward only (#887, from the owner's process audit). A report is permanent at its own address, and the build that can read it only ever moves forward: a stored blob carries the version that wrote it, `readStoredReport` lifts a blob forward and never back, and anything it cannot lift throws rather than returning a partial report. So an older build put back on production does not degrade on a newer report — it fails on it, at six of its nine readers, which is what the owner met on 2026-09-17 ("stored report version 9 is not readable by this build (expected 6)") and what made a frozen production read as a broken app. **Production is therefore never rolled back.** A bad build is fixed by the next deploy: revert on main, deploy that. The one exception is not a rollback — a deploy that fails to build is never aliased, so production keeps serving the build it already had, and nothing was replaced to put back. The alternative was costed and declined: supporting a rollback would mean `readStoredReport` tolerating a newer blob by reading only the members it understands, a not-present arm for every member at every reader, and every future version bump stating what an older build silently loses — to buy back one click that has never been needed. The deploy scripts already agree (none takes a commit); the door Vercel leaves open stays shut by decision. Procedure: docs/RUNBOOK.md §1.
- 2026-09-17  (#835, owner ruling) The free scan walks the same seed ladder as the deep pass (§6 thin markets): up to 3 extra seeds — head term, then vocabulary — plus the ranked rows already bought, with the 50 → 20 → 10 steps, stopping as soon as it has questions, all inside the 12¢ cap and the free time budget. The extra seeds are paid from the twelve’s share of the cap: a seed is bought only while that share still holds it and a search for every question already selected.
- 2026-10-04  (#1235) "As soon as it has questions" is **four questions**, not one. The ladder stopped at the first question it found, so a category seed that answered one search (gumroad.com, 2026-10-03: one seed bought, one question, 4.7¢ of the 12¢ spent) gave a report of one question, and when that one search timed out the report had no score and no band. The free scan now buys the next seed while it holds fewer than four questions and its purse still affords the seed and a search for every question it has. A market that still yields fewer than four is reported with what it yielded. Nothing else about the cap, the purse or the 50 s changes.
- 2026-09-18  Free scans are bounded so a script cannot walk past them (#885; drafted defaults, the owner may correct the numbers). Beside the existing 5 scans/IP/hour, 1 in flight/IP and 200/day: at most **20 free scans per IP-hash in 24 h** — 5 an hour for 24 hours is 120 scans and 1440¢, more than half the free path’s own worst case, from one script that only has to wait — and at most **3 free scans per domain in 24 h**, whoever asks, because the stored report that makes a repeat of one address free is read at the report address and never by the admission order the scan API calls. Both are counted in admission, which is the one place a caller cannot go around, and each refuses in writing with the wait until the oldest counted scan ages out of its window — never a stack trace. The two lines are drafted; the owner corrects the wording. `domain_blocks` is not a rate limit and never was: it is the takedown list REQ-002 c4 gives no writer anywhere in the product.
- 2026-09-18  A model answer is read generously and refused honestly (#898, from the owner’s production smoke of `figma.com`). The site profile’s four bounded lists — audience terms 2–4, named rivals at most 5, vocabulary at most 12, brand tokens at most 6 — are what the model is asked for and still what the tool schema states; a list that comes back **one or more past its cap is cut to the cap in the model’s own order and the answer is kept**, because those caps exist so a prompt cannot return a thousand items, not as a claim that a business with seven names for itself has failed. An answer that is really unusable still fails: a missing field, a value of the wrong type, an eighth field, or a list empty where its floor is above zero. A schema miss whose every issue is the size of a list is not re-asked — the same question cannot get a different answer to it, and the second call is money. A pass that never read its market at all is not a pass that simply ended: the `scan_pass` line names the step (issue 865’s `because`), and the report address says the report could not be built rather than showing an empty one and naming a driver the missing market took down with it. The line is drafted; the owner corrects the wording.

- 2026-09-22  What the report says about its own measurement, and what a cut-off card offers (owner, #715). Three things, one budget. (1) The **measurement line** — "US Google in English" — is one always-visible line on every report state that shows a figure; it is `report.measurement` in the registry and is on the report today. (2) Answers a recent scan already bought are marked **"reused from {date}"**: a second scan of the same address inside §6.4's SERP window is answered out of the ledger and spends nothing, and the date is that ledger row's own, stored with the report and never guessed from the report's own measurement date. It is beside, not instead of, the correction's existing "reused from an earlier scan" disclosure: one says *when* the answers were read, the other says *which* answers the correction could see. (3) A card a ceiling cut off offers **"Retry this part"**, and the control **re-runs only that part inside the same budget**: the market is read back off the stored report rather than bought again (the free pass's two model calls are the one thing no cache returns), every other measurement the report holds comes back from the ledger at zero cents, and the retried row's spend is the new pass's opening balance — so the original pass and every retry of it share one 12¢ cap. A report whose pass has spent the cap is offered no buying retry. Each pass keeps its own 50 s ceiling. A retry never leaves the report with less on it than it had: a section it did not re-measure is carried forward.

- 2026-10-02  **The score stays here** (owner, D22, #1172). The Discoverability Score is the free report's one-off grade for strangers. The app does not lead with it (§4).
- 2026-09-20  The report fills in as the scan runs (owner, #908): named stages with elapsed times still stand on the left (no spinner, %, countdown), and each report section appears in place as its stage finishes, a daisyUI `skeleton` holding its place until then. No full-page wait.
- 2026-09-24  A public gallery page of free scans (owner, #1059 (2026-09-24); listing ruled on #1058): every finished free scan is listed as a card — the logo read from the site (a first-letter tile when none is usable), company, domain, score, category, date — with search by company or domain, category filter and sort, each card linking to its report. **Listed unless removed**: every finished free scan appears, as its report already does, until its owner asks through remove@reachkit.app; a removed or refused site appears in neither the gallery nor the landing's logo strip. The gallery page is **indexed**; the reports it links to stay `noindex` and out of the sitemap.

**Done when** A stranger gets a scored report from `/` with no account; the same URL is the same report a day later; a production free scan is ≤ 12¢ and ≤ 50 s; an unreadable site says so and shows no score.

- Buying with no report still completes; the site address is asked afterwards.

## §7 Calendar and daily publish

**User gets** One calendar of planned / in review / live / waiting. At most one page a day. Whole draft to read, 24 h to stop it from the mail without signing in. Markdown edit with live preview. Published assets link into their own site.

**Rules**
- Autopilot only: generate → veto → publish. No Copilot, no mode picker, on no screen and in no mail (2026-09-11).
- Veto default 24 h, range 1–7 days, never zero. Same window for updates as for new pages (2026-09-11).
- At most one publish a day, eight a week. Kinds: new post, new page, update of an existing page.
- Fail closed (2026-09-16, #792): when the day's or week's publishes cannot be counted, the page is held, not published, the log says why, and the next tick asks again.
- Every asset links to real inventory pages (pricing, about, features, product) and to earlier assets in its cluster. No link known to go nowhere. Drafts follow the stored voice. A fact about the business comes only from their site, the profile or the knowledge base; a fact about the market comes only from a public page cited beside it (2026-09-29).
- A closed Markdown subset (with the 2026-09-20 blocks below); one serialiser for screen, copy-as-HTML and copy-as-Markdown. One fix call before review (D41, below); never after. Edits save with no save button.
- A page is written from its context pack and its type's template, and from nothing else (D41, D42, below). A pack short of what the template asks writes no page.
- Publish is one idempotent call to the destination on the customer’s domain. At +24 h: reachable, indexable, in a sitemap, AI-readable.
- Public veto link redeems on GET once. MVP paid service ends at a page on the customer’s own domain (2026-09-11).
- Opportunity status (2026-09-15): a written draft queues its opportunity; a vetoed draft dismisses it.
- 2026-09-17  Target choice (owner walk, #858): a Write or Earn target's competition band reads the search's own keyword difficulty where the vendor gave one — the measurement of that very top ten — beside the top ten's ranked counts, taking the better of the two; above the difficulty ceiling (§6) the target is outsized for every type. So a cold-start site's long-tail question whose small domains nobody sized is still a winnable or reach target, never an unmeasured one.
- 2026-09-16  Draft edits (owner, #789): the editor saves the title, the body and the meta description. Every save re-runs the stage-4 checks (D41) on the edited text. An edit that breaks a rule is still saved, the page says so, and the page is held from publishing on every route until a later save passes. The state and the veto window do not change.
- Stopped drafts (2026-09-16, #788): a date keeps one draft row across every attempt. A draft the checks still stop after its one fix call moves to needs_attention with the checks that stopped it. Regenerate writes that date's draft again on the next hourly draft tick.

A page that tells every reader `noindex` is kept out of search on purpose and is skipped — a public report of another domain, a sign-in page — unless it is the home page or the site’s own sitemap lists it: those two are what “a page that should be indexed” means in the `noindex` check. A skipped page is not in the inventory, so no check counts it, no Fix opportunity is made for it, and it is neither known content nor a cross-link target. Answerability never read the crawl: it is the home page and the detected pricing page, with the measured pages a caller passes, each a page at its own canonical address.
