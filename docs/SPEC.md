# ReachKit — MVP Specification

The nine features below are the product. Anything not needed by one of them is out of scope (§11).
UI is `docs/DESIGN.md` — this file does not describe pixels. What is on each screen, in what order and with what wording, is that screen's board named there: structure and wording, never a pixel baseline.
Every customer-visible sentence is a key in `src/lib/presentation/copy/keys/`. A missing sentence is drafted and shipped, never `TODO(copy)`; the owner corrects the wording (owner, 2026-09-16, #759). A mail carrying the marker still sends nothing.
Pinned numbers live in `src/lib/config/constants.ts`. Each section states the rule that is current; a line a later ruling replaced is deleted, and git keeps the history.

## §0 Terms

**Goal (owner, 2026-09-16)** Users pay ReachKit to generate content that improves their SEO and GEO ranking. Each day the system decides the best content for that business — from what it knows of the business, the posts the site already has, and what its rivals rank for — and either publishes a new post or updates an existing page (§7). Everything is right-sized to the business’s own presence — its footprint (§6): never compete for searches outsized for the site. A new site that ranks for almost nothing — ReachKit itself — still gets winnable, small targets, not an empty calendar. Every change serves this goal.

**Current focus (owner, 2026-10-03, D35, #1210)** *"the sole goal we are currently trying to solve is that our users site measurement returns correct rivals, category, keywords to work towards and AI answers to optimize for. this context, together with the users own site information are used to build the daily content post."* So the work in hand is the measurement: a site's pass must return the correct rivals, market category, keywords to work towards and AI answers to optimise for (§6). That context and the site's own information are what the daily page is built from (§7). The Goal above stands; this names what is being solved now.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **SEO / GEO** | Google organic findability of the customer’s domain / whether ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews name them. |
| **Discoverability Score** | One 0–100 number. `Score = round(∛(Foundations × Answerability × Presence))`, `Presence = max(1, √(SearchPresence × AIPresence))`. Each factor 0–100. Bands: invisible 0 · hard-to-find 25 · findable 50 · dominant 75. An unmeasured factor nulls the whole score — no partial score. Shown on the free report only, as a one-off grade for strangers; the app's headline is §4's four numbers (owner, 2026-10-02, D22, #1172). |
| **Market** | Category the scan measures. It fixes the twelve questions, the rivals and the volumes. For a paying site ReachKit identifies and maintains it from evidence, re-checked each pass; the founder may override it but never has to (owner, 2026-10-02, D28, #1186). The free report's visitor may correct it once (§2). |
| **Site profile** | Page inventory (URL, title, h1, purpose), site name, products/claims, brand-voice summary. Up to 100 pages from sitemap + internal links. Free scan stores inventory + name; voice is paid-only, shown at setup (2026-09-12). |
| **Rival** | One of the 3–5 real competitors ReachKit identifies from evidence and re-checks each pass; the founder may override them but never has to (§5; owner, 2026-10-02, D28, #1186). A publisher, aggregator or platform in a top ten is a source, not a rival (owner, 2026-10-02, D24, #1161). |
| **Question** | One of the twelve buyer searches the scan measures: searches this business could answer, chosen by business fit first and demand second, right-sized per §6 (2026-09-16, 2026-09-29). They stay the Google searches; the AI measure asks tracked buyer prompts instead (owner, 2026-10-03, D19, §6). |
| **Tracked buyer prompt** | One of the about 25 prompts a buyer would put to an answer engine, generated from the site's structured market (D28) and asked every week. The AI measure is the share of their answers that name or link the site — a 4-week rolling mention rate with its margin, never an AI position (owner, 2026-10-03, D19, §6). |
| **Business fit** | 0–3, how much the business's own offering is the answer to a search: 3 it is the answer, 2 it plainly helps, 1 it can only be mentioned, 0 no relation. Judged against the knowledge base (§6, 2026-09-29). |
| **Knowledge base** | What the business sells and how: each offering with its features, prices and uses; who it serves; the problems it solves; how it differs from each rival; its proof (customers, results, testimonials); its buyers' common questions; integrations and places. Every item carries the page it was read from, or says the founder gave it (§5, 2026-09-29). |
| **Footprint** | How many keywords the site itself ranks for, and each rival’s count. The measure of a business’s presence that right-sizing scales with (§6, 2026-09-16) — not the score’s Presence factor. An internal right-sizing input only, never shown as a score or a lead (owner, 2026-10-02, D22, D24). |
| **Opportunity** | Write (missing page) · Improve (owned URL) · Fix (technical) · Earn (first-party citable asset on their domain — never outreach). |
| **Asset** | What one day publishes: new post, new page, or update to an existing page. At most one a day. |
| **Veto** | Finished draft waits, visible and stoppable, before it publishes. Default 24 h, range 1–7 days, never zero. |

## §2 Free scan

**User gets** A scan of any domain with no account. Named stages with elapsed times (no spinner, %, countdown). A permanent public report: score, band, limiting factor, AI-answers card, Google-search card, technical issues (§9), the DIY method, one first-page proposal. Full first page mailed on request.

**Rules**
- Cap 12¢ and 50 s (target 40 s) under a 60 s platform limit. The pass outlives the HTTP response.
- Same domain inside 7 days: stored report, spend nothing. Failed scan: 24 h cool-down. 5 scans/IP/hour, 1 in flight/IP, 200/day.
- Cut-off factor: "—" and no band word; one line per unmeasured driver; measured cards still render with “Retry this part”.
- Unreadable home page: no score, no rivals, no invented numbers; one line saying the home page could not be read.
- Reports are `noindex` forever, not in the sitemap, nothing blurred or paywalled.
- Measurement is US Google in English, stated in one always-visible line. Category may be corrected once within 7 days; correction reuses cached AI answers and says so.
- Twelve questions: search text only — no per-question volume.
- Driver mini-bars `n/10` live in the report header only.
- Scan builds the site profile (up to 100 pages) inside the same 12¢ cap; weekly pass refreshes it (2026-09-12).
- Fail closed (2026-09-16, #792): a free-scan bound or the day's spend that cannot be read refuses the scan with the paused line and logs the step; nothing is spent on a count nobody has. Every paid call reads the day's spend again first; an unreadable total skips the call and the pass holds.
- 2026-09-16  (#826, owner) The free first page is written once per report and stored with its scan. Every lead on that report is mailed the same stored page; a second lead costs nothing. A page the hard rules refused is recorded once, and later leads get the "no page to write" notice without another attempt.
- 2026-09-18  Roll forward only (#887, from the owner's process audit). A report is permanent at its own address, and the build that can read it only ever moves forward: a stored blob carries the version that wrote it, `readStoredReport` lifts a blob forward and never back, and anything it cannot lift throws rather than returning a partial report. So an older build put back on production does not degrade on a newer report — it fails on it, at six of its nine readers, which is what the owner met on 2026-09-17 ("stored report version 9 is not readable by this build (expected 6)") and what made a frozen production read as a broken app. **Production is therefore never rolled back.** A bad build is fixed by the next deploy: revert on main, deploy that. The one exception is not a rollback — a deploy that fails to build is never aliased, so production keeps serving the build it already had, and nothing was replaced to put back. The alternative was costed and declined: supporting a rollback would mean `readStoredReport` tolerating a newer blob by reading only the members it understands, a not-present arm for every member at every reader, and every future version bump stating what an older build silently loses — to buy back one click that has never been needed. The deploy scripts already agree (none takes a commit); the door Vercel leaves open stays shut by decision. Procedure: docs/RUNBOOK.md §1.
- 2026-09-17  (#835, owner ruling) The free scan walks the same seed ladder as the deep pass (§6 thin markets): up to 3 extra seeds — head term, then vocabulary — plus the ranked rows already bought, with the 50 → 20 → 10 steps, stopping as soon as it has questions, all inside the 12¢ cap and the free time budget. The extra seeds are paid from the twelve’s share of the cap: a seed is bought only while that share still holds it and a search for every question already selected.
- 2026-09-18  Free scans are bounded so a script cannot walk past them (#885; drafted defaults, the owner may correct the numbers). Beside the existing 5 scans/IP/hour, 1 in flight/IP and 200/day: at most **20 free scans per IP-hash in 24 h** — 5 an hour for 24 hours is 120 scans and 1440¢, more than half the free path’s own worst case, from one script that only has to wait — and at most **3 free scans per domain in 24 h**, whoever asks, because the stored report that makes a repeat of one address free is read at the report address and never by the admission order the scan API calls. Both are counted in admission, which is the one place a caller cannot go around, and each refuses in writing with the wait until the oldest counted scan ages out of its window — never a stack trace. The two lines are drafted; the owner corrects the wording. `domain_blocks` is not a rate limit and never was: it is the takedown list REQ-002 c4 gives no writer anywhere in the product.
- 2026-09-18  A model answer is read generously and refused honestly (#898, from the owner’s production smoke of `figma.com`). The site profile’s four bounded lists — audience terms 2–4, named rivals at most 5, vocabulary at most 12, brand tokens at most 6 — are what the model is asked for and still what the tool schema states; a list that comes back **one or more past its cap is cut to the cap in the model’s own order and the answer is kept**, because those caps exist so a prompt cannot return a thousand items, not as a claim that a business with seven names for itself has failed. An answer that is really unusable still fails: a missing field, a value of the wrong type, an eighth field, or a list empty where its floor is above zero. A schema miss whose every issue is the size of a list is not re-asked — the same question cannot get a different answer to it, and the second call is money. A pass that never read its market at all is not a pass that simply ended: the `scan_pass` line names the step (issue 865’s `because`), and the report address says the report could not be built rather than showing an empty one and naming a driver the missing market took down with it. The line is drafted; the owner corrects the wording.

- 2026-09-22  What the report says about its own measurement, and what a cut-off card offers (owner, #715). Three things, one budget. (1) The **measurement line** — "US Google in English" — is one always-visible line on every report state that shows a figure; it is `report.measurement` in the registry and is on the report today. (2) Answers a recent scan already bought are marked **"reused from {date}"**: a second scan of the same address inside §6.4's SERP window is answered out of the ledger and spends nothing, and the date is that ledger row's own, stored with the report and never guessed from the report's own measurement date. It is beside, not instead of, the correction's existing "reused from an earlier scan" disclosure: one says *when* the answers were read, the other says *which* answers the correction could see. (3) A card a ceiling cut off offers **"Retry this part"**, and the control **re-runs only that part inside the same budget**: the market is read back off the stored report rather than bought again (the free pass's two model calls are the one thing no cache returns), every other measurement the report holds comes back from the ledger at zero cents, and the retried row's spend is the new pass's opening balance — so the original pass and every retry of it share one 12¢ cap. A report whose pass has spent the cap is offered no buying retry. Each pass keeps its own 50 s ceiling. A retry never leaves the report with less on it than it had: a section it did not re-measure is carried forward.

- 2026-10-02  **The score stays here** (owner, D22, #1172). The Discoverability Score is the free report's one-off grade for strangers. The app does not lead with it (§4).
- 2026-09-20  The report fills in as the scan runs (owner, #908): named stages with elapsed times still stand on the left (no spinner, %, countdown), and each report section appears in place as its stage finishes, a daisyUI `skeleton` holding its place until then. No full-page wait.
- 2026-09-24  A public gallery page of free scans (owner, #1059 (2026-09-24); listing ruled on #1058): every finished free scan is listed as a card — the logo read from the site (a first-letter tile when none is usable), company, domain, score, category, date — with search by company or domain, category filter and sort, each card linking to its report. **Listed unless removed**: every finished free scan appears, as its report already does, until its owner asks through remove@reachkit.app; a removed or refused site appears in neither the gallery nor the landing's logo strip. The gallery page is **indexed**; the reports it links to stay `noindex` and out of the sitemap.

**Done when** A stranger gets a scored report from `/` with no account; the same URL is the same report a day later; a production free scan is ≤ 12¢ and ≤ 50 s; an unreadable site says so and shows no score.

## §3 Pay and sign-in

**User gets** Stripe Checkout at €49/month tax-inclusive, no account beforehand. Country for records; VAT number optional. After payment the browser lands on setup, signed in. Magic-link mail is for later visits. Later sign-in is email only. Card, invoices, cancel in Stripe’s portal from Settings.

**Rules**
- One live Stripe Price; boot refuses a mismatch.
- 2026-09-18  Boot also refuses a price the vendor says does not exist (#889) — a test-mode `STRIPE_PRICE_ID` asked for with a live-mode key, which is the state production is in. The refusal names the mode of the configured key and never the key. A vendor that could not be read at all is still logged and still serves: a read that did not happen establishes nothing.
- Account is created by the payment webhook, never a signup form. A second payment from the same address buys no second subscription and says so.
- Magic link only: no password, no social. Link lasts 24 h; a new one spends older ones. Sign-in copy is identical whatever the address, revealing nothing about who has an account.
- 2026-09-14  Stripe success URL is this deployment’s `/auth/checkout`, which signs the payer in and sends them to `/setup`. Cancel returns to the page they started from. Never a host this process does not name.
- Plan and price are ours; every other billing number is read from Stripe.
- Cancel names the exact date access ends; export stays with no end date.
- Account routes are server-gated. Sign-out is global.
- Buying with no report still completes; the site address is asked afterwards.
- Delete: tombstone at once, purge within 30 days (2026-08-31). Danger-zone unlocks only after the customer types the words on the screen (2026-09-07).

**Done when** A Stripe test-mode payment on this deployment creates the account and lands the payer on `/setup`, signed in, with no form before pay. No password control exists. Settings shows €49 and links to Stripe’s portal. Cancel names the end date.

## §7 Calendar and daily publish

**User gets** One calendar of planned / in review / live / waiting. At most one page a day. Whole draft to read, 24 h to stop it from the mail without signing in. Markdown edit with live preview. Published assets link into their own site.

**Rules**
- Autopilot only: generate → veto → publish. No Copilot, no mode picker, on no screen and in no mail (2026-09-11).
- Veto default 24 h, range 1–7 days, never zero. Same window for updates as for new pages (2026-09-11).
- At most one publish a day, eight a week. Kinds: new post, new page, update of an existing page.
- Fail closed (2026-09-16, #792): when the day's or week's publishes cannot be counted, the page is held, not published, the log says why, and the next tick asks again.
- Every asset links to real inventory pages (pricing, about, features, product) and to earlier assets in its cluster. No link known to go nowhere. Drafts follow the stored voice. A fact about the business comes only from their site, the profile or the knowledge base; a fact about the market comes only from a public page cited beside it (2026-09-29).
- A closed Markdown subset (with the 2026-09-20 blocks below); one serialiser for screen, copy-as-HTML and copy-as-Markdown. One auto-regeneration before review; never after. Edits save with no save button.
- The brief picks facts by index from the two pools of 2026-09-29 and writes none; no fact picked, no draft. The outline is chosen per page within its type's required blocks (2026-09-29). A draft that claims a test it did not run, invents a case study, a person or a review, states a number no fact holds, or does not open with an answer is stopped.
- Publish is one idempotent call to the destination on the customer’s domain. At +24 h: reachable, indexable, in a sitemap, AI-readable.
- Public veto link redeems on GET once. MVP paid service ends at a page on the customer’s own domain (2026-09-11).
- Opportunity status (2026-09-15): a written draft queues its opportunity; a vetoed draft dismisses it.
- 2026-09-17  Target choice (owner walk, #858): a Write or Earn target's competition band reads the search's own keyword difficulty where the vendor gave one — the measurement of that very top ten — beside the top ten's ranked counts, taking the better of the two; above the difficulty ceiling (§6) the target is outsized for every type. So a cold-start site's long-tail question whose small domains nobody sized is still a winnable or reach target, never an unmeasured one.
- 2026-09-16  Draft edits (owner, #789): the editor saves the title, the body and the meta description. Every save re-runs the hard rules and the claim check on the edited text. An edit that breaks a rule is still saved, the page says so, and the page is held from publishing on every route until a later save passes. The state and the veto window do not change.
- Stopped drafts (2026-09-16, #788): a date keeps one draft row across every attempt. A draft the hard rules stop for the last time moves to needs_attention with the rules that stopped it. Regenerate writes that date's draft again on the next hourly draft tick.
- 2026-10-02  **A stopped draft is repaired from its failures, not re-rolled blind** (#1178, part of #1127). A page stopped by a mechanical rule is first repaired in code and put through every rule again, at no model cost: an unlinked number is linked to the customer's own page that states it beside the same word, or its sentence is dropped; a byline, date or case-study sentence is dropped; a question heading the outline did not have is dropped, its text kept; an opening list is joined into a paragraph and an over-long opening is split after the sentences that fit. The repair is kept only if the page then passes. Otherwise the automatic second attempt is told each rule the first broke and the sentences that broke it. Every repair is recorded on the row (`drafts.meta.repairs`), per rule, cleared or not. Still one automatic regeneration; nothing a repair does writes a word.
- 2026-09-16  (#813) A step that could not run after the date's row exists (the claim check, answerability, any later step) ends the same way: after the one automatic second attempt, the row moves to needs_attention naming the step. No second row; Regenerate restarts it.
- 2026-09-18  **Who decides what, once** (owner ruling, #884: *"right-sizing should only occur once, during the deep scan. The calendar content generation should occur based on the information that is passed to it"*). Three layers, and no rule lives in two:
  - **The pass** measures and stores. The ceilings of §6 are applied while it selects its questions, and what it measured — volume, keyword difficulty, the site's own footprint, each rival's size — is written into the report. Nothing downstream re-measures.
  - **Derivation** decides and stores. It bands every target against the site's footprint (`fit_band`) and records whether the target may fill a publishing day at all (`ready`, `unready_reason`) — including §6's right-sizing law, whose reason is `outsized`. A row banded under an older policy is brought up to date from its own stored evidence on the next pass; no vendor call is made to do it.
  - **The calendar, the ranking and the daily job** only read. They order and render what the row already states; none of them re-applies a bar, re-bands a target or holds a filter of its own. A row that reaches the ranked list is a row the stored answer called ready, and a disagreement is a defect to be asserted against, not repaired at read time.
- 2026-09-18  A `not-yet` target never fills a day (owner's dogfood, #881; the enforcement half of §6's right-sizing law). It is excluded from the ranked list rather than weighed 0 in it — a zero weight orders and does not exclude, and where every candidate scored 0 the tie-break handed the day to the biggest keyword: ReachKit wrote "best seo software" (1 000/mo) for a site ranking for 3. An outsized row stays on file, because the site may grow into it, but it is not a day of supply and is not counted towards pages planned; a site whose every target is outsized has zero days of pages, and the calendar and Overview say that is why rather than calling the market used up or unmeasured. A re-measure adds rather than replaces, so a pass's right-sized candidate now takes the place of an outsized row already held on the same topic — a queued row still survives, since its page is already being written.
- 2026-10-02  **A stopped draft frees its date** (owner, D26, #1186; rewrites #833 of 2026-09-17). When a draft fails its second attempt, the same run tries the next-ranked opportunity for the same date, at most once more, inside `CAPS.DRAFT_C`. The stopped draft keeps its row in needs_attention but no longer holds the date. A topic stopped twice, or skipped by the founder, rests for 28 days (#1179). The founder still resolves a stopped draft from the draft: Regenerate rewrites the one row; Skip rests its topic as above.
- 2026-10-02  **What the hard rules are for** (owner, D27, #1186): *"If a draft has failed twice, i would be highly concerned to why. the pass criteria should mainly be there to guard the content for obvious violations, and sticking to a structure."* The hard rules exist to stop obvious violations — invented facts, people or reviews; false claims about the business; adverts posing as answers; unsafe markup — and to enforce the page structure. They are not stylistic filters, and a rule that stops good pages is a defect in the rule. A draft that fails twice is an alert to investigate, never a normal outcome: its causes are reported per week (#1184), and the rules are reviewed against this purpose (#1178 and the issue that review files).
- 2026-10-02  Fixing a stopped page (owner, #1180; D26 above): the draft screen of a page that rests in needs_attention without ever entering review offers *Edit*, *Write it again* and *Skip* — the exception to #882's "every other stage opens as a read". An edit saves and re-checks as #789 says; a save that passes every hard rule and the claim check moves the page into review on a fresh veto window, told by the draft-ready mail as a generated page is. A page that entered review and came back to needs_attention is read and offers *Skip* only.
- 2026-09-16  Daily decision (owner, #777): each day chooses between a *new post* (Write/Earn) and an *update of an existing page* (Improve/Fix), whichever ranks higher for this site. Update candidates are the site’s own pages — its ranked URLs and crawled inventory, not only the home page — matched to the market’s questions; both sides are right-sized per §6. An empty day is still a stated cause, never filler.
- 2026-09-16  (#781) An update is offered only where the site's destination can deliver it: otherwise it is not ready (`destination_cannot_address`) and never fills a day. A hosted destination updates a page ReachKit published there — a new version at the same address, served in place of the old one and listed once; a page outside ReachKit is not updatable there. WordPress keeps updating any page of the site.
- 2026-09-17  (#855) A `fix_page` the site's destination cannot deliver — every one on a hosted-only site — is never the day's work and never supply: it is stored not ready (`destination_cannot_address`), no draft is written from it, and supply depth does not count it. It fills a day only where a WordPress destination can deliver it.
- Hosted index (2026-09-16): the root of a hosted host lists every live page, newest first, with a search; a site with nothing published says so instead of answering 404.
- 2026-09-18  Why a page needs you, and the act that matches (owner walk, #880): a page resting in needs_attention states its own cause, read off its last move into that state — the writing rules that stopped it, a step that could not run, a delivery no retry clears, or a publish tried to exhaustion. One written sentence per cause, the same on the calendar and on Overview. *Reconnect* is offered only where the page is waiting on delivery **and** the destination carries a credential **and** that credential is broken; a hosted destination is never offered it and is never shown a WordPress sentence — it is pointed at where its pages publish. A page the rules stopped is read or written again, never reconnected. The pass records the cause; no surface derives it from a stage.
- 2026-09-18  Every written page is readable (owner walk, #882): from the calendar, a page that has a draft opens the page as written, at every stage — in review, needs-you, scheduled and live alike. A page still generating has no text yet and offers none; an empty day offers nothing (REQ-043 c11). The stage in review keeps its own word, "Read the full page"; the others state plainly that they open the page as written. The screen it opens offers only what that state allows: a page in review can be approved, edited and vetoed, and every other stage opens as a read.
- 2026-09-18  A hosted page follows its site's host (#888; drafted default, the owner may correct it). A hosted page is its `publications` row and that row belongs to a site, so every live page is served at whatever host the site's hosted destination carries **now** — a host that changes under live pages (the owner's test destination on a deploy, a customer repointing their record) re-serves all of them at the new address. The address the page declares as canonical, the entry its sitemap carries, §9's recorded live URL, the link in the `published` mail and the address the 24-hour check fetches are that one current address, never the one stored when it was published. The old host is no longer a host ReachKit serves: it answers **404** — never 410, which would claim a page was taken down when it is live, and never a redirect or another site's page, because a host we can no longer prove is this customer's must never serve their content. A page is never recorded `page_not_found` because its host moved. A WordPress page is on a host this product does not own: its stored address is the only one there is and is never recomposed.
- 2026-09-18  A published page is inert (#888). The body is model-written markdown on the customer's own domain: the one renderer escapes every text node and emits only its own closed set of elements, so `<script>`, an `onerror` attribute and an embedded iframe render as the visible text a reader would expect, and a link whose scheme is not one of the four addressable ones renders as its label with no address at all. There is no sanitiser to call and no allow-list to keep: nothing from a body reaches the served page as markup.
- 2026-09-17  Plan horizon (owner, #857): the calendar plans from today to the day before the next weekly pass — through that week's Sunday in the site's own zone, and on a Monday through the coming Sunday whether or not the pass has run. Only those dates carry a planned page or an empty day's account. A past date shows what happened on it (published, stopped, skipped, held) and nothing else. A date after the horizon carries no planned page and no line; one quiet marker (“Planned after Monday’s scan”) stands on its first date. A supply state is stated once, at the top of the calendar, and never repeated per cell or in the day panel; a law cause (ReachKit stopped) is stated on the first empty date in the horizon only. The daily draft job agrees: it writes one draft, for tomorrow, from the current pass's ranking — so Sunday evening's draft for Monday is written before Monday's pass, and nothing further ahead is ever drafted.

- 2026-09-19  **A page answers the question it targets** (owner, #900, from the live re-measure of reachkit.app). Both drafts this product had ever written were advertisements for the site that pays for it, grounded in its own price list; three hard rules stopped both, which is the safety net working and the writing failing. Three things follow, and none of them weakens an existing rule:
  - **What the page is for is stated, per type.** Each opportunity type carries one sentence saying what its page must do, and the brief, the outline and the draft all carry it. For a Write or an Earn page on a question a reader brings from the market — "best X", "which X", what to choose — that job is to help the reader choose: what the options are, what distinguishes them, which case each suits. How the customer's own product appears is set by the page's type (2026-09-29, *honesty by page type*).
  - **Grounding carries more than one page.** The facts a brief chooses among are read from the customer's pages in turn — one passage per page per round, never one page emptied before the next is opened — and where a target search is known, the pages whose words it shares are offered first. A page on a market question then stands on a fact about the question rather than on eight sentences off the price list.
- 2026-09-20  **What a page is written from.** The confirmed business profile — what the business does, sells and promises, who it serves, its vocabulary — joins the writer’s inputs, beside the voice, the target search, the facts read from the site and the site’s own pages. Facts follow the two pools of 2026-09-29; no invented people, reviews or figures.
- 2026-09-20  **Known content.** Before a page is planned or written ReachKit knows every page it wrote for the site, every post on a connected WordPress (read through the connection when it is made and every Monday — all posts, not only ours), and the main site’s crawled pages. A planned page that overlaps one of them becomes an **update** of that page where the destination can publish to it; otherwise the topic is skipped and the existing page is linked from related pages. New pages link to known content where it helps the reader. The page’s checks say it is not a repeat and name the closest existing page.
- 2026-09-20  **Plan.** A month with a closable day panel. A day card holds the date and exactly one number (searches a month before the page is live, its position and change once it is), the title cut to two lines, and one stage word (Live, Review, Scheduled, Planned, Needs you) with a mark for an update. It is the same height at every width and nothing wraps past it. A day with no page says why in at most two words; the day panel carries the full reason. The day panel is label-and-value rows — search, AI answers, deadline, actions — never sentences. On a phone the month is a list of days and the day opens as a sheet.
- 2026-09-20  **The page as it publishes.** The review screen shows the whole page exactly as readers and search engines receive it, in the customer’s own page frame, with beside it: why this page; how it appears in search results; *written from your business* — each fact, quote and promise used and where it was read; what gets published (address, title, description, structured data, links, length); the checks. A switch labels what each part of the page is for and underlines the lines an AI answer is most likely to quote.
- 2026-09-20  **Richer blocks.** The published subset gains a table, a short-answer box and related guides, in the first release of the new viewer; a `:::takeaways` block joins the closed set for the key takeaways (owner, 2026-09-29, D4).
