## How do you get your business named when buyers ask ChatGPT what to use?

published 7 Oct 2026 · updated 9 Oct 2026 · by Tim, Reachkit Founder based in Berlin, Germany6 min read

The short answer

Ranking on ChatGPT means being one of the handful of companies it names when someone asks what to use. You get there by finding the questions your buyers ask, checking whether AI answers name you or a rival, then publishing pages on your own site that answer those questions with facts, so there's something for AI to point to.

Key takeaways

- ChatGPT names a small handful of companies per answer, so you need to know which buyer questions it's answering and whether you're in the list.
- A free scan can show how often AI names you versus rivals, and which buyer searches your rivals rank for and you don't.
- Pages written from facts on your own site give ChatGPT something concrete to cite — vague marketing copy doesn't.
- Technical blockers like a blocked AI reader or missing sitemap can keep you out of answers even if your content is good.
- Checking this weekly matters more than checking it once, because the answers change as pages get published and re-crawled.

## What do you need before you start?

You need your own website, access to publish pages on it, and a list of the real questions your buyers type into ChatGPT and Google before they pick a company like yours — not guesses, actual phrasing. You also need a way to see what AI currently says in response to those questions, because you can't fix what you haven't measured.

A free scan shows where AI answers and Google search send buyers to your rivals instead of you. That one check gives you the starting point: which questions get asked, who gets named, and where your own site falls short technically. Without it you're writing pages against guesses about what buyers ask, which wastes the time you're trying to save.

You'll also want patience for a slower kind of progress. This isn't a setting you flip — it's pages published and re-crawled over weeks, so plan to check progress on a schedule rather than expecting a single fix to move the needle overnight.

## What are the steps to rank on ChatGPT?

Ranking on ChatGPT comes down to finding out what it currently says, then giving it better material to say instead. Here's the order that works.

1. Run a scan of your site and market. This shows how often AI currently names you and which rivals it names instead. One such measurement found that on 5 October 2026, AI answers named reachkit.app for 0 of the 11 buyer questions they answered — a result that only becomes visible once you measure it.
2. List the buyer questions AI is answering. These are phrased the way a buyer thinks, not the way your homepage is written — "best X for Y" or "how does X work," not your tagline.
3. Check which rivals get named for each question. A scan logs this per question. In the same measurement, AI answers cited otterly.ai for 1 of the 11 buyer questions and rankscale.ai for 1 of the 11 — showing that even narrow, specific citations are how these answers work, not broad brand mentions.
4. Fix technical blockers first. A blocked AI reader, a missing title tag, no sitemap, or a slow page can keep a page out of consideration before content quality even matters.
5. Write one page per question, from facts on your own site. Each page should answer a single buyer question plainly, using real details — prices, features, specifics — rather than general claims, since AI answers need something concrete to point to.
6. Publish on your own domain, in your own voice. The page has to live where the facts live, so it can be checked and cited.
7. Review before it goes live. Every page should wait a day for you to read it — or stop it — before it goes live, so nothing goes out that doesn't represent your business accurately.
8. Re-measure weekly. A single scan tells you where you stand today; a weekly re-measure of your whole market tells you whether the pages you published are changing what AI says.

## How do you check whether it worked?

You check by re-running the same measurement and comparing the count of questions where you're named now against where you started. A single improvement — say, moving from 0 named questions to even 1 or 2 — is a real, countable signal, not a vague sense that things feel better.

Look specifically at the questions you targeted with new pages, not just an overall score. If you wrote a page answering a specific buyer question and AI still doesn't cite it a week or two later, that page likely needs stronger facts, clearer structure, or the underlying technical issue hasn't been fixed yet.

Also check which rivals still get named instead of you on those same questions. If a competitor keeps showing up for a question your new page targets, that tells you the gap between their page and yours — often it's specificity: a rival's page states a number or a feature plainly where yours still speaks in generalities.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

The most common mistake is writing content aimed at how your business wants to describe itself rather than the exact question a buyer typed. AI answers respond to the question asked, not to a slogan near it, so content needs to open with a direct answer to a stated question, not a brand pitch.

Another mistake is skipping the technical check. A page full of good facts still won't get read if AI readers are blocked, there's no sitemap, or the page loads too slowly — these are often invisible to a site owner but visible in a scan, so check this before you spend time writing.

A third mistake is publishing once and expecting a lasting result. AI answers shift as new pages get crawled across the whole market, including your rivals' pages, so a one-time push fades without a weekly re-measure to confirm the gain held and to catch new gaps opening up.

Last, don't skip reading what you publish. Rushing pages live without a review step risks a page that states something inaccurate about your own business — far worse for trust than being unranked.
