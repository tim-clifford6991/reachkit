## What Does It Mean to "Get Found" by ChatGPT and Google AI?

Being found isn't one event. As one guide puts it, "a site can be retrieved, a brand mentioned, a page cited, and a user clicked through: four different outcomes." [M39](https://www.dynadot.com/blog/how-to-get-found-in-chatgpt/) Underneath all four sits the same plumbing search has always used: "a page cannot rank until it is indexed." [M45](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)

### The three stages a page passes through

- "Crawled: Google has visited the page." [M44](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)
- "Indexed: Google has decided to store the page in its search database." [M44](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)
- "Ranked: Google has chosen to show the page for a relevant search." [M44](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)

## Why Isn't My Business Showing Up in AI Answers?

The behavior that changed is where buyers start. "Buyers now ask ChatGPT and Google's AI what to use, and the answer names a handful of companies." If yours isn't one of them, "that buyer never meets you — and nothing on your side tells you it happened." Meanwhile "your competitors are being found." The gap in the answer AI gives — "the empty one is you" — is a specific, findable gap, not a vague feeling. See the [ReachKit](https://reachkit.app/) product page for how the gap is measured.

## Does Traditional SEO Still Matter for AI Search?

Yes. AI answers still lean on the same signals classic search has always used. "Google says its generative AI features are rooted in core Search ranking and quality systems, while OpenAI and Microsoft still depend on accessible web content." [M40](https://www.dynadot.com/blog/how-to-get-found-in-chatgpt/) That said, you don't need to bolt on exotic AI-only formats: "Google's AI guidance says you do not need special AI schema, an AI-only version of your article, or an llms.txt file for Google AI search." [M50](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)

## Where Do AI Answers Actually Get Their Citations From?

Citations concentrate near the top of a page. A "February 2026 analysis of 18,012 verified ChatGPT citations found that 44.2% came from the top 30% of a page's content," and "a separate CXL analysis of Google AI Overviews found a similar distribution: 55% of citations came from the top 30% of a page." [M54](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/how-to-show-up-in-chatgpt-results), [M55](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/how-to-show-up-in-chatgpt-results)

Most citations, though, don't come from a brand's own site at all. "A McKinsey analysis found that only 5-10% of Google AI Overview citations come from a brand's own website." [M56](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/how-to-show-up-in-chatgpt-results) And the field is crowded at the top: research covering "50 million+ user journeys across ChatGPT, Copilot, Google AI Overview, and Perplexity" found that "among ChatGPT's top 10 most-cited domains, Wikipedia alone accounted for 40% of citations." [M57](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/how-to-show-up-in-chatgpt-results)

### What this means for your page

- Put the answer to the question near the top, not buried under scroll.
- Don't expect your own domain to carry every citation — but the ones you do earn are worth chasing on purpose.

## How Fast Is AI Search Traffic Actually Growing?

Fast enough to change where a first-time buyer starts looking. "Semrush reported 206% growth in outbound ChatGPT referral traffic from January 2025 to January 2026 in a 17-month clickstream analysis." [M42](https://www.dynadot.com/blog/how-to-get-found-in-chatgpt/) In one dataset behind that finding, ".COM, .ORG, .IO, and .AI pages each averaged 1.7 citations per cited URL during the study window." [M41](https://www.dynadot.com/blog/how-to-get-found-in-chatgpt/) Google is also now tracking this shift formally: "On June 3, 2026, Google announced dedicated Search Generative AI performance reports for Search and Discover, initially rolling them out to a subset of websites." [M43](https://www.dynadot.com/blog/how-to-get-found-in-chatgpt/)

## What Technical Basics Do You Need to Get Right?

Before a page can be cited, it has to be reachable by the right crawler. "A robots.txt file gives crawlers instructions about which areas of a website they may access," and "for AI search, permissions differ by service. For example, GPTBot relates to AI training, while OAI-SearchBot is used for ChatGPT search discovery." [M46](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search), [M47](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)

### Basic hygiene worth checking today

- "Keep URLs short, lowercase, descriptive, and hyphen-separated." [M48](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)
- "Ensure the site consistently uses either www or non-www. Ensure HTTP redirects to HTTPS." [M49](https://elhamgrowthstudio.substack.com/p/can-google-chatgpt-and-ai-search)

*Buyer questions, stored with the page (`drafts.meta.faq`):*

**What's the difference between ranking in Google and being cited by ChatGPT?**

Ranking is Google choosing to show your page for a search; being cited is an AI model quoting or naming you inside its generated answer. A page usually needs to be crawled and indexed before either can happen, but citation in an AI answer is a separate, additional outcome on top of ranking.

**Do I need special AI-only pages or an llms.txt file?**

No — Google's own AI guidance says you don't need special AI schema, an AI-only version of your article, or an llms.txt file for Google AI search. What matters more is having accessible, crawlable pages with the answer stated clearly near the top.

**Why does my competitor show up in ChatGPT and I don't?**

Buyers now ask ChatGPT and Google's AI what to use, and the answer names a handful of companies; if a rival's site answers the question you don't, the model has something of theirs to cite and nothing of yours. A scan can show you exactly which questions are naming them instead of you.

**Where in a page should the answer go to get cited?**

Near the top. Analyses of both ChatGPT and Google AI Overview citations found that a large share of citations — 44.2% and 55% respectively — came from the top 30% of a page's content, so burying the answer under scroll works against you.

**How much of my AI Overview traffic will actually land on my own site?**

Less than you'd expect for now: a McKinsey analysis found only 5-10% of Google AI Overview citations come from a brand's own website, with large reference sites like Wikipedia taking a big share of citations elsewhere. That's a reason to focus on the specific, narrow questions where your own page is genuinely the best source.
