:::answer
AI answers name the businesses whose pages answer the buyer's exact question plainly, with evidence, in a place the AI can read. If a rival has that page and you don't, or AI readers are blocked from your site, the answer names them. You can check this in minutes, and fix it with pages that answer those questions.
:::

**Key takeaways**

- An AI answer is assembled from pages it can find, read and quote. Being a better business does not help if no page of yours answers the question.
- Most searches on Google now end without a click, so the names inside the answer are often the only ones a buyer sees.
- The usual causes are a missing page, a page that reads like a brochure, or a page answer engines can't read. All three can be checked for free.
- Pages that add quotations, statistics and cited sources were quoted up to 40% more in a Princeton study. Keyword stuffing did not help.
- Write first for the questions where an AI answer already names a rival. That is where a buyer is being sent somewhere else today.

## Why do AI answers name my competitors and not me?

Because an AI answer is not a ranking of businesses. It is a summary of pages. When a buyer asks ChatGPT or Google's AI Mode which accountant, bike shop or CRM to use, the engine searches, picks a handful of pages that answer the question, and writes its reply from them. The businesses it names are the businesses those pages name.

So the useful question is not "why does the AI prefer them?" but "which page is it reading, and do I have one like it?" Usually the rival has a page that answers the buyer's question outright — prices, a comparison, a guide to choosing — while your site says what you do and never answers what the buyer typed.

ReachKit puts the cost of that plainly on its own home page: ["Buyers now ask ChatGPT and Google's AI what to use, and the answer names a handful of companies. If yours isn't one of them, that buyer never meets you — and nothing on your side tells you it happened."](https://reachkit.app/)

That last part is the problem: a lost ranking shows up in your reports, a lost mention in an AI answer shows up nowhere.

## Does this matter if my Google rankings are fine?

Yes, because fewer searches end in a click on any ranking at all. In the first four months of 2026, [68.01% of US Google searches ended without a click](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/), up from 60.45% in 2024, according to SparkToro's analysis of Similarweb panel data.

Where Google shows an AI Overview, the drop is steeper. Seer Interactive measured organic click-through at [2.94% on searches without an AI Overview and 0.84% on searches with one](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/how-ai-overviews-are-impacting-ctr-5-initial-takeaways). Brands cited as a source inside the Overview did better than brands that were not: 1.08% against 0.6%.

The visitors who do arrive from AI answers are worth more. Semrush found that [the average AI search visitor is 4.4 times as valuable as the average visit from traditional organic search](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-search-seo-traffic-study/), because they arrive having already asked their questions. Google says something similar about its own AI features:

> "We've seen that when people click to a website from search results pages with AI Overviews, these clicks are higher quality, where users are more likely to spend more time on the site." — John Mueller, Google Search Central, [21 May 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search)

Rankings still matter, because AI answers read the same web. But a ranking under an answer that names three rivals is worth less than it was.

## How do I check what AI tells buyers about my market?

Ask it the way a buyer would. It takes about ten minutes to do by hand:

1. Write down five questions a buyer asks before they choose a business like yours: "what does X cost", "best X for Y", "X or Z", "how do I choose an X".
2. Ask each one in ChatGPT and in Google (look at the AI Overview or AI Mode), logged out, from the country your buyers are in.
3. For each answer, note which businesses are named and which pages are linked as sources.
4. Open the source pages. Note what each one has that your site does not: a price, a table, a direct answer in the first paragraph.
5. Repeat monthly. Answers change as pages change.

Answers vary between runs, and five questions is a small sample, so treat the result as a sketch. A free ReachKit scan does the same at more scale; nothing in this guide needs it.

## What are the usual reasons a rival gets named instead of you?

| Reason | How to check | What fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| No page answers the question | Search your own site for the buyer's question. If no page answers it in its first paragraph, you have none. | A page per question, answering it first and backing the answer with facts. |
| Your page reads like a brochure | Read your service or product page. Are its headings questions a buyer asks, with a plain answer under each? | Rewrite the headings as questions and put a two- or three-sentence answer under each. |
| Answer engines can't read or quote you | Open yoursite.com/robots.txt and look for rules blocking Googlebot, OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot. Check your pages for a `nosnippet` or `noindex` tag. | Allow those readers and remove the tags. [Google's AI answers need only a page that is indexed and eligible for a snippet](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). |
| Nothing on the page can be quoted | Look for a figure, a date or a source on the page. A page with none gives the answer nothing to cite. | Add your own prices, turnaround times, results and a source for each claim you make. |
| Rivals are written about elsewhere | Ask who the answer's sources are. If they are review sites and roundups, the rival is named there. | Slower: get listed on the directories and roundups your market reads. |

The first four are on your own site and can be fixed this week. Work on the fifth after them.

## What makes a page something an AI answer will quote?

The best evidence so far comes from a Princeton study of generative engines, which tested changes to the same pages. [Adding quotations, statistics and cited sources gave a relative improvement of 30–40%](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) in how much of a page the engine used, while adding keywords gave little to no improvement. Ahrefs, looking at 1.4 million ChatGPT prompts, found that [cited pages had titles closer to the prompt](https://ahrefs.com/blog/why-chatgpt-cites-pages/) than pages that were not cited, and that pages with plain, readable addresses were cited 89.78% of the time against 81.11% for those without.

In practice that comes down to four habits:

- **Title the page with the buyer's question**, in the buyer's words. "How much does a bike service cost?" beats "Our services".
- **Answer in the first paragraph.** Two or three sentences a reader could lift out on their own.
- **Back it with evidence**: your own figures, dates and names, and a link for any claim that is not yours.
- **Keep it on your own domain**, at a plain address, linked from a page that already gets visits.

ReachKit's free report gives the same advice to every site it scans: ["AI answers quote pages that answer a question directly. For each page of yours that has search value but reads like a brochure: turn its headings into the questions a buyer would ask, put a direct answer of two or three sentences right under each heading, and add evidence — a figure, a date, a source you link to."](https://reachkit.app/scan/linear.app)

Google's own advice points the same way. Its guidance for AI Overviews and AI Mode asks site owners to ["focus on making unique, non-commodity content that visitors from Search and your own readers will find helpful and satisfying"](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/05/succeeding-in-ai-search). A page that only restates what ten other pages say is commodity content. Your prices, your process and your customers' results are not.

## Which pages should I write first?

Start where a buyer is being sent elsewhere today, and work outward:

1. **Questions where an AI answer already names a rival.** Each one is a buyer lost right now, and the rival's source page shows you what the answer wanted.
2. **Questions about your own offer**: what it costs, how it compares with a named alternative, who it suits. You can answer these better than anyone.
3. **Problems your product or service solves**, asked the way a buyer describes the problem rather than the way you describe the solution.
4. **Broad "best X" questions last**, and only where you belong on the list. Write them as an honest comparison that includes where you are not the right choice.

A page a week on the first two will do more than a page a day on the last one.

## Questions buyers ask

### Can I pay to be named in ChatGPT's answers?

Not into the answer itself. ChatGPT now shows ads to some users, but they are labelled as sponsored and set apart from the reply, and [OpenAI says advertisers do not influence the answer](https://openai.com/index/testing-ads-in-chatgpt/). An ad is a separate purchase. Being named in the answer comes from the pages the answer reads, and one of those can be yours.

### Should I block AI crawlers to protect my content?

Only if you would rather not be named. A blocked reader cannot quote you, so the answer will quote someone who let it in. You can refuse the crawlers that only collect training data, such as GPTBot, and still allow the ones that answer questions. For Google, [blocking Google-Extended limits training in some of its other systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features); AI Overviews follow Googlebot and your snippet settings.
